Hotels around Duomo, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Duomo that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

Milan’s hotel bill changed twice in 2026. From 1 January the city set new rates for its tourist tax under a decree-law that let cities near the Milano Cortina Winter Olympics adjust the tax for the year, and from 1 April it reset them by hotel category: €12 a person a night at a five-star, €10 at a four-star, €7 at a three-star. A couple spending four nights in a four-star hotel pays €80 on top of the room. Where you stay decides the rest: how much of the centre you walk, and how many metro rides you take.

This guide covers six areas, from the Duomo to Porta Venezia, 12 hotels, the airport links and the tax. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

Milan’s city tax in 2026

Every hotel collects the imposta di soggiorno for the Comune di Milano, per person and per night, and the rate follows the hotel’s star rating. The Comune published new rates from 1 January 2026 and redefined them from 1 April; these are the rates in force for stays in October 2026:

Where you stay Per person, per night
Five-star hotel €12
Four-star hotel, and four-star tourist residence €10
Three-star hotel, and three-star tourist residence €7
Two-star hotel, and two-star tourist residence €4
One-star hotel €3
Holiday houses (case per ferie) €7
Youth hostels and campsites €3
B&Bs, guest rooms, holiday homes and short-term lets €9.50

From 1 January to 31 March the Comune’s rates were different: €10 at four- and five-star hotels, €7.40 at three-star, €5 at two-star and €4 at one-star, with the same €9.50 for B&Bs and short-term lets. The Comune said in November 2025 that this adjustment was valid only for 2026, under decree-law 156 of 29 October 2025. When we checked in October 2026 we found no Comune decision on 2027 rates, so check the rate again if you book for next year.

The tax is charged on non-residents. Children and teenagers under 18 pay nothing, and so do people with disabilities and their companions, and university students of 26 or under, enrolled at a Milan university, who are staying in student residences. At a hotel there is no limit on the number of nights; the 14-night limit in the Comune’s regulation applies only to residences, non-profit holiday houses and holiday apartments.

By our arithmetic, a couple staying four nights pays €80 at a four-star (2 × 4 × €10), €56 at a three-star and €96 at a five-star. A couple with a 12-year-old pays the same €80 at a four-star, because the child is exempt.

One warning: the Comune’s own customer-support page on the tax still shows older, lower figures. The rate sheet the Comune published for 1 April 2026, linked under the sources below, is the one to trust.

Getting in from the airports, and around

  • From Malpensa, the Malpensa Express runs to Milano Centrale in 51 minutes, calling at Porta Garibaldi, and to Milano Cadorna in 37 minutes. Trains leave every 30 minutes from each station and serve both terminals. A one-way ticket costs €15 and a return €25; children aged 4 to 13 pay €7.50, and under-4s travel free with a paying parent.
  • By taxi from Malpensa, the Comune’s tariff sheet sets a fixed fare of €114 to any address in Milan, with no supplements.
  • From Linate, the M4 metro line (blue) takes 12 minutes between the airport and the centre, on an ordinary ATM ticket at €2.20. Taxis from Linate are metered, with a minimum fare of €16 for trips that start at the airport; the Comune’s list of fixed fares has no Linate-to-centre fare.
  • From Bergamo, buses link Milan Bergamo Airport with Milano Centrale: the airport lists Autostradale, Flibco, Orioshuttle and Terravision. Autostradale runs every 30 minutes, takes 50 to 60 minutes and shows fares from €9, labelled a limited-period promotion. A taxi between Orio al Serio and Milan is a fixed €128.
  • In town, an ATM ticket costs €2.20 and lasts 90 minutes on metro, bus and tram; a day ticket is €7.60 and a three-day ticket €15.50. Children under 14 travel free. You can tap a contactless card or phone at the metro gates, in and out, and on ATM vehicles, but not on the S lines or regional trains.

The Duomo and the Centro Storico: the first-trip base

The Duomo is the centre of Milan in every sense: the cathedral, the Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II with its glass roof, La Scala and the shopping streets of the fashion Quadrilateral are all within a short walk, and metro lines M1 and M3 meet at the Duomo station. Leonardo’s Last Supper is across town, but the Duomo area is where most first trips start. It is busy by day and quieter after the shops close.

It has a bar, G Bar, a restaurant, Le Noir, and a terrace lounge, Aria. Choose it for a small, stylish hotel next to the cathedral. It is a two-minute walk from the Duomo metro station and six minutes from the cathedral and La Scala, with a buffet breakfast, free Wi-Fi and a 24-hour reception. Choose it for art on the walls and a family-run welcome. Watch out: the hotel calls its own style “charmingly a little vintage”. Check rates on Booking.com →

The hotel says its rates include a buffet breakfast, Wi-Fi, the soft drinks in the minibar and taxes. Choose it for the centre at a three-star price. Brera and La Scala: art and quiet streets

North-west of the Duomo, Brera is the district around the Pinacoteca di Brera, the art gallery at Via Brera 28, which expanded into Palazzo Citterio in December 2024. It is a quieter base than the Duomo, with La Scala and the Quadrilateral nearby.

Where to stay in Brera and La Scala

  • Mandarin Oriental, Milan, splurge. A five-star on Via Andegari, which the hotel places steps from La Scala, Piazza del Duomo, Via Montenapoleone and Via della Spiga, with an Italian restaurant, Seta, and and Mandarin Garden, which serves cocktails and Italian fare. Choose it for the opera house and the shops at the door. Watch out: a five-star pays the top city tax, €12 a person a night, on top of five-star rates. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Urban Hive Milano, comfortable. A four-star on Corso Garibaldi with 97 rooms, each inspired by a traditional Milanese parlour, a café and bar, Portico84, a fitness area, terraces, a library and a 24-hour reception. Choose it for a modern base on Brera’s edge. Watch out: the hotel sells its smallest room, La Mini, for solo travellers and short stays; for two people it points to La Cosy. Check rates on Booking.com →

Navigli: evenings by the canal

The Navigli are the old canals on the south-west side, and the Naviglio Grande is the one that matters for visitors: its banks are lined with bars, restaurants and clubs, and the Tortona district of design showrooms is close by. The Duomo is about 2 km away by Art Hotel Navigli’s count, so the metro on line M2 at Porta Genova does the work, and the Malpensa Express to Cadorna connects to M2 for the same stop.

Where to stay in Navigli

  • Maison Borella, comfortable. A four-star in a former “balustrade home” with a courtyard, overlooking the Naviglio Grande on Alzaia Naviglio Grande, in a pedestrian area, 300 metres from Porta Genova on M2 and a five-minute walk from it, by the hotel’s directions. The rooms have parquet floors and exposed beams, and the hotel calls them soundproof. Choose it for a canal-side address with a quiet courtyard. Watch out: the Maison Privée rooms are in a separate old railing house 20 metres from the main building. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Art Hotel Navigli, good value. On Via Angelo Fumagalli, five minutes from Porta Genova by the hotel’s count, with a contemporary art collection, a roof garden on the sixth floor, a garden and an internal garage. Choose it for a modern Navigli base near Via Tortona and the MUDEC. Watch out: it is about 2 km from the Duomo, so plan on the metro. Check rates on Booking.com →

Porta Nuova: the new skyline

Porta Nuova is the part of Milan rebuilt since the 1990s: the Bosco Verticale towers, Piazza Gae Aulenti with its restaurants, and Corso Como for shopping and nightlife. The neighbouring Isola, which the Dorchester Collection describes as a working-class district before the building boom, has vintage shops and street art. It suits travellers who prefer a modern city to a medieval one; the old centre is a short metro ride or a long walk away.

Where to stay in Porta Nuova

  • Hotel Principe di Savoia, splurge. A five-star Dorchester Collection hotel on Piazza della Repubblica, which the hotel places in the centre of Porta Nuova, two minutes from Repubblica metro station and 10 minutes from Milano Centrale. The Classic Room is 27 m² for up to two guests, and there is a restaurant, Acanto, a lounge, Il Salotto, and Principe Bar. Choose it for grand-hotel service by the station and Porta Nuova. Watch out: it is a five-star, so the city tax is €12 a person a night. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • NH Collection Milano Porta Nuova, comfortable. On Via Melchiorre Gioia, with 100 rooms renovated in 2018, the smallest 27 m², 600 metres from Porta Garibaldi station and a short walk from Gioia metro. By the hotel’s count, Corso Como is five minutes away, Brera 10 and the centre 15 on foot. Choose it for large rooms and the new district at the door. Watch out: on-site parking costs €40 a day. Check rates on Booking.com →

Centrale: trains, the airport and lower prices

Milano Centrale is where the Malpensa Express arrives, the Bergamo buses stop and metro lines M2 and M3 meet; it is also where the main-line and high-speed trains leave for other Italian cities. The streets around the station are busier than charming, but for an early train, a day trip by rail or a lower price it is the most convenient base, and the Duomo is a metro ride away on M3.

Where to stay near Milano Centrale

  • Starhotels Anderson, comfortable. A four-star on Piazza Luigi di Savoia, facing the station, with 106 rooms and suites, a restaurant and a bar, both called Black. Choose it for a design hotel with the trains across the square. Watch out: the Duomo and Brera are a metro ride away, not a walk. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Ostello Bello Grande Milan Central Station, budget. A hostel on Via Roberto Lepetit, 100 metres from the station, with private rooms, a guests’ kitchen with a terrace, workstations, free luggage storage before check-in at any hour and self-service lockers after check-out. Choose it for the lowest price in this guide, beside the airport train. Watch out: the bar is open 24 hours and runs live music and DJ sets, so light sleepers should ask for a quiet room. Check rates on Booking.com →

Porta Venezia: gardens and Corso Buenos Aires

East of the Quadrilateral, Porta Venezia is the district of the Giardini Pubblici, the public gardens, with the Natural History Museum, and of Corso Buenos Aires, a shopping street. It is calmer than the Duomo, and metro line M3 stops at Turati, two stops from Centrale and two from the Duomo by the hotel’s count.

Where to stay in Porta Venezia

  • Hotel Manin, comfortable. A four-star on Via Manin, facing the Giardini Pubblici, owned by the Colombo family since 1904, with 124 rooms, a private garden, a free gym, and Turati metro station 100 metres away. By the hotel’s count, the Duomo is 10 to 15 minutes on foot. Choose it for a historic family hotel by the park. Watch out: the restaurant is closed at lunchtime on Saturdays and Sundays, and the nearby garage starts at €42 a night. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Milan

  • Count the tax. It is per person, per night, and set by the stars: €12 at a five-star, €10 at a four-star, €7 at a three-star. Under-18s pay nothing. Gran Duca di York says taxes are included in its prices; ask whether that covers the city tax, and compare totals.
  • Book the Last Supper first. Leonardo’s Last Supper museum says reservation is mandatory for every ticket; it is open Tuesday to Sunday, and the September to December 2026 tickets went on sale on 23 June. Fix the date, then choose the hotel.
  • Check the room size. Among the hotels here, the Principe di Savoia’s Classic Room and the smallest rooms at the NH Collection Porta Nuova are both 27 m². The cheapest category at Urban Hive is for one person.
  • Leave the car. Parking is an extra: the NH Collection charges €40 a day on site, and the garage near Hotel Manin starts at €42 a night. The airports have trains and a metro.
  • Compare totals. Hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book. Italy’s other tourist cities charge their own tax: see where to stay in Rome, Florence and Venice.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Milan for a first trip?

The Duomo and the Centro Storico: the cathedral, the Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II and La Scala are within a short walk, and metro lines M1 and M3 meet at the Duomo. Choose Brera for quieter streets, Navigli for evenings by the canal, Porta Nuova for the new skyline and Centrale for early trains and the airport link.

How much is the city tax in Milan in 2026?

From 1 April 2026 it is €12 a person a night at a five-star hotel, €10 at a four-star, €7 at a three-star, €4 at a two-star and €3 at a one-star, and €9.50 at B&Bs and short-term lets. Under-18s pay nothing. A couple spending four nights in a four-star hotel pays €80 on top of the room.

How do you get from Malpensa Airport to central Milan?

Take the Malpensa Express to Milano Centrale (51 minutes, via Porta Garibaldi) or Milano Cadorna (37 minutes), every 30 minutes from each station, for €15 one way or €25 return. A taxi to any address in Milan is a fixed €114 under the Comune's tariff sheet.

How do you get from Linate Airport to the centre of Milan?

Take the M4 metro line (blue), which takes 12 minutes between the airport and the centre, on an ordinary ATM ticket at €2.20. Taxis from Linate are metered, with a minimum fare of €16 for trips that start at the airport; there is no fixed fare to the centre.

Is it better to stay near Milano Centrale or near the Duomo?

Near the Duomo for a first trip on foot; near Centrale for early trains, the Malpensa Express and the lowest prices in this guide. The Duomo is a metro ride away on M3 from Centrale. Hotel Manin in Porta Venezia and the Porta Nuova hotels sit in between.

Do children pay the tourist tax in Milan?

No. Children and teenagers under 18 are exempt from the imposta di soggiorno, so a couple with a 12-year-old pays the same €80 as the couple alone for four nights at a four-star hotel. Hotels charge the tax for the whole stay, with no limit on nights.
