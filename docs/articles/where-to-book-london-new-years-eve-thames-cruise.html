Three things separate one New Year’s Eve boat on the Thames from the next, and the headline price is only one of them: what kind of night it is (a seated dinner or a standing party), how long you can cancel for, and whether the listing promises a fireworks view or only a position near one. On the night we priced, 31 December 2026, the cruises we read ran from about $273 for a boat party with one welcome drink to about $706 for a four-course dinner sailing, and the gap is mostly explained by those lines.

Two facts about the market change how you should buy. First, many listings are already gone: several Viator pages say “Sorry, this product is unavailable” or “isn’t bookable for now”, two GetYourGuide pages return a “page isn’t here” error, and both KKday listings we found show SOLD OUT. Second, most of the cruises that are still open cannot be cancelled. Of the five listings we could price, three are non-refundable, so the cancellation line deserves more attention than the price.

    GetYourGuide: London: NYE Masked Ball Boat Party with Fireworks. From $273, 5 hours, run by 1201 Events as the Pukka Up “Masked Ball”.     Which listing is the safe, quick buy

    For most readers it is GetYourGuide’s Masked Ball boat party. It is the cheapest cruise we could read that mentions a midnight fireworks view, it has free cancellation up to 24 hours before, and you can reserve now and pay later, so you are not committing cash today. That combination matters most on a date when forecasts, trains and plans can still shift.

    Say where the others win, plainly. If you want a seated dinner, the Viator City Cruises dinner is the cheaper of the two pages we found for what looks like the same sailing, at $665.43 against $706.45 on GetYourGuide, and both are non-refundable. If cancellation flexibility matters most, Tea on the Thames on GetYourGuide has the longest window (14 days) and drinks included, from $523. If you want a boat leaving from Westminster, the London Rose does, with unlimited drinks, but it is non-refundable. What we could not read on any listing is a refund term if the fireworks are cancelled or the view is poor, so treat each ticket as a seat on a boat, not a guarantee of a particular view.

    What decides the view

    • The boat’s position is not yours to choose. The Masked Ball listing says the boat takes its position for the fireworks; the Viator and GetYourGuide dinner cruises describe a spot near Big Ben and the London Eye. Reviewers on the GetYourGuide dinner listing describe a slightly off-centre view and, in an earlier year, a view partly behind a bridge. Expect a good view of the display, not a perfect centre.
    • Where you stand on board. Tea on the Thames says the fireworks view is from the open top deck, so you spend part of the night outside. Wear layers.
    • Boarding is strict. The Rose listing says no refunds for those who miss the boat, and Tea on the Thames says latecomers cannot be accommodated once the vessel departs. Leave early.
    • Getting home. Tea on the Thames warns that transport is very limited on New Year’s Eve and tells guests to plan night buses or a pre-booked taxi. Most cruises return between about 1am and 1:30am.
    • A cruise ticket is its own ticket. None of the listings we read is a ticket to any land-based viewing area; each is a place on a boat.

    Ages, dress and the small print

    Age limits differ by listing. The Masked Ball is 18 and over, the London Rose does not allow under 18s (with ID checks if you look under 25), and Tea on the Thames lists adults only and not suitable under 16. The City Cruises dinner on Viator is not recommended for children aged five and under, and GetYourGuide’s version of it is the only listing we read flagged as wheelchair accessible; the others say they are not suitable for wheelchair users or that stairs may make access difficult. The Masked Ball and the dinner cruise both ask you to dress up (a mask for the Ball, no jeans or sneakers for the City Cruises dinner). Luggage and large bags are not allowed on the Rose.

    Pier details matter. The Masked Ball boards at Greenwich and finishes at Blackfriars, two different piers. The City Cruises dinner starts and ends at Tower Pier. The Rose leaves from and returns to Westminster Millennium Pier. Look at the meeting-point line on your listing and set a route before the day.

    Frequently asked questions

    Which New Year's Eve Thames cruise is the safest to book?

    Of the listings we read, GetYourGuide's Masked Ball boat party: $273 on 31 December 2026, free cancellation up to 24 hours ahead, reserve now and pay later, adults only.

    What is the cheapest New Year's Eve cruise in London?

    On 31 December 2026 the lowest price we read was $273 for the Masked Ball boat party on GetYourGuide, then $484 for the London Rose, $523 for the Tea on the Thames fireworks cruise, and $665.43 on Viator for the City Cruises dinner.

    Can I cancel a New Year's Eve cruise?

    Some can. The Masked Ball allows free cancellation up to 24 hours ahead and Tea on the Thames up to 14 days ahead. The Viator and GetYourGuide dinner cruises and the London Rose are non-refundable.

    Is the fireworks view guaranteed?

    No listing we read guarantees a particular view. The Masked Ball says the boat takes its position for the fireworks; the dinner cruises describe a spot near Big Ben and the London Eye, and reviewers on one of them mention an off-centre or partly blocked view.

    Are any cruises sold out?

    Both KKday London New Year's Eve pages we found showed SOLD OUT, and several Viator listings were unavailable on 27 November 2026. The GetYourGuide cruises above showed 31 December as bookable that day.

    Can children go?

    Mostly not. The Masked Ball is 18 and over, the London Rose does not allow under 18s, and Tea on the Thames is adults only. Viator's City Cruises dinner is not recommended for children aged five and under.
