Hotels around Santa Monica, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Santa Monica that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

A Los Angeles hotel bill depends on which city the hotel stands in. The City of Los Angeles, which covers Downtown, Hollywood and Venice, charges 14%; Santa Monica charges 15%, West Hollywood 12.5% and El Segundo 12%, and some add a district assessment. On a $300 room that is between $336 and $352.25 a night, by our arithmetic below, before any daily resort fee. The larger cost is distance: the sights are spread out, so where you stay decides how much of the trip you spend in transit.

This guide covers six areas, from Santa Monica to El Segundo, 12 hotels, the airport links and the taxes. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

Los Angeles has no single centre. Metro’s own times show the scale: Downtown to the airport’s Metro station is about 45 to 55 minutes by rail, and Downtown Santa Monica 55 to 60. Santa Monica and Downtown are on Metro’s E Line and Hollywood on the B Line; Venice, West Hollywood and El Segundo mean buses, rides or a car. Plan one area a day.

Los Angeles hotel taxes in 2026

Each city sets its own rate:

City Hotel tax Added assessment
City of Los Angeles (Downtown, Hollywood, Venice) 14% of the room rate Hotels of 50 or more rooms: a Tourism Marketing District assessment, 2% under the district’s 2025–2034 plan
Santa Monica 15% A Tourism Marketing District assessment per room night, set by the hotel’s average rate
West Hollywood 12.5% 3% Tourism Improvement District assessment, which may be passed on
El Segundo 12% None listed on the city’s page

The Office of Finance states the 14% rate. The Tourism Marketing District is a hotel assessment, not a tax: its 2025 to 2034 plan, filed with the City Clerk, sets an initial rate of 2% for hotels of 50 or more rooms, lets the district raise it to 3%, and says an assessment passed on to guests must be shown separately. Santa Monica’s 2026 rates are $2.30 a room night for hotels averaging $100 to $199.99, $6.25 for $200 to $299.99, and $7.25 above $300.

What changed in 2026. On 2 June Los Angeles voters rejected Proposition TT, which would have raised the city rate from 14% to 16% through 2028; the certified count was 364,855 yes and 409,260 no, so 14% stands. Proposition TC, on online travel companies, passed 451,741 to 331,644; we have not found whether it changes what a guest pays.

Our arithmetic for a $300 room, if the hotel passes it all on:

  • City of Los Angeles, hotel of 50 or more rooms: $42.00 plus $6.00, so $348.00 a night.
  • Santa Monica, hotel averaging over $300: $45.00 plus up to $7.25, so $352.25.
  • West Hollywood, hotel in the district: $37.50 plus $9.00, so $346.50.
  • El Segundo: $36.00, so $336.00.

Daily fees. Many hotels add a daily destination, amenity or resort fee; check the hotel’s own. Hotel Casa del Mar’s guide lists a $60 resort fee plus 15% tax, on top of nightly district charges of $7.25, $1.75 and $2.00. The Hollywood Roosevelt lists a $37 destination fee inclusive of tax, Hotel Figueroa a $35 amenity fee plus tax, and Chamberlain West Hollywood $40 plus taxes and a separate $10 city worker protection fee. The pages we read for Hotel Erwin, Sea Blue Hotel and Magic Castle Hotel list none.

Getting in from LAX, and around

  • Taxis and ride apps pick up at LAX-it, beside Terminal 1: a 3 to 8 minute walk from Terminals 1 and 7 and about 19 from Terminals 4 and 5, or take the free LAX-it shuttle from the green sign on the arrivals level.
  • By Metro, the LAX/Metro Transit Center opened on 6 June 2025, on the C Line and the K Line. A free shuttle runs every 10 minutes between its Bus Bays 1 and 2 and every terminal; Metro’s blog calls it temporary until the LAX people mover opens, which NBC Los Angeles reported on 24 September 2026 had slipped to 6 January 2027.
  • Metro’s fare is $1.75 a ride, with free transfers for two hours and caps of $5 a day and $18 a week when you tap.
  • To Downtown or Santa Monica, take the K Line to Expo/Crenshaw and change to the E Line: about 45 to 55 minutes to 7th St/Metro Center and 55 to 60 to Downtown Santa Monica, by Metro’s count.
  • The FlyAway bus runs nonstop between LAX and Union Station, and Van Nuys, for $12.75 one way, from the blue FlyAway columns on the arrivals level; from Union Station the B Line heads for Hollywood.
  • Around town, the E Line runs from East Los Angeles to Santa Monica and the B Line from Union Station to North Hollywood; Line 33 links Santa Monica and Downtown by way of Venice Boulevard, and Lines 10 and 16 West Hollywood and Downtown.

Santa Monica: the pier and the beach

Santa Monica is its own city, with the pier and Pacific Park, Third Street Promenade and a long beach below Palisades Park. It suits a trip without a car best: the E Line runs to Downtown, and the pier, the Promenade and the sand are on foot from the hotels below. Its base tax is the highest here, 15%.

Venice: the Boardwalk

Venice is part of the City of Los Angeles, so the 14% rate applies. Its centre is the Boardwalk, with its stalls, Muscle Beach and skatepark. There is no rail here; Metro’s Line 33 runs between Santa Monica and Downtown by way of Venice Boulevard, and Hotel Erwin puts LAX about 7 miles away.

Where to stay in Venice

  • Hotel Erwin, comfortable. At 1697 Pacific Avenue, steps from the Boardwalk, with 86 rooms, 33 suites, many with balconies, and the Kassi rooftop bar. Choose it for a balcony by the beach. Watch out: valet parking is $60 a vehicle a night. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Samesun Venice Beach, budget. A hostel at 25 Windward Avenue, with the Boardwalk at its door, dorms, private rooms, female-only dorms, free breakfast and a 24-hour reception. Choose it to keep money for the trip. Watch out: dorm rooms are shared; book a private room for your own. Check rates on Booking.com →

Hollywood: the Walk of Fame

Hollywood is the old film district, with the Walk of Fame along Hollywood Boulevard. It is the base for Universal Studios too: Magic Castle Hotel’s site says the studios are one Metro stop away, and the B Line runs on to Union Station.

Where to stay in Hollywood

  • The Hollywood Roosevelt, comfortable. At 7000 Hollywood Boulevard, built in 1927 and host of the first Academy Awards by its own account, with the Tropicana Pool, the restaurant Gable and the bowling-lane bar The Spare Room. Choose it for the history and the pool. Watch out: a $37 daily destination fee, inclusive of tax, and valet parking at $79 a night. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Magic Castle Hotel, good value. At 7025 Franklin Avenue, in a former apartment building, with a heated pool at 88°F, free snacks, free laundry and a Popsicle Hotline. Choose it for the extras at a fair price. Watch out: access to the private Magic Castle club comes with direct bookings and a two-night minimum, so a booking-site rate may not include it. Check rates on Booking.com →

West Hollywood: the Sunset Strip

West Hollywood is a small city of its own, with the restaurants, bars and billboards of the Sunset Strip along Sunset Boulevard. It has its own tax, 12.5% plus a 3% district assessment, and buses to Downtown, but no train; most visitors rely on rides.

Where to stay in West Hollywood

  • Sunset Tower Hotel, splurge. At 8358 Sunset Boulevard, with floor-to-ceiling windows, the Tower Bar, a guests-only pool and 24-hour room service; Superior Queen rooms are about 350 sq ft. Choose it for glamour on the Strip. Watch out: the pages we read give no fee table, so ask for the total with any daily fee. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Chamberlain West Hollywood, comfortable. At 1000 Westmount Drive, one block off Sunset, with 115 all-suite rooms, private balconies and fireplaces, a rooftop pool and Deluxe Suites of 350 sq ft. Choose it for a suite off the Strip. Watch out: a $40 amenity fee plus taxes, a $10 city worker protection fee, and valet parking at $70 a night. Check rates on Booking.com →

Downtown: the rail hub

Downtown is the base with the most trains: Union Station, the FlyAway bus and the E Line’s 7th St/Metro Center all sit here. It is offices, museums and hotels rather than beaches, in the City of Los Angeles at 14%.

Where to stay Downtown

  • Hotel Figueroa, good value. At 939 S. Figueroa Street, restored in the style of its 1926 original, steps from Crypto.com Arena and the Convention Center, with Café Fig and Bar Magnolia. Choose it for history near the arena. Watch out: every booking carries a $35 amenity fee plus tax a night, and valet parking is $62 plus tax. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • The Hoxton, Downtown LA, comfortable. At 1060 S. Broadway, with 174 rooms and a rooftop pool over Broadway with Inanna Bar. Choose it for style and a pool. Watch out: valet parking is $66 a night. Check rates on Booking.com →

El Segundo: by the airport

El Segundo is a separate city beside the airport, with the lowest base tax here, 12%. It suits a late arrival, an early flight or an event at SoFi Stadium or the Intuit Dome, but not sightseeing: Hilton puts the Santa Monica Pier 9.5 miles from its hotel here.

Where to stay in El Segundo

  • Hampton Inn & Suites LAX-El Segundo, good value. At 888 N. Pacific Coast Highway, one mile from LAX and under five miles from SoFi Stadium and Manhattan Beach, with free hot breakfast, free Wi-Fi and an indoor pool. Choose it for a night by a flight. Watch out: self-parking is $30 a day, and the sights are a ride away. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Los Angeles

  • Count the tax for the city. Add 14%, 15%, 12.5% or 12% by city, plus any assessment the hotel passes on: a $300 room costs between $336 and $352.25.
  • Add the daily fee and the parking. Parking is a second bill: from $30 a night at the Hampton Inn and $33 at Sea Blue Hotel to $79 at The Hollywood Roosevelt and $90 at Hotel Casa del Mar and Santa Monica Proper Hotel.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Los Angeles for a first trip?

Santa Monica suits a first trip best without a car: the pier, Third Street Promenade and the beach are on foot from the hotels, and Metro's E Line runs to Downtown. Choose Hollywood for the Walk of Fame, West Hollywood for restaurants and nightlife, and Downtown for the trains.

How much is the hotel tax in Los Angeles?

It depends on the city. The City of Los Angeles, which covers Downtown, Hollywood and Venice, charges 14%, and hotels of 50 or more rooms also pay a 2% Tourism Marketing District assessment that may be passed on. Santa Monica charges 15%, West Hollywood 12.5% plus a 3% assessment, and El Segundo 12%. On a $300 room that is between $336 and $352.25 a night.

How do you get from LAX to your hotel?

Taxis and ride apps pick up at LAX-it, beside Terminal 1, a 3 to 8 minute walk from Terminals 1 and 7 or a free shuttle ride from the arrivals level. By rail, a free shuttle runs every 10 minutes to the LAX/Metro Transit Center, then Metro costs $1.75 a ride. The FlyAway bus to Union Station or Van Nuys is $12.75 one way.

Do you need a car in Los Angeles?

Not always, but the sights are spread out. Santa Monica and Downtown sit on Metro's E Line and Hollywood on the B Line, but Venice, West Hollywood and El Segundo mean buses, rides or a car. By Metro's count the rail trip from Downtown to the airport station takes about 45 to 55 minutes.

Did Los Angeles raise its hotel tax in 2026?

No. On 2 June 2026 voters rejected Proposition TT, which would have raised the city rate from 14% to 16% through 2028; the certified count was 364,855 yes to 409,260 no. A separate measure about online travel companies, Proposition TC, passed.

Do Los Angeles hotels charge resort fees?

Many add a daily fee, so check the hotel's own. Hotel Casa del Mar lists a $60 resort fee plus 15% tax, The Hollywood Roosevelt a $37 destination fee inclusive of tax, Hotel Figueroa a $35 amenity fee plus tax, and Chamberlain West Hollywood $40 plus taxes and a $10 city worker protection fee.
