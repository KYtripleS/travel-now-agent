The headline price is the least useful number on a Reykjavik aurora tour. Three lines matter more: what happens if the sky stays dark, how big the vehicle is, and whether the tour runs on your night at all. That last one catches people over Christmas. On the listings we opened, several do not sell 24 December, so we priced Saturday 26 December 2026 instead and say below which listings we could see for the 24th.

Doing the Golden Circle by day? Golden Circle tours compared sets the bus, hotel-pickup and Secret Lagoon versions side by side. Need a bed? Where to stay in Reykjavik for the northern lights compares city bases with countryside hotels.

One thing has to come first, because it changes what you are buying. The aurora is never guaranteed. Visit Iceland, the national tourism site, says there are no guarantees you will see the Northern Lights during your stay, and that you need dark, clear skies. What a tour sells is a guide who drives towards gaps in the cloud, plus terms for a second try. Those terms are the part that differs between sellers, and we quote them as each listing words them.

    Which listing is the safe, quick buy

    For most readers it is the GetYourGuide bus tour with hot cocoa. It has by far the largest review record of the listings we read (15,654), it meets in the city centre at Harpa, free cancellation runs to 24 hours before, and its free-retry wording is spelled out on the page, including an open ticket valid for 3 years if you leave Iceland first. That last point suits a short stay.

    Say where the others win, plainly. KKday is the lowest price we read at €72.98 and shows a child fare, but its listing carries a 2.3 rating from 8 reviews, needs four travellers to depart and cancels free only up to a day ahead. GetYourGuide’s premium minibus is the pick if you want hotel pickup and a smaller vehicle and can accept $33 more than the bus, noting that its retry is on the bus tour. Viator’s Local Hunters states a cap of 16 and showed a night on 24 December, but has only 17 reviews. Viator’s Midnight Adventure states a cap of 18, includes photos and has 1,011 reviews at 4.8, but says operated tours are not refundable. The cruise is the one to pick for a family with young children, because infants are free and children have a discounted fare, and it needs no long road trip.

    What decides whether you see the lights

    • Season. Visit Iceland says the main season runs from September through April. A late-December date is inside that window.
    • Clear sky and darkness. The same page says you need dark, clear skies, and that sightings are greatly improved outside populated areas. A chase tour is a ride towards a gap in the cloud, not a trip to a fixed viewing spot.
    • Give yourself more than one night. Nobody can say weeks ahead which evening will be clear. The retry terms above are what make a second night possible, but each has conditions: a bus-tour retry on GetYourGuide, another date on KKday, and a boat re-booking on Viator’s cruise.
    • A cold night. The listings run from about 2 to 5 hours, and the time you get back depends on the drive and on activity. Plan the next morning loosely.

    Clothing, children and the small print

    Dress for standing still. GetYourGuide’s bus listing asks for warm clothing and comfortable shoes, and KKday recommends thermal layers, a warm cap, gloves, warm socks and sturdy shoes, and notes that winter paths can be icy. The cruise provides warm floatable overalls. The KKday coach listing warns that modern facilities including restrooms may not be available along the route, which is worth knowing before you choose a bus over a boat.

    Age limits differ. GetYourGuide’s bus tour is not suitable under 6; its premium minibus takes no children under 8; KKday’s minimum is 6; the Viator listings we read gave no minimum age, and the cruise lists free infant fares from 0 to 5. If you are booking for a family, check the age line on the listing before paying.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is a northern lights sighting guaranteed on a Reykjavik tour?

    No. Visit Iceland says there are no guarantees you will see the Northern Lights during your stay. The listings offer retry terms, which differ, and none of them promises lights.

    Which seller is the safest to book?

    Of the listings we read, GetYourGuide's Northern Lights Bus Tour with Hot Cocoa: $83.41 on 26 December 2026, 4.2 from 15,654 reviews, free cancellation up to 24 hours ahead and a free retry tour written into the listing.

    What is the cheapest northern lights tour from Reykjavik?

    On 26 December 2026 the lowest price we read was €72.98 on KKday's coach tour, then €97.52 for Viator's boat cruise and €106.81 each for Viator's Local Hunters minibus. GetYourGuide's prices were in US dollars, at $83.41 for the bus and $116.34 for the premium minibus.

    Do tours run on 24 December?

    Some do not. On the dates we opened, GetYourGuide's two tours and Viator's Midnight Adventure and cruise were not available for 24 December, while KKday's coach tour showed a price and Viator offered the Local Hunters small group. Check your own date on the listing.

    What happens if we see no lights?

    It depends on the listing. GetYourGuide's bus tour offers one free retry bus tour, or an open ticket valid for 3 years if you are leaving Iceland; Viator's listings say free retry or re-trial; KKday says you may participate again on another date but the tour is non-refundable once you have joined.

    Can children join a northern lights tour?

    Rules differ. GetYourGuide's bus tour is not suitable under 6 and its premium minibus takes no one under 8; KKday's minimum age is 6; Viator's cruise lists infants 0 to 5 as free and children 6 to 15 at a discount.
