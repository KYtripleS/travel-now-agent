London is a vast collection of villages that happened to grow into one another. For travelers, this means there is no single "downtown." Where you sleep determines which side of London you see at breakfast and how many Tube transfers you face at the end of the night. Choosing the right neighbourhood is the single most important logistical decision of your trip.

First, choose the Tube line

In London, the Underground (the Tube) is your lifeline. To make your arrival and departure stress-free, prioritize hotels near stations on these three key lines:

  1. The Elizabeth Line: The fastest way from Heathrow to Paddington, Tottenham Court Road, and Liverpool Street.
  2. The Piccadilly Line: The direct, slower-but-cheaper Tube link from Heathrow to South Kensington, Covent Garden, and King's Cross.
  3. The Jubilee Line: Fast connections between the West End, Waterloo, and the South Bank.

Being within a five-minute walk of a station on one of these lines will save you hours over a week-long trip.

Covent Garden: the beating heart

Covent Garden is the most popular choice for first-time visitors for a reason. You are in the West End, surrounded by theatres, the Royal Opera House, and the British Museum (a 10-minute walk). It is superbly connected via the Piccadilly Line and the Leicester Square hub.

South Kensington: museums and quiet

South Kensington is where you find the Victoria & Albert, the Natural History Museum, and Hyde Park. It is one of London's most beautiful neighbourhoods, filled with white stucco townhouses and French cafes. It feels more residential and relaxed than the West End.

Where to stay in South Kensington

  • The Kensington, splurge. Set in four interconnected townhouses. Choose it for the quintessential upscale London residential experience. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • The Ampersand Hotel, comfortable. A Victorian building with rooms themed around the nearby museums. Watch out: Very close to the Tube station, so expect some street bustle. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • The Resident Kensington, good value. Rooms include mini-kitchenettes, making it ideal for longer stays or families. Watch out: No full-service restaurant on site. Check rates on Booking.com →

Shoreditch: creative and lively

East London is the city's creative engine. Shoreditch and Spitalfields are home to street art, Boxpark, and Brick Lane's curry houses and markets. It's the best area for those who find the West End too "touristy" and want to see where Londoners actually hang out.

Where to stay in Shoreditch

  • The Hoxton, Shoreditch, comfortable. The hotel that started the Shoreditch trend. Choose it for the vibe and proximity to Old Street. Watch out: Can be loud on weekend nights. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • One Hundred Shoreditch, splurge. Replaced the Ace Hotel. Choose it for the rooftop bar and larger-than-average East End rooms. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Shoreditch House, comfortable. Part of Soho House, you can stay here if you aren't a member. Choose it for the rooftop pool and exclusive atmosphere.

What kind of hotel? London types

  • The Luxury Grande Dame: Historical hotels like The Savoy or Claridge's. Expect afternoon tea, dress codes, and legendary service.
  • The Modern Boutique: The Hoxton or TRUNK-style properties. Focus on lobby culture, coworking spaces, and local coffee.
  • Budget Chains: **Premier Inn** (especially "Hub" hotels) and **Travelodge** are the most reliable way to stay central for under £150. Book directly on their sites for the best rates.
  • Apart-hotels: Brands like Locke or Wilde offer small kitchens and living spaces, perfect for avoiding eating out every night.

Before you book: five checks

  1. Tube distance. Don't settle for more than a 10-minute walk. Google Maps' "5 minutes" is often 8 with luggage.
  2. The "Shoebox" room. London hotels are notorious for tiny rooms. Check the square footage (or square metres) before booking.
  3. AC availability. Many older London hotels do not have air conditioning. If you visit in July or August, this is non-negotiable.
  4. Service charges. Check if VAT (20%) is included in the quoted price (it usually is for consumers, but check the fine print).
  5. Heathrow connection. If you have an early flight, stay on the Elizabeth Line or near Paddington.

If you are still undecided

Stay in **Covent Garden** or **Soho** for your first time. You will pay a premium, but you will save it in transport time and stress. For your second trip, head East to **Shoreditch** or West to **South Kensington** to see a more authentic side of London life.

Frequently asked questions

Is London safe to stay in?

Yes, central London is generally very safe for travelers. Standard city precautions apply: watch your bag in crowded areas like Leicester Square and avoid poorly lit parks late at night. Areas listed in this guide are well-trodden and safe.

How do I get from Heathrow to my hotel?

The Elizabeth Line is the best balance of speed and cost. The Heathrow Express is faster to Paddington only. The Piccadilly Line is cheapest but takes an hour to reach central London. A pre-booked car (Welcome Pickups) is best for late arrivals.

Do I need to tip in London hotels?

Tipping is not mandatory but appreciated. £1–£2 per bag for porters and optional rounding up of bills in hotel restaurants is standard. A service charge (12.5%) is often added to restaurant bills automatically.
