Hotels around Covent Garden, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Covent Garden that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

Unlike Paris, Rome or Amsterdam, London charges no city tax on a hotel room. What you pay instead is VAT at 20%, the standard UK rate, and it is usually already inside the price you see. That may change: in September 2026 the government confirmed it will let mayors in England, London’s included, charge an overnight visitor levy, but none is in force yet. Where you stay decides the rest of the cost, in Tube fares and in time.

London’s hotel tax: 20% VAT, and no city tax yet

Charge Rate in 2026
VAT on a hotel room 20%, the UK’s standard rate
City or tourist tax None
Stays of more than 28 consecutive days VAT is charged on a reduced value of the room after the 28th day

Because UK room prices normally include VAT, the tax is easy to miss, so compare totals that include taxes and fees. To see how much of a price is VAT, take one-sixth of it. On a £250 room that is £41.67 a night, or £83.33 over two nights (our arithmetic from the 20% rate).

The levy that may come. On 10 September 2026 the government published its response to the visitor levy consultation and said it will legislate for an overnight visitor levy that mayors in England can choose to charge. It will be a percentage of the cost of the accommodation, not a flat fee. Subject to Parliament’s approval, the government expects local leaders to set out how they would spend the money by March 2028. No levy has been set for London, so in 2026 there is nothing extra to pay.

Getting in from the airports, and around

  • From Heathrow, the Elizabeth line costs £14.60 to Paddington and £15.50 to Tottenham Court Road with a contactless card, and takes about 27 and 33 minutes on TfL’s journey planner.
  • From Heathrow, the Heathrow Express reaches Paddington in 15 minutes, every 15 minutes. A standard single is £26; advance singles bought 30 days or more ahead start at £10. It is not covered by TfL’s fare caps.
  • From Heathrow, the Piccadilly line is the cheapest rail route: £5.90 into Zone 1, about 50 minutes, and it runs straight to South Kensington, Covent Garden and King’s Cross.
  • From Gatwick, the Gatwick Express runs to Victoria in as little as 31 minutes for £24 with contactless, outside the caps. Southern and Thameslink trains cost £10.70 off-peak and £19.20 at peak times, to Victoria, London Bridge or St Pancras; Thameslink takes about 44 minutes to St Pancras.
  • From Stansted, the Stansted Express takes about 50 minutes to Liverpool Street, with fares from £9.90.
  • In town, tap a contactless card or phone. A Zone 1 Tube ride is £3.10 at peak times and £3.00 off-peak, against £7 for a paper single. Pay-as-you-go travel in Zones 1-2 is capped at £8.90 a day and £44.70 from Monday to Sunday. A bus ride is £1.75, with unlimited bus and tram journeys within an hour of the first tap.

    Covent Garden and the West End: the first-trip base

    Covent Garden is the London of the market hall, the Royal Opera House and the theatres of the West End, with Seven Dials, Soho and Trafalgar Square all on foot. The Piccadilly line runs from Heathrow to Covent Garden and Leicester Square without a change. It is crowded and loud until late, which is the reason people stay here.

    Where to stay in Covent Garden

    How the hotel links work: a hotel’s name goes to its own website. “Check rates” opens it on Booking.com through our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book, at no extra cost to you.

    • The Savoy, splurge. On Savoy Court, off the Strand, beside the Thames since 1889, with 263 rooms and suites in two styles: Edwardian rooms in grey and tangerine, some with views across the river, and Art Deco rooms on the Strand side with marble bathrooms. The American Bar, afternoon tea under the glass dome of the Gallery and a pool are all in the building. Choose it for London’s grand hotel at the edge of Covent Garden. Watch out: only some rooms face the river, so book a Thames view by name; two weeknights in February cost more than £1,400 when we checked. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Radisson Blu Hotel, London Mercer Street, comfortable. A four-star hotel in Seven Dials, with newly renovated rooms, Monmouth Kitchen for Peruvian and Italian cooking, and a gym open around the clock. Choose it for a quieter street a few minutes from the theatres. Watch out: the Cosy Double is 14 m²; the Deluxe King, at 18 m², suits two better. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • hub by Premier Inn London Covent Garden, budget. In Theatreland, a few minutes from the piazza, with compact rooms that put a double bed, touchscreen controls and a slide-out desk into what the brand calls about half the space of a Premier Inn room. Choose it for the West End at the lowest price here. Watch out: the Standard rooms are 11 m², tight for two, and the hotel is sold on Premier Inn’s own site.

    The South Bank and Bankside: the river and the galleries

    The South Bank runs along the Thames from the London Eye past the Southbank Centre to Bankside, where Tate Modern and Shakespeare’s Globe face the City across the water and Borough Market is a short walk east. The riverside path joins it all up, and the Millennium Bridge crosses to St Paul’s. Evenings are quieter than in the West End, and the theatres are a walk over a bridge.

    Where to stay on the South Bank

    • Sea Containers London, splurge. On the river on the South Bank, designed to recall a 1920s ocean liner, with 354 rooms, Thames Suites that include Riverview Balcony Suites, Gordon Ramsay at Sea Containers, the 12th Knot rooftop bar, the Lyaness cocktail bar, the agua Spa and a Curzon cinema. Choose it for the river and a spa in one building. Watch out: the river is the point of the Thames Suites; check what a standard room looks onto before you book. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • citizenM London Bankside, good value. On Lavington Street, with Tate Modern ahead and Borough Market close by, rooms with an XL king bed, a rainfall shower and a tablet that runs the lights, blinds and TV, and canteenM, open 24 hours. Choose it for Bankside at a modest price. Watch out: every room is the same design for two adults, so it does not suit families; it is now booked through Marriott. Check rates on Booking.com →

    South Kensington: museums and quiet streets

    South Kensington is the museum quarter: the Natural History Museum, the Science Museum and the V&A sit within a few streets of each other, with the Royal Albert Hall and Hyde Park to the north. The streets are white stucco and calmer at night than the centre. South Kensington station is on the District, Circle and Piccadilly lines, so Heathrow is one train away.

    Where to stay in South Kensington

    • The Kensington, splurge. A five-star hotel on Queen’s Gate, moments from the Royal Albert Hall, the V&A and Kensington Gardens, with drawing rooms with open fires and high ceilings, afternoon tea, the K Bar and a 24-hour gym. South Kensington and Gloucester Road stations are close by. Choose it for a townhouse-style stay near the museums. Watch out: the entry rooms are Cosy and Classic rooms, and a rollaway bed for a third guest aged 13 or over costs £70 a night. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • The Ampersand Hotel, comfortable. On Harrington Road, a minute’s walk from South Kensington station, with each room type given a theme, from astronomy in the Singles and Small Doubles to birds in the Deluxe Rooms, a Science Afternoon Tea and the Apero restaurant under Victorian cellar arches. Choose it for the museums, with children or without. Watch out: the hotel itself calls the Singles and Small Doubles intimate; the Superior Rooms are the next size up. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Shoreditch: bars, markets and late nights

    Shoreditch is East London’s evening quarter: street art, Boxpark, and the curry houses and markets of Brick Lane. Old Street on the Northern line and Shoreditch High Street on the Overground’s Windrush line connect it, and the Elizabeth line at Liverpool Street is a walk away. Stay here for the nights out; Old Street to Leicester Square takes about 16 minutes by Tube.

    Where to stay in Shoreditch

    • The Hoxton, Shoreditch, comfortable. On Great Eastern Street, with rooms refurbished in 2025, from the Shoebox, 12 m² on a high floor, to the Cosy at 18 m² and the Roomy at 25 m². Booked on its own site, it lets you check in and out at any time at no charge, and cancel free until 2 pm the day before arrival. Choose it for Shoreditch with flexible hours. Watch out: the Shoebox is, as the name says, small, and the flexible check-in comes only with a direct booking. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • One Hundred Shoreditch, comfortable. On Shoreditch High Street, with 258 rooms and suites, a rooftop bar and terrace with views across East London, the Seed Library bar from Mr Lyan’s team, and a lobby bar. Choose it for the rooftop and a social hotel. Watch out: DJs play in the lobby on Thursday and Friday evenings, so it is not a quiet base. Check rates on Booking.com →

    King’s Cross and St Pancras: the Eurostar and the trains north

    King’s Cross and St Pancras is a rail hub that became a neighbourhood. The Eurostar leaves from St Pancras International, King’s Cross station sends trains north, and the Tube station between them serves six lines, the Piccadilly line from Heathrow among them. Behind the stations, Coal Drops Yard and the Regent’s Canal have shops, restaurants and towpath walks.

    Where to stay in King’s Cross

    • St. Pancras London, splurge. The five-star hotel in the Grade I listed Gothic Revival building above St Pancras International, opened in 1873 as the Midland Grand Hotel and now part of Marriott’s Autograph Collection, with 207 renovated bedrooms and 36 Signature Suites, the Booking Office 1869 restaurant in the station’s original ticket hall, and a spa. Choose it for the Eurostar from your door. Watch out: price, and the hotel’s free transfer to the Eurostar is for Chambers Suite guests only, requested 72 hours ahead. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • The Standard, London, comfortable. On Argyle Street, steps from St Pancras International, in a former Brutalist building, with 266 rooms and suites, Double Standard downstairs, Decimo’s Mexican cooking on the 10th floor and the Sweeties cocktail bar. Choose it for design and nightlife next to the trains. Watch out: the Rooftop is closed for the season and reopens in April 2027. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • hub by Premier Inn London King’s Cross, budget. Next to King’s Cross and St Pancras International and the Regent’s Canal, with Standard rooms of 11 m² with a double bed and Bigger rooms of 14 m² with a king-size bed. Choose it for an early Eurostar on a budget. Watch out: two people should book a Bigger room; like the Covent Garden hub, it is sold on Premier Inn’s site.

    How to book London

    • Count the tax. London has no city tax in 2026, and the 20% VAT is normally inside the room price: a £250 room includes £41.67 of it.
    • Watch the season. When we checked on 13 October for two weeknights, 18 to 20 November 2026, The Savoy and The Hoxton, Shoreditch had no rooms left on Booking.com, and citizenM London Bankside cost £508. The same two nights in mid-February 2027 cost £321 at citizenM, £488 at The Hoxton, Shoreditch and £1,482 at The Savoy.
    • Check the room size. Small rooms are normal here: 11 m² at both hub hotels, 12 m² for The Hoxton’s Shoebox and 14 m² for the Cosy Double at Mercer Street. If you need space, book the category above the cheapest.
    • Compare totals. Hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book; the two hub hotels are sold on Premier Inn’s own site. Hotel taxes around the world sets London against Paris, Rome and Amsterdam.

    Before you go

    • Check whether you need an ETA. If you are from Europe, the USA, Australia, Canada or certain other countries, you usually need an electronic travel authorisation rather than a visa. It costs £20 on GOV.UK or the UK ETA app, lasts two years or until the passport expires, and a decision usually comes within a day, though it can take up to 3 working days. Each traveller needs one, children included; British and Irish passport holders do not. An ETA does not guarantee entry.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the best area to stay in London for a first trip?

    Covent Garden and the West End: the market, the Royal Opera House and the theatres are on foot, and the Piccadilly line runs from Heathrow to Covent Garden without a change. Choose the South Bank for the river and the galleries, South Kensington for the museums, Shoreditch for nights out and King's Cross for the Eurostar.

    Does London have a tourist tax on hotels?

    Not in 2026. There is no city tax; hotel rooms carry VAT at 20%, the UK's standard rate, which is normally included in the room price. In September 2026 the government said it will legislate to let mayors in England charge an overnight visitor levy as a percentage of the room cost, but no levy has been set for London.

    What is the cheapest way from Heathrow to central London?

    The Piccadilly line: £5.90 into Zone 1 with a contactless card, about 50 minutes, direct to South Kensington, Covent Garden and King's Cross. The Elizabeth line is faster, £14.60 to Paddington and £15.50 to Tottenham Court Road, and the Heathrow Express reaches Paddington in 15 minutes for £26, or from £10 if booked 30 days or more ahead.

    Do I need an ETA to visit London?

    If you are from Europe, the USA, Australia, Canada or certain other countries, you usually need an electronic travel authorisation. It costs £20, lasts two years or until your passport expires, and each traveller needs one, children included. British and Irish passport holders do not need one.

    How much is the Tube in London?

    A Zone 1 ride is £3.10 at peak times and £3.00 off-peak with a contactless card or phone, against £7 for a paper single. Travel in Zones 1-2 is capped at £8.90 a day and £44.70 from Monday to Sunday, and a bus ride is £1.75.

    When are London hotels cheaper?

    Of the two periods we compared, February. For two weeknights in mid-November 2026, The Savoy and The Hoxton, Shoreditch had no rooms left on Booking.com and citizenM London Bankside cost £508; the same nights in mid-February 2027 cost £321 at citizenM.
