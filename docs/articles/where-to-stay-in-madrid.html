Hotels around Sol, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Sol that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

Unlike Barcelona, Paris or Rome, Madrid adds no tourist tax to the hotel bill. Spain has no national one, and in 2026 the Comunidad de Madrid levies no taxes of its own, so the only tax on your room is Spain’s 10% VAT. The same four nights that cost a couple €67.20 in tourist tax at a four-star in Barcelona cost nothing extra here. Where you stay decides the rest: how much of the city you see on foot, and how you get in from Barajas.

This guide covers six areas, from Sol to Salamanca, 12 hotels, the airport links and what the bill really includes. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

Madrid’s hotel tax: none, but 10% VAT

Tourist taxes in Spain are set by the regions, not by Madrid or by the state. Catalonia charges one, which is why Barcelona’s hotels add it per person and per night. The Comunidad de Madrid does not: Spain’s Ministry of Finance records that the region has no taxes or surcharges of its own in force in 2026, and the city council charges no tourist tax either. What you pay on top of the room is VAT.

Charge Rate in Madrid, 2026
Tourist tax, per person and per night None
VAT (IVA) on hotel and other hospitality services 10%
Spain’s standard VAT rate, for comparison 21%

The 10% is Spain’s reduced rate, which the Agencia Tributaria applies to hospitality services, restaurants included. It is a percentage of the price, not a charge per guest.

Our arithmetic: if a site quotes a room at €180 a night before VAT, the total is €198 a night, or €792 for four nights. A couple paying the same €180 in a Barcelona four-star would also pay €67.20 in tourist tax over four nights (2 people × 4 nights × €8.40). For other cities, see hotel taxes around the world.

Getting in from the airport, and around

Madrid-Barajas has three ways into town by public transport, plus a fixed taxi fare.

  • The Exprés Aeropuerto bus, run by EMT, the city’s bus company, links Terminals T1, T2 and T4 with Cibeles and Atocha station 24 hours a day. The fare is €5, paid on board in cash or by card or phone; travel passes are not valid on it. From 6am to 11.30pm it runs to Atocha every 15 to 20 minutes, and EMT puts Atocha to T1 at 30 minutes; through the night it ends at Cibeles and runs every 35 minutes.
  • Metro line 8 stops at Aeropuerto T1-T2-T3 and Aeropuerto T4 and runs to Nuevos Ministerios, where you change, from 6.05am to 2am. A single in zone A costs €1.50 for up to five stations, rising by €0.10 a station to €2 for longer trips, and the airport stations add a €3 supplement: €4.50 to €5 in all. Tickets are loaded onto the Multi card, €2.50 from the machines.
  • The Cercanías C1 train leaves from Terminal T4 only (floor -1) for Chamartín, Atocha and Príncipe Pío. Aena, the airport operator, lists singles from €1.70 to €2.60 by zone, and the ride is free if you hold an AVE high-speed train ticket connecting with your flight.
  • By taxi, a fixed fare of €33 applies, day and night, between the airport and anywhere inside the M-30 ring road, which covers every area in this guide. No supplements can be added to it.
  • In town, the Abono Turístico, the tourist travel pass, gives unlimited travel in zone A, which is roughly the city of Madrid, on the metro, EMT buses and Cercanías trains. It costs €10.30 for one day, €17.50 for two, €23.10 for three, €27.80 for four, €33.40 for five and €43.20 for seven, counted in calendar days from first use; under-11s pay half. It covers the metro’s airport stations with no supplement, but not the Exprés Aeropuerto bus.

Sol and the Centro: the first-trip base

The streets between the Puerta del Sol and Gran Vía are where most visitors start, and the city’s own tourist office calls them the centre of Madrid’s tourist, cultural and recreational life. The clock on the Real Casa de Correos, on Sol, rings in the New Year to the crowds below every 31 December. A few minutes west, the arcaded Plaza Mayor leads on to the old streets of La Latina. Sol is busy at every hour; the payoff is that almost everything a first trip needs is on foot.

Barrio de las Letras: tapas squares and literary streets

The Barrio de las Letras, the Literary Quarter, runs between Plaza de Santa Ana and the Paseo del Prado. It takes its name from the Golden Age writers who lived here: Cervantes is buried in the convent of the Trinitarias on Calle de Lope de Vega, and Lope de Vega’s house museum is, by a neat reversal, on Calle de Cervantes. Calle de las Huertas, with quotations from Spanish writers set into its paving, runs downhill to the Prado. Plaza de Santa Ana, with its terraces and tapas bars, is busy into the night, especially at weekends.

The Paseo del Arte: the Prado and the Retiro

The Paseo del Arte, the Art Walk, puts three of the world’s great galleries within two kilometres: the Prado, the Thyssen-Bornemisza and the Reina Sofía. Since July 2021 the Paseo del Prado, from Cibeles to Atocha, and the 120-hectare Retiro gardens have been a UNESCO World Heritage site, “Paseo del Prado and Buen Retiro, a landscape of Arts and Sciences”. It is calmer than Sol at night, and Atocha, at its southern end, has the Exprés Aeropuerto bus, the Cercanías trains and the high-speed trains.

Malasaña and Gran Vía: bars and late nights

Gran Vía, begun in 1910, is Madrid’s avenue of theatres, cinemas and department stores, and a showcase of early 20th-century buildings such as the Metrópolis. North of it, Malasaña is a square of streets bounded by Gran Vía, Calle Fuencarral, Calle Carranza and Calle de San Bernardo. It is named after Manuela Malasaña, a heroine of the uprising of 2 May 1808 against Napoleon’s troops, remembered in the Plaza del Dos de Mayo, and in the 1980s it was the birthplace of the movida madrileña. Today it is vintage shops by day and small bars by night.

Chueca: nightlife and Pride

Chueca, beside Gran Vía to the east of Malasaña, is famous for its nightlife and as Madrid’s LGTBIQA+ quarter, and every summer it is the centre of the Madrid Pride festivities, MADO. Plaza de Chueca is its hub, the San Antón Market has a rooftop terrace, and the Museum of Romanticism sits in an 18th-century palace. To the east, towards the Paseo de Recoletos, Salesas is quieter.

Where to stay in Chueca

Choose it for design and a quieter street next to the nightlife. Watch out: it is in Salesas rather than Chueca proper; for the plaza itself, Room Mate Óscar is closer. Check rates on Booking.com →

Choose it for Chueca at the door. Watch out: Plaza de Pedro Zerolo is one of Madrid Pride’s stages; expect noise in late June and early July. Check rates on Booking.com →

Salamanca: the Golden Mile

Salamanca was planned in the second half of the 19th century by the Marquis of Salamanca, as a grid of straight streets for Madrid’s aristocracy and bourgeoisie. Calle Ortega y Gasset is the Golden Mile of fashion, and Calle Serrano and Calle Claudio Coello hold the designer boutiques, with Michelin-starred restaurants among them. It is quieter at night than the centre, with the Retiro on its southern edge and the old town a metro ride away.

Where to stay in Salamanca

  • Rosewood Villa Magna, splurge. On the Paseo de la Castellana, on Salamanca’s western edge, with the restaurants Amós by Jesús Sánchez and Las Brasas de Castellana, Sense, a Rosewood Spa, and rooftop “Houses” with views over Madrid. Choose it for a five-star base by the shops. Watch out: Sol and the old town are a metro or taxi ride away. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Wellington Hotel & Spa Madrid, splurge. Choose it for a classic grand hotel by the park. Watch out: with 237 rooms it is a large hotel, not a boutique one. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Madrid

  • Count the VAT. There is no tourist tax, but check that the 10% VAT is in the price you are shown: a €180 room before VAT is €198 with it.
  • Watch the calendar. Madrid Pride fills Chueca and the streets around it in late June and early July (MADO 2026 ran from 25 June to 5 July), and New Year’s Eve fills Sol. Book early for either, or stay away from the party.
  • Check the room size. Some rooms here are small by design: the single rooms at Room Mate Alicia are 10 m², and Only YOU Hotel Atocha’s cheapest category is the Small Double. If you need space, book the category above the cheapest.
  • Mind the noise. Sol, the Plaza Mayor and Plaza de Santa Ana are busy late; ask for an inside or courtyard room if you sleep lightly.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Madrid for a first trip?

Sol and the Centro: the Puerta del Sol, the Plaza Mayor and Gran Vía are on foot, and Sol is a metro and Cercanías hub. Choose the Barrio de las Letras for tapas squares, the Paseo del Arte for the Prado and the Retiro, Malasaña or Chueca for nightlife and Salamanca for designer shopping.

Does Madrid have a tourist tax?

No. Spain has no national tourist tax, and in 2026 the Comunidad de Madrid levies no taxes of its own, so Madrid's hotels add no tourist tax. The room carries Spain's reduced VAT rate of 10%, which applies to hospitality services.

How much VAT is added to a hotel room in Spain?

10%, Spain's reduced VAT rate for hospitality services. If a site quotes €180 a night before VAT, the total is €198 a night, or €792 for four nights.

How do you get from Madrid airport to the city centre?

The Exprés Aeropuerto bus runs 24 hours from T1, T2 and T4 to Cibeles and Atocha for €5. Metro line 8 runs to Nuevos Ministerios: a zone A single plus the €3 airport supplement comes to €4.50 to €5. The Cercanías C1 train leaves from T4, and a taxi costs a fixed €33 to anywhere inside the M-30.

Is there a tourist travel pass in Madrid?

Yes. The Abono Turístico gives unlimited travel in zone A on the metro, EMT buses and Cercanías trains, from €10.30 for one day to €43.20 for seven. It covers the metro's airport stations with no supplement, but not the Exprés Aeropuerto bus.

How much is a taxi from Madrid airport?

A fixed €33, day and night, between the airport and anywhere inside the M-30 ring road, which covers every area in this guide. No supplements can be added to it.
