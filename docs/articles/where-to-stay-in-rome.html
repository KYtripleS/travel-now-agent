By Casey, Gently Yonder editor

Hotels around Centro Storico, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Centro Storico that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

Rome adds its own charge to every hotel bill: the contributo di soggiorno, a city tax per person and per night that rises with the hotel’s star rating, from €4 at a one-star to €10 at a five-star. A couple spending four nights in a four-star hotel pays €60 on top of the room. Where you stay decides the rest: how much of the old city you cover on foot, and how often you queue for a bus.

This guide covers six areas, from the Pantheon to the Vatican, 12 hotels, the airport links and the tax. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

Rome’s city tax

Every hotel collects the contributo di soggiorno for Roma Capitale, per person and per night. The rates below have applied since 1 October 2023, and the city still lists them as current in October 2026:

Where you stay Per person, per night
Five-star hotel €10
Four-star hotel €7.50
Three-star hotel €6
Two-star hotel €5
One-star hotel €4
Guest houses, B&Bs, holiday homes and short-term rentals €5 to €7, by type and category
Hostels €3.50

Three rules soften it. Children under 10 pay nothing. The tax stops after 10 consecutive nights in the same property. And it is charged per person, so a family of four with two children under 10 pays for two.

By our arithmetic, a couple staying four nights pays €60 at a four-star, €48 at a three-star and €80 at a five-star. A fortnight in one four-star hotel costs the couple €150, not €210, because only the first 10 nights count.

Getting in from the airports, and around

  • From Fiumicino, the Leonardo Express runs non-stop to Termini in 32 minutes, every 15 minutes, for €14. Four people travelling together can buy a Mini-groups ticket for €40. The first train leaves the airport at 05:38 and the last at 23:27.
  • The FL1 regional train costs €8 from Fiumicino to any station inside the city’s rail ring, and suits Trastevere and the south: it calls at Roma Trastevere and Ostiense and reaches Roma Tiburtina in 48 minutes. It runs every 15 minutes on working days and every 30 minutes on Sundays and public holidays.
  • From Ciampino, Ciampino Airlink combines a 10-minute bus to Ciampino station with a train to Termini, €2.70 for both.
  • In town, a BIT ticket costs €1.50 and lasts 100 minutes from validation; passes cost €8.50 for 24 hours, €15 for 48 and €22 for 72. You can also tap a contactless card at the metro gates and on buses.

Centro Storico: the first-trip base

The Centro Storico is the Rome of the Pantheon, Piazza Navona and Campo de’ Fiori, packed into a bend of the Tiber, with the Trevi Fountain a few streets east. There is no metro in the middle of it, and you will not need one: most of what a first trip covers is a walk away, and the evenings are spent in the same squares.

Where to stay in the Centro Storico

  • Hotel Raphaël, splurge. A five-star on Largo Febo, a few metres from Piazza Navona, behind a façade covered in wisteria and Virginia creeper, with 50 rooms, 20 of them suites, an art collection with works by Miró, Picasso and de Chirico, two floors designed by Richard Meier, and a rooftop restaurant, Mater Terrae, with a 360-degree view over the city. A Relais & Châteaux member since 2010. Choose it for art and a roof terrace by Navona. Watch out: Mater Terrae’s cooking is organic, vegetarian and vegan, so meat-eaters dine elsewhere.   • Albergo del Senato, comfortable. A three-star on Piazza della Rotonda, facing the Pantheon, with a rooftop terrace over its dome and a buffet breakfast included in the rate; the Superior rooms and the two Penthouse Suites look at the Pantheon. Choose it for the Pantheon outside your window. Watch out: the Standard rooms are on the lower floors, and the hotel does not guarantee them a Pantheon view. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Albergo Santa Chiara, good value. Run by the same family for nearly a century, with 96 rooms in three adjoining buildings, an open-air bar on Piazza della Minerva and quadruple rooms for families. By the hotel’s measure the Pantheon is 50 metres away and Piazza Navona 220; doubles are 17 m². Choose it for the centre of the centre at a lower price. Watch out: breakfast comes only with rates that include it, and the hotel is inside the limited traffic zone, with no parking. Check rates on Booking.com →

Spanish Steps and Tridente: the grand staircase

The Tridente is the triangle of streets between Piazza del Popolo and the Spanish Steps: Via del Corso, Via del Babuino and the boutiques of Via Condotti. The Villa Borghese gardens sit above it, and Spagna, on metro line A, runs to Termini one way and the Vatican the other. It is polished and busy by day, and quieter than the Centro Storico at night.

Where to stay by the Spanish Steps

  • Hassler Roma, splurge. At the top of the Spanish Steps, beside Trinità dei Monti, with Imàgo, a Michelin-starred restaurant, a 7th Floor Terrace bar over the city, and free use of the wellness club’s steam bath, sauna and gym for Deluxe guests. Choose it for the top of the Spanish Steps. Watch out: Deluxe rooms face either the garden or the city, so ask for the city view if the view is the point. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Panda, budget. A two-star in a 19th-century building on Via della Croce, a few steps from Piazza di Spagna, which describes itself as one of the oldest hotels in the historic centre; breakfast, Wi-Fi and air conditioning are included, and some rooms keep their 19th-century paintings. Choose it for the Spanish Steps on a small budget. Watch out: it is a simple two-star in the pedestrian zone, with no parking. Check rates on Booking.com →

Monti: the Colosseum and a village feel

Monti climbs from the Imperial Forums towards Termini, with Via Urbana, Via del Boschetto and the small square of the Madonna dei Monti at its heart: wine bars, vintage shops and trattorias in streets that feel residential. The Colosseum is at the bottom of the hill and Cavour, on metro line B, is in the middle of it.

Where to stay in Monti

  • Hotel Forum, comfortable. A superior four-star of 78 rooms and suites in an 18th-century building on Via Tor de’ Conti, above the Imperial Forums and, by the hotel’s account, five minutes’ walk from the Colosseum, with breakfast served in its Roof Garden and an American bar on the top floor. Choose it for breakfast with a view of the Forums. Watch out: only the Deluxe rooms and suites look over the Forums. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • The Fifteen Keys Hotel, comfortable. A four-star of 15 individually designed rooms in a five-storey townhouse on Via Urbana, converted in 2015; rooms look over a leafy courtyard or the rooftops, the Classic rooms are 20 m², and the Deluxe rooms have a balcony over the courtyard. Termini is about 1 km away. Choose it for a small, quiet hotel in the middle of Monti. Watch out: its discounted offers are charged when you book and cannot be changed or cancelled. Check rates on Booking.com →

Trastevere: evenings and cobbled lanes

Trastevere sits across the Tiber from the Centro Storico, around the church and piazza of Santa Maria in Trastevere: ivy on ochre walls, cobbled lanes and the city’s busiest restaurant streets. There is no metro, but the Centro Storico is a walk over the river, and the FL1 airport train stops at Roma Trastevere station on the district’s southern edge. Expect noise on the main lanes until late.

Where to stay in Trastevere

  • Hotel Santa Maria, good value. A three-star of 19 rooms in a 17th-century cloister, steps from Piazza Santa Maria in Trastevere, with every room on the ground or first floor and family rooms up to a Senior Suite for six; the owners are there every day. Choose it for a quiet courtyard in the middle of Trastevere. Watch out: the Double rooms are 14 m². Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Donna Camilla Savelli, comfortable. A former Baroque convent by Borromini on Via Garibaldi, with cloisters, a garden, a terrace looking over Trastevere and a restaurant of Roman cooking, Contempo; the Deluxe rooms, converted from the monastic quarters, are 20 m². Choose it for a convent’s calm with Trastevere at the door. Watch out: the Classic rooms, 16 m², have a private entrance and no breakfast in the rate, and in most categories the room is assigned at random. Check rates on Booking.com →

Prati: the Vatican and quieter nights

Prati is the 19th-century grid of wide streets between the Vatican and the Tiber, with Via Cola di Rienzo for shopping and Ottaviano and Lepanto on metro line A. It is residential and calm after dark, and the place to stay if the Vatican Museums and St Peter’s are high on the list. The Centro Storico is a walk across the river.

Where to stay in Prati

  • Hotel dei Mellini, comfortable. 80 rooms over six floors on Via Muzio Clementi, designed by the architect Harry Gregory and hung with contemporary Italian art, with a terrace and sun deck and a gym; Superior rooms are 25 m², and family rooms sleep up to seven. By the hotel’s account, the Spanish Steps are a 10-minute walk and St Peter’s about 15. Choose it for space and calm between the Vatican and the centre. Watch out: the Petit Chambre, sold only on the hotel’s own site, is a 12 m² room with a single bed. Check rates on Booking.com →

Termini and Esquilino: the station quarter

Termini is where the Leonardo Express arrives, the high-speed trains leave for Florence and Naples, and metro lines A and B meet. Around it, the Esquilino holds the Baths of Diocletian and the basilica of Santa Maria Maggiore. The streets right by the station are busy and less charming than the centre, but for early trains, a day trip by rail or a tighter budget, nothing is more convenient.

Where to stay near Termini

  • Palazzo Montemartini, splurge. A five-star Radisson Collection hotel beside a stretch of the ancient Servian Walls, a few steps from the Baths of Diocletian and Termini, with a spa, a restaurant and a fifth-floor terrace over the rooftops; Collection rooms are 25 m². Choose it for five-star comfort by the airport train. Watch out: the terrace bar opens only from June to September. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • The RomeHello, budget. A hostel on Via Torino, a short walk from Termini and Repubblica on line A, run as a social enterprise that says its profits go to social and environmental projects, with dorms, private rooms with their own bathroom, a 24-hour front desk, a garden, a social kitchen and a bar and kitchen, Timoty. Choose it for the lowest price in this guide. Watch out: the Private Twin has a bunk bed, and dorm bunks are assigned at check-in. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Rome

  • Count the tax. It is per person and set by the star rating: €7.50 a night at a four-star, €6 at a three-star, nothing for children under 10, and nothing after the 10th consecutive night.
  • Watch the season. Rates move with the date, and several hotels here run their own seasonal offers: when we checked, Hotel Forum advertised autumn rooms from €118 a night on its own site. Price a few different weeks before you fix the trip.
  • Check the room size. Among the hotels in this guide, the smallest rooms run from 12 m² at the Mellini’s Petit Chambre and 14 m² at Hotel Santa Maria to 25 m² at Palazzo Montemartini. If you need space, book the category above the cheapest.
  • Book the view by name. Only the rooms sold with it are sure to have it: the Superior rooms at the Albergo del Senato, the Deluxe rooms and suites at Hotel Forum.
  • Leave the car. The Centro Storico and the Tridente sit inside a limited traffic zone; the Albergo Santa Chiara and Hotel Panda have no parking.
  • Compare totals. Hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book, and where to stay in Paris helps if Rome is one stop of several.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the best area to stay in Rome for a first trip?

    The Centro Storico, around the Pantheon and Piazza Navona: most of what a first trip covers is a walk away. Choose the Spanish Steps and Tridente for boutiques and metro line A, Monti for the Colosseum, Trastevere for evenings out, Prati for the Vatican, and Termini for the airport train and lower prices.

    How much is the tourist tax in Rome?

    Rome's contributo di soggiorno is charged per person and per night by star rating: €10 at a five-star hotel, €7.50 at a four-star, €6 at a three-star, €5 at a two-star and €4 at a one-star, with €3.50 at hostels. A couple spending four nights in a four-star hotel pays €60 on top of the room.

    Is there a maximum number of nights for Rome's city tax?

    Yes. The tax stops after 10 consecutive nights in the same property, and children under 10 pay nothing. A couple spending a fortnight in one four-star hotel pays €150, not €210.

    How do you get from Fiumicino Airport to central Rome?

    The Leonardo Express runs non-stop to Termini in 32 minutes, every 15 minutes, for €14, or €40 for a Mini-groups ticket for four. The FL1 regional train costs €8 to any station inside the city's rail ring, including Roma Trastevere, Ostiense and Tiburtina.

    How do you get from Ciampino Airport to Rome?

    Ciampino Airlink combines a 10-minute bus to Ciampino station with a train to Termini, €2.70 for both.

    How much is the metro in Rome?

    A BIT ticket costs €1.50 and lasts 100 minutes from validation. Passes cost €8.50 for 24 hours, €15 for 48 and €22 for 72, and you can also tap a contactless card at the metro gates and on buses.
