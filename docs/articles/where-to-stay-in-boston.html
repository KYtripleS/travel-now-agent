Boston hotels add three charges to the room rate, and together they come to 14.95%: a state excise, a city excise and a convention-centre fee. On a $300 room that is $44.85 a night before you reach the lobby. Where you stay decides which sights you reach on foot and which you reach by subway fare.

Boston’s hotel tax

Three charges apply to a hotel room in the city of Boston, and hotel operators pay all three to the Massachusetts Department of Revenue:

Charge Rate
Massachusetts room occupancy excise 5.7% of the room rate
Boston local option room occupancy excise 6.5% of the room rate
Convention center financing fee 2.75% of the room rate

Together that is 14.95%, our arithmetic from the three official rates. The state says cities may charge up to 6% locally, and 6.5% for Boston; the convention-centre fee applies in six cities, including Boston and Cambridge. On a $300 room, 14.95% is $44.85, so the room costs $344.85 a night.

Cambridge, across the river, charges its local excise at 6%, according to the state’s own rate table, so a Cambridge hotel adds 14.45%: 5.7% plus 6% plus 2.75%. On a $300 room that is $43.35. The state publishes no separate per-night city charge in the pages we read, but individual hotels can add their own fees, as one of the picks below does. Compare the total, not the headline rate.

Getting in from Logan, and around

  • The Silver Line SL1 is the simplest route from Logan Airport to downtown. Massport says it is free from the airport, it picks up at every terminal, and it runs to South Station through the Seaport, with a free transfer to the Red Line. Its stops include World Trade Center and Courthouse in the Seaport, and South Station.
  • The Blue Line also serves the airport: take the free on-airport shuttle to Airport Station, then the subway, $2.40. Massport says the Blue Line connects to the Orange Line at State and the Green Line at Government Center.
  • Logan Express Back Bay runs between the airport and two Back Bay stops, 800 Boylston Street by the Prudential Center and Dartmouth Street at Copley Place. Tickets from Back Bay to the airport are $3, the ride from the airport is free, and buses run every half hour: from Back Bay 5 am to 9 pm, from Logan 6 am to 10 pm.
  • The MBTA ferry links the Logan Airport ferry terminal and Long Wharf year-round, and an inner-harbour trip costs $2.40. The ferry does not take a CharlieCard; contactless payment works.
  • In town, the subway is $2.40 a ride and a local bus $1.70, by CharlieCard, CharlieTicket, contactless card or phone, or cash. A one-day pass is $11 and a seven-day pass $22.50. Contactless payment does not work with those passes. From 1 September 2026 the MBTA is piloting unlimited transfers between subway, bus and ferry within two hours, if you use the same form of payment for each.

Back Bay: Copley Square and Newbury Street

Back Bay is the Boston of brick row houses, the Boston Public Library and Newbury Street’s boutiques and cafés, and it is the first-trip base we would pick. Back Bay Station, opposite Copley Place, has the Orange Line, the commuter rail and Amtrak, and the Logan Express leaves from here for the airport.

Where to stay in Back Bay

  • Fairmont Copley Plaza, splurge. On Copley Square at 138 St James Avenue, with 383 guest rooms and suites and the OAK Long Bar + Kitchen. The hotel puts the Prudential Tower a 10-minute walk away.   • The Lenox Hotel, comfortable. Choose it for a classic Back Bay address and the Newbury Street boutiques. Watch out: the Classic Room is 220 sq ft; the Superior King is 330 sq ft. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Newbury Guest House, good value. A 35-room hotel at 261 Newbury Street, in a historic brownstone, in a registered historic district, with bay windows in several rooms. Choose it for a small hotel on Newbury Street at a lower price. Watch out: the Standard Room is about 210 sq ft, and its rooms page does not list parking or breakfast, so ask before you book. Beacon Hill and Downtown: the Common and the Freedom Trail

Boston Common, at 139 Tremont Street, was founded in 1634, and the city calls it America’s oldest park. Beacon Hill rises behind it, with the State House and narrow brick streets, and Downtown spreads south and east. The Freedom Trail, which the Freedom Trail Foundation describes as a 2.5-mile route connecting 16 historic sites, starts nearby, so a base here puts much of the first-time itinerary on foot.

Where to stay in Beacon Hill and Downtown

  • XV Beacon, splurge. A 61-room boutique hotel at 15 Beacon Street, in a Beaux Arts building with a marble grand staircase, mahogany panelling and an original caged-glass elevator, with Mooo as its restaurant and a 24-hour concierge. Choose it for Beacon Hill at the door. Watch out: with only 61 rooms, compare the room categories on the hotel’s own site before you choose.
  • Omni Parker House, comfortable. A hotel on School Street, a local icon since 1855, with Parker’s Restaurant, which the hotel calls the birthplace of Boston Cream Pie and Parker House Rolls. Choose it for history and the Freedom Trail at the door. Watch out: check-in is at 4 pm, guests must be 21 to rent a room, and leaving early incurs a fee equal to one night plus tax. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • HI Boston, budget. A hostel at 19 Stuart Street, with a Private Queen Ensuite and 4-, 6- and 8-bed dorms, free daily activities such as walking tours, and Boston Common about 0.2 miles away by the hostel’s count. Choose it to save money for the sights. Watch out: dorms are shared, check-in is at 4 pm, and groups of eight or more must book through the hostel’s group sales. Check rates on Booking.com →

The Waterfront and the North End: harbour streets

The North End, beside the harbour, holds the Paul Revere House at 19 North Square. It has few hotels of its own, so the practical base is the waterfront and Faneuil Hall area next to it, where the two hotels below stand.

Where to stay on the Waterfront

  • Boston Harbor Hotel, splurge. At 70 Rowes Wharf, beneath an archway on the edge of the water, with Rowes Wharf Sea Grille, The Dark Bar, afternoon tea and a wellness area with an indoor pool. Choose it for the harbour at your feet. Watch out: the page we read lists neither room sizes nor rates, so compare the category and the total before you book. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Harborside Inn, good value. A 200-room hotel at 185 State Street, in downtown Boston, near Faneuil Hall and the Freedom Trail, with a skylit atrium, rooms of 190 to 350 sq ft, free Wi-Fi and a stated policy of no hidden fees. Choose it for the waterfront at a lower price. Watch out: the King Cabin rooms have no windows, so book a City View or Atrium View room if you want daylight. Check rates on Booking.com →

The Seaport: a modern district on the harbour

The Seaport has the most direct link to Logan: the Silver Line runs through it from the airport. The Institute of Contemporary Art, at 25 Harbor Shore Drive, charges adults $20, and admission is free on Thursday evenings from 5 to 9 pm with advance tickets; it is closed on Mondays.

Where to stay in the Seaport

  • The Envoy Hotel, comfortable. At 70 Sleeper Street, with 136 guest rooms and suites, a seventh-floor rooftop with the Skyline View Bar and the Harbor Lounge, and the restaurant Para Maria. Choose it for the harbour from the roof. Watch out: Classic rooms are 289 to 309 sq ft and have only a partial water view; the Deluxe King is 396 sq ft. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • YOTEL Boston, budget. At 65 Seaport Boulevard, with compact cabins from 152 sq ft, the Deck 12 rooftop bar and the ground-floor restaurant Vela Seaport, and the Courthouse Silver Line station a four-minute walk away. Choose it to be in the Seaport for less. Watch out: a mandatory $45 facility fee is charged on top of the room rate, as the hotel’s own site states. Check rates on Booking.com →

Cambridge: Harvard Square and the river

Cambridge is a separate city across the Charles River, with its own local hotel excise. Massport lists the Silver Line, with a free transfer to the Red Line, as the way from the airport to Cambridge, Harvard University and MIT.

Where to stay in Cambridge

  • The Charles Hotel, splurge. At 1 Bennett Street in Harvard Square, with Henrietta’s Table, Noir Bar and Bar Enza, and the Regattabar jazz room, which the hotel says has drawn crowds since 1985. Choose it for jazz and Harvard Square at the door. Watch out: breakfast is part of the Bed & Breakfast package, not the standard rate. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Harvard Square Hotel, good value. A 73-room hotel at 110 Mount Auburn Street, next to Harvard University by the hotel’s own description, with king, double and full beds and a lobby café. Choose it for Harvard Square at a lower price. Watch out: the hotel’s self-parking costs $25 a day or $50 overnight. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Boston

  • Count the tax. Add 14.95% in Boston, or 14.45% in Cambridge, to every rate: a $300 room in Boston costs $344.85. Hotels can add their own fees: YOTEL Boston charges $45 on top.
  • Watch the extras. Parking at the Fairmont Copley Plaza costs extra, the Harvard Square Hotel charges $25 a day or $50 overnight, and the Omni Parker House charges an early-departure fee of one night plus tax.
  • Check the room size. Several picks are compact by design: YOTEL’s cabins are 152 sq ft, Newbury Guest House’s Standard Room is about 210 sq ft and The Lenox’s Classic Room is 220 sq ft. If you need space, book the category above the cheapest.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Boston for a first trip?

Back Bay: Copley Square, the Boston Public Library and Newbury Street are close, Back Bay Station has the Orange Line, the commuter rail and Amtrak, and the Logan Express runs from here to the airport. Choose Beacon Hill and Downtown for the Common and the Freedom Trail, the Waterfront and the North End for the harbour, the Seaport for the Silver Line link, and Cambridge for Harvard Square.

How much is the hotel tax in Boston?

14.95% of the room rate, made up of the 5.7% Massachusetts room occupancy excise, Boston's 6.5% local option excise and the 2.75% convention center financing fee. On a $300 room that is $44.85 a night. Cambridge's local rate is 6%, so a Cambridge hotel adds 14.45%.

Is the Silver Line free from Logan Airport?

Yes, from the airport. Massport says the Silver Line SL1 is free from Logan to downtown; it picks up at every terminal and runs to South Station through the Seaport, with a free transfer to the Red Line.

How do you get from Logan Airport to Back Bay?

Take the Logan Express Back Bay bus. Massport says it runs every half hour, the ride from the airport to Back Bay is free, and a ticket from Back Bay to the airport is $3. From Back Bay it leaves from 800 Boylston Street by the Prudential Center and from Dartmouth Street at Copley Place.

How much is the subway in Boston?

$2.40 a ride on the subway and $1.70 on a local bus, with a CharlieCard, CharlieTicket, contactless card or phone, or cash. A one-day pass is $11 and a seven-day pass $22.50, and from 1 September 2026 the MBTA is piloting unlimited transfers between subway, bus and ferry within two hours.

Can you get a cheap hotel in Boston?

Two of the 12 hotels here are budget picks: HI Boston, a hostel at 19 Stuart Street with dorms and a Private Queen Ensuite, and YOTEL Boston in the Seaport, whose cabins start at 152 sq ft. YOTEL charges a mandatory $45 facility fee on top of the room rate, so compare the total.
