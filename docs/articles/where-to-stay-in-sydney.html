Where to Stay in Sydney (2026): Six Areas and 13 Hotels, Compared

By Casey, Gently Yonder editor

The city centre by Hyde Park for a first trip, Circular Quay for the harbour, Surry Hills for food, Bondi or Manly for the beach. Six Sydney areas and 13 named hotels compared, including the Sheraton Grand Sydney Hyde Park, where we stayed.

Sydney spreads itself along a harbour, and where you sleep decides what the trip feels like. Wake near Hyde Park and the whole city is a short walk or two train stops away. Wake at Circular Quay and the ferries are your front door. Wake at Bondi or Manly and it becomes a beach holiday with a city attached. None of these is wrong. They are just different trips.

So this guide starts with the six areas worth considering, then names thirteen hotels across them, with the reason to pick each and the catch. One of them is first-hand: one of us stayed at the Sheraton Grand Sydney Hyde Park in September 2026, and it is our pick for a first trip. The rest are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in September 2026. No hotel pays to appear here, and our Sheraton stay was not a hosted one.

City centre: Hyde Park, St James and Town Hall

For a first visit, this is the most practical base. You are between the harbour and Central Station. The train stations at St James, Museum, Town Hall and Martin Place put Circular Quay a couple of stops away, and since August 2024 the Metro has run under the city through Martin Place, Gadigal and Barangaroo. Hyde Park, the Art Gallery and the Royal Botanic Garden are all on foot. Evenings are quieter than in Surry Hills or Darling Harbour, which, after a long flight, is not a bad thing.

Where to stay in the city centre

Our pick in Sydney · we stayed here in September 2026

Sheraton Grand Sydney Hyde Park: on Hyde Park, a couple of minutes from St James and Museum stations, with a rooftop pool and spa. A calm, central base for a first trip. Compare its rates on Klook

  • Sheraton Grand Sydney Hyde Park: our pick, and where one of us stayed in September 2026. It sits at 161 Elizabeth Street facing Hyde Park, with St James and Museum stations both a couple of minutes’ walk away. There are 558 rooms, 48 of them suites, and a rooftop health club with an indoor heated pool, a 24-hour gym and a day spa. It was known for years as the Sheraton on the Park. Choose it for the most balanced first-trip base in the city: central, calm, and on the park. A full first-hand review is coming.
  • Kimpton Margot Sydney, comfortable, heritage. 172 rooms in the heritage-listed 1939 former head office of Sydney Water on Pitt Street, near Town Hall, open since February 2022; the art-deco lobby and its scagliola columns survived the conversion. Choose it for character in the middle of the city.
  • Capella Sydney, splurge. 192 rooms in the former Department of Education building of 1912, in the sandstone quarter near Circular Quay, open since March 2023. Choose it for the newest luxury in the old city.
  • Great Southern Hotel Sydney, good value. A heritage-listed hotel dating from 1854 at 717 George Street, on the Chinatown edge of the city and a few minutes from Central Station. Watch out: it is being upgraded, with noisy works from 10am to 4pm between 31 August and 30 November 2026, so check before booking in that window.

Circular Quay and The Rocks: the harbour at the door

This is the postcard: the Opera House on one side of the cove, the Harbour Bridge on the other, and the ferry wharves between them. The Rocks, the oldest European quarter of the city, climbs behind in sandstone lanes and old pubs. It is the right base if you plan to spend your days on the water (our guide to booking a harbour cruise compares the options), and the wrong one if the price of a harbour view makes you wince.

Where to stay at Circular Quay and The Rocks

  • Park Hyatt Sydney, splurge. On the water in The Rocks, directly across the cove from the Opera House, with harbour-facing rooms that look straight at it. Choose it for the most famous view in Sydney. Watch out: it is one of the city’s most expensive hotels.
  • Pullman Quay Grand Sydney Harbour, comfortable, apartment-style. One- and two-bedroom suites at 61 Macquarie Street, East Circular Quay, a few steps from the Opera House, with balconies over the Quay or the Botanic Garden. Choose it for space, a kitchen and the harbour on your doorstep.
  • YHA Sydney Harbour, budget. A hostel built above the excavated remains of the old Rocks neighbourhood, with a rooftop that looks at the Opera House and the Bridge. Choose it for the harbour view at a hostel price.

Darling Harbour and Barangaroo: new hotels on the water

The western side of the city centre has been rebuilt over the past decade: Barangaroo’s towers and harbourside walk, and Darling Harbour’s convention precinct and restaurants. It is lively, family-friendly, and home to the city’s newest large hotels. Barangaroo has had its own Metro station since August 2024.

Where to stay at Darling Harbour and Barangaroo

  • Crown Towers Sydney, splurge. 349 rooms high in the tower at Barangaroo, open since December 2020, with floor-to-ceiling windows and a multi-level infinity pool. Choose it for height and harbour views on the western side.
  • W Sydney, comfortable, playful. Opened on 12 October 2023 at 31 Wheat Road above Darling Harbour, with 588 rooms and suites and a rooftop pool. Choose it for a lively, design-led base close to the water. Watch out: Darling Harbour is busy, and it is a walk to the eastern side of the city.

Surry Hills and Woolloomooloo: food first

South-east of the centre, Surry Hills is where many of Sydney’s best small restaurants, cafés and wine bars are, in terraced streets a short walk from Central Station. North of it, Woolloomooloo sits on the harbour below the Botanic Garden, with a converted wharf at its heart.

Where to stay in Surry Hills and Woolloomooloo

  • Paramount House Hotel, comfortable, boutique. 29 rooms in the former Sydney headquarters of Paramount Pictures, about ten minutes’ walk from Central. Choose it for a small, design-conscious hotel in the city’s best eating neighbourhood.
  • Ovolo Sydney, Woolloomooloo, comfortable, waterfront. Loft-style rooms on the heritage-listed Finger Wharf, with a heated indoor pool. Choose it for a hotel that is itself a piece of Sydney history. Watch out: a major interior renovation is rolling through 2026; the hotel stays open, but ask which rooms have been redone.

Bondi: the beach every morning

Bondi is for people who want to swim before breakfast and walk the Bondi to Coogee coastal path before lunch. The trade-off is the commute: there is no train to the beach itself. The usual route is a train to Bondi Junction and a bus down to the sand, so allow the best part of an hour to the Opera House each way.

Where to stay in Bondi

  • Hotel Ravesis, comfortable. A small hotel in a historic building at 118 Campbell Parade, facing the beach. Choose it to wake up with the ocean across the road. Much of Bondi’s accommodation is apartments, so compare those too if you are staying a week.

Manly: the ferry commute

Manly is the other beach option, and the journey is half the point: the ferry from Circular Quay takes about half an hour across the harbour, past the Opera House and out towards the Heads. Manly has its own ocean beach, a calmer harbour cove on the other side of the Corso, and North Head’s walking tracks.

Where to stay in Manly

  • Q Station, comfortable, unusual. The former quarantine station at North Head, inside Sydney Harbour National Park, where 65 heritage buildings across 30 hectares are now rooms, restaurants and a café. Choose it for somewhere quiet and completely unlike a city hotel. Watch out: you are on a headland, so plan how you will get to the ferry.

Getting around from your hotel

  • From the airport: the Airport Link train reaches Central in about 13 minutes, and the airport stations add a station access fee on top of the normal fare. With heavy bags or a late landing, a pre-booked car with Welcome Pickups takes you to the door.
  • Paying: tap on and off trains, buses, ferries and the Metro with an Opal card or a contactless bank card or phone.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Sydney for first-timers?

The city centre around Hyde Park, St James and Town Hall. It sits between the harbour and Central Station, Circular Quay is a couple of train stops away, and most sights are walkable. Our pick there is the Sheraton Grand Sydney Hyde Park, where one of us stayed in September 2026.

Is it better to stay at Circular Quay or in the CBD?

Circular Quay and The Rocks put the Opera House, the Harbour Bridge and every ferry at your door, at a premium price. The rest of the CBD, around Hyde Park and Town Hall, is usually better value and just as well connected, two or so train stops from the Quay.

Should I stay in Bondi or the city?

Stay in the city for sightseeing and Bondi for a beach holiday. There is no train to Bondi Beach itself; the usual route is a train to Bondi Junction and a bus, so allow most of an hour each way to the Opera House.

Is Manly a good place to stay in Sydney?

Yes, if you enjoy the commute: the ferry from Circular Quay takes about half an hour across the harbour. Manly has an ocean beach, a calm harbour cove and North Head's walking tracks. Q Station, the former quarantine station at North Head, is its most unusual place to stay.

How do I get from Sydney Airport to my hotel?

The Airport Link train reaches Central in about 13 minutes; the airport stations add a station access fee on top of the normal fare. You can tap on with an Opal card or a contactless bank card or phone. With heavy bags or a late arrival, a pre-booked car or taxi goes door to door.
