Hotels around San Marco, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around San Marco that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

Every Venice hotel adds the imposta di soggiorno, a tourist tax per person and per night that follows the star rating and the part of the city you sleep in: €4.50 at a four-star in the historic centre, €3.10 at a four-star on the mainland. In 2026 the city also charged day-trippers an access fee on 60 days; hotel guests were exempt, but had to register. Where you stay decides the rest: how much of the city you see on foot before the day visitors arrive, and how many water-bus rides you pay for.

This guide covers six areas, from San Marco to Mestre, 12 hotels, the airport links, the tax and the access fee. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

Venice’s tourist tax in 2026

Every hotel, B&B and rental in the municipality collects the tax and hands you a receipt. The rate depends on the type and star rating of the property, the zone and the season; high season runs from 1 February to 31 December. For hotels, per person and per night:

Hotel category Historic centre and Giudecca Lido and the lagoon islands Mainland (Mestre)
Five stars €5.00 €4.50 €3.50
Four stars €4.50 €3.60 €3.10
Three stars €3.50 €2.80 €2.40
Two stars €2.00 €1.60 €1.40
One star €1.00 €0.80 €0.70

In January, the low season, the rates drop: €3.10 at a four-star in the historic centre, €2.40 at a three-star. B&Bs and guest houses are graded in “lions” rather than stars, and pay from €2 to €5 a night in the historic centre.

Three rules soften it: children under 10 pay nothing and those aged 10 to 16 pay a reduced rate; the tax stops after five consecutive nights; and if you move hotels, the first hotel’s receipt counts towards the five.

By our arithmetic, a couple spending four nights in a four-star hotel in the historic centre pays €36 on top of the room; in a four-star in Mestre, €24.80. A week in one four-star in the historic centre costs the couple €45, not €63, because only the first five nights count.

The access fee for day-trippers

In 2026 the city ran a further trial of its contributo di accesso, a fee for entering the historic city on busy days: 60 days from 3 April to 26 July, mostly weekends and holidays, between 8.30am and 4pm. It cost €5 if paid by the fourth day before the visit and €10 after that, and did not apply on the smaller islands such as the Lido, Murano and Burano.

Hotel guests did not pay, because they pay the tourist tax, but they had to obtain a free exemption voucher with a QR code for the days of their stay, or carry a signed declaration; the city told guests to ask their hotel how. The trial ended on 26 July: since 27 July 2026 no payment or exemption is needed. Whether the fee returns in 2027 is for the city to decide, and in October 2026 it had not announced dates. Booking for spring or summer 2027? Check the official access fee site before you travel.

Getting in from the airport, and around

  • By water, Alilaguna runs from the airport dock to the historic centre for €18 one way or €32 return, with one large bag and one piece of hand luggage included. The Blue Line calls at Murano, the Lido, the Arsenale, San Zaccaria, San Marco, the Zattere and the railway station; the Orange Line calls at Madonna dell’Orto and Guglie, then runs down the Grand Canal by San Stae, Rialto, Sant’Angelo, Ca’ Rezzonico and Santa Maria del Giglio.
  • By bus, ACTV’s Aerobus line 5 runs to Piazzale Roma, at the edge of the historic city, for €10, or €18 return; an €18 combined ticket adds 90 minutes on the water buses. ATVO’s Venice Airport Bus Express also runs to Piazzale Roma and to Mestre station, which it times at about 20 minutes.
  • On the water buses, a single ACTV ticket costs €9.50 and lasts 75 minutes. Passes cost €25 for a day, €35 for two, €45 for three and €65 for seven, and cover the Lido and the mainland buses and trams; for the airport buses, add a €7 one-way or €13 return supplement when you buy one.
  • Under 30? The Rolling Venice card, €6, for anyone aged 6 to 29, unlocks a three-day pass for €27.

San Marco: the first-trip base

San Marco is the Venice of the basilica, the Doge’s Palace and La Fenice, with water-bus stops at San Marco-Vallaresso and Santa Maria del Giglio. By day it is the most crowded part of the city; early and late, when the day visitors have gone, staying here pays for itself.

Where to stay in San Marco

  • Hotel Monaco & Grand Canal, comfortable. A four-star on the Grand Canal a few steps from St Mark’s Square, in Palazzo Dandolo, where the Ridotto, the city’s gambling house, opened in 1638. You can arrive through its water gate, and the Grand Canal Restaurant has a terrace on the canal. Rooms run from the 22 m² Golden Leaf rooms to the 50 m² Casanova Alcove. Choose it for a family-run hotel by St Mark’s. Watch out: the corner rooms are the ones the hotel says overlook the garden or the courtyard of Palazzo Contarini; ask for one if the view matters. Castello: the waterfront, for a little less

Castello, the largest of the six sestieri, runs east from St Mark’s along the Riva degli Schiavoni, the waterfront promenade, past the Arsenale, the Republic’s old shipyard, to the Biennale Gardens. The stretch nearest San Marco is as busy as San Marco; a few streets east, the campi belong to the people who live there.

Where to stay in Castello

  • Londra Palace Venezia, splurge. A five-star on the Riva degli Schiavoni, a short walk from San Marco, receiving guests since 1853, with 52 rooms and suites, a restaurant, a bar and an altana, a rooftop terrace in the Venetian style; the Deluxe rooms face the lagoon and the island of San Giorgio. Choose it for the lagoon from your window. Watch out: the Classic rooms, 18 to 24 m², look over the city’s rooftops and lanes, not the lagoon; book a Deluxe Lagoon View room if the water is the point. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel La Residenza, good value. A two-star in a historic residence on Campo Bandiera e Moro, with period features, a walk from St Mark’s Square and the Arsenale; it also lets apartments for families and longer stays. Choose it for Castello at a lower price. Watch out: it is a two-star, and the hotel itself promises “essential comforts”. Check rates on Booking.com →

Dorsoduro: galleries and quieter canals

Dorsoduro lies across the Accademia bridge from San Marco: the Gallerie dell’Accademia, the Peggy Guggenheim Collection, the Salute at its tip, and the Zattere, the long quay on the Giudecca Canal. It is quieter than San Marco and a short walk from it.

Where to stay in Dorsoduro

  • Ca’ Pisani, comfortable. A four-star design hotel on Rio Terà Foscarini, near the Accademia, in a late-14th-century palace furnished with original 1930s and 1940s pieces, with a restaurant, La Rivista. Choose it for Art Deco design a few minutes from the galleries. Watch out: the Superior rooms measure 14 m².
  • Hotel Pensione Accademia, comfortable. A three-star superior in Villa Maravege, a 17th-century residence on Fondamenta Bollani with two gardens, run by the same family for more than thirty years; most rooms look onto the canal or the garden. Choose it for a garden and a canal by the Accademia. Watch out: the building has no lift, and the Basic Double rooms are 12 m². Check rates on Booking.com →

Cannaregio: neighbourhood evenings and the station

Cannaregio runs from Santa Lucia station along the city’s northern edge, with the Jewish Ghetto, the Ca’ d’Oro and long canal-side fondamente lined with bars. The station end is busy and practical; the middle, around the Ghetto and the Misericordia, is where to eat. San Marco is a vaporetto ride away.

Where to stay in Cannaregio

  • Ca’ Sagredo Hotel, splurge. A 15th-century palace on the Grand Canal by the Ca’ d’Oro, declared a national monument, with a frescoed ballroom, 42 rooms and suites, and a restaurant, L’Alcova, with a terrace on the canal opposite the Rialto Market. Choose it for a palace on the Grand Canal. Watch out: it is also a wedding and events venue, so ask whether anything is booked for your dates.
  • Hotel Abbazia, good value. A three-star in a former convent of the Discalced Carmelite friars, about a hundred metres from Santa Lucia station, with no bridges to cross on the way, and a garden where breakfast is served in summer. Choose it for arriving by train with heavy bags. Watch out: you are at the station end of the city, a vaporetto ride from San Marco. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Combo Venezia, budget. A hostel in the restored former Convento dei Crociferi on Campo dei Gesuiti, with en-suite rooms, some with a kitchenette, a 24-hour reception, a bar and a restaurant. Choose it for a hostel price in the historic centre. Watch out: it is a hostel with its own bar, restaurant and events programme, so expect company in the shared spaces. Check rates on Booking.com →

Santa Croce: a palazzo off the main routes

Santa Croce runs from Piazzale Roma, the bus terminal, along the Grand Canal towards Rialto, with Ca’ Pesaro and San Stae on the water. Away from the canal it is one of the quieter parts of the historic city.

Where to stay in Santa Croce

  • Palazzo Venart, splurge. A five-star of 17 rooms and suites on Calle Tron, with a garden and a private pier on the Grand Canal, about 10 minutes from the Rialto Bridge by the hotel’s count. Its restaurant, Glam, is run by Enrico Bartolini, and the hotel says it is the only restaurant in Venice with two Michelin stars. Choose it for a small palace and a dinner to remember. Watch out: only one of the Prestige rooms faces the Grand Canal; book a category with the canal in its name if you want the view. Check rates on Booking.com →

Mestre: the lowest tax and the train

Mestre is the mainland half of the municipality, not the Venice of the postcards: a modern town around a railway station, and a practical base. The tax is lower, regional trains run to Santa Lucia, and the ACTV one-day pass, €25, covers those trains as well as the water buses. The cost is time: every evening ends with a ride back across the lagoon.

Where to stay in Mestre

  • Hotel Plaza, good value. A four-star of 209 rooms facing Venice Mestre station; by the hotel’s count Venice is less than 10 minutes by train and the airport 17 minutes by bus. Choose it for a big hotel by the trains at the mainland’s lower tax. Watch out: you are on the mainland, so build the train into every plan. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Anda Venice Hostel, budget. A design hostel on Via Ortigara, about 200 metres from Mestre station, with mixed and women-only dorms of six, seven and nine beds, each with its own shower and toilet, en-suite double rooms, and a bar. Choose it for a bed at hostel prices. Watch out: dorms sleep up to nine, and Venice is a train or bus ride away. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Venice

  • Count the tax. It is per person, set by the star rating and the zone: €4.50 a night at a four-star in the historic centre, €3.10 in Mestre, nothing for children under 10, and nothing after the fifth night.
  • Watch the season. The tax is lower in January, its low season, and the Biennale Arte runs until 22 November 2026. If the access fee returns, check whether your arrival falls on one of its dates.
  • Check the room size. The smallest rooms here run from 12 m² at the Pensione Accademia and 14 m² at Ca’ Pisani to 22 m² at the Monaco & Grand Canal; if you need space, book the category above the cheapest.
  • Book the view by name. Only the rooms sold with it are sure to have it: the Deluxe Lagoon View rooms at the Londra Palace, the Grand Canal categories at Palazzo Venart and the Monaco & Grand Canal.
  • Compare totals. Hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book, and where to stay in Rome helps if Venice is one stop of several.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Venice for a first trip?

San Marco: St Mark's Square, the Doge's Palace and the Grand Canal water buses are at the door, and you have the square early and late, when the day visitors have gone. Choose Castello for the lagoon waterfront for a little less, Dorsoduro for the galleries and quieter canals, Cannaregio for neighbourhood evenings and the station, and Mestre for the lowest tax.

How much is the tourist tax in Venice?

It is per person and per night, set by the star rating and the zone. In high season, 1 February to 31 December, a hotel in the historic centre charges €1 at one star up to €5 at five stars, and €4.50 at a four-star; on the mainland a four-star charges €3.10. Children under 10 pay nothing, and the tax stops after five consecutive nights.

Do hotel guests have to pay the Venice access fee?

No. Overnight guests pay the tourist tax instead, but on the days the access fee applied in 2026 they had to get a free exemption voucher with a QR code, or carry a signed declaration, and ask their hotel how. The 2026 trial ran on 60 days from 3 April to 26 July; since 27 July 2026 no payment or exemption is needed, and the city had not announced 2027 dates in October 2026.

How do you get from Venice Marco Polo Airport to the city?

By water, the Alilaguna boat runs to the historic centre for €18 one way or €32 return, on the Blue Line by San Marco and the Zattere or the Orange Line down the Grand Canal. By bus, ACTV's Aerobus line 5 runs to Piazzale Roma for €10, and ATVO's Venice Airport Bus Express runs to Piazzale Roma and Mestre station in about 20 minutes.

How much is a vaporetto ticket in Venice?

A single ACTV ticket costs €9.50 and lasts 75 minutes. Passes cost €25 for a day, €35 for two, €45 for three and €65 for seven; they do not cover the airport buses unless you add a €7 one-way or €13 return supplement when you buy one.

Is it worth staying in Mestre instead of Venice?

It can be, as a practical base: the tourist tax is lower, €3.10 a night at a four-star against €4.50 in the historic centre, regional trains run to Santa Lucia, and the ACTV one-day pass covers those trains as well as the water buses. The cost is time, because every evening ends with a ride back across the lagoon.
