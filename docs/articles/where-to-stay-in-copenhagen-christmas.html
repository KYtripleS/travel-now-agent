Copenhagen at Christmas has one thing most European Christmas-market cities do not: no tourist tax on the hotel bill. Denmark has none, and Danish VAT at 25% is already inside the price you are shown. The other thing to plan around is Tivoli, whose Christmas season runs from 13 November 2026 to 3 January 2027, according to its own pages, and which is closed on 24 December. The market map has also changed this year, so older guides may send you to the wrong square.

This guide covers six areas, 12 hotels, Tivoli’s dates and hours, the markets we could confirm and the way in from the airport. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in November 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

The 2026 season: Tivoli’s hours and the markets

Tivoli. Tivoli’s Christmas page gives the season as 13 November to 3 January, and the Tivoli pages we read dated it to 2026 and 2027. Visit Copenhagen’s own Tivoli page still showed last season’s dates (14 November 2025 to 4 January 2026) when we read it, so use Tivoli’s own site. Tivoli’s opening-hours calendar, which we read on 4 November 2026, shows these hours:

Date Tivoli hours
Sunday to Thursday 11:00 to 22:00
Friday and Saturday 11:00 to 23:00
24 December Closed
25 and 26 December 11:00 to 23:00
31 December 11:00 to midnight
1 and 2 January 11:00 to 23:00
3 January, last day 11:00 to 22:00

Tivoli sells an entrance ticket, which does not include rides, a separate ride pass, and a package of the two; the Tivoli Pass gives entry through the open seasons. Tivoli says prices vary by visit date and sometimes by time of entry, and that tickets are date-specific. The prices sit in its webshop, so we quote none. The Christmas page lists a visit to Father Christmas from 13 November to 23 December, a Lucia parade on 13 December and a Christmas fireworks show on 25 and 26 December, which has its own Buy tickets button.

Two things changed. The traditional Højbro Plads market is not held in 2026: its organiser says it moves to City Hall Square, and its own page gives no dates there. The city’s visitor service describes a new market, Christmas at Højbro Plads, built around Denmark, Greenland and the Faroe Islands, and Visit Copenhagen gives it as running from 14 November to 20 December. The Nytorv & Gammeltorv organiser says the market sits next to Strøget, with a free ice rink, and the city’s listing says skates can be borrowed free of charge. For Nyhavn, we found no 2026 Christmas market on the city visitor service’s Christmas page or from Visit Copenhagen, and the guides that give dates are not official, so we give none. Copenhagen’s hotel bill: no tourist tax, 25% VAT included

A Danish city cannot simply charge visitors per night. When Copenhagen’s City Council discussed a tourism levy in June 2024, its papers set out the obstacle: a levy needs legal authority from Parliament, and the council voted to ask the government for it. In October 2026 a charge of DKK 20 a night for Copenhagen and Aarhus was reported to be planned for the 2027 budget, but the Danish Chamber of Commerce, Dansk Erhverv, said on 9 October that the final finance bill contained none. We found nothing that would apply to a Christmas 2026 stay.

Charge Copenhagen, Christmas 2026
Tourist tax, per person per night None
VAT (moms) on the room 25%
VAT as a share of the price you see 20%

The Danish Tax Agency puts the VAT rate at generally 25%, and says that in retail prices VAT is included and amounts to 20% of the price. Our arithmetic, for illustration only and not a quoted price: if a room were shown at DKK 2,000 a night, DKK 400 of it is VAT, and four nights cost DKK 8,000 with nothing added at the desk. Extras are where totals move: Cabinn Metro lists its breakfast buffet at DKK 125 per person per day, so a couple adds 2 × 4 × 125 = DKK 1,000 over four nights.

Getting in from the airport, and around

At Copenhagen Airport the Metro station is part of Terminal 3. The M2 line runs to Christianshavn, Kongens Nytorv and Nørreport, and the Metro puts the ride to the centre at 14 minutes. It runs through the night: about every 2 minutes at rush hour (07 to 09 and 14 to 18), about every 3 minutes off-peak and at weekends, and from 1am to 7am about every 8 minutes after Friday and Saturday and about every 20 minutes after Sunday to Thursday. We found no Christmas timetable notice for the Metro, so check m.dk for 24 and 25 December.

  • The fare. A 3-zone ticket is DKK 30 and is valid for 1 hour 30 minutes; the Metro says it covers travel between the city centre and the airport. A 2-zone ticket is DKK 24. Youths aged 12 to 15 pay half price, and an adult may bring two children under 12 free.
  • The train. DSB trains also leave from the airport’s own station, and DSB’s ticket office and machines are in Terminal 3. Our general Copenhagen guide goes through the train and the City Pass options.
  • To Tivoli. Central Station sits beside Tivoli. We found no official walking time from the Metro stops to the park, so check a map for your hotel.

    Near Tivoli and Central Station: the park at your door

    Tivoli is at the western edge of the old centre, next to Central Station, on Vesterbrogade. Staying here puts a ticket, the lights and the station within a short walk. The cost is that the area around a station and a big park is busy, and Tivoli is closed on 24 December.

A five-star superior boutique hotel at Bernstorffsgade 5 with 38 rooms and suites from 23 m² to 240 m², a 300 m² wellness area and, by its own account, views to Tivoli Gardens. Its Christmas page lists a Christmas Eve dinner at Nimb Brasserie, the one evening Tivoli itself is shut. Choose it for a night inside the Tivoli setting.     Indre By: City Hall Square, Strøget and the Latin Quarter

    Indre By, the inner city, is where the Nytorv & Gammeltorv market sits, beside Strøget, the main pedestrian shopping street, and the new City Hall Square market is 200 metres away by the organiser’s count. The area suits a first visit on foot. Rooms in the centre can be small, as the sizes below show.

    Where to stay in Indre By

Its Copenhagen Holiday Escape offer, valid for stays from 14 November 2026 to 3 January 2027, includes daily breakfast and entrance tickets to Tivoli Gardens. Choose it to have the Tivoli ticket in the room rate. Watch out: the page lists no price or minimum stay, the tickets are for Tivoli only, and Tivoli is closed on 24 December. Choose it for a large-hotel base on City Hall Square, 200 metres from the Nytorv market by the organiser’s count. Watch out: the Economy rooms are the smallest on the page, and the City Hall Square market’s hours are still to be confirmed. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Nyhavn and Bredgade: harbour lights

    Nyhavn, the 17th-century quay of painted houses, is a short walk from Kongens Nytorv, and Bredgade runs from there up to Amalienborg Palace. Both hotels below are on the harbour side of the old centre. We could not confirm a 2026 Nyhavn market, so choose this area for the quay itself, not for a market.

    Where to stay near Nyhavn

    • Admiral Hotel, comfortable. At Toldbodgade 24-28, in a warehouse from 1787, which says it is just steps from Nyhavn and Amalienborg. Its Classic room from 2019 is 20 m², and it offers Classic Waterfront and Deluxe Waterfront rooms over the water. Choose it for a harbour view a few steps from the quay. Watch out: the hotel’s pages give no Christmas prices, and the nearest market is not confirmed. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Phoenix Copenhagen, comfortable. A hotel of 213 rooms at Bredgade 37, between Amalienborg Palace and Nyhavn, with a view of the Marble Church’s dome from some rooms. Choose it for a classic address on Bredgade. Watch out: the Standard rooms are 12 to 17 m², so check the room type if you have luggage. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Christianshavn: canals and a quiet evening

    Christianshavn was established in the early 17th century, with canals, houseboats and the Church of Our Saviour. Christianshavn is on the M2 airport line, next to Kongens Nytorv, and it is usually quieter in the evening than the centre.

    Where to stay in Christianshavn

    • CPH Living, good value. A floating hotel on a converted barge at Langebrogade 1A, opened in 2009, with 12 double rooms, each with a glass wall onto the harbour, a rooftop terrace for guests and a free basic breakfast. Choose it for the harbour view at a lower price. Watch out: with 12 rooms there is little choice, the terrace is outdoors in December, and arrivals after 6pm must contact the hotel first. Check rates on Booking.com →

    The Lakes and Nørrebro: a calm street by the water

    Nørrebro proper starts across the Lakes. We could not verify a hotel on a Nørrebro street from a current official site, so the one pick here is on the Lakes at its edge, a short way from the old centre.

    Where to stay by the Lakes

    • Hotel Kong Arthur, comfortable. At Nørre Søgade 11, which the hotel describes as a peaceful corner of the inner city in the Nansensgade neighbourhood, with the Ni’mat Spa and a 24-hour reception. Its Christmas stay offer includes the New Nordic breakfast buffet and two cocktail vouchers for the lobby bar. Choose it for the spa and a walk by the water. Watch out: the Christmas page gives no rates, so ask what the offer costs for your dates. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Ørestad and the airport side: lower-cost and early flights

    This side of town is for an early flight or a lower room rate, and not for the markets. Ørestad has its own station, the Metro serves it by Cabinn’s account, and Cabinn gives the hotel as 6.5 kilometres from Central Station and Tivoli, so expect a ride in.

    Where to stay near the airport and Ørestad

    • Cabinn Metro, budget. At Arne Jacobsens Allé 2, built in 2009, with 708 rooms and 1,555 beds, a breakfast buffet at DKK 125 per person per day and paid parking at DKK 175 for 24 hours. The hotel says it is 10 minutes from the airport, and tells arrivals to take the train from Terminal 3 to Ørestad Station and walk through Field’s shopping centre. Choose it for a budget room near the airport. Watch out: it is 6.5 kilometres from Tivoli by the hotel’s count, and rooms are compact by design. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Clarion Hotel Copenhagen Airport, comfortable. At Ellehammersvej 20 in Kastrup, which says it is just 2 minutes from Terminal 3, with standard doubles of 33 m², a spa and a Metro ride to the capital of under 15 minutes. Choose it for an early flight or a long layover with a large room. Watch out: it is an airport hotel, so every market is a Metro ride away. Check rates on Booking.com →

    How to book Copenhagen at Christmas

    • Book early and read the terms. We did not check live availability or prices, so we quote none; the tiers reflect category and facilities, not rates.
    • Count what is in the price. There is no tourist tax and VAT is already included, so the figure shown is close to the total. Breakfast and parking are the usual extras.
    • Plan around 24 December. Tivoli is closed that day; Nimb lists a Christmas Eve dinner and Kong Arthur a Christmas stay offer. Check the Metro timetable for 24 and 25 December on m.dk.
    • Check the market dates. The Nytorv & Gammeltorv market runs 6 November to 21 December, the new Højbro Plads market 14 November to 20 December, and the City Hall Square market has only 11 December listed so far.
    • Check room size. Several rooms here are 12 to 17 m², so look at the room type if you have luggage.

    Frequently asked questions

    When is Tivoli open for Christmas in 2026?

    Tivoli's Christmas season runs from 13 November 2026 to 3 January 2027. Its opening-hours calendar, read on 4 November 2026, shows 11:00 to 22:00 from Sunday to Thursday and 11:00 to 23:00 on Friday and Saturday, with 24 December closed. It is open 11:00 to 23:00 on 25 and 26 December. Check tivoli.dk for changes.

    Which Christmas markets are on in Copenhagen in 2026?

    Nytorv & Gammeltorv runs 6 November to 21 December, according to its organiser. A new market, Christmas at Højbro Plads, runs 14 November to 20 December, and the city lists a City Hall Square market on 11 December with hours to be confirmed. We found no official 2026 listing for a Nyhavn market, so check before relying on one.

    What is the best area to stay for Tivoli at Christmas?

    Near Tivoli and Central Station puts the park within a short walk: Wakeup Copenhagen says Tivoli Gardens is within a few minutes' walk, and Nimb Hotel's restaurant is inside the park. Indre By suits the Nytorv & Gammeltorv market, which its organiser puts 200 metres from City Hall Square. Nyhavn suits harbour views, but we could not confirm a 2026 market there.

    Is there a tourist tax in Copenhagen in 2026?

    No. A Danish city needs legal authority from Parliament to charge a tourism levy, and Copenhagen's City Council asked for it in June 2024 without being granted it. Dansk Erhverv reported on 9 October 2026 that a DKK 20 a night charge for Copenhagen and Aarhus was left out of the 2027 finance bill. Danish VAT of 25% is already in the price shown.

    How do I get from Copenhagen Airport to the city at Christmas?

    The M2 Metro leaves from the station at Terminal 3 and the Metro puts the ride to the centre at 14 minutes. A 3-zone ticket is DKK 30 and valid for 1 hour 30 minutes; a 2-zone ticket is DKK 24. The Metro runs through the night, but we found no Christmas timetable notice, so check m.dk for 24 and 25 December.

    Is Tivoli open on Christmas Eve?

    No. Tivoli's own opening-hours calendar shows 24 December as closed in 2026, and it reopens on 25 December from 11:00 to 23:00. Nimb Hotel lists a Christmas Eve dinner at Nimb Brasserie, and 1 Hotel Copenhagen's holiday offer includes Tivoli tickets, which cannot be used that day.
