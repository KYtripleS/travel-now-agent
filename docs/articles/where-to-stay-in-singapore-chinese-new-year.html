    Singapore’s Chinese New Year is a public holiday with a festival laid over it, and two of the most useful things about 2027 are already fixed. The Ministry of Manpower lists Chinese New Year as Saturday 6 and Sunday 7 February 2027, and the Singapore Tourism Board’s festival page gives the same dates. The hotel bill has a quirk of its own: hotels commonly add a 10% service charge and then 9% GST on the total, so a headline rate rises by 19.9%. What is not yet published is the programme: the Chinatown light-up and festive fair, River Hongbao and the Chingay Parade had no 2027 dates when we looked on 1 November 2026.     The dates for 2027: what is published

    The Ministry of Manpower’s list gives the first two days as Saturday 6 and Sunday 7 February 2027, and adds a condition: Monday 8 February is a public holiday only if your rest day falls on 7 February. Many people will have a long weekend from Saturday to Monday, but not everyone, so do not assume a Monday holiday for staff, shops or the person you are meeting. In 2026 the two days were Tuesday 17 and Wednesday 18 February.

    Chinatown: the street light-up and festive fair

    The Tourism Board’s page lists a street light-up, a festive fair and food fair and weekly stage shows in Chinatown, and gives no dates for any of them. The organiser’s site, run by the Kreta Ayer community, put three goat-themed designs for the 2027 centrepiece to a public vote that closed on 19 July 2026, and says the winner “may become” the centrepiece. The People’s Association published a tender on 6 August 2026 for the 2027 light-up decorations, so the light-up is planned, though not dated.

    Last year’s is described on the organiser’s page, no longer on its live site. The 2026 theme was “Galloping into the Prosperous Year”, with an 8.8-metre golden horse as the centrepiece and overhangs along New Bridge Road and Eu Tong Sen Street, Upper Cross Street and South Bridge Road. We could not open an official page that still carries the 2026 light-up or fair dates, and we will not print dates from unofficial sites. Check chinatownfestivals.sg once the 2027 dates are posted.

    River Hongbao

    River Hongbao is a free festival of giant lanterns, fireworks, stage performances, rides and a food street, organised by the Singapore Federation of Chinese Clan Associations with the Singapore Chinese Chamber of Commerce and Industry, SPH Media’s Chinese Media Group, the Tourism Board and the People’s Association. Its 40th edition ran at Gardens by the Bay from 15 to 24 February 2026, opening at 6.30pm on the first two evenings and at 2.30pm daily from the 17th, the first day of the new year. That is last year’s pattern: it opened two days before the first day. Its site still describes 2026 and carries no 2027 dates.

    The Chingay Parade

    The Chingay Parade is a multicultural street parade, listed on the Tourism Board’s festival page. Its official site says the 2026 parade was held on 27 and 28 February, ten days after the first day, under the theme “WISH”. It had no 2027 date when we looked, so do not build a stay around it until one is announced.

    Getting between the festivals

    The Chinatown organiser lists Chinatown station (North East and Downtown lines), Outram Park (East-West line) and Maxwell (Thomson-East Coast line) as the stations for its sites. One Chinatown hotel’s page counts two MRT stops from Chinatown to Bayfront, for Marina Bay and Gardens by the Bay. We found no 2027 notice about extended MRT hours, road closures or crowd arrangements, so none is described here.

    What shuts

    We found no official list of which shops and stalls close at Chinese New Year. It is widely reported that many family-run hawker stalls, independent shops and traditional restaurants shut for the first days of the holiday, and that malls, chain restaurants and tourist-facing places are more likely to stay open. Treat that as a general pattern, not a promise; it varies stall by stall. The National Environment Agency publishes a separate schedule of hawker centres closed for cleaning, which on data.gov.sg covered 2026 only when we looked. If a particular stall or restaurant matters, check it before you go.

    The rules that decide where you sleep

    • Chinatown is the festival; the rest is transport. The lights, the fair and the stage shows are in Chinatown. Everywhere else in this guide is a short MRT ride away.
    • Treat the holiday as a long weekend, not a week. Saturday and Sunday are fixed, Monday is conditional, and ordinary working days resume after.
    • We have no 2027 rates and publish none. We found no source for what hotels will charge during the holiday, so we give no percentage. The long weekend and the festival are reasons to book early, not a measured rise.

    Singapore’s hotel prices: service charge and GST

    Singapore has no separate tourist or accommodation tax that we could find; the extras on a room are the service charge and GST. The Inland Revenue Authority of Singapore says hotels and restaurants often add a service charge, typically 10%, and that GST is calculated on the total including it. The current GST rate is 9%. Hotels may display prices before GST if they show a prominent statement that GST and service charge apply, which is why a booking site’s first price can be lower than the final one.

    Getting in from Changi Airport

    • By MRT. Take the train from Changi Airport station to Tanah Merah and change onto the East-West line towards Tuas Link, or go to Expo and change onto the Downtown line towards Bukit Panjang. The first train to Tanah Merah is at 5.31am Monday to Saturday and 5.59am on Sundays and public holidays, which covers 6 and 7 February. The last connection to Tuas Link is at 11.18pm daily.
    • By taxi. Changi’s own page says a ride to the city takes about 30 minutes and costs between S$25 and S$45, all metered, plus an airport surcharge of S$8 from 5pm to 11.59pm and S$6 at other times. Midnight and peak-hour surcharges apply on top.
    • Paying and late arrivals. Changi lists contactless Mastercard and Visa cards, mobile wallets and the Singapore Tourist Pass for public transport, with fees on foreign-issued bank cards. Most buses and the MRT do not run through the night; taxis and private hire cars stay available at every terminal.

    Chinatown and Outram: the light-up on foot

    Chinatown is the quarter south of the river with the Buddha Tooth Relic Temple, the Sri Mariamman Temple, hawker centres and shophouses, and it is where the festival’s lights and fair are held. Staying here means you walk to the lights and back, at the price of busy pavements in the evenings. Our Singapore guide for first-timers covers the district, and things to do in Singapore covers the rest of the city.

    Clarke Quay and the river: evenings by the water

    The Singapore River runs from the Bay past Boat Quay and Clarke Quay, where old godowns and shophouses are now restaurants and bars, up to Robertson Quay, where the riverside walk is quieter. At its mouth stands the Fullerton Building, once the General Post Office. Clarke Quay station is on the North East line and the Downtown line stops at Fort Canning, so Chinatown is a short ride away, though we have not timed it from either hotel below.

    Where to stay on the river

    • The Fullerton Hotel Singapore, splurge. 400 rooms in the Fullerton Building, completed in 1928 as the General Post Office, opened as a hotel in 2001 and made a National Monument in 2015, at the mouth of the river. Choose it for heritage between the river and the Bay. Watch out: price. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • The Warehouse Hotel, comfortable. 37 rooms in a godown built in 1895 on the river at Robertson Quay, opened as a hotel in January 2017, with a rooftop pool and free bicycles. Choose it for character at the quiet end of the river. Watch out: with 37 rooms there are few to go round, so a holiday weekend can sell out early. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Marina Bay: River Hongbao and the skyline

    Marina Bay is the Singapore of the postcards: Marina Bay Sands, the ArtScience Museum and Gardens by the Bay, where River Hongbao was held in 2026. The Circle and Downtown lines meet at Bayfront and the Thomson-East Coast line stops at Gardens by the Bay. The Chinatown lights are not here, so this is the area for River Hongbao, if it returns to the Gardens, and for the view.

    Where to stay at Marina Bay

    • Marina Bay Sands, splurge. Opened in 2010, three towers joined at the top by the SkyPark, whose 150-metre infinity pool, 200 metres up, is for hotel guests only. A US$1.75 billion renovation has been rebuilding its rooms into about 1,850, some 770 of them suites. Choose it for the pool and the view. Watch out: price, and the crowds in the mall below.     • Pan Pacific Singapore, comfortable. 790 rooms at Marina Square, linked to the Marina Square mall and the Suntec convention centre, with an outdoor pool. Choose it for the Bay on foot at a lower price than the hotels on its edge. Watch out: its size; with 790 rooms it is a busy hotel. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Bugis and City Hall: transport and a calmer base

    Bugis and City Hall sit between the civic core and Kampong Glam, with the National Museum, Bugis Junction and Haji Lane around them. The festival is not here, but the MRT is: the hotels below each name a station within minutes, and the Bay is a short ride. It is a sensible base if you want Chinatown for an evening and somewhere quieter to come back to.

    Where to stay in Bugis and City Hall

    • Raffles Singapore, splurge. It opened in 1887 and reopened on 1 August 2019 after a restoration that began in 2017, as an all-suite hotel with 115 suites around its gardens and verandahs, a short walk from City Hall station. Choose it for the history. Watch out: price. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Rendezvous Hotel Singapore, comfortable. At 9 Bras Basah Road, with Bencoolen station next to the hotel, Bras Basah three minutes’ walk and the National Museum five, by its own count, plus an outdoor pool. Choose it for a station at the door. Watch out: its Club Lounge is for guests aged 18 and over, so check before booking with children. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Village Hotel Bugis, good value. At 390 Victoria Street in Kampong Glam, with Bugis station three minutes’ walk and Bugis Junction five, by its own count, an outdoor pool and a halal-certified restaurant, Tarboush. Its Superior Rooms are 32 square metres. Choose it for room size and a station nearby. Watch out: its page carried a notice of façade works until 6 November 2026, so ask whether any work continues into February. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Orchard Road: malls and a rest

    Orchard Road is more than two kilometres of malls, with the Singapore Botanic Gardens at its western end. The North-South line runs beneath it at Orchard, Somerset and Dhoby Ghaut. The Tourism Board’s festival page lists Chinatown, River Hongbao and Chingay, not Orchard Road, so this is the area for a calmer evening, not for the festival, and malls are the likelier places to find shops open if the family-run ones in Chinatown are shut.

    Where to stay on Orchard Road

    • Hilton Singapore Orchard, comfortable. 1,080 rooms at 333 Orchard Road, the former Mandarin Orchard, reopened as a Hilton on 24 February 2022 after a S$150 million renovation; it is the brand’s largest hotel in Asia Pacific. Choose it for the middle of Orchard Road. Watch out: its size. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • YOTEL Singapore Orchard Road, good value. 610 compact rooms, which the brand calls cabins, over 30 floors at 366 Orchard Road, opened in 2017, with a rooftop pool. Choose it for a lower price on Orchard Road. Watch out: the rooms are small by design. Check rates on Booking.com →

    How to book Singapore at Chinese New Year

    • Add the 19.9% before you compare. A price marked “++” excludes the 10% service charge and 9% GST, and booking sites differ in what they show first.
    • Keep free cancellation. The light-up, River Hongbao and Chingay dates were unannounced for 2027 when we looked, and free cancellation lets you move once they are.
    • Decide on Chinatown first. Furama City Centre, PARKROYAL COLLECTION Pickering and The Clan are the three hotels in Chinatown and Telok Ayer here.
    • Ask for the holiday terms. None of the hotels’ own pages that we read listed a Chinese New Year minimum stay for 2027, but we did not check every offers page.
    • Check room size, then compare totals. YOTEL’s cabins are small by design and Village Hotel Bugis states 32 square metres for its Superior Room. Our hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book.

    Frequently asked questions

    When is Chinese New Year 2027 in Singapore?

    The Ministry of Manpower lists Chinese New Year as Saturday 6 and Sunday 7 February 2027. Monday 8 February is a public holiday only if your rest day falls on 7 February. Chinese New Year's Eve is Friday 5 February, by our arithmetic.

    Where should I stay in Singapore for Chinese New Year?

    Chinatown and Outram, if you want the street light-up and festive fair on foot; last year's lights ran along South Bridge Road, New Bridge Road, Eu Tong Sen Street and Upper Cross Street. Marina Bay suits River Hongbao if it returns to Gardens by the Bay, and Bugis, City Hall or Orchard Road make calmer bases on the MRT.

    When is the Chinatown light-up in 2027?

    Not announced when we looked on 1 November 2026. The Tourism Board lists a street light-up and festive fair without dates, and the organiser's site had only put three 2027 centrepiece designs to a vote, which closed on 19 July 2026. The 2026 theme was a golden horse; the 2027 designs show a goat.

    When are River Hongbao and the Chingay Parade in 2027?

    Neither had a 2027 date when we looked. River Hongbao 2026 ran at Gardens by the Bay from 15 to 24 February 2026, opening two days before the first day, and the Chingay Parade 2026 was held on 27 and 28 February.

    Is there a hotel tax in Singapore?

    We found no separate accommodation tax. Hotels commonly add a 10% service charge, and GST, currently 9%, is charged on the total including it, so a headline rate rises by about 19.9%. A S$300 room shown before charges comes to S$359.70 by our arithmetic.

    Do shops and food stalls close at Chinese New Year in Singapore?

    It is widely reported that many family-run hawker stalls, independent shops and traditional restaurants close for the first days, while malls and chain restaurants are more likely to stay open. We found no official list, so check a place you are counting on before you go.
