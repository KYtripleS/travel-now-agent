A car is the practical way to see Iceland outside Reykjavik. Public transport between towns is thin, the sights sit along one long ring road and the daylight in winter is short, so a hire car buys you time as well as reach. If you are staying in Reykjavik only, you probably do not need one, and we say so below.

On one set of dates, the cheapest listed offer on DiscoverCars was lower than the price on Blue Car Rental’s own site in every class we could compare, though the two are not like for like (see the price section). DiscoverCars is a partner and pays us a commission if you book through it. The rental firms’ own sites pay us nothing.

How to read it:

  • DiscoverCars was lower in every row where both sell the class. By our arithmetic from Blue’s own dollar figures, about $104 lower for a small automatic and about $72 lower for the SUV.
  • The two prices do not cover the same things. Blue’s page says all its cars come with CDW, SCDW, TP and GP insurances and shows the total “with insurances”. DiscoverCars’ listing showed a “Low deposit”, “Average deposit” or “No deposit” label and its own cover is sold separately, so the DiscoverCars total will rise if you add cover.
  • The listed DiscoverCars offers are “or similar” cars and its landing-page prices are marked approximate. Its page said prices were last updated at 22:24 on 17 November and may vary by dates and length of rental. Re-run the search for your own dates.
  • The Duster at Blue is a “used model”. Blue’s standard Dacia Duster, manual 4WD, was 63,971 ISK (about $514).
  • Not checked. Hertz Iceland and Lotus Car Rental show prices only after a dated search. Hertz’s page showed none without one, and Lotus’ date picker would not accept our dates in our automated browser, so we print no figure for either rather than a guess.

Insurance and the excess

Blue Car Rental says on its own site that its prices include CDW, SCDW, Gravel Protection (GP) and Theft Protection (TP). It also sells a Liability Waiver that it says can reduce your liability for damage to zero, excluding intentional damage, negligence and driving where it is prohibited, and it adds: “Insurances can only be applied before the rental starts, not during the rental period.” The page we read gave no excess figure.

Hertz Iceland lists CDW, theft protection and third-party liability as included in every rental. Optional extras are MAX Cover, which it calls a zero-excess package, plus Gravel Protection and Sand & Ash Protection, and roadside assistance. That page gave no excess figure either, so ask for the amount before you pay.

DiscoverCars labels each offer with its deposit level, as above. It also sells its own Full Coverage; its terms page returned a server error when we tried to read it on 19 November, so we describe nothing about who pays the desk first or how a claim works. Read that page before you rely on it.

The practical point from these terms: gravel, sand and ash damage are separate items, and whether you have them depends on the firm and the package, so check the line by name. Safetravel adds one rule that no package changes: “no insurance covers any damage of a vehicle while crossing a river”.

Rules of the road that change the booking

  • F-roads. Safetravel says F-roads are usually shut from mid-September until June or July. The road authority (Vegagerdin) says most mountain roads open in late June or early July and that it “maintains strict closure policies which travellers must respect, or face heavy fines”. Driving on F-roads and gravel roads is not itself off-road driving, and off-road driving is illegal. For a December trip, F-roads are not on the table.
  • Winter conditions. Vegagerdin warns that roads can close without warning in severe storms and that new winter drivers may be better off avoiding unnecessary risks when sudden weather arrives. Safetravel says an impassable road means do not drive, with or without 4x4. Check the road authority’s Traffic info site (umferdin.is) and Safetravel before and during each day, or call the traffic service on 1777.
  • Winter tyres. Hertz Iceland says winter tyres are fitted on all its cars from November to April. We did not find an official page stating legal tyre dates, so ask your firm.
  • Daylight. Our reading of timeanddate (not an official source) for Reykjavik in 2026 is sunrise 11:14 and sunset 15:29 on 14 December, 11:22 and 15:29 on 21 December, and 11:20 and 15:41 on 31 December: roughly four hours of light. Plan driving legs for the middle of the day.
  • Speed. Vegagerdin gives 90 km/h as the highest legal speed on highways outside urban areas, 80 km/h on gravel roads and 50 km/h by default in urban areas.
  • Licence and age. We could not open an official island.is or Samgongustofa page for visitor licences, so this is the rental firms’ own terms. Blue says drivers must be 20 for 2WD or medium 4WD, 23 for larger 4WD, SUVs or luxury models, must have held a licence for a year and must carry the physical licence; a licence not in Roman script needs an International Driving Permit. Confirm against island.is before you fly if your licence is not in the Latin alphabet.

When not to rent

  • Reykjavik only. Stay central and book day tours that collect from your hotel. A car there is parking cost and little use. See where to stay in Reykjavik, below.
  • Northern lights nights. Tours collect you after dark, so you do not drive icy roads tired. Our Reykjavik northern lights guide covers where to stay for them.
  • First winter drive, nervous in snow. A guided tour for the one day you want the south coast removes the decision. Tour operators publish their own cancellation terms for weather; read them before booking.
  • F-road routes in winter. The road authority and Safetravel say these roads are usually closed, so there is nothing to rent a car for.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a car in Iceland?

Not if you stay in Reykjavik only: you can rely on walking and day tours that collect from town. A car matters if you want the south coast, the ring road or your own timetable. In winter, leave extra days because roads can close without warning.

How much does a rental car in Iceland cost for a week?

For pick-up at Keflavik Airport on 21 November 2026 and return on 29 November (8 days), read on 19 November, DiscoverCars listed from $269.44 for a small manual, $305.76 for a small automatic and $367.60 for a compact SUV, by our arithmetic from its per-day prices. Blue Car Rental's own site showed about $410 and $440 for an automatic small car and a used-model Duster, with insurances included. Prices change daily.

Is DiscoverCars cheaper than booking with Blue Car Rental directly?

On the dates we read, DiscoverCars' lowest offers were lower than Blue's in both classes both sell, but Blue's totals include CDW, SCDW, theft and gravel protection, so the two are not like for like.

Can I drive on F-roads in winter?

No. Safetravel says F-roads are usually closed from mid-September until June or July, and the Icelandic Road and Coastal Administration says it maintains strict closure policies that travellers must respect or face heavy fines.

Does rental insurance cover gravel and ash damage?

It depends on the firm and package. Blue Car Rental says its prices include Gravel Protection; Hertz Iceland lists Gravel Protection and Sand & Ash Protection as optional extras. Safetravel warns that no insurance covers damage while crossing a river.

What licence do I need to rent a car in Iceland?

We could not open an official visitor-licence page. Blue Car Rental says you must be 20 for 2WD or medium 4WD and 23 for larger 4WD or SUVs, have held a licence for a year, carry the physical licence, and have an International Driving Permit if it is not in Roman script. Hertz Iceland gives a minimum age of 20.
