Where to Stay in Kyoto (2026): Five Areas and 14 Hotels and Ryokan, Compared

By Casey, Gently Yonder editor

Stay downtown near a subway line for the best all-round base, by Kyoto Station for the Shinkansen and Kansai Airport, in Gion and Higashiyama for the old city at dawn. Five Kyoto areas, 14 hotels and ryokan, and the tax that rose in March 2026.

Kyoto is small on the map and slow on the ground. The temples you came for are spread around the edges of the city, the buses that reach them are some of the most crowded in Japan, and a base that looks central can leave you in queues for an hour a day. So in Kyoto, more than anywhere, the right place to stay is the one that puts you on a train or subway line, not just near the sights.

This guide covers the five areas worth considering, then fourteen places to stay across them: grand hotels, a hostel, two of the most famous ryokan in Japan, and a hotel in a 1930s schoolhouse. They are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in September 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

Two things to know before you choose

The accommodation tax went up sharply on 1 March 2026. Kyoto now charges, per person per night: ¥200 on rooms under ¥6,000, ¥400 from ¥6,000, ¥1,000 from ¥20,000, ¥4,000 from ¥50,000, and ¥10,000 on rooms of ¥100,000 or more. On a luxury room for two, that is up to ¥20,000 a night on top of the rate, usually collected at the hotel.

The buses are the bottleneck. The bus-only one-day pass was withdrawn in September 2023 to ease overcrowding; the subway-and-bus one-day pass (¥1,100) remains. A base near a subway or rail station lets you use the trains for most of the distance and the buses for as little as possible.

Downtown: Karasuma, Kawaramachi and Sanjo

For most first trips, this is the base. Downtown is the grid between Karasuma and Kawaramachi streets, from Oike down to Shijo, and it has the most transport in the city: the Karasuma and Tozai subway lines, the Hankyu railway to Osaka (and, with a change at Katsura, Arashiyama), and, across the river, the Keihan line to Gion-Shijo and Fushimi Inari. Nishiki Market, the lanes of Pontocho and the Kamo River are all on foot, and so are more restaurants than you will have evenings for.

Where to stay downtown

  • HOTEL THE MITSUI KYOTO, splurge. 160 rooms beside Nijo Castle, open since 2020, with interiors by André Fu, a central garden and a spa fed by hot-spring water drawn from 1,000 metres below. Choose it for the most serene luxury hotel in the centre.
  • Ace Hotel Kyoto, comfortable, design. 213 rooms on Karasuma Street, partly inside the 1926 Shin-Puh-Kan, Kyoto’s old Central Telephone Office, with a new wing by Kengo Kuma and the first Stumptown Coffee in Japan in the lobby. Choose it for a lively hotel with a sense of place.
  • Mitsui Garden Hotel Kyoto Sanjo PREMIER, comfortable. On historic Sanjo-dori, built around eight gardens, with a large public bath and a 2025 Good Design Award to its name. Choose it for calm and a Japanese bath at a mid-range price.
  • Cross Hotel Kyoto, comfortable. 301 rooms at Kawaramachi-Sanjo, four minutes on foot from the Tozai line and the Keihan line. Choose it for a well-run base where the city is busiest.
  • Len Kyoto Kawaramachi, budget. A hostel near Kawaramachi with a café and bar downstairs that locals use too. Choose it for a dorm bed or a simple room with good company.

The two famous ryokan

A few streets west of Kawaramachi, on a narrow lane in Fuyacho, two of the most celebrated ryokan in Japan face each other. Tawaraya opened in 1709, has been run by the same family for generations, and keeps just 18 rooms. Hiiragiya, founded in 1818, received the feudal lords and writers of its day. Neither is a hotel with a Japanese theme: expect tatami, futons, a kaiseki dinner served in your room, and customs to follow. Choose one for a single unforgettable night, not a week.

Kyoto Station: the transport base

The area around the station is not the Kyoto of postcards, but it is the most practical base if you are arriving by Shinkansen (see Tokyo to Kyoto, compared), flying into Kansai Airport on the Haruka express, or planning day trips to Nara and Osaka. The Karasuma subway line runs north from here into downtown.

Where to stay near Kyoto Station

  • THE THOUSAND KYOTO, comfortable to splurge. 222 rooms two minutes’ walk from the station, open since January 2019, with calm, pared-back rooms. Choose it for a refined base that is still a suitcase-roll from the platforms.
  • Hotel Granvia Kyoto, comfortable. 541 rooms inside the Kyoto Station building itself, with an indoor pool. Choose it for zero walking with luggage and the trains downstairs.

Gion and Higashiyama: the old city, early and late

Gion’s teahouse streets, Yasaka Shrine, the lanes of Ninenzaka and Sannenzaka climbing to Kiyomizu-dera: this is the Kyoto most visitors picture, and staying here lets you see it at seven in the morning, before the tour groups, and again after dark; a self-guided audio walk from WeGoTrip is a good companion for those quiet hours. The price is crowds by day, higher room rates, and fewer subway stations, so you will lean on the Keihan line and the buses. Our Gion guide has the streets and the etiquette.

Where to stay in Gion and Higashiyama

  • Park Hyatt Kyoto, splurge. 70 rooms and 9 suites on the Ninenzaka slope, some looking straight at the Yasaka Pagoda. Choose it for the old city at your door and the view that goes with it.
  • Four Seasons Hotel Kyoto, splurge. At the foot of the Higashiyama hills, built around Shakusui-en, a pond garden about 800 years old. Choose it for a resort’s calm with the temples a short ride away.
  • The Hotel Seiryu Kyoto Kiyomizu, splurge, heritage. 48 rooms in the former Kiyomizu Elementary School, built in 1933 and reopened as a hotel in March 2020, near Kiyomizu-dera. Choose it for a hotel with a story, in the heart of Higashiyama.

Arashiyama: the river at dusk

On the western edge of the city, Arashiyama has the bamboo grove, Tenryu-ji and Togetsukyo Bridge, and it is packed by day. By evening the day-trippers are gone and it is one of the quietest places in Kyoto. It is a short train ride from Kyoto Station on the JR Sagano line, or from downtown on the Hankyu line.

Where to stay in Arashiyama

  • HOSHINOYA Kyoto, splurge, ryokan. 25 rooms upriver, every one facing the water; you check in near Togetsukyo Bridge and arrive by the hotel’s own boat. Choose it for a ryokan night you reach by river.

The northern hills: seclusion

  • Aman Kyoto, splurge. 26 rooms, suites and pavilions in a hidden forest garden in Takagamine, at the foot of Hidari Daimonji, with an onsen spa. Choose it if you want Kyoto’s silence more than its sights. Watch out: almost everywhere is a taxi ride away.

If you are still undecided

Choose downtown, near a subway station. It gives you the most of Kyoto for the least time on buses, and the old city is a short ride away when you want it. If you are arriving from Kansai Airport or leaving early by Shinkansen, stay by Kyoto Station for those nights. If Kyoto is a day trip from Osaka, our Osaka or Kyoto guide weighs the two bases. Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Kyoto for first-timers?

Downtown, between Karasuma and Kawaramachi streets. It has the most transport in the city (the Karasuma and Tozai subway lines plus the Hankyu and Keihan railways), puts Nishiki Market, Pontocho and the Kamo River on foot, and keeps your bus time to a minimum.

Is it better to stay near Kyoto Station or in Gion?

Near Kyoto Station if you are arriving by Shinkansen or from Kansai Airport on the Haruka, or planning day trips to Nara and Osaka. In Gion and Higashiyama if you want to walk the old streets at dawn and after dark. Gion costs more and relies more on buses and the Keihan line.

How much is the accommodation tax in Kyoto?

Since 1 March 2026 Kyoto charges, per person per night: ¥200 for rooms under ¥6,000, ¥400 from ¥6,000, ¥1,000 from ¥20,000, ¥4,000 from ¥50,000 and ¥10,000 for rooms of ¥100,000 or more. It is usually collected at the hotel.

Is there still a one-day bus pass in Kyoto?

The bus-only one-day pass was withdrawn in September 2023 to ease crowding. The Subway & Bus One-day Pass, ¥1,100, is still sold and covers the city subway and buses and some other bus routes.

Should I stay in a ryokan in Kyoto?

For a night or two, yes. Tawaraya (1709) and Hiiragiya (1818), facing each other in Fuyacho, are among the most celebrated ryokan in Japan, and HOSHINOYA Kyoto in Arashiyama is reached by boat. Expect tatami, futons, a kaiseki dinner and customs to follow; many travellers pair one ryokan night with a hotel for the rest of the stay.

Is Arashiyama a good place to stay in Kyoto?

For quiet evenings, yes: once the day-trippers leave, it is one of the calmest parts of the city. It is on the western edge, a short ride from Kyoto Station on the JR Sagano line, so it suits a night or two rather than a whole stay.
