The map shows the hotels and apartments around Roma Norte that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

A Mexico City hotel bill carries two percentages before you reach the lobby: the city’s 3.5% lodging tax and Mexico’s 16% IVA, which makes 19.5% in all on the room rate. Unlike Cancún, we found no separate visitor fee on top, so the choice that matters more is the area: the capital is large, and a hotel in the wrong district can add a long taxi ride to every plan.

This guide covers five areas, from Roma Norte to the Centro Histórico, 12 hotels, the airport links and the charges. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

What a Mexico City stay costs on top of the room

Two charges matter, and both sit on the hotel bill:

Charge Rate Where it appears
Mexico City lodging tax (impuesto por la prestación de servicios de hospedaje) 3.5% of the value of the lodging at hotels, hostels and motels; 5% for flats and houses when a platform collects the payment On the hotel bill
IVA, Mexico’s value added tax 16% of the room rate On the hotel bill

The city’s Código Fiscal, in article 164, says hotels apply the 3.5% to the total value of what they receive for lodging. Article 162 makes the provider the taxpayer but requires it to pass the tax on to the guest, so it appears on your bill. For flats and houses where an intermediary collects the payment, the same article sets the rate at 5%, which is why a platform stay can cost more in tax than a hotel stay.

Article 163 explains what counts as the room price: it includes deposits, advances and other charges tied to the stay. If a package includes extras such as meals or transport and the hotel does not itemise and prove them, the whole value is treated as lodging. Ask for an itemised invoice when a rate bundles services.

The December 2025 decree that reformed the Código Fiscal for 2026 changed the audit threshold for lodging-tax payers but did not amend articles 162 to 164. We could not load a consolidated 2026 text, so check the rate on your invoice.

Worked example (our arithmetic, not an official quote). Four nights, a room at 3,000 pesos a night:

  • Room: 4 × 3,000 = 12,000 pesos
  • IVA at 16%: 1,920 pesos
  • Lodging tax at 3.5%: 420 pesos
  • Total: 14,340 pesos, which is 19.5% above the room rate

This assumes both percentages apply to the plain room rate, and we have not confirmed in what order a given hotel applies them. A service charge or a parking fee would be extra, so compare the final total, not the headline rate.

Getting in from the airport, and around

  • At Mexico City International Airport (AICM, code MEX), the airport’s mobility page lists public transport (buses, Metro and Metrobús), authorised taxis, car rental, parking and the Aerotrén between Terminals 1 and 2. If your ticket names a different airport, such as AIFA, the transfers differ and are not covered here.
  • By Metrobús, Line 4 runs an airport service to Terminals 1 and 2, starting from Amajac station on Paseo de la Reforma. The fare is 30 pesos. The city’s tourism site says it takes no cash, only the Integrated Mobility Card, a contactless bank card or a digital wallet. The airport lists boarding at Terminal 1, door 7; for Terminal 2 its page gives door 2, while a February 2026 airport notice names door 4 as a temporary point during works, so follow the signs. Service runs Monday to Saturday from 4:30 a.m. to midnight and on Sundays from 5:00 a.m. to midnight. The city describes the route as reaching the Centro Histórico and Bellas Artes, and says the bus has an area for heavy luggage.
  • By Metro, Terminal Aérea station on Line 5 is at Terminal 1. The fare is 5 pesos a trip, paid with the Integrated Mobility Card (15 pesos) or a contactless card or wallet. The city’s tourism site does not recommend it with heavy luggage, because of the stairs, the turnstiles and the changes of line.
  • By taxi, the airport publishes a list of authorised taxi companies, among them TaxiRide, Yellow Cab and Sitio 300. The city’s tourism site says official airport taxis are bought in advance at the authorised booths inside the terminals, with fixed fares by zone, and advises ignoring anyone offering a ride in the arrivals corridor. Neither source gives fares, so ask at the booth.
  • Altitude. Mexico City sits at 2,240 metres. The city’s visitor guidance says many people have no symptoms, and advises resting, drinking extra water and avoiding alcohol and strenuous activity if you feel the effects.

Roma Norte: cafés, mansions and walking

Roma Norte is the district most first-timers end up in. The city’s tourism site describes Colonia Roma as an architectural showpiece and a centre of the capital’s bohemian culture, and its tree-lined blocks of old houses hold galleries, bookshops and cafés. Hotels here are small, and many are converted houses.

Condesa: leafy and design-led

Condesa sits beside Roma, and the city’s tourism site calls it the centre of design, youth and cosmopolitan life, with art deco architecture and an elliptical street layout. It is quieter than the old centre and strong on restaurants.

Where to stay in Condesa

  • Condesa df, comfortable. A Grupo Habita hotel of 40 rooms and suites, with furniture by the Paris designer India Mahdavi, the Terraza rooftop with a sushi bar and views of Parque España, and El Patio, a courtyard restaurant. Choose it for a boutique stay on a tree-lined street. Watch out: the entry Patio Room is 21 m², and the rooftop is open until 1 a.m. Thursday to Saturday, so ask where your room sits. Choose it for a pool and full hotel services in Condesa. Watch out: it is a large, social hotel, not a quiet guest house. Check rates on Booking.com →

Polanco: Masaryk and the park

Polanco is the upscale district. The city’s tourism site calls Avenida Presidente Masaryk the most exclusive and elegant street in the capital, with luxury boutiques and restaurants, and it has been pedestrianised. The big hotels here face Chapultepec Park.

Where to stay in Polanco

  • Camino Real Polanco México, splurge. Open since 1968, designed by Ricardo Legorreta and built for the Olympic Games, with rooms such as the 42 m² Luxe Plus King and the 110 m² Junior Suite Superior King, and the Perpetual Motion Fountain at the door. Choose it for an architectural landmark. Watch out: its street address is Calzada General Mariano Escobedo 700, so check the map for the walk to Masaryk. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hyatt Regency Mexico City, comfortable. At Campos Eliseos 204 in Polanco, with renovated rooms, a heated pool and sundeck that look over Chapultepec Park, the Teppan Grill and a Presidential Suite on the 43rd floor. Choose it for park views and a full-service tower. Watch out: Hyatt’s page calls the pool indoor in one place and outdoor in another, so confirm which before you pack. Check rates on Booking.com →

Centro Histórico: the old city

The Centro Histórico is the historic core, and UNESCO lists the Historic Centre of Mexico City and Xochimilco as a World Heritage Site, noting a cathedral, the largest on the continent, and the Palacio de las Bellas Artes. It suits a stay built around the Zócalo and the museums, with less of the café-and-boutique texture of Roma.

Where to stay in the Centro Histórico

  • Gran Hotel Ciudad de México, comfortable. At Avenida 16 de Septiembre 82, in an Art Nouveau building with stained glass that the hotel calls one of the largest in the world, a terrace restaurant looking over the Plaza Mayor, and rooms from the 26 m² Junior Suite with a Zócalo view to the 45 m² Master Suite. Choose it for a historic hotel above the square. Watch out: the hotel says it takes reservations only through its own website or by phone, and warns about third-party messages that ask for payment.   • Hotel Gillow, good value. Describes itself as the oldest hotel in the Centro Histórico, a few steps from the Zócalo, with the restaurant-bar La Capilla, free Wi-Fi, free bottled water in the room and breakfast included with the bed-and-breakfast rate. Choose it for the lowest bill in the old city. Watch out: room service costs extra, and the site does not publish room sizes. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Catedral, good value. At Guatemala 16, with 13 room types across two towers and about 185 rooms, free valet parking subject to availability, and a pre-Hispanic temple preserved beneath the lobby. Choose it for a large central hotel with a cathedral-view terrace. Watch out: the site publishes no fixed nightly prices, and the two towers differ, so check which one you are booking. Juárez and Reforma: towers and Zona Rosa

Paseo de la Reforma is the avenue of business towers, and Colonia Juárez, with its Zona Rosa, lies beside it. NH describes the Zona Rosa as the city’s LGBTQ+ district, with shops, bars and restaurants, a two-minute walk from the financial district and 15 minutes from the historic centre by subway. It is a practical base for a short or work-led stay.

Where to stay in Juárez and Reforma

  • Hotel Geneve, comfortable. At Londres 130 in Colonia Juárez, with 226 rooms in a classic European style with carved wood furniture, a Master Suite of 51 m² and free parking for guests who arrive by car. Choose it for a traditional hotel in Zona Rosa. Watch out: the site gives a size only for the Master Suite, and warns about fraudulent sites and callers, so pay only through the hotel’s official channels. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Marquis Reforma Hotel & Spa, splurge. An Art Deco hotel on Paseo de la Reforma with 124 rooms and 80 suites, within steps of Chapultepec Park and castle, and a spa of more than 1,500 m² with more than 60 treatments. Choose it for a spa stay on the avenue. Watch out: the hotel describes itself as surrounded by business towers, so expect a corporate district. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • NH Collection Mexico City Reforma, comfortable. On Liverpool 155 in the Zona Rosa, with 306 recently renovated rooms with 48-inch TVs, a heated outdoor pool, a gym and a restaurant. Choose it for a mid-size chain hotel with a pool. Watch out: indoor parking is listed at 14 US dollars a day, and pets cost 630 pesos a night each. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Mexico City

  • Count the tax. Add 19.5% to every room rate: a 3,000-peso room for four nights comes to about 14,340 pesos.
  • Check what the rate includes. If breakfast or other services are bundled, the lodging tax can apply to the whole value unless the hotel itemises; ask for the itemised invoice.
  • Check the room size. Sizes vary a lot: Condesa df’s Patio Room is 21 m², while the INNSiDE rooms run 39 to 45 m². If you need space, book the category above the cheapest.
  • Book direct where the hotel says so. Gran Hotel Ciudad de México accepts reservations only through its own website or phone, and Hotel Geneve warns about look-alike sites.
  • Compare totals. Hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book, and how hotel taxes work around the world puts the 3.5% in context. For the Caribbean side of Mexico, see where to stay in Cancún, which has a different state tax and a separate visitor fee.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Mexico City for a first trip?

Roma Norte, a walkable district of old mansions, galleries and cafés. Choose Condesa for a leafy, design-led base, Polanco for Avenida Presidente Masaryk and Chapultepec Park, the Centro Histórico for the Zócalo and the cathedral, and Juárez and Reforma for business towers and the Zona Rosa.

How much tax is added to a Mexico City hotel room?

Mexico City's lodging tax is 3.5% of the lodging value at hotels, hostels and motels, on top of Mexico's 16% IVA: 19.5% in all. On a 3,000-peso room that is 585 pesos a night, by our arithmetic, assuming both apply to the plain room rate. For flats and houses where a platform collects the payment, the city rate is 5%.

Does the 2026 reform change the Mexico City lodging tax?

The December 2025 decree that reformed the city's Código Fiscal for 2026 changed the audit threshold for lodging-tax payers but did not amend articles 162 to 164, which set the tax. We could not load a consolidated 2026 text, so check the rate on your invoice.

How do you get from Mexico City airport to your hotel?

By Metrobús Line 4, which runs an airport service to Terminals 1 and 2 for 30 pesos, paid by card or wallet and not in cash; by Metro Line 5 from Terminal Aérea, 5 pesos a trip, which the city does not recommend with heavy luggage; or by an authorised taxi bought at the booths inside the terminals, with fixed fares by zone.

Is the Metrobús to the airport cash-free?

Yes. The city's tourism site says the airport service takes no cash: pay with the Integrated Mobility Card, a contactless bank card or a digital wallet. The airport lists boarding at Terminal 1, door 7, and a February 2026 notice names door 4 at Terminal 2 as a temporary point during works, so follow the signs.

Does the altitude matter when you choose a hotel in Mexico City?

The city sits at 2,240 metres, and its visitor guidance says many people have no symptoms. If you feel the effects, it advises resting, drinking extra water and avoiding alcohol and strenuous activity, so a hotel with a restful first evening is worth more than a packed first day.
