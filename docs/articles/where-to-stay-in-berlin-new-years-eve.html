Berlin’s big New Year’s Eve party at the Brandenburg Gate is free, but it is not a walk-in event, and it redraws the map of the night. Last year the state-owned organiser gave away free tickets for around 20,000 guests that had to be reserved in advance, the streets around the Tiergarten were closed from 6am, and the U-Bahn stopped serving Brandenburger Tor from 4pm. For 2026 the picture is less clear: berlin.de’s event listing still said the date was not confirmed when we checked in November 2026. Hotel bills in Berlin carry two charges, a 7% VAT rate and a separate City Tax of 7.5% of the net room price.

This guide covers what the city and the transport operators have published about 31 December, five areas matched to the party, the fireworks rules and the trip home, 12 hotels, and the terms that catch people out. For the rest of your trip, where to stay in Berlin covers the city’s neighbourhoods in more depth, and three hotels there also appear here. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in November 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

31 December 2026: what is confirmed and what is last year’s

The night falls on a Thursday, with 1 January a Friday. We found no official 2026 programme, ticket page or closure notice for the Brandenburg Gate. berlin.de’s listing for the Silvesterparty am Brandenburger Tor 2026 says the date is not yet confirmed and that it is not yet known whether a party is planned this year. On 16 September 2026 the Berliner Kurier reported that the Senate Chancellery had confirmed to it a party for the turn of 2026/27 in front of the gate, with DJs, fireworks and free entry. That is a news report, not an official page, so treat the party as likely rather than certain until the city publishes it. Last year the announcement came on 19 November 2025, with free tickets promised from December, so watch berlin.de from mid-November.

Until then, last year’s arrangements are the best guide to the shape of the night. They are labelled as last year’s throughout this guide, and they may change.

Police also declared a weapons and knife ban zone around the party last year, covering the Großer Tiergarten, parts of the Hansaviertel and the government quarter. The practical point is that the Brandenburg Gate area is a restricted zone for many hours before midnight, so a hotel inside or beside it is a decision about access, not just distance.

Fireworks on the street: what the city says

Private fireworks are allowed in Germany only on 31 December and 1 January. The city’s own guide says many districts allow them only from 6pm until 7am on New Year’s Day, naming Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg and Pankow, which includes Prenzlauer Berg, among them. For the night of 31 December 2025 the police banned private fireworks, with the weapons and knife ban alongside, in four areas from 6pm to 6am: Alexanderplatz in Mitte, the Steinmetzkiez in Schöneberg, part of Sonnenallee and its side streets in Neukölln, and the Admiralbrücke and adjoining streets in Kreuzberg. The ban covered the larger fireworks (categories F2 to F4) and any unapproved pyrotechnics; sparklers and similar category F1 items were allowed.

The 2026 order had not been published when we checked. Last year the police announcement appeared on 23 December, and the exact zone boundaries are on the police website. Outside the zones, expect fireworks in the streets of the busier districts from early evening. If a quieter night matters, ask a hotel which side its rooms face, and read the zone map before you book near Alexanderplatz or the Admiralbrücke.

Mitte: beside the party

Mitte is the historic centre, and it holds the Brandenburg Gate, Unter den Linden and Alexanderplatz. A hotel here is the shortest way to the party, and the way back is short too, as long as you are on the right side of the closures. Ask each hotel in writing how guests are let through the restricted streets on the night; none of the hotels below publishes that. One name to cross off: The Circus Hotel on Rosenthaler Platz closed on 1 November 2026, according to its own site, though its hostel and apartments stay open.

Tiergarten, Potsdamer Platz and the Hauptbahnhof

South and west of the Brandenburg Gate, the Tiergarten district runs from the park to Potsdamer Platz, and the Hauptbahnhof sits just north of the government quarter. Last year the Tiergarten streets around the Großer Stern were closed from 6am and the weapons ban zone included the Großer Tiergarten, so choose here for the transport links rather than for a walk to the gate. Expect the roads nearest the park to be restricted.

Where to stay near the Tiergarten and the Hauptbahnhof

  • Hotel Berlin, Berlin, comfortable. On Lützowplatz 17, with what the hotel calls spacious, quiet rooms and suites, a sauna and fitness area, the Lütze garden and more than 4,000 m² of meeting space. Choose it for a large hotel on the south side of the Tiergarten. Watch out: its site publishes no New Year’s Eve terms, and does not give its room count. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • MEININGER Hotel Berlin Central Station, budget. By the Hauptbahnhof, with 296 rooms and 820 beds over nine floors, a 24-hour reception and en-suite bathrooms in every room. Its page marks the 7.5% City Tax as included. Choose it for the cheapest bed beside the long-distance trains. Watch out: it is a cashless hotel, and the page gives no New Year’s Eve terms. Charlottenburg and Kurfürstendamm: the west side

Charlottenburg is Berlin’s west-side centre, built around the Kurfürstendamm, Savignyplatz and Zoologischer Garten station. It is a different night from the Brandenburg Gate: dinners, hotel bars and a ride across town if you want the party. None of last year’s four police fireworks ban zones was here.

Where to stay in Charlottenburg

  • Bristol Berlin, splurge. At Kurfürstendamm 27, a few minutes’ walk from KaDeWe and Zoo Berlin by the hotel’s own account. For New Year’s Eve it advertises Midnight in Berlin, a gala dinner on 31 December from 7pm for €169 in its Flaneur brasserie, and a champagne and wine experience at midnight for €69. Choose it for a grand Ku’damm evening. Watch out: these are dining prices, not a room package, and a notice on its site says the pool and sauna are expected to be closed temporarily from September to December for renovation, without naming a year, so ask whether it covers 31 December. Prenzlauer Berg: a quieter old-Berlin street

Prenzlauer Berg is a residential district of restored 19th-century blocks, cafes and restaurants, in Pankow, one of the districts the city’s guide says allows private fireworks only from 6pm until 7am on New Year’s Day. It is a base for people who want to eat well and then ride in to the party, or skip it. Last year’s four police fireworks ban zones were elsewhere.

Where to stay in Prenzlauer Berg

  • Hotel Oderberger, comfortable. In a public bathhouse built in 1902, now a hotel of 70 rooms, five tower suites and two apartments, with the historic pool, the Kaminbar, open daily from 5pm, and a rooftop terrace. Choose it for the building. Watch out: its site publishes no New Year’s Eve terms, and the discounts it advertises apply to longer stays. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • EastSeven Berlin Hostel, budget. Between Kastanienallee and Senefelderplatz, with dorm, private double, family and single rooms, a free muesli breakfast, a fully equipped kitchen and a garden. Choose it for the lowest bill in a good neighbourhood. Watch out: its page says check-in is until 10pm, so plan your arrival on 31 December around that. Check rates on Booking.com →

Friedrichshain and Kreuzberg: the lively streets

These two districts are Berlin’s busiest nightlife area, either side of the Oberbaum Bridge. They are also where official guidance matters most: Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg is on the city’s list of districts where private fireworks are limited to 6pm until 7am on New Year’s Day, and the Admiralbrücke and its adjoining streets in Kreuzberg were one of last year’s four police ban zones. The city’s guide said hundreds of thousands of people would be out and about. Choose here if you want to be among them, and ask the hotel which side of the building has the quietest rooms.

Getting around on the night

  • Berlin’s 2026 plan had not been published when we checked. The details below are the BVG’s and berlin.de’s for 31 December 2025, which we label as last year’s. Check bvg.de before you go.
  • Last year the U-Bahn ran all night. Lines ran every 5 minutes until about 5pm and every 10 minutes after that (the U4 was an exception). The BVG said the U5 ran only between Alexanderplatz and Hönow from 10pm to about 12:30am.
  • Last year the S-Bahn ran all night too. The S41 and S42 circle lines ran every 10 minutes, and eleven other lines every 20 minutes. On 1 January, trains followed the Sunday timetable from around 9am.
  • Last year, parts of the centre closed to transport. The BVG warned of partial closures of entrances or stations between the Hauptbahnhof and Alexanderplatz later in the evening, and said trams did not cross Alexanderplatz from about 4:30pm to 2am. Buses and trams mostly ran the Saturday timetable.
  • Choose your hotel for the ride home. Allow extra time in the hours after midnight, when the stations nearest the party are likely to be restricted again.

    From BER Airport

    BER is served by trains that need only a single ABC ticket, which costs €5.00 in 2026 with no airport surcharge. The FEX airport express runs from BER to the Hauptbahnhof in about 23 minutes, to Potsdamer Platz in about 19 and to Südkreuz in about 14, and the airport station is under Terminal 1. For more routes and fares, see where to stay in Berlin.

    Berlin’s hotel taxes: 7% VAT and the 7.5% City Tax

    Charge Rate in 2026
    City Tax (Übernachtungsteuer) 7.5% of the net price of the stay, excluding VAT and extras such as breakfast
    VAT on a hotel room 7%, the reduced rate
    VAT on services that are not part of the room, such as breakfast 19%, the standard rate

    Berlin’s service portal gives the City Tax as 7.5% of the net amount paid for the stay, excluding extras such as breakfast; the Senate Finance Department says the rate rose to 7.5% on 1 January 2025. As a worked example, our arithmetic: a room advertised at €220 a night including 7% VAT costs €205.61 before VAT, so the City Tax is €15.42 a night, and two nights add €30.84. Some hotels sell New Year’s Eve only as a package, as the Adlon does for two nights, so work out the total for the whole stay, not the nightly rate.

    How to book New Year’s Eve in Berlin

    • Check the party first. If the city confirms the Brandenburg Gate party, check whether tickets are needed, as they were last year, and let the hotel follow.
    • Read the prepayment terms. The Adlon’s New Year’s Eve packages need full prepayment by 2 December and are non-refundable after that date. Bristol Berlin’s gala is priced as a dinner, not a room.
    • Ask about minimum stays and the closures. The Adlon’s packages are two nights, but none of the other hotels publishes a New Year’s Eve minimum or says how guests get through restricted streets that we found, so ask.
    • Mind the arrival time. EastSeven’s page says check-in is until 10pm, so plan your arrival on 31 December around that.
    • Count the tax. Add the 7.5% City Tax to your total; one hotel, MEININGER, marks it as included on its page.
    • Compare totals. Our hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book, and hotel taxes around the world sets Berlin beside other cities.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is the Brandenburg Gate New Year's Eve party on in 2026?

    Not officially confirmed when we checked in November 2026. berlin.de's event listing said the date was not yet confirmed and that it was not known whether a party is planned. On 16 September 2026 the Berliner Kurier reported that the Senate Chancellery had confirmed a party for 2026/27 with DJs, fireworks and free entry, but that is a news report, not an official page.

    Do you need a ticket for the Brandenburg Gate party?

    Last year, yes: the city said tickets were free but had to be reserved in advance, for around 20,000 guests, with admission from 9:30pm. We found no official 2026 ticket arrangements, so check berlin.de from mid-November; the 2025 announcement came on 19 November.

    Where is the best place to stay for the Brandenburg Gate party?

    Mitte is the closest: Hotel Adlon Kempinski Berlin faces the gate and sells four New Year's Eve packages. Be aware that last year the Großer Stern and access roads closed from 6am and the U5 skipped Brandenburger Tor from 4pm, so ask any hotel nearby how guests get through the closures.

    Are fireworks banned in Berlin on New Year's Eve?

    Not citywide. Private fireworks are allowed only on 31 December and 1 January, and many districts, including Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg and Pankow, allow them only from 6pm to 7am on New Year's Day. Last year police banned them from 6pm to 6am at Alexanderplatz, the Steinmetzkiez, part of Sonnenallee and the Admiralbrücke, but allowed sparklers. The 2026 order had not been published when we checked.

    Do Berlin's trains run all night on New Year's Eve?

    Last year they did: the BVG said the U-Bahn ran all night, the S41 and S42 every 10 minutes and eleven other S-Bahn lines every 20 minutes, though trams did not cross Alexanderplatz from about 4:30pm to 2am. The 2026 plan had not been published when we checked.

    Does Berlin have a hotel tax?

    Yes. The City Tax is 7.5% of the net price of the stay, excluding VAT and extras such as breakfast. Room VAT is the reduced 7%, and services such as breakfast are taxed at 19%. On a €220 room including VAT, our arithmetic gives €15.42 a night in City Tax.
