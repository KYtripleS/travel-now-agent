Hotels around Waikiki, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Waikiki that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

A Honolulu hotel room costs more than its headline rate. Hawaii’s state hotel tax rose to 11% on 1 January 2026, the City and County of Honolulu adds 3%, and hotels may add the general excise tax, up to 4.712%, on top: up to 18.712% in all. On a $300 room that is $56.14 a night, before any daily resort charge the hotel adds. Where you stay decides the rest: how far you walk to the beach, and whether the airport bus is a ride away or a car journey.

This guide covers five areas, from central Waikiki to Ko Olina, 12 hotels, the airport links and the taxes. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

Honolulu’s hotel taxes in 2026

Three taxes apply to a hotel room on Oahu:

Charge Rate
Hawaii transient accommodations tax (state) 11% of the room rate, from 1 January 2026 to 31 December 2030
Oahu transient accommodations tax (City and County of Honolulu) 3% of the room rate
General excise tax, with the Oahu surcharge 4.5% a hotel owes; at most 4.712% may be added to your bill

The state rate was 10.25% until the end of 2025; Act 96 of 2025, known as the “green fee”, raised it. The Oahu tax is a county add-on, set by ordinance, and may be passed on to the guest. The general excise tax is a business tax: the 4.712% is the most a hotel may show on your bill, because the hotel’s own gross income includes the tax it passes on. A hotel that passes it on visibly may leave it out of the amount the accommodations tax is charged on.

Our arithmetic for a $300 room, if the hotel passes on everything: $33.00 state tax, $9.00 Oahu tax and $14.14 general excise tax, $56.14 in all, so $356.14 a night.

Resort charges. Many Waikiki hotels add a daily resort or amenity charge, and it differs by hotel; check the hotel’s own fee. Hilton Hawaiian Village’s rooms page lists $55 a night, and the Embassy Suites by Hilton Waikiki Beach Walk lists $45, both included in the “from” price they show and both before taxes. Halekulani’s site says its guests face no resort fee surprises, and Surfjack’s says no amenity fees when you book direct.

What else changed. Hurricane Lala passed Hawaii in mid-August 2026. The Star-Advertiser reported that damage to hotels was mostly debris and fallen palm fronds, though power cuts affected about half of all hotel properties. Some hotel sites still carried service notices in October, so read the hotel’s own page before you book.

Getting in from the airport, and around

  • By bus from Daniel K. Inouye International Airport (HNL), the W Line runs from the Lelepaua airport Skyline station to Waikiki, by way of Nimitz Highway, Kakaako and Ala Moana Center. Weekdays it runs every 10 minutes at peak, every 15 minutes off-peak and every 30 minutes after 7pm; on Saturdays, Sundays and holidays, every 15 minutes, every 30 after 7pm. The stop is east of the lei stands on Ala Onaona Street.
  • The fare on TheBus is $3.25 in cash, with no transfers. A HOLO card costs $2 to buy; a 2-hour pass on it, which covers transfers, is $3.00, and a 24-hour pass is $7.50. HOLO vending machines are at the Lelepaua station.
  • Luggage on TheBus is limited: one standard medium suitcase and one smaller carry-on, or a carry-on backpack, per passenger. Everything must fit under or in front of your seat, between your feet or on your lap, and must not block the aisle.
  • Skyline, Honolulu’s rail line, now stops at the airport, where Lelepaua station sits between the International Parking Garage and the Terminal 2 Parking Garage, but it does not reach Waikiki. A HOLO card is required to ride, and Segment 3, from Middle Street to Civic Center in Kakaako, is still under construction.
  • Route 42 also serves the airport, but only after 10:30pm, with a single eastbound trip to Waikiki.
  • By taxi or app, taxis queue on the median in front of baggage claim, and app rides use their own pick-up zones. OUTRIGGER Waikiki Beachcomber Hotel puts the drive to Waikiki at about 25 minutes, and warns an app ride can take 25 minutes to an hour.

Central Waikiki: the first-trip base

Waikiki is a narrow strip of hotels and towers beside the beach, and its centre is Kalakaua Avenue, with the Royal Hawaiian Center and the pink Royal Hawaiian resort on the sand. The W Line runs along Kalakaua Avenue, so the airport bus drops you in the middle of it.

Kapiolani Park and Diamond Head: the quiet end

East of the crowds, Waikiki thins out toward Kapiolani Park and Diamond Head, the volcanic crater at the end of the beach. Diamond Head State Monument, the crater itself, requires non-residents to reserve entry and parking; entry is $5 and the park is open from 6am to 6pm.

Where to stay near Diamond Head

  • Lotus Honolulu, comfortable. A boutique hotel at 2885 Kalakaua Avenue, at the quiet edge of Waikiki, with rooms of about 380 sq ft, a restaurant, Arden’s, and a penthouse suite of 1,701 sq ft. Choose it for the calm end of the beach. Watch out: the hotel’s page mentions an amenity fee, waived on one direct-booking offer; ask what it is. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Renew, good value. At 129 Paoakalani Avenue, locally owned, with a lobby cooler of locally crafted drinks and As You Like Waikīkī serving matcha and musubi. Choose it for the east end at a fair price. Watch out: standard rooms are 240 to 321 sq ft, and the hotel also mentions an amenity fee, waived on one direct-booking offer. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Waikiki Beachside Hostel, budget. At 2556 Lemon Road, one block from the beach, with co-ed and female-only dorms, semi-private and private rooms, a community kitchen and free pancakes from 8 to 10am. Choose it for a dorm bed or a private room at hostel prices. Watch out: it runs nightly events and has social lounges, so take a private room if you want quiet. Check rates on Booking.com →

Kalia Road and Beach Walk: the resort end

At the western end of Waikiki, around Kalia Road and Beach Walk, the hotels are bigger and the grounds larger. It suits travellers who want the resort on their doorstep.

Where to stay on Kalia Road

  • Halekulani, splurge. At 2199 Kalia Road, with the dining of House Without A Key, La Mer and Orchids, and the newly reopened HalekulaniSpa. Choose it for an oceanfront stay with no resort fee surprises, by the hotel’s own account. Watch out: price. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hilton Hawaiian Village Waikiki Beach Resort, comfortable. At 2005 Kalia Road, a 22-acre resort with five towers, pools and waterslides, and the Waikiki Starlight Luau. Choose it for a family resort with everything on site. Watch out: its rooms page lists a $55 resort charge a night. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Embassy Suites by Hilton Waikiki Beach Walk, comfortable. An all-suite hotel at 201 Beachwalk Street, one block from the beach, with free made-to-order breakfast and an evening reception on the Grand Lanai. Choose it for a suite and breakfast included. Watch out: its page lists a $45 resort charge a night. Check rates on Booking.com →

Ala Moana and the harbour: a city base

Ala Moana centres on Ala Moana Center, which the Ala Moana Hotel’s site calls the world’s largest open-air shopping mall, with the Hawaii Convention Center nearby. The W Line passes through it on the way to Waikiki.

Where to stay in Ala Moana

  • Ala Moana Hotel by Mantra, good value. At 410 Atkinson Drive, a landmark with 1,176 refurbished rooms in two towers, beside Ala Moana Center and a five-minute walk from the Hawaii Convention Center. Choose it for a city base and shopping next door. Watch out: by the hotel’s count it is 1.5 miles from Waikiki Beach. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Prince Waikiki, comfortable. At 100 Holomoana Street, where the hotel says all its rooms and suites have ocean views or are oceanfront, with three pools, an adults-only infinity pool, a saltwater retreat and a hot tub, and a spa. Choose it for the view and the pools. Watch out: it sits at the harbour end of Waikiki, away from Kalakaua Avenue’s shops; check the walk on a map. Check rates on Booking.com →

Ko Olina: the far option

Ko Olina is a resort area on Oahu’s west coast, not a neighbourhood, and Waikiki is a car or transfer away. The Four Seasons puts it 35 minutes from downtown Honolulu.

Where to stay in Ko Olina

  • Four Seasons Resort Oahu at Ko Olina, splurge. At 92-1001 Olani Street, Kapolei, a beachfront resort with a tower, four pools and the Naupaka Spa & Wellness Centre. Choose it for a resort stay with the beach at the door. Watch out: it is 20 miles from the airport, 30 minutes by car by the hotel’s count, and overnight valet parking is $55 a night. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Honolulu

  • Count the tax. Add up to 18.712% to every room rate: a $300 room costs up to $356.14.
  • Add the resort charge. A $55 charge a night over a five-night stay is $275 before tax, so compare the total price, not the headline rate.
  • Check the hotel’s current notices. After Hurricane Lala some hotels posted service notices; read the hotel’s own page before you book.
  • Check the room size. Hotel Renew’s standard rooms are 240 to 321 sq ft; Lotus Honolulu’s are about 380 sq ft. If you need space, an apartment-style suite such as Ohia’s may suit better.
  • Compare totals. Hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book, and hotel taxes around the world shows how Honolulu’s bill compares.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the best area to stay in Honolulu for a first trip?

    Central Waikiki, around Kalakaua Avenue: the Royal Hawaiian Center and the beach are on foot, and the W Line airport bus runs along Kalakaua Avenue. Choose the Kapiolani Park and Diamond Head end for something quieter, Kalia Road and Beach Walk for the big resorts, Ala Moana for the mall and a city base, and Ko Olina for a resort stay away from the city.

    How much is the hotel tax in Honolulu?

    Up to 18.712% of the room rate: Hawaii's 11% state transient accommodations tax, in force from 1 January 2026, the City and County of Honolulu's 3% Oahu tax, and the general excise tax, at most 4.712% if the hotel passes it on. On a $300 room that is $56.14 a night, our arithmetic, before any daily resort charge.

    How do you get from Honolulu airport to Waikiki by bus?

    Take the W Line from the Lelepaua airport Skyline station to Waikiki, by way of Nimitz Highway, Kakaako and Ala Moana Center. Weekdays it runs every 10 minutes at peak, every 15 off-peak and every 30 after 7pm. The fare is $3.25 in cash, or $3.00 for a 2-hour pass on a HOLO card, which costs $2 to buy.

    Can you take a suitcase on TheBus in Honolulu?

    Each passenger may bring one standard medium suitcase and one smaller carry-on, or a carry-on backpack. Everything must fit under or in front of your seat, between your feet or on your lap, and must not block the aisle, so a large suitcase will not do.

    Does the Skyline train go to Waikiki?

    No. Skyline now stops at the airport, at Lelepaua station, but it does not reach Waikiki: Segment 3, from Middle Street to Civic Center in Kakaako, is still under construction. A HOLO card is required to ride, so from the airport to Waikiki take the W Line bus.

    Do Honolulu hotels charge resort fees?

    Many Waikiki hotels add a daily resort or amenity charge, and it differs by hotel, so check the hotel's own fee. Hilton Hawaiian Village lists $55 a night and the Embassy Suites by Hilton Waikiki Beach Walk $45, both before taxes; Halekulani says its guests face no resort fee surprises, and Surfjack says no amenity fees when you book direct.
