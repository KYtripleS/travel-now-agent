Munich’s hotel bill lacks a line that many European cities add, a city tax: we found none in force, because Bavarian law bans municipalities from charging one and the Bavarian Constitutional Court upheld the ban in November 2025. What is left is VAT on the room. Where you stay decides the rest, in walking time, noise and what the hotel includes.

This guide covers seven areas, from the Altstadt to Haidhausen, 12 hotels, the airport links and the tax. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in November 2026. No hotel pays to appear here. Munich’s hotel tax: 7% VAT, and no city tax

Charge Rate
VAT on the room 7%, the reduced rate for short-term lodging
VAT on breakfast and other extras 19%, the standard rate
City or overnight tax None in force

The German VAT law sets a reduced rate of 7% for letting rooms to guests for a short stay, and says that rate does not apply to services that do not directly serve the letting, even if the room price covers them. Our arithmetic: on a room that costs €200 including 7% VAT, the VAT is €13.08 (200 × 7 ÷ 107). A rate that includes breakfast is split across two rates, so look at the invoice, not the headline price.

The city council decided in March 2023 on a 5% overnight tax, but the Bavarian Municipal Taxes Act says a municipality may not levy an Übernachtungsteuer. On 14 November 2025 the Bavarian Constitutional Court rejected the challenge by Munich and two other cities. On 26 February 2026 the city said its mayor and treasurer would propose a complaint to the Federal Constitutional Court. That court’s list of new cases for 2026 includes a municipal complaint against the Bavarian ban, but the list does not name the complainant, and we found no ruling. We found no tax in force in Munich, so none is added to the room rates here.

Getting in from the airport, and around

  • S-Bahn (S1 and S8). Munich Airport says the trip to the main station takes about 40 minutes, and that the S1 and S8 run alternately, so there is a 10-minute interval. The S8 runs around the clock, with a train every 40 minutes from about 1 a.m. to 4 a.m.
  • Day ticket. The MVV’s Airport-City-Day-Ticket covers unlimited journeys between the airport and the city centre on one day, for one person or a group of up to five, from €17.50. We did not confirm a single-fare price, so check the MVV fare finder if you only need one trip.
  • Lufthansa Express Bus. It runs every 20 minutes from the Hauptbahnhof via Munich North (Schwabing) to the airport. The airport gives the journey as 45 minutes from the Hauptbahnhof and 25 minutes from Schwabing. An adult single is €12.50 online and €13.50 from the driver.
  • Which area suits which route. The S-Bahn and the bus both arrive at the Hauptbahnhof; the bus also stops in Schwabing, which is why that area has the shorter ride.

    The Altstadt: sights on foot

    The old town is the Munich of Marienplatz, the Viktualienmarkt and the pedestrian streets, and most first-time sights lie within walking distance. It is the most expensive part of the city to stay and the best for a short visit with no wish to use public transport.

Its restaurants include the Pfistermühle in a historic vaulted cellar, and a public underground car park lies directly beneath it. Choose it for an old-town base with restaurants in the building. Watch out: breakfast is not automatically included in the room price, and the hotel says short-term parking for loading is at Rindermarkt 2, a few steps away. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Maxvorstadt: museums and the universities

    North of the Hauptbahnhof and the old town, Maxvorstadt is the museum and university quarter, with the Pinakotheken and Königsplatz. It is calmer than the Altstadt in the evening, and the old town is a walk or one U-Bahn stop away.

    Where to stay in Maxvorstadt

    • The Charles Hotel, splurge. At Sophienstrasse 28, a five-star Rocco Forte hotel that describes itself as close to the historic Königsplatz. Its site lists the Florio restaurant, the Circle Bar and a spa. Choose it for a luxury base beside the museums. Watch out: price. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Hotel Antares, good value. At Amalienstraße 20, in what the hotel calls the museum, Pinakotheken and university quarter. It says it reopened in early March 2026 after its rooms were renovated. Choose it for the museums at a lower price. Watch out: the hotel divides its rooms into standard rooms on the first to third floors and comfort rooms on the fourth and fifth, and only the comfort rooms list a coffee and tea station. The site is in German. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Schwabing: the English Garden and quieter streets

    Schwabing runs north from the university along Leopoldstraße, with cafés, Art Nouveau houses and the English Garden on its eastern edge. It suits a second visit or a slower trip, and the Lufthansa Express Bus stops here on its way to the airport.

    Where to stay in Schwabing

    • Hotel Biederstein, comfortable. At Keferstraße 18, a quiet side street by the English Garden, with 34 comfortable rooms, a private balcony among the room features and free Wi-Fi. Choose it for the park and the Art Nouveau streets. Watch out: arrivals after 10 p.m. are not possible, and children are welcome from 12 years of age. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Glockenbachviertel and Isarvorstadt: cafés and boutiques

    South of the old town, between Gärtnerplatz and the Isar, this is the part of Munich with the bars, small shops and cafés, and the Viktualienmarkt a short stroll away. It is livelier at night than the Altstadt, so ask for a quiet room.

    Where to stay in the Glockenbachviertel

    • Hotel Olympic, good value. At Hans-Sachs-Straße 4, a family-run hotel on a street that the hotel says has been declared a conservation area, with the city centre and the Viktualienmarkt a short stroll away. Choose it for a historic street at a fair price. Watch out: reception is staffed from 7 a.m. to midnight, so tell the hotel if you will arrive late. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Hotel Deutsche Eiche, good value. At Reichenbachstraße 13, 200 metres from the Viktualienmarkt by the hotel’s own account, behind a listed neoclassical façade of 1864, with 36 rooms and a restaurant. Choose it for an old inn with modern rooms. Watch out: when we checked, the hotel’s page said the roof terrace was closed, and in August 2026 the Süddeutsche Zeitung reported that the family’s succession, and so the inn’s future, was uncertain; it was still taking bookings in November, but confirm before you pay.     Ludwigsvorstadt and the Hauptbahnhof: convenient, and rougher at the edges

    The streets around the main station are the practical choice: the airport S-Bahn, long-distance trains and the U-Bahn meet here, and Maxvorstadt and the old town are within reach. It is a transport district first, busy at all hours and less polished than the old town, and the budget pick in this guide is here.

    Where to stay near the Hauptbahnhof

    • 25hours Hotel The Royal Bavarian, comfortable. At Bahnhofplatz 1, in a building that was once a post office and royal telegraph station, with the NENI restaurant and the Boilerman Bar. Choose it for stepping off the train into a design hotel. Watch out: the hotel lists small rooms with a single bed, so two guests should book a larger category. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Motel One München-Hauptbahnhof, budget. At Schillerstraße 3-3a, with rooms from the Cosy One at 14 m² to The One at 16 to 18 m² and larger Special One rooms. Choose it for a budget base by the station. Watch out: the rooms are compact by design; the Cosy One is the smallest. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Lehel and Haidhausen: the Isar and a grand street

    Lehel is the small, quiet quarter between Maximilianstraße and the Isar, close to the English Garden and the Bavarian State Opera. Across the river, Haidhausen and its neighbour Bogenhausen are residential, with a calmer rhythm and a ride into the centre.

    Where to stay in Lehel and Haidhausen

    • Hotel Opéra, comfortable. At St.-Anna-Str. 10, an owner-run boutique hotel in a Mediterranean-style Renaissance palais, a few steps from the English Garden, the Isar and the Bavarian State Opera, with the Lehel U-Bahn station nearby. Choose it for a quiet, historic house near Maximilianstraße. Watch out: the hotel’s underground garage costs €25 a night. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Hotel Vier Jahreszeiten Kempinski Munich, splurge. A five-star hotel on Maximilianstraße that King Maximilian II built in 1858, by the hotel’s account. Choose it for the grand old address. Watch out: price. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Hotel München Palace, splurge. At Trogerstraße 21, a five-star hotel in the Bogenhausen quarter, as the hotel puts it, with the La Grenouille restaurant, a wellness area with a sauna and steam room, and a roof terrace. Choose it for a residential base with spa facilities. Watch out: the hotel lists the Central Station as 15 minutes away, so it is a base for the east bank rather than the old town. Check rates on Booking.com →

    How to book Munich

    • Count the tax. We found no city tax in force, and VAT is 7% on the room: always compare the total and read the invoice, because breakfast is taxed at 19%.
    • Check arrival rules. Hotel Biederstein does not take arrivals after 10 p.m., and Hotel Olympic’s reception closes at midnight. Late trains need a word with the hotel first.
    • Check room size and breakfast. Motel One’s Cosy One is 14 m², 25hours lists small single-bed rooms, and Platzl Hotel and Louis Hotel both sell breakfast separately.
    • Check the dates. Festival weeks change the market: for Oktoberfest, use the Oktoberfest guide.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the best area to stay in Munich for a first trip?

    The Altstadt: Marienplatz, the Viktualienmarkt and the old town's streets are on foot. Choose Maxvorstadt for the museums and universities, Schwabing for the English Garden, the Glockenbachviertel for cafés and bars, and the Hauptbahnhof area for trains and the airport.

    Does Munich have a city tax on hotel rooms?

    We found no city tax in force. The Bavarian Municipal Taxes Act says a municipality may not levy an Übernachtungsteuer, and on 14 November 2025 the Bavarian Constitutional Court rejected the challenge by Munich and two other cities. The room carries 7% VAT, and breakfast and extras 19%.

    How do you get from Munich Airport to the city centre?

    Take the S1 or S8 S-Bahn: Munich Airport says the trip to the main station takes about 40 minutes, with a 10-minute interval. The Lufthansa Express Bus runs every 20 minutes to the Hauptbahnhof (45 minutes) and Schwabing (25 minutes); an adult single is €12.50 online.

    Is there a day ticket from the airport into Munich?

    Yes. The MVV's Airport-City-Day-Ticket covers unlimited journeys between the airport and the city centre on one day, for one person or a group of up to five, from €17.50. We did not confirm a single-fare price.

    Is breakfast included at Munich hotels?

    It varies. Platzl Hotel and Louis Hotel do not include breakfast automatically; both offer it as an option. Check each rate, because breakfast is taxed at 19% against 7% for the room, so the invoice splits it.

    Where should you stay in Munich for Oktoberfest?

    Oktoberfest has its own rules and its own set of hotels, so use our separate guide, where to stay in Munich for Oktoberfest. This guide is for the rest of the year.
