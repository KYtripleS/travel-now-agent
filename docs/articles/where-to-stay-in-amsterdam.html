Amsterdam is a city of canals, gabled houses, and endless bicycles. For travelers, this means there is no single "center." Where you sleep determines whether you wake up to houseboats, bustling markets, or quiet residential streets — and how many tram or bike rides you take to reach the next wonder. Choosing the right neighbourhood is the single most important logistical decision of your trip.

First, choose the area

Amsterdam's central districts are compact but distinct. To make your sightseeing stress-free, prioritize hotels near these key zones:

  1. Jordaan: West of the canals, known for narrow streets, houseboats, and independent boutiques.
  2. De Pijp: South of the centre, famous for the Albert Cuyp Market and a lively, multicultural atmosphere.
  3. Centrum: The inner canal belt, home to the major museums, nightlife, and historic core.
  4. Oost: East of the centre, featuring Amsterdamse Bos, newer residential areas, and more space.

Being within a ten-minute walk of a major tram stop (lines 2, 12, or GVB ferry) will save you hours over a week-long trip.

Jordaan: charming and picturesque

Jordaan is Amsterdam's most beloved neighborhood for its atmosphere. You are surrounded by 17th-century canal houses, art galleries, and cafes that have served locals for generations. It is connected to the centre via several tram lines and is extremely bike-friendly.

De Pijp: lively and local

De Pijp is where you find the Albert Cuyp Market, the Sarphatipark, and some of Amsterdam's best cafes. It's the best area for those who want to eat like a local and avoid the tourist crowds of the centre.
