Berlin is a city of layers, where every street tells a story of empire, war, division, and reunification. For travelers, this means there is no single "center." Where you sleep determines whether you wake up to Prussian palaces, street art-covered walls, or quiet residential streets — and how many U-Bahn or S-Bahn rides you take to reach the next wonder. Choosing the right neighbourhood is the single most important logistical decision of your trip.

First, choose the area

Berlin's central districts are compact but distinct. To make your sightseeing stress-free, prioritize hotels near these key zones:

  1. Mitte: The historic centre, home to Brandenburg Gate, Museum Island, and major attractions.
  2. Kreuzberg: South-east of the centre, known for alternative culture, Turkish markets, and a lively, multicultural atmosphere.
  3. Friedrichshain: East of the centre, famous for the East Side Gallery, young crowds, and a riverfront vibe.
  4. Prenzlauer Berg: North-east of the centre, characterised by tree-lined streets, cafes, and a village feel.
  5. Charlottenburg: West of the centre, upscale, residential, and home to Kurfürstendamm and the zoo.

Being within a ten-minute walk of a U-Bahn station (U2, U5, U8) will save you hours over a week-long trip.

Mitte: the historic heart

Mitte is where you find Brandenburg Gate, the Reichstag, Museum Island, and the TV Tower at Alexanderplatz. It is one of Berlin's busiest neighbourhoods, filled with hotels, souvenir shops, and cafes that have served visitors for decades.

Kreuzberg: alternative and lively

Kreuzberg is where you find Görlitzer Park, the Turkish Market on Maybachufer, and some of Berlin's best street art. It's the best area for those who want to eat like a local and avoid the tourist crowds of Mitte.
