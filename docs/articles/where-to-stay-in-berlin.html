Hotels around Mitte, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Mitte that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

Berlin adds its own tax to every hotel bill: 7.5% of the price of the room, before VAT and without breakfast. It is charged on the room, not per person, and since April 2024 business trips pay it too. On a €160 room that is about €11 a night. The bigger choice is the district. Berlin has no single old centre; it is a ring of neighbourhoods, each with its own character, and where you sleep decides how much of your trip is spent on the U-Bahn.

This guide covers six areas, from Mitte to Schöneberg, 12 hotels, the airport links and the tax. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

Berlin’s City Tax in 2026

The Übernachtungsteuer, which the city calls the City Tax, applies to every paid overnight stay in a hotel or other short-term accommodation in Berlin:

What Rule
Rate 7.5%
Charged on The price of the room, without VAT and without extras such as breakfast
Business stays Taxed since 1 April 2024
Long stays Taxed since 1 January 2025, when the old exemption for stays of more than 21 days ended

The tax follows the room price, so two people sharing a room pay the same as one. Hotel rooms in Germany also carry VAT at the reduced rate of 7%; breakfast and other services that are not part of the room are taxed at the standard 19%.

A worked example (our arithmetic). A room advertised at €160 a night including VAT costs €149.53 before VAT. The City Tax is 7.5% of that, €11.21 a night, so three nights add €33.64. Check whether a quoted price includes the City Tax, and compare totals.

Getting in from the airport, and around

BER, Berlin Brandenburg Airport, lies outside the city boundary, in fare zone C. Its station is under Terminal 1, on level U2; from Terminal 2, walk to Terminal 1.

  • The ticket. From the city centre to BER you need a single ABC ticket, €5.00 in 2026. The regular fare applies on every train and bus, with no airport surcharge. A single ticket is valid for 120 minutes in one direction, with changes.
  • The FEX Airport Express runs from BER to Südkreuz in about 14 minutes, Potsdamer Platz in about 19 and the Hauptbahnhof, the central station, in about 23. It is the fastest way into the centre. Until December 2026, the FEX and the hourly RE20 together run four trains an hour on that route.
  • The RB24 and RB32 regional trains each run hourly from BER to Ostkreuz, the station for Friedrichshain.
  • The S-Bahn S9 runs every 20 minutes from BER through Treptower Park, Warschauer Straße, Ostbahnhof, Alexanderplatz, Friedrichstraße, the Hauptbahnhof and Zoologischer Garten: slower than the FEX, but one train to many hotel areas. The S85 also runs every 20 minutes, to Ostkreuz.
  • Works in 2026. Until 12 December 2026, the Stadtbahn, the east-west line between Charlottenburg and Ostbahnhof, is closed to regional and long-distance trains for maintenance. Check the VBB journey planner before you travel.
  • In town, a single AB ticket covers the city and costs €4.00; a 24-hour ticket costs €11.20 for zones AB and €12.90 for ABC, so an ABC day ticket covers the airport trip as well.
  • The Deutschlandticket costs €63 a month as a subscription. It covers all local and regional transport in Germany, but not IC, EC or ICE trains; as a subscription, it suits a longer stay with day trips around the region.

Mitte: the first-trip base

Mitte is the historic centre: the Brandenburg Gate and Pariser Platz at one end of Unter den Linden, Museum Island and the cathedral in the Spree at the other, then Alexanderplatz and the TV Tower. North of the river, around Hackescher Markt and Rosenthaler Platz, the streets turn to courtyards, galleries and small shops. Most first-time visitors can walk to half of what they came to see.

Prenzlauer Berg: cafés and quiet streets

North of Mitte, Prenzlauer Berg is a district of restored 19th-century apartment houses, tree-lined streets and squares such as Kollwitzplatz, with the Mauerpark on its western edge, where the Wall once ran. Trams run along the main streets into Mitte. It suits a slower trip, or a second visit.

Where to stay in Prenzlauer Berg

  • Hotel Oderberger, comfortable. A listed former city bathhouse, opened in 1902 and reopened as a hotel in February 2016, with 70 rooms, two apartments and five tower suites. Its Neo-Renaissance pool hall is open to hotel guests without booking a time slot, and the fitness room is free and open 24 hours. Choose it for a swim in a historic bathhouse. Watch out: the pool and sauna close on days when events are held in the hall, and the pool will close for maintenance from 12 July to 1 August 2027. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Myer’s Hotel Berlin, comfortable. A town house built in 1877 in the neoclassical style, on a quiet 19th-century street near Kollwitzplatz, with a garden, Finnish, steam and infrared saunas in the cellar vaults, and a tram stop at the next corner. Choose it for a quiet, individual hotel. Watch out: rooms started at €194 on the hotel’s own site when we checked, and no two are alike, so read the room description before you book.

Kreuzberg: the canal and the late evenings

Kreuzberg, south of Mitte, is the district of the Landwehr Canal and of Oranienstraße’s bars, restaurants and shops. Neukölln, across the canal to the south-east, carries the same mood further. It is lively until late, and that is the point of staying here.

Where to stay in Kreuzberg and Neukölln

  • Orania.Berlin, splurge. A privately run, dog-friendly hotel in a heritage-listed building on Oranienplatz, with 41 rooms and suites, a restaurant with live concerts five days a week, and a bar. Choose it for design and music in Kreuzberg. Watch out: the smallest rooms are 17 m², with a 150 cm bed and a courtyard view. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hüttenpalast, good value. A small family hotel in a former factory courtyard on Hobrechtstraße in Neukölln, where guests sleep in vintage caravans and wooden cabins set inside two halls, or in six loft-style hotel rooms; there is a garden for the evening. Choose it for a night in a caravan under a factory roof. Watch out: the caravans and cabins share communal bathrooms, separate for men and women; only the six hotel rooms have their own. Check rates on Booking.com →

Friedrichshain: the Wall and the nightlife

Friedrichshain, in the former East across the Spree from Kreuzberg, holds the East Side Gallery, the longest remaining stretch of the Wall, painted by artists, and the Oberbaum Bridge. Around Warschauer Straße and Ostkreuz are the clubs, bars and cheaper hotels. Warschauer Straße and Ostbahnhof are on the S9 to the airport.

Where to stay in Friedrichshain

  • Michelberger Hotel, good value. On Warschauer Straße, with rooms from the Cosy, with a 160 cm bed, to lofts and band rooms, and a barrel sauna in the cold months and an in-house cinema for guests. Choose it for character on a budget near the Wall. Watch out: the Cosy room is 12 m² with no desk; the hotel suggests other categories if you need space to work. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • MEININGER Hotel Berlin East Side Gallery, budget. Across from the East Side Gallery, with Ostbahnhof next door, private rooms and dormitories, including female dorms, a guest kitchen and a bar. Choose it for the lowest price by the river. Watch out: the hotel itself points out that Berghain, the club, is around the corner; this is a nightlife district. Check rates on Booking.com →

Charlottenburg: classic West Berlin

Charlottenburg was the centre of West Berlin while the city was divided: the Kurfürstendamm, or Ku’damm, with its shops, the ruined tower of the Kaiser Wilhelm Memorial Church, the zoo and the edge of the Tiergarten. Zoologischer Garten station is on the S9 from the airport. It is calmer at night than the eastern districts and well placed for Charlottenburg Palace.

Where to stay in Charlottenburg

  • Hotel Zoo Berlin, splurge. On the Kurfürstendamm, with 127 rooms from the Petit Room to the 40 m² Grand Deluxe, an inner courtyard of ivy-covered giraffes and elephants, the Grace restaurant and bar, and a rooftop bar. Choose it for the Ku’damm in style. Watch out: the Petit Rooms are 16 to 22 m². Check rates on Booking.com →
  • 25hours Hotel Bikini Berlin, comfortable. In the Bikini Berlin building, designed by Studio Aisslinger, with a hammock lounge, a jungle sauna, and the NENI restaurant and Monkey Bar on the 10th floor looking over the Tiergarten. Choose it for views of the zoo and the park. Watch out: the Urban rooms look over the city and Breitscheidplatz; book a Jungle room for the Tiergarten side. Check rates on Booking.com →

Schöneberg: Nollendorfplatz and the West, for less

South of Charlottenburg, Schöneberg is residential and relaxed, with cafés, markets and the long-established gay neighbourhood around Nollendorfplatz, where four U-Bahn lines meet. The KaDeWe department store and the Ku’damm are a walk away, and Südkreuz, on the FEX to the airport, is a short ride.

Where to stay in Schöneberg

  • Hotel Sachsenhof, good value. A historic house with high ceilings a few metres from Nollendorfplatz, where the poet Else Lasker-Schüler and the painter Oskar Kokoschka once lived, and where Erich Kästner sent Emil and the Detectives after a thief. Rooms range from doubles to four-bed rooms, each with a shower and WC, and the breakfast buffet runs until noon; by the hotel’s count, the KaDeWe and the Ku’damm are about 10 minutes on foot. Choose it for a quiet, central base at a lower price. Watch out: the hotel describes its rooms as functionally furnished. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Berlin

  • Count the tax. Add 7.5% of the room price before VAT: about €11 a night on a €160 room. Breakfast is not part of the taxed amount, so a rate with breakfast does not raise the City Tax.
  • Watch the calendar. Check your dates against the big events before you book: the Berlinale film festival runs from 10 to 21 February 2027, and the ITB travel trade fair from 16 to 18 March 2027.
  • Check the room size. Small rooms are common at every price: 12 m² at Michelberger’s Cosy, about 14 m² at The Circus Hotel’s Small Room, 16 m² at Motel One, and 16 to 22 m² for the Petit Rooms at Hotel Zoo. If you need space, book the category above the cheapest.
  • Book the view by name. At 25hours Bikini, choose a Jungle room for the Tiergarten; at Orania, some of the larger rooms face Oranienplatz.
  • Compare totals. Hotel booking sites compared and booking direct or through a booking site go through where to book.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Berlin for a first trip?

Mitte: the Brandenburg Gate, Unter den Linden, Museum Island and Hackescher Markt are on foot, and the FEX from the airport reaches the Hauptbahnhof in about 23 minutes. Choose Prenzlauer Berg for cafés and quiet streets, Kreuzberg or Friedrichshain for bars and nightlife, and Charlottenburg for the Kurfürstendamm and West Berlin.

How much is the City Tax in Berlin?

7.5% of the price of the room, without VAT and without extras such as breakfast. It is charged on the room, not per person, and business stays have been taxed since 1 April 2024. A room advertised at €160 a night including VAT adds €11.21 a night.

Do business travellers pay Berlin's City Tax?

Yes. Since 1 April 2024, business stays are no longer exempt, and since 1 January 2025 the old exemption for stays of more than 21 days no longer applies.

How do you get from BER Airport to central Berlin?

Take the FEX Airport Express from the station under Terminal 1: about 14 minutes to Südkreuz, 19 to Potsdamer Platz and 23 to the Hauptbahnhof. The S9 S-Bahn, every 20 minutes, is slower but runs through Alexanderplatz, Friedrichstraße and Zoologischer Garten. You need a single ABC ticket, €5.00 in 2026.

How much is a public transport ticket in Berlin?

A single AB ticket, valid for 120 minutes in one direction, costs €4.00 in 2026, and a 24-hour ticket €11.20 for zones AB or €12.90 for ABC, which includes the airport.

Is the Deutschlandticket worth it for visitors to Berlin?

It suits a longer stay with day trips around the region. It costs €63 a month as a subscription and covers all local and regional transport in Germany, but not IC, EC or ICE trains; for a short city break, single tickets and 24-hour tickets are simpler.
