Hotels around Tulum Pueblo, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Tulum Pueblo that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

A Tulum hotel bill carries two percentages before you reach the lobby: Quintana Roo’s 5% lodging tax and Mexico’s 16% IVA, 21% in all on the room rate. Foreign visitors also pay the state’s Visitax, which is not on the hotel bill, and the Tulum archaeological site charges its own entry fee. Where you stay decides the rest: the town, a jungle neighbourhood, the beach road or a bay up the coast.

This guide covers five areas, from Tulum Pueblo to Akumal, 12 hotels, the airport links and the charges. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

What a Tulum stay costs on top of the room

Four charges matter, and only two of them are on the hotel bill:

Charge Rate Where it appears
Quintana Roo lodging tax (impuesto al hospedaje) 5% of the room invoice at hotels; 6% for apartments, houses and private villas On the hotel bill, shown separately
IVA, Mexico’s value added tax 16% of the room rate On the hotel bill
Visitax, the state visitor fee About 285 pesos per foreign visitor, children included Not on the bill: you pay it online
Tulum archaeological site and national park 265 pesos for a foreign visitor doing the full visit At the site, if you go

The state law makes the guest the taxpayer and requires the hotel to work out, collect and pay over the lodging tax expressly and separately. The tax is never part of the value of the room, so the two percentages are charged on the room rate itself, not on each other. On an all-inclusive package, the law sets the taxable part as the lodging element of the bill, and at no less than 60% of the invoice total when the hotel does not itemise.

The Visitax is a state fee on foreign visitors. Its official portal says it is mandatory for every foreign tourist, that there is no adult or child rate, and that you pay at visitax.gob.mx or through companies authorised by the state tax authority. The state government said in December 2025 that the fee would not rise in 2026; press reports put it at 283 to 285 pesos, and we use 285 below. The portal shows the exact amount when you pay. Pay only there.

The entry fee changed in July 2026. The Presidency announced on 17 July that a foreign visitor doing the whole visit pays 265 pesos, and INAH’s page for the Tulum site lists the same 265 pesos for foreigners as a unified cost, which it says includes the charges of CONANP and/or Grupo Mundo Maya. The Presidency’s notice also made entry to the Parque del Jaguar free for Mexican visitors. The site is open from 8:00 to 17:00, with the last entry at 15:30, and the fee and exemptions can change, so check INAH’s page before you go.

Worked example (our arithmetic, not an official quote). Two foreign guests, three nights, a room at 3,000 pesos a night, and one visit to the site:

  • Room: 3 × 3,000 = 9,000 pesos
  • IVA at 16%: 1,440 pesos
  • Lodging tax at 5%: 450 pesos
  • Hotel bill: 10,890 pesos, which is 21% above the room rate
  • Visitax: 2 × 285 = 570 pesos, paid separately
  • Tulum site and park: 2 × 265 = 530 pesos, paid at the site
  • Total: about 11,990 pesos

This assumes both percentages apply to the plain room rate and the hotel adds no other fee, so compare the final total, not the headline rate.

Getting in from the airports, and around

  • At Tulum International Airport (TQO), the airport’s own site lists the flight board, and the Tren Maya’s official station list includes a Tulum Aeropuerto stop. ADO’s fare for the airport route was not readable when we checked, so look on ado.com.mx or in its app.
  • From Cancún International Airport (CUN), ADO’s international site lists a bus to Tulum from Terminals 2, 3 and 4, usually around 475 pesos and 2 hours 45 minutes to 3 hours 5 minutes, with up to 30 departures a day. From ADO’s downtown Cancún terminal the fare starts around 268 pesos and the direct trip takes about 2 hours 40 minutes, with departures about every 30 minutes.
  • Arriving by ADO bus, most services stop at the Tulum terminal on Avenida Tulum in Tulum Pueblo; a less frequent stop is the Tulum Ruinas terminal near the archaeological site.
  • By train, the Tren Maya’s official site lists Cancún Aeropuerto, Tulum and Tulum Aeropuerto among its 34 stations, and sells tickets online. Fares and times appear when you search a date on its booking site.
  • To Akumal, Bahia Principe’s own page puts its Akumal resort 63 km from Tulum airport and 100 km from Cancún airport.
  • Sargassum. The floating seaweed can wash up on Caribbean beaches in some seasons; Mexico’s CONABIO runs SATsum, which publishes satellite alerts and a 10-day drift forecast for the coast. Look at it before booking a beach-first stay.

Tulum Pueblo: the town base

Tulum Pueblo is the town itself, built along Avenida Tulum, with the ADO terminal. It suits a first trip because the buses arrive here and the town’s hotels, restaurants and services are close; what you give up is the beach at the door. The archaeological site is open from 8:00 to 17:00 with the last entry at 15:30, so plan it for the morning.

Aldea Zamá: apartments and pools

Aldea Zamá is a neighbourhood of hotels and apartments. NAJ Tulum’s own site describes it as a place where the cenotes, the archaeological sites, the beach and downtown are within cycling distance, and recommends a bicycle. Many rooms here have kitchens, which helps on a longer stay.

Where to stay in Aldea Zamá

  • NAJ Tulum, good value. At Aldea Zama Manzana 3 Lote 3, with contemporary apartments, studios and rooms, a fully equipped kitchen in the rooms and swimming pools; the hotel says it is five minutes from Tulum beach. Choose it for a flat-style stay at a fair price. Watch out: the site does not mention breakfast, so plan to self-cater. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Aluna Resort Tulum, comfortable. On Avenida Itzamná in Aldea Zama, with two pool options, the Parallel 20 restaurant, the rooftop Bhanu Sky Kitchen and a pool bar. Choose it for a resort with pools and a rooftop in the neighbourhood. Watch out: it is not on the beach; the hotel’s site says the beach is minutes away. Check rates on Booking.com →

La Veleta: small hotels and gardens

La Veleta is a district of small hotels, many with only a handful of rooms: eight at Hotel Era Tulum and five suites at Casa Kobaab Tulum. The trade-off is that the beach is not at the door, and with so few rooms the best ones fill early.

Where to stay in La Veleta

  • The Strabon Tulum, good value. At Calle 16 Sur in La Veleta, with a King Room of 32 m², a Deluxe Room with two queen beds of 32 m² for up to four, and a 100 m² Two Bedroom Suite for up to six, and Cafe Strabon serving breakfast daily from 8 to 11. Choose it for space for a family or a group at a fair price. Watch out: the site mentions no pool, so check what the hotel offers before booking. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Era Tulum, comfortable. On Calle 6 Sur in La Veleta, with just eight rooms in four types, from Standard to Superior Family, a pool, a spa, a fitness centre and The Garden Bar & Lounge. Choose it for a small hotel with a spa. Watch out: with eight rooms it fills early, and the site lists no room sizes. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Casa Kobaab Tulum, comfortable. A jungle sanctuary on Calle 15 in La Veleta, with five suites, a pool and terrace in the jungle, and a Jade Premium suite with a private pool. Choose it for a private-feeling base among trees. Watch out: the suites are listed for two guests, so it does not suit groups. Check rates on Booking.com →

The Hotel Zone: the beach road

The Hotel Zone is the coast road, Carretera Tulum–Boca Paila, lined with beach hotels. It is the choice if the beach is the point of the trip. The ADO terminal is in Tulum Pueblo, so expect to take a ride whenever you leave.

Where to stay in the Hotel Zone

  • Azulik, splurge. A luxury eco-resort of seven villas, Aqua, Sea, Sky, Moon, Jungle, Mayan and Aztec, joined by suspended walkways through the forest, with eight restaurants and the Maya Spa. Choose it for architecture and a stay that is part of the experience. Watch out: the 25% direct-booking offer for stays from 1 October to 15 December 2026 is non-refundable, so read the terms. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Nômade Temple Tulum, splurge. A larger property of 38 rooms, 20 suites, 5 treehouses and 36 bungalows, with three restaurants, La Popular, Macondo and Kuu Jū, and a wellness programme of yoga, spa treatments and ice baths. Choose it for a beachfront setting with a lot going on. Watch out: with 99 units it is bigger than the word boutique suggests, and the site lists no room sizes. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Diamante K, budget. At km 2.5 of the Boca Paila road, inside the Tulum National Park, on about one hectare with more than 200 palm trees, with eight room categories including economic rooms, one with a shared bathroom, and the Casa Maria restaurant. Choose it for a budget stay on the beach road. Watch out: the hotel sits inside the national park, and its site does not say how park entry applies to guests, so ask before you arrive. Check rates on Booking.com →

Akumal: a bay up the coast

Akumal is a bay on the highway north of Tulum, a resort stop in its own right. It suits a beach week that does not need the town, and both hotels below are a transfer from the airports.

Where to stay in Akumal

  • Akumal Bay Beach & Spa Resort, comfortable. An all-inclusive resort of 310 oceanfront junior suites in four beachfront buildings, with direct access to the coral reef for snorkelling; the hotel’s site puts Tulum airport at about 40 miles, a 55-minute drive. Choose it for an all-inclusive stay on the reef. Watch out: it is all-inclusive, so compare the rate with what you would spend, and ask for an itemised invoice, because the lodging tax is charged on the lodging element of the bill. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Bahia Principe Explore Akumal, comfortable. A beachfront, 24-hour all-inclusive resort at km 250 of the Chetumal highway, with pools, à la carte dining, a kids’ club and access to a PGA-standard golf course; it lists Tulum airport at 63 km and Cancún at 100 km. Choose it for a family all-inclusive. Watch out: it is positioned for families, and only its Junior Suite Deluxe categories are marked adults only. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Tulum

  • Count the charges. Add 21% to every room rate, and 570 pesos for two foreign visitors’ Visitax: a 3,000-peso room for three nights comes to about 10,890 pesos at the hotel and about 11,990 pesos with the Visitax and one visit to the site for two.
  • Check what the rate includes. The two Akumal resorts here sell all-inclusive rates; compare the price per night with what you would spend on meals, and ask the hotel for the itemised invoice.
  • Check the room count. Several hotels here are tiny: Hotel Era Tulum has eight rooms and Casa Kobaab Tulum five suites. Book early in a busy season.
  • Plan the transfer. ADO’s Cancún airport bus takes up to about 3 hours 5 minutes, so choose an area with that journey in mind.
  • Compare totals. Hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book, and how hotel taxes work around the world puts Quintana Roo’s 5% in context. Heading up the coast? See where to stay in Cancún.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Tulum for a first trip?

Tulum Pueblo, the town centre on Avenida Tulum, where ADO's Tulum terminal stands and the hotels are close to the town's restaurants and services. Choose Aldea Zamá for apartments with kitchens and pools, the Hotel Zone for the beach at the door, La Veleta for small hotels with gardens and Akumal for an all-inclusive bay.

How much tax is added to a Tulum hotel room?

Quintana Roo's lodging tax is 5% of the room invoice at hotels, and 6% for apartments, houses and private villas, on top of Mexico's 16% IVA: 21% in all on a hotel room. On a 3,000-peso room that is 630 pesos a night, by our arithmetic, assuming both apply to the plain room rate.

What is the Visitax in Tulum and how do you pay it?

A state fee paid by every foreign visitor to Quintana Roo. The official portal says it is mandatory for all foreign tourists, with no adult or child rate, and is paid at visitax.gob.mx or through companies authorised by the state tax authority. The state said in December 2025 it stays at 285 pesos in 2026; it is not on the hotel bill.

How much does it cost to enter the Tulum ruins in 2026?

From 20 July 2026 a foreign visitor doing the whole visit pays 265 pesos, according to the Presidency's announcement of 17 July and INAH's page for the site, which lists the same unified cost for foreigners. The site is open from 8:00 to 17:00, with the last entry at 15:30. Check INAH's page before you go, because fees and exemptions can change.

How do you get from Cancún airport to Tulum?

ADO's international site lists a bus to Tulum from Cancún airport Terminals 2, 3 and 4, usually around 475 pesos and 2 hours 45 minutes to 3 hours 5 minutes, with up to 30 departures a day. From ADO's downtown Cancún terminal the fare starts around 268 pesos and the direct trip takes about 2 hours 40 minutes. Most buses stop at the Tulum terminal on Avenida Tulum.

Does the Tren Maya go to Tulum?

Yes. The Tren Maya's official site lists Tulum and Tulum Aeropuerto among its 34 stations, along with Cancún Aeropuerto and Playa del Carmen, and sells tickets online. Its fares and times appear when you search a date on its booking site, so check them there.
