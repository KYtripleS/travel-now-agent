How to choose between Hotels.com, Booking.com, Trip.com, Agoda, and HotelsCombined — strengths, common mistakes, and how to actually save money.

Five hotel booking sites can show five different prices for the same room. This practical comparison of major hotel booking sites covers what each platform is genuinely good at, common mistakes that cost travelers money, and how to pick the right one for your trip without spending hours comparing tabs.

The 5 main hotel booking sites at a glance

  1. Booking.com — Largest inventory, most flexible cancellation, strong Europe coverage.
  2. Hotels.com — Solid global coverage; its rewards scheme depends on the country you book from.
  3. Trip.com — Strong in Asia-Pacific, mobile-first, often runs aggressive promotional pricing.
  4. Agoda — Asia specialist, strong inventory in Thailand, Japan, Indonesia, and Vietnam.
  5. HotelsCombined — Price comparison aggregator that searches multiple sites at once.

What each site is actually good for

Booking.com — when flexibility matters

Search hotels on Booking.com →

Booking.com has the widest hotel inventory of any major platform and offers the most liberal free-cancellation policy on most properties. If your dates might change, or you want maximum choice, this is the default starting point. Strongest in Europe, but covers most of the world.

Hotels.com — when you travel often

Search hotels on Hotels.com →

Its rewards depend on the country you book from. In the UK, Hotels.com Rewards returned in spring 2026: every 10 eligible nights (each costing at least £75) earn £100 of Hotels.com Cash. Canada kept the classic scheme of 10 nights for 1 reward night. US members are still on One Key, the scheme shared across Expedia Group, which pays back a much smaller share as OneKeyCash. Where a 10-night scheme applies, travelers who stay in hotels regularly can save real money over a year. Coverage is competitive globally, with strong US and UK presence.

Trip.com — when you're going to Asia

Trip.com (formerly Ctrip) is the largest travel platform in China and has strong coverage across Asia. Promotional pricing on the app is often more aggressive than on the desktop site. The customer service operates in multiple time zones, which helps for last-minute booking issues abroad.

Agoda — when you're going to Southeast Asia

Search hotels on Agoda →

Agoda is the strongest single platform for hotels across Thailand, Vietnam, Indonesia, and Japan. The inventory includes many smaller local hotels that other platforms miss. The interface is busy, but the prices are often the best for Asia-Pacific destinations.

HotelsCombined — when you want one comparison

HotelsCombined is not a booking site — it is a meta-search tool that compares prices across many sites (Booking, Hotels.com, Agoda, hotel direct sites, and more) in a single search. Useful as a final price check before booking, or when you're not sure which site has the best deal for your destination.

The best site for your kind of trip

“Which hotel site is best” usually hides a narrower question. These are the ones we hear most.

Easiest to use

Booking.com and Hotels.com have the calmest search screens, and both show free cancellation plainly in the results. Agoda packs more onto the page, which takes a few searches to get used to, and Trip.com is easiest in its app. If you only want to compare, not browse, HotelsCombined is the quickest single search.

Flexible cancellation

Booking.com, because you can filter to free-cancellation rates and the terms sit next to each room. The catch is that each property sets its own cancellation and prepayment rules, so read the room you are booking, not the hotel’s headline. A booking made direct with a chain is usually the simplest to change, with no middle party in the way.

Beach and resort holidays

Compare the total, not the nightly rate: resort fees and local taxes are often added only at checkout. At chain resorts, booking direct earns points, and Hilton charges no resort fee on reward stays paid with points. For Southeast Asian beaches, start on Agoda, whose small-hotel stock is deepest there. Our guides to Phuket, Bali, Okinawa, Cancún and Honolulu compare the beach areas first.

Budget travellers

Booking.com and Agoda both list guesthouses and hostels alongside hotels. Filter before you sort by price: a location you can live with and a review score you trust, then the total including taxes. Non-refundable rates are cheaper and fine when your dates cannot move; otherwise the small premium for free cancellation is the better bargain.

International travellers

Watch the currency. When a site or a card terminal offers to charge you in your home currency, decline and pay in the local one; the conversion it offers is usually worse than your card’s. A card with no foreign transaction fee saves the rest. For help in your time zone, Trip.com and Agoda are strongest in Asia, Booking.com in Europe.

Four-star and luxury hotels

At chain hotels this is where booking direct matters most, because upgrades, late checkout and breakfast for elite members usually apply only to direct bookings (see the Hilton example below). At independent luxury hotels, compare: a booking site is sometimes cheaper, and the hotel may match it if you ask. A four-star rating means different things in different countries, so judge the room by recent reviews and guest photos.

Long stays of a week or more

An apartment with a kitchen often works out cheaper per night than a hotel over a long stay, and Booking.com lists many. For a hotel, ask it directly about a weekly or monthly rate; some offer one that never appears on booking sites. The longer the booking, the more free cancellation is worth.

Last-minute bookings

Check the apps, where Trip.com and others put some of their lowest prices, and then the hotel’s own site, which on the day can match them. If you will arrive late, check the reception hours before you pay; some small hotels close the desk in the evening.

Festivals, holidays and religious celebrations

Book as early as you can on a free-cancellation rate, then keep checking: prices climb as rooms fill, and peak dates often carry minimum-stay rules. Our stay hub lists the trips whose dates fill hotels early.

Booking direct vs a booking site: the Hilton example

Hilton is the clearest case because its rules are written down. The Hilton Honors terms, which we checked on 21 October 2026, say that stays booked through third-party websites, whatever the rate, are not eligible stays: they earn no points and no credit toward status. The same terms say on-property elite benefits are not given on those bookings either. Members who book on Hilton.com or the Hilton app get the Hilton Honors Discount rate, and can choose their room and use a digital key.

So at a Hilton, if you are a member, or would join (it is free), book direct. A booking site wins only when its total is clearly lower and the points and perks do not matter to you. Marriott, Hyatt and IHG also tie most of their rewards to direct bookings; check each programme’s terms, and read our guide to booking direct vs booking sites.

How to judge a hotel booking site: seven checks

  1. Total price. Does the first price you see include taxes and fees, or only the room?
  2. Cancellation and prepayment. When is your card charged, and until when can you cancel for free?
  3. Who charges you, in which currency. The site now, or the hotel at check-in?
  4. Customer service. Can you reach a person, in your language, if the hotel cannot find your booking?
  5. Reviews. Are they recent, and written only by guests who stayed?
  6. Rewards. Do you earn anything, and is it worth more than a chain’s own programme?
  7. Map and filters. Can you see where the hotel is before you sort by price?

HotelsCombined and its alternatives

HotelsCombined is a metasearch site: it compares prices from booking sites and hotels’ own sites, then sends you to the one you choose to book. It belongs to Booking Holdings and reports to Kayak, another metasearch site in the same group. Alternatives that work the same way are Kayak, Google Hotels (the prices shown in Google Search and Maps), Trivago and Skyscanner. They differ less in price than in which sites they include, so if one shows few offers for your hotel, try another.

How to actually choose

For most trips, the practical approach is:

  1. Start on HotelsCombined or Booking.com to see the broad price range.
  2. If you're traveling in Asia, also check Agoda and Trip.com — prices can differ noticeably.
  3. If you have a hotel rewards account (Hilton, Marriott, Hyatt, IHG), check that hotel's direct site too.
  4. Pick the cheapest option that includes the cancellation flexibility you want.

Spending more than 20–30 minutes comparing rarely saves more than a few dollars beyond this routine. The exception is luxury or long stays, where price gaps can widen significantly.

Common booking mistakes

  • Booking non-refundable too early. If your trip is more than 4 weeks out, free cancellation usually pays for itself in peace of mind.
  • Not checking the hotel's direct site. Loyalty members often get upgrades, late checkout, or breakfast for matching rates.
  • Ignoring the total price. Resort fees, city taxes, and service charges can add 10–25% at checkout. Always look at the final total before comparing.
  • Trusting one star rating system. 4-star in Bangkok ≠ 4-star in Paris. Read recent reviews instead.
  • Booking from a hotel email link. Some are phishing. Always go to the official URL directly.
  • Skipping the map view. A "central" hotel can be 30 minutes from anything you actually want to see.

How to maximise loyalty across sites

If you travel more than a few times a year, picking one booking site as your main platform builds rewards faster. The two best programs for casual travelers:

  • Hotels.com — depends on your country: Hotels.com Rewards in the UK and Canada, One Key in the US.
  • Booking.com Genius — 10% off participating stays once you sign in, 10–15% after 5 bookings in two years and 10–20% after 15, with free breakfasts and room upgrades on some stays.

Hotel chains' own programs (Marriott Bonvoy, Hilton Honors, IHG One Rewards) are usually more valuable per night — but only if you stay at chain properties. For independent hotels, sticking with one booking site rewards program is the simpler path.

FAQ: hotel booking sites

Which is the best hotel booking site overall?

No single site is best for every traveler. Booking.com has the widest inventory and flexible cancellation. Hotels.com’s rewards depend on your country. Trip.com and Agoda excel in Asia. HotelsCombined compares prices across sites. The right pick depends on destination, trip length, and loyalty priorities.

Do hotel booking sites really offer the lowest price?

Not always. Hotel chains sometimes match or beat third-party prices on their own websites, especially when combined with loyalty programs. For independent hotels, booking sites usually have competitive prices, but comparing 2–3 sites plus direct is worth the few minutes.

Is free cancellation worth paying more for?

Free cancellation rates are usually only slightly higher and can save you significantly if plans change. For trips more than a few weeks out, free cancellation is usually worth it. For non-refundable, only commit if dates are 100% locked in.

Should I book directly with the hotel?

Direct booking can offer perks like upgrades, late checkout, complimentary breakfast, or loyalty points. Check the hotel's own website after finding a price on a booking site — match the rate or beat it, and you may get extra benefits.

What is the safest way to pay when booking online?

Use a credit card from a reputable issuer. Credit cards offer chargeback protection if something goes wrong. Avoid debit cards or bank transfers on unfamiliar platforms. Always check that the URL starts with https before entering payment details.

Do you earn Hilton Honors points when you book a Hilton through Booking.com or Expedia?

No. Hilton Honors terms say stays booked through third-party websites, whatever the rate, earn no points and no stay credit toward status, and on-property elite benefits are not given on them. Book on Hilton.com or the Hilton app to earn them.

Which hotel booking site is best for budget travellers?

Booking.com and Agoda, because both list guesthouses and hostels alongside hotels. Filter by location and review score first, then sort by the total price including taxes, and take a non-refundable rate only when your dates cannot change.

What are the best alternatives to Booking.com?

Hotels.com for its rewards in the UK and Canada, Agoda and Trip.com for Asia, and the hotel’s own site for chains where you have status. To compare them all at once, use a metasearch site such as HotelsCombined, Kayak or Google Hotels.

Gently Yonder tip: Save the booking confirmation as a PDF and screenshot it. If you arrive at the hotel and they don't have your booking on file, having the proof on your phone (and offline) is the fastest way to resolve it.

Bottom line

There is no single best hotel booking site. For most trips, start with HotelsCombined or Booking.com for broad coverage, check Agoda or Trip.com if you're going to Asia, and verify with the hotel's direct site if you have any loyalty status. The 20-minute version of this routine is enough to find a fair deal on almost any trip.
