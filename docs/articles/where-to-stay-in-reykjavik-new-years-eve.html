    New Year’s Eve in Reykjavik is outdoors and mostly unticketed: ten city bonfires early in the evening, then fireworks that the public may buy and set off from 28 December to 6 January. Iceland charges an accommodation tax of 800 ISK a room a night, plus 11% VAT that is already in the room price, and Reykjavik adds no city tax of its own. What changes on 31 December is the getting about: Strætó, the city bus operator, ran a Saturday timetable with its last trips around 15:00 on New Year’s Eve last year, and the airport is 50 km away.

    31 December: what the city and the church have published

    Bonfires. The City of Reykjavik had not published its 2026 list when we checked in November 2026, so this is the 2025 list, labelled as such. Ten bonfires were planned, all at 8:30pm except Skerjafjörður at 9pm.

    Bonfire (2025) Size Time
    Ægisíða Small 8:30pm
    Skerjafjörður (opposite Skildinganes 48-52) Small 9pm
    Suðurhlíðar (below Fossvogur Cemetery) Small 8:30pm
    Laugardalur (below Laugarásvegur 18) Small 8:30pm
    Geirsnef (north side) Large 8:30pm
    Stekkjarbakki Small 8:30pm
    Rauðavatn (north side) Small 8:30pm
    Gufunes (by Gufunesbær) Large 8:30pm

    Úlfarsfell (Lambhagavegur) Small 8:30pm

    The city checks the forecast before noon on New Year’s Eve and decides then: bonfires will not be lit if the wind speed is over 10 m/s. You may not bring fireworks or flares to the bonfires, though sparklers and torches without sparks are allowed, and the city urges people to walk, because vehicle access is limited. None of the ten is in the old centre, and the Laugardalur bonfire is the one near two of our hotels.

    Fireworks. Icelandic regulation 952/2003 allows general use and sale of fireworks to the public only from 28 December to 6 January, both days included. The police have explained that use is banned from 22:00 to 10:00 the next day during that period, except on New Year’s night. In December 2023 Visit Reykjavik listed one area dedicated to fireworks downtown, Skólavörðuholt by Hallgrímskirkja; the city’s 2025 notice does not repeat it, so ask your hotel whether it still applies. The city also warned in 2025 that air quality may be poor in the first hours of the new year, and that people with breathing or heart conditions and children should stay indoors around midnight with windows closed.

    Hallgrímskirkja. The church has already published its 2026-27 hours. On Thursday 31 December the visiting hours are 10:00 to 12:00, a festive concert starts at 16:00 (tickets from the church shop and tix.is, church open to concert guests from 15:15), and the New Year’s Eve service is at 18:00. On Friday 1 January 2027 the church is open only for the 14:00 festive service, from 13:15, and the tower is closed. Seats cannot be reserved.

    Pools. The city’s list of public-holiday opening hours for 2026 gives all its pools, including Laugardalur and Sundhöll, 8am to 1pm on 31 December, and 1pm to 5pm on 1 January 2027. We found no official list of which restaurants and shops close; ask your hotel before you plan a dinner.

    Reykjavik’s hotel tax: 800 ISK a room a night, plus 11% VAT

    Charge Rate in 2026
    Accommodation tax at hotels, guesthouses and other licensed lodgings (categories II-IV) 800 ISK per room, per night
    Accommodation tax at campsites and pitches 400 ISK per unit, per night
    VAT on hotel and guest-room rental 11%

    Skatturinn, the Icelandic tax authority, sets the 800 ISK charge; the hotel collects it, and Fosshotel Baron says on its page that it is charged on arrival. The government’s VAT guidance says hotel rental is always charged at 11%, whatever the length of stay. Our arithmetic: a room at 40,000 ISK a night, VAT included, for three nights holds 11,892 ISK of VAT (40,000 × 11 ÷ 111 × 3), and the accommodation tax adds 2,400 ISK, so the stay costs 122,400 ISK in all.

    One thing to watch: Deloitte Iceland reports that the draft 2027 budget would raise several fixed-sum charges, including the accommodation tax, by 5.2%. It was still a proposal when we checked and we found no start date, so the 800 ISK stands for 2026 stays; check it if you stay past 1 January.

    Getting in from the airport, and around

    • Flybus. The coach from Keflavík to the BSÍ terminal takes about 45 minutes. One way costs 3,999 ISK for adults and 1,999 ISK for ages 6 to 15, and children aged 1 to 5 travel free; Flybus to hotel costs 5,299 ISK for adults and 2,649 ISK for ages 6 to 15. Its FAQ says it runs daily in connection with all arriving and departing flights and operates year-round in all conditions; we found no separate 31 December or 1 January timetable, so check flybus.is. Going back, coaches leave BSÍ every 30 to 60 minutes from 04:00 until 22:30.
    • Strætó route 55. The public bus between the airport and the capital area runs every day of the week; an adult pays 2,400 ISK to Reykjavik and children 11 and under ride free. We found no 31 December timetable for it.
    • City buses. Strætó had not published its 2026-27 holiday notice when we checked. Last year’s, issued on 2 December 2025, said that on 31 December buses ran to a Saturday schedule with a few extra morning trips, with the last trips around 15:00, and on 1 January to a Sunday schedule. If this year follows it, you cannot count on a bus home at midnight; plan a walk or a taxi, and check straeto.is.
    • Roads. The Icelandic Road and Coastal Administration says its traffic centre monitors road conditions 24 hours a day, 365 days a year and posts them on umferdin.is. If you drive from Keflavík or leave Reykjavik, check road.is or umferdin.is on the day.

    Hallgrímskirkja, Laugavegur and Austurvöllur: the hill and the main street

    Hallgrímskirkja stands at the top of Skólavörðuholt, and Laugavegur, the main shopping street, runs below it. This is the nearest base to the church’s evening programme and the fireworks area, and the noisiest after midnight. Austurvöllur, the square beside the parliament, is a few minutes downhill.

    The Old Harbour and Grandi: Harpa, the water and the west

    The Old Harbour is a short walk from Laugavegur and a longer one from the church. Grandi, the harbour district beyond it, is quieter. Sleep here if you would rather watch the harbour than join the hill.

    Hlemmur: the quieter end of Laugavegur

    Hlemmur sits at the eastern end of Laugavegur, beyond the busiest shops. You are on foot to the church and the main street, but not in the thick of the crowd at midnight.

    Where to stay in Hlemmur

    • Skuggi Hotel by Keahotels, comfortable. At Hverfisgata 103, with four room types, a bar and lounge, breakfast included and limited free parking. The hotel says Laugavegur is a short walk away. Choose it for breakfast and a bar near the east end. Watch out: parking is limited, and its page lists no New Year’s Eve package or minimum stay. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Fosshotel Baron, good value. At Barónsstígur 2-4, with 120 rooms, a bar, a lift and breakfast from 7am to 10am. Choose it for a plain, central base.     Laugardalur: the valley park, with parking

    Laugardalur is the valley east of the centre, with the Laugardalslaug pool and, in the city’s 2025 list, a bonfire below Laugarásvegur 18. It is a drive or a taxi from the old centre.

    Where to stay in Laugardalur

    • Grand Hotel Reykjavík, comfortable. At Sigtún 28, with more than 300 rooms, the Grand Brasserie open daily for lunch and dinner and breakfast from 6:30am to 10am. Its festive page lists a New Year’s Eve dinner at 36,900 ISK a person in a fourth-floor hall, with a view of the fireworks, but does not give the year or mention a stay, so confirm it is for 2026. Choose it for a dinner without leaving the building. Watch out: its spa is closed, with a new one planned, and parking is charged in the garage. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Reykjavik Lights Hotel by Keahotels, good value. At Suðurlandsbraut 12, with 105 rooms, complimentary breakfast, free parking and a bar and lounge. It says it is close to Laugardalur and 2.9 km from the centre. Choose it to park for free. Watch out: it is 2.9 km from the centre and publishes no New Year’s Eve terms. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Keflavík: the airport base

    If you fly on 1 January, a hotel in Reykjanesbær removes the 50 km transfer. You will not be near the church or any city bonfire, and we found no official bonfire list for Reykjanesbær.

Choose it for an early flight. Watch out: its page states neither parking nor a shuttle, and gives no room count or New Year’s Eve terms. Check rates on Booking.com →

    How to book New Year’s Eve in Reykjavik

    • Count the tax. Add 800 ISK a room a night if the rate does not show it; the 11% VAT is already in the price.
    • Ask what the hotel publishes. Only one hotel here, Grand Hotel Reykjavík, states a New Year’s Eve event, and none states a minimum stay for 31 December, so ask before you pay.
    • Plan the way back. If last year’s Strætó pattern holds, there is no bus after about 15:00 on the 31st. Choose a hotel you can walk to from where you will be at midnight.

    Frequently asked questions

    Are there fireworks and bonfires in Reykjavik on New Year's Eve?

    Yes. The City of Reykjavik's 2025 notice listed ten bonfires, all at 8:30pm except Skerjafjörður at 9pm, and the 2026 list was not yet published. Fireworks may be sold and used by the public from 28 December to 6 January. Bonfires are not lit if the wind speed is over 10 m/s, and fireworks may not be brought to them.

    Where is the best place to stay in Reykjavik for New Year's Eve?

    For the crowd, stay around Hallgrímskirkja and Laugavegur: three hotels here are within 0.6 km of the church by their own pages. For a harbour setting choose the Old Harbour and Grandi, for a quieter street Hlemmur, for parking Laugardalur, and for a 1 January flight Keflavík.

    Is Hallgrímskirkja open on New Year's Eve 2026?

    Its published 2026-27 hours give visiting from 10:00 to 12:00 on 31 December, a festive concert at 16:00 and a service at 18:00. On 1 January 2027 it is open only for the 14:00 service, from 13:15, and the tower is closed.

    Do buses run in Reykjavik on New Year's Eve?

    Strætó had not published its 2026-27 notice when we checked. Last year's said buses ran to a Saturday schedule on 31 December with the last trips around 15:00, and to a Sunday schedule on 1 January. Plan a walk or a taxi home, and check straeto.is.

    Is there a hotel tax in Reykjavik?

    Yes. Iceland charges an accommodation tax of 800 ISK per room per night at hotels and guesthouses, and hotel rooms carry 11% VAT, which is normally in the price. Reykjavik adds no city tax. A draft 2027 budget would raise the accommodation tax by 5.2%, but it was only a proposal when we checked.

    How do I get from Keflavík Airport to Reykjavik?

    Flybus takes about 45 minutes to the BSÍ terminal and costs 3,999 ISK for adults, or 5,299 ISK to a hotel. Strætó route 55 costs 2,400 ISK for adults. We found no separate Flybus timetable for 31 December or 1 January, so check flybus.is.
