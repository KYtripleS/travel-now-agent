    Chinese New Year in Hong Kong is two big evenings and a long holiday weekend, and the dates for 2027 are already fixed. The Government’s list of general holidays gives Lunar New Year’s Day as Saturday 6 February 2027, with the third and fourth days on Monday 8 and Tuesday 9 February. The Leisure and Cultural Services Department said on 7 September that the harbour fireworks will be held on Sunday 7 February, the second day. What is not yet published is the rest of the programme: the start time of the fireworks, the date and time of the Night Parade, and the Metro and road arrangements. We read the official pages on 26 October 2026, and where one still describes last year, we say so and give it as last year’s.

    This guide covers what has and has not been announced for 2027, four areas matched to the kind of festival you want, 12 hotels, the Hotel Accommodation Tax and what to book first. For the rest of your trip, where to stay in Hong Kong covers five neighbourhoods and 12 hotels, nine of which also appear here.     The Night Parade

    The Tourism Board’s Chinese New Year page says the Night Parade is held on the first day of the holiday, along the streets of Tsim Sha Tsui. It gives no 2027 date, time or route yet, and the page we opened on 26 October carried no tickets or programme. Last year’s parade began at 8pm on the first day, and the Transport Department said roads in the Tsim Sha Tsui area would close in phases from 3.30pm until about 11.45pm. The closures covered the parade route along Canton Road, Haiphong Road and Nathan Road, and Salisbury Road between Chatham Road South and the Star Ferry Pier. Taxi stands, minibus stands and car parks inside the closure were suspended, and some bus routes were diverted or suspended. The first day is Saturday 6 February, so if the parade follows the first-day pattern it falls that evening; that is our inference, not an announcement.

    The harbour fireworks

    The Leisure and Cultural Services Department’s notice of 7 September 2026, which sought sponsors for the display, says it will be held on 7 February 2027, a Sunday. The notice gives no start time. Last year’s display, coordinated by the Culture, Sports and Tourism Bureau and sponsored by the Hong Kong Jockey Club, ran at 8pm on the second day, lasted about 23 minutes and used 31,888 shells. The Government named Tsim Sha Tsui, Mid-Levels, Central, Wan Chai, Causeway Bay and Hung Hom among the places it could be seen from, on both sides of the harbour. Last year the time and theme were announced on 11 February, six days beforehand, so 2027’s may arrive in the first days of February.

    The Police crowd plan for last year gives the shape of the evening. In Tsim Sha Tsui the first road closures began at 5pm, including Salisbury Road, Canton Road and Nathan Road south of Austin Road, and the Police said they would pedestrianise roads in phases and could close pedestrian subways. If the Hong Kong Cultural Centre, the Clock Tower area or the Avenue of Stars reached capacity, people would be diverted elsewhere. On the Island, closures began in Wan Chai at 3pm on Expo Drive East and widened at 6pm, 7pm and 7.45pm to cover roads including Harbour Road, Fleming Road and parts of Lockhart Road and Jaffe Road.

    The fairs, the temples and the race day

    • Lunar New Year Fairs. The Food and Environmental Hygiene Department’s page for 2027 says the fairs run for seven days from 31 January to 6 February, to the early morning of Lunar New Year’s Day, with Victoria Park, Causeway Bay among the venues. Its release for the 2026 fairs said the busiest day was Lunar New Year’s Eve, with more than 210,000 visitors at Victoria Park in a day, and that a footfall light on its website showed green, amber or red for each fair. We found no 2027 opening hours. The Tourism Board adds that the flower markets usually last a week and close in the early hours of the first day, that Victoria Park’s is the largest, and that Prince Edward has another.
    • Wong Tai Sin first incense. The Tourism Board describes the scramble to offer the first incense stick of the year at Wong Tai Sin Temple as a cherished custom, often with worshippers dressed up. We found no 2027 hours from the temple itself, and its own site did not load for us. Last year the Transport Department listed road closures and diversions near the temple from the evening of Lunar New Year’s Eve.
    • Race day at Sha Tin. The Tourism Board lists the Chinese New Year Raceday for 8 February 2027, the third day, at Sha Tin Racecourse. Public enclosure entry is HK$10, visitors to Hong Kong with a valid travel document get in free, and racecourse entrants must be 18 or over. The page gives no first-race time. Racecourse is an MTR station, and MTR’s Year of the Horse page names it, with Wong Tai Sin station, among the stations it decorated.
    • Che Kung Temple. The Tourism Board says it is busy on the second or third day, when visitors pray, draw fortune sticks and turn the windmill clockwise.

    Trains on the nights

    • Lunar New Year’s Eve. For 2027 that is Friday 5 February, by our arithmetic from the first day on the 6th. Last year the Transport Department said MTR lines other than the Airport Express and Disneyland Resort Line would run more often from 3pm. The Island, Tsuen Wan, Kwun Tong, South Island, Tung Chung, Tuen Ma and East Rail lines (Admiralty to Sheung Shui), parts of the Tseung Kwan O Line and seven Light Rail routes would run overnight, and the Airport Express’s hours would be extended. The Central to Mid-Levels Escalator ran until 3am.
    • Parade and fireworks nights. Last year MTR services were strengthened on the relevant lines after both, “subject to passenger demand”, but the Government did not announce overnight running for either night. On the fireworks night the Star Ferry and five other ferry operators adjusted or suspended parts of their services from 5.05pm, tram services were strengthened subject to demand, and the Escalator ran until 2am.
    • 2027. Neither MTR nor the Transport Department had posted notices for February 2027 when we looked. Last year’s came on 12 February, five days before the holiday.
    • Mainland Golden Week. Last year the Government warned of heavy cross-boundary traffic during the mainland’s Chinese New Year Golden Week and asked people to book coaches early. We found no 2027 notice.

    What shuts

    The Government’s list gives three general holidays, 6, 8 and 9 February, and Sunday 7 February is the second day, so Saturday to Tuesday is four days in a row off for many people. The 9th is a substitute: the Government spokesman said that because the second day falls on a Sunday, the fourth day is designated a general holiday instead. We found no official list of which shops and restaurants close, so check any place you are counting on for a meal before you plan around it.

    The rules that decide where you sleep

    • Inside the closure, or beside it. Last year the fireworks closed the roads in front of several hotels from 5pm in Tsim Sha Tsui and from 6pm in Wan Chai. A hotel inside a closure is a walk, not a taxi ride, after about 5pm, and a taxi pick-up inside it may be suspended.
    • Walk to one show, train to the other. The parade is on the Kowloon side, the Island side has the fair, and the harbour fireworks can be watched from both. If you stay on one side, check that you can walk back after both evenings.
    • Ferries stop early on the fireworks night. Last year the Star Ferry and five other operators began suspending parts of their services at 5.05pm. Plan to be on the side you want before then.
    • Holiday crowds. Because 6, 8 and 9 February are general holidays, expect the fair, the temples and the racecourse to be busy all day, not only in the evening.
    • The tax is on the room. Hong Kong’s Hotel Accommodation Tax is 3% of the room charge, and the service charge is not taxed. See the tax section below.

    Tsim Sha Tsui: the parade and the promenade

    Tsim Sha Tsui holds both the Night Parade and the Kowloon side of the fireworks, so the area is the most convenient and the most disrupted. The Police named the Cultural Centre, the Clock Tower and the Avenue of Stars among its viewing spots last year. Staying here means the walk to the parade is short, and the cost is that taxis, buses and the roads in front of the hotel can be closed. Our Hong Kong first-timers guide covers the district in detail, and the harbour cruises guide covers the water.

    Wan Chai and Causeway Bay: the fair and the Island side

    Wan Chai and Causeway Bay hold the fair and the Island-side viewing, and both sit inside last year’s closure maps. The Victoria Park fair is the largest of the fairs, according to the Tourism Board, and Causeway Bay is where the crowd gathers on Lunar New Year’s Eve. Our Hong Kong guide goes through the district’s shops and trams.

    Central and Sheung Wan: a calmer Island base

    Central and Sheung Wan are neither the parade nor the fair, which is the point if you want sleep, the Mid-Levels Escalator and an easier walk to a ferry. Central was inside last year’s fireworks closure from 6pm around Man Kwong Street and the General Post Office, and the Escalator ran until 2am on the fireworks night and 3am on Lunar New Year’s Eve.

    Jordan, Mong Kok and Wong Tai Sin: temples and the races

    North of Tsim Sha Tsui, Nathan Road runs through Jordan and Mong Kok. Both sit north of the roads closed last year for the parade and the fireworks, and Wong Tai Sin Temple and Sha Tin Racecourse are reached on the MTR, which is what the Government urged people to use last year. We have not timed the journey from either hotel to the temple or the course.

    Where to stay in Jordan and Mong Kok

    • Eaton HK, good value. At 380 Nathan Road in Jordan, with 465 rooms and suites and a heated rooftop pool on the 21st floor next to its fitness centre.     Hong Kong’s hotel accommodation tax

    Hong Kong charges a Hotel Accommodation Tax on room charges, administered by the Inland Revenue Department. It was waived from 1 July 2008 and restored at 3% from 1 January 2025. The Government said in September 2025 that it had no plan to adjust the rate, and we found no change for 2026/27.

    What Rate or rule
    Rate 3% of the accommodation charge
    Service charge (normally 10% of the room charge) Not taxed
    Stays of 28 or more consecutive nights Treated as long-term, not taxed
    Hotels with fewer than 10 rooms Exempt

    As our arithmetic, a HK$2,500 room has a HK$250 service charge and HK$75 of tax, so HK$2,825 for the night, about 13% over the room rate. Booking sites differ in whether the first price includes the service charge and the tax, so compare totals. Hotel taxes around the world shows how other cities charge theirs.

    Getting in from the airport, and around

    • Airport Express. The MTR says the 35.3km journey from the airport to the city takes as little as 24 minutes. An adult single costs HK$120 to Hong Kong station or HK$105 to Kowloon station with an Octopus card, a contactless bank card or a QR code, and HK$130 or HK$115 with a smart ticket.
    • Stations to hotels. Hong Kong station serves the Island, Kowloon station is in West Kowloon, and the MTR links both to the districts above. Last year the Airport Express’s hours were extended on Lunar New Year’s Eve, but the Government’s notices did not mention it on the parade or fireworks nights.
    • On the nights. Use the MTR, not a taxi, after about 5pm on the parade and fireworks evenings, and check the Transport Department’s HKeMobility app, which it named for live traffic.
    • For the water. The Star Ferry crosses between Tsim Sha Tsui and Central and Wan Chai, but it adjusted its service from 5.05pm on last year’s fireworks night.

    How to book Hong Kong at Chinese New Year

    • Count the 3%. Add the service charge and the tax to the room rate before you compare totals.
    • Check the closure map before the hotel. Last year’s Police and Transport Department notices were issued on 10 to 12 February, only days before the holiday, so the 2027 maps may come late. Keep free cancellation until they are out.
    • Read the minimum stay. None of the hotels’ own pages that we read list a Chinese New Year minimum stay or package for 2027. The offers we saw, such as Mandarin Oriental’s complimentary third night or 99 Bonham’s advance purchase, are general. Ask each hotel for the terms on your dates.
    • Wait for the dates that matter. The parade date and the fireworks time were not published when we looked. Last year the fireworks time came on 11 February.

    Frequently asked questions

    When is Chinese New Year 2027 in Hong Kong?

    Lunar New Year's Day is Saturday 6 February 2027. The Government's general holidays are 6, 8 and 9 February; the fourth day, Tuesday 9 February, stands in for the second day, which falls on a Sunday.

    When are the Chinese New Year fireworks in Hong Kong in 2027?

    The Leisure and Cultural Services Department said on 7 September 2026 that the harbour fireworks will be held on Sunday 7 February 2027, the second day. No start time or programme was published when we checked. Last year's display ran at 8pm for about 23 minutes.

    When is the Chinese New Year Night Parade in 2027?

    Not announced. The Tourism Board says the parade is held on the first day of the holiday in Tsim Sha Tsui, which is Saturday 6 February 2027, but it gave no date, time or route when we checked. Last year it began at 8pm, with Tsim Sha Tsui roads closed in phases from 3.30pm.

    Where is the best place to stay for the parade and fireworks?

    Tsim Sha Tsui is the most convenient for the parade, which ran along Nathan Road, Canton Road and Haiphong Road last year, and for the Kowloon side of the fireworks. Wan Chai and Causeway Bay suit the Victoria Park fair and the Island side. Expect road closures from late afternoon on both evenings.

    Do hotels in Hong Kong have a minimum stay at Chinese New Year?

    None of the 12 hotels' own pages that we read listed a Chinese New Year minimum stay or package for 2027. The offers we saw, such as Mandarin Oriental's complimentary third night and 99 Bonham's advance-purchase discount, are general, so ask each hotel for its terms on your dates.

    Is there a hotel tax in Hong Kong?

    Yes. The Hotel Accommodation Tax was restored at 3% of the room charge on 1 January 2025. The service charge, normally 10%, is not taxed, so a total runs about 13% over the room rate. The Government said in September 2025 it had no plan to adjust the rate.
