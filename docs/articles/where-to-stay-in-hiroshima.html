Hiroshima’s hotel bill is no longer just the room rate. Since 1 April 2026 Hiroshima Prefecture charges an accommodation tax of ¥200 per person per night on rooms of ¥6,000 or more before consumption tax, and anyone stepping onto Miyajima by boat pays a further ¥100 visitor tax. The choice that matters more is where to sleep: in the city, where the Shinkansen, the airport bus and the streetcars meet, or on Miyajima island itself.

This guide covers five areas, from Hiroshima Station to Miyajima, 12 hotels, both taxes and the routes from the airport. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

Hiroshima’s accommodation tax and the Miyajima visitor tax

Hiroshima Prefecture’s accommodation tax has been in force since 1 April 2026. It is ¥200 per person per night when the room-only price is ¥6,000 or more before consumption tax; below that line nothing is charged. The room-only price includes the service charge but not meals or consumption tax. The guest pays the hotel, and the hotel passes it on. The prefecture’s page describes only this tax, and we found no separate city or Hatsukaichi accommodation tax on the official pages we read.

Miyajima has its own charge. Hatsukaichi City’s Miyajima Visitor Tax has applied since 1 October 2023 to people who enter the island by boat. It is ¥100 per person per visit, or ¥500 for a year’s worth of visits, and the ferry operator adds it to the fare. Residents, commuters, pre-school children, school trips and holders of disability handbooks are exempt.

The tax is a flat ¥200 however high the rate goes. For a worked example, ours: a couple pays ¥22,000 a night for a room only, including the 10% consumption tax. Before that tax the room costs ¥20,000, or ¥10,000 each, which is over the ¥6,000 line, so the tax is ¥200 each, ¥400 a night and ¥1,200 over three nights. A day trip to Miyajima on the JR ferry then costs ¥200 for the fare out, ¥100 visitor tax and ¥200 back: ¥500 each, ¥1,000 for two.

Japan’s standard consumption tax rate is 10%, and Japanese hotel rates are normally quoted with it included.

Getting in from Hiroshima Airport, and around

  • By limousine bus. Hiroshima Electric Railway (Hiroden) runs airport buses to Hiroshima Station’s Shinkansen exit and to the Hiroshima Bus Center, some of them via Astram Nakasuji station. The adult fare is ¥1,500 (children ¥750), and transit IC cards are accepted, by the airport’s fare table. The city’s tourism FAQ gives about 50 minutes to the station and about 55 to the Bus Center. Allow about an hour in all.
  • By bus and train. The city also lists a ¥490 local bus to JR Shiraichi station (about 15 minutes), then the Sanyo Line to Hiroshima Station (about 45).
  • By streetcar. Hiroden’s page gives a flat adult fare of ¥240 (children ¥120), paid as you get off, and its sightseeing page puts Genbaku Dome-mae, where the Atomic Bomb Dome, Peace Memorial Park and the museum are listed, 13 minutes from Hiroshima Station. Line 2 runs on to Miyajima-guchi, 49 minutes beyond that stop; the flat fare covers the city section, and the ride out to Miyajima-guchi costs more, so check the fare chart on board. A 1-Day Streetcar Pass costs ¥700, and a 1-Day Streetcar and Ferry Pass ¥1,000 including the Miyajima visitor tax; the ferry part is valid on Miyajima Matsudai Kisen boats, not on the JR Miyajima Ferry.
  • To Miyajima by JR. The JR ferry’s own page says the Sanyo Line from Hiroshima Station to Miyajimaguchi takes about 28 minutes. From Miyajimaguchi station you walk through an underpass to the terminal; the ferry is about 10 minutes and costs ¥200 for adults and ¥100 for children one way, with the ¥100 visitor tax on top. IC cards work, and the JR ferry accepts the Japan Rail Pass. Its route page notes that a restoration project is under way for the Great Torii.

Hiroshima Station: the Shinkansen and the airport bus

For a short stay or a late arrival, this is the simplest base. The airport bus stops at the Shinkansen exit, and the streetcars to the park and to Miyajima-guchi and the JR line to Miyajimaguchi all start at the station. The park is a streetcar ride away rather than a walk.

Peace Memorial Park and Hondori: the central district

The Peace Memorial Park, the Atomic Bomb Dome and the Hiroshima Peace Memorial Museum stand close together on the river, and Hiroden lists all three at the Genbaku Dome-mae stop. The museum says it uses the belongings of victims, A-bombed artifacts and survivors’ testimonies to convey the horror and inhumanity of nuclear weapons.

The museum’s own site gives the practical details. It opens from 07:30 to 19:00 in March to July and September to November, to 20:00 in August and to 18:00 in December to February, with the last entry 30 minutes before closing. Admission is ¥200 for adults and ¥100 for high school students, and junior high school students and younger go free. It closes on 30 and 31 December and in the middle of February; the next closure is 15 to 20 February 2027, for an exhibition change. In the extended early and late slots, online reservation is needed. The museum says it is very crowded in the daytime, recommends early morning or evening, and notes that web tickets can sell out. It also lists free guided tours by Hiroshima Peace Volunteers and daily talks by A-bomb legacy successors.

Staying here puts the park within walking distance, which makes the early and late slots easy to use. The Hiroshima Bus Center, where the airport bus ends, is near the Prefectural Office and the park, by the airport’s page.

Hatchobori: shops, streetcars and evening streets

Hatchobori is the district of the streetcar stops and shopping streets. Nest Hotel’s page puts Hondori shopping street ten minutes’ walk away, and Dormy Inn’s puts Hatchobori, Kamiyacho and Nagarekawa within a 10-minute walk of its door on Peace Boulevard, so a base here is also a base for dinner.

Where to stay in Hatchobori

At 14-1 Hatchobori, two minutes from Hatchobori Station by the hotel’s figure, with a rooftop open-air bath called SkySpa and a Double with Desk of 16 square metres. Miyajima: a night on the island

Itsukushima Shrine and its Great Torii are why most visitors cross the water, and staying overnight puts the shrine within a short walk. Hotel Miyajima Villa’s access page says the main sights have no car parks, so it suggests leaving a car at the hotel and walking. Iwaso and Mikuniya are each about a 15-minute walk from the pier, so check transfers before you book.

Where to stay on Miyajima

  • Iwaso, splurge. A ryokan founded in 1854 in the Momijidani valley, with four detached one-storey cottages built from the Taisho to the Showa era and a radon hot spring, open from 15:00 to 24:00 and from 06:00 to 09:30. It is 300 metres, about three minutes’ walk, from Itsukushima Shrine. Choose it for a night in a historic ryokan. Watch out: the pier is a 15-minute walk away; the shuttle runs from 08:00 to 20:00 but not to a timetable, so phone 0829-44-2233 once your boat time is set. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Miyajima Villa, comfortable. Produced by the Kinsuikan ryokan, founded in 1902, with three kinds of concept room (town house, sea and mountain) and a viewing bath floored with tatami. Choose it for a modern, adult-oriented stay. Watch out: the hotel closes from 14 to 18 December 2026 for maintenance, the bath is an artificial quasi-natural spring and is not open-air, and guests with tattoos are not admitted to it. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Miyajima Guest House Mikuniya, budget. Japanese-style private rooms (one of ten tatami mats, four of eight) and a four-bed dormitory, with a lounge, a Japanese garden and rental yukata for ¥300. By its page it is a five-minute walk from the shrine and about 15 from the ferry. Choose it for the cheapest bed on the island. Watch out: there is no pick-up, and a check-in after 21:00 costs an extra ¥1,000 per hour. Check rates on Booking.com →

Miyajimaguchi: the mainland port

Miyajimaguchi is where the ferries leave, so a hotel here makes the crossing a matter of minutes. You can cross after breakfast and come back for dinner; check the last ferry times before you plan an evening on the island.

Where to stay in Miyajimaguchi

  • Miyajima Coral Hotel, good value. About a five-minute walk from JR Miyajimaguchi station and 30 seconds from the pier, with free parking for guests from noon on arrival to noon on departure, a coin laundry and 11 room types, including a Japanese-modern twin of 20 square metres. Choose it for being beside the ferry. Watch out: it stands on the mainland, so Itsukushima Shrine is a boat ride away, about 10 minutes by the hotel’s figure. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Grandvrio Hotel Miyajima Wakura, comfortable. A natural hot spring with an open-air bath and sauna, with views of the Meoto Iwa rocks off Hanaguri island, and 171 free parking spaces. It runs a free shuttle bus from JR Miyajimaguchi station. Choose it for a hot-spring night with the car. Watch out: the pier is a 12-minute walk or about 3 minutes by car, so plan to use the shuttle. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Hiroshima

  • Count the tax. From 1 April 2026 each person pays ¥200 a night on a room-only price of ¥6,000 or more, before consumption tax. Add ¥100 for each visit to Miyajima unless your pass includes it.
  • Check closures and work. Hotel Miyajima Villa closes from 14 to 18 December 2026, the Mitsui Garden is being renovated until 31 May 2027, and the Granvia’s official-site bookings pause from 8 to 10 November 2026.
  • Decide on the island first. Sleeping on Miyajima means a walk or a shuttle from the pier; sleeping in Miyajimaguchi or the city means the ferry each day.
  • Watch for phishing. Dormy Inn Hiroshima and Mitsui Garden Hotel Hiroshima both posted notices in May 2026 warning Booking.com guests about suspicious messages; Mitsui Garden names WhatsApp. Pay only through your booking’s own page.
  • Compare totals. Hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book, and Where to stay in Japan puts Hiroshima beside Osaka and Kyoto.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Hiroshima for a first trip?

The Peace Memorial Park and Hondori district, if you want the park and museum on foot: Dormy Inn Hiroshima, the Rihga Royal and the Mitsui Garden are on or near Peace Boulevard and the streetcar lines. Choose Hiroshima Station for the Shinkansen and the airport bus, Hatchobori for shops and evening streets, and Miyajima or Miyajimaguchi for the island.

Does Hiroshima have an accommodation tax in 2026?

Yes. Hiroshima Prefecture has charged an accommodation tax since 1 April 2026: ¥200 per person per night when the room-only price is ¥6,000 or more before consumption tax, with nothing charged below that. Meals and consumption tax are not part of the base. We found no separate city or Hatsukaichi accommodation tax on the official pages we read.

How much is the Miyajima visitor tax?

¥100 per person per visit, or ¥500 for a year's visits, for people who enter the island by boat since 1 October 2023. The ferry operator adds it to the fare. Residents, commuters, pre-school children, school trips and holders of disability handbooks are exempt. The JR ferry fare is ¥200 for adults one way, so a day trip is ¥500 each by that ferry.

How do you get from Hiroshima Airport to the city?

By Hiroden's limousine bus: the adult fare is ¥1,500 (children ¥750), and the city's tourism FAQ gives about 50 minutes to Hiroshima Station and about 55 to the Hiroshima Bus Center. The city also lists a ¥490 local bus to JR Shiraichi station, about 15 minutes, then the Sanyo Line to Hiroshima Station in about 45.

How do you get from Hiroshima Station to Miyajima?

By JR Sanyo Line to Miyajimaguchi, about 28 minutes by the JR ferry's own page, then the JR Miyajima Ferry, about 10 minutes, at ¥200 for adults one way plus the ¥100 visitor tax. The Hiroden streetcar also runs to Miyajima-guchi: its sightseeing page gives 49 minutes on line 2 from Genbaku Dome-mae, and its flat adult fare is ¥240.

Should you stay on Miyajima or in Hiroshima city?

Stay on Miyajima if you want a night in a ryokan within a short walk of Itsukushima Shrine: Iwaso is about 300 metres from the shrine, but a 15-minute walk from the pier. Otherwise stay in Miyajimaguchi, where the Miyajima Coral Hotel is about 30 seconds from the pier, or in the city, and take the ferry each day.
