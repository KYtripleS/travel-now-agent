Where to Stay in Bangkok (2026): Five Areas and 12 Hotels, Compared

By Casey, Gently Yonder editor

Sukhumvit from Nana to Phrom Phong for a first trip, Siam for the centre of the Skytrain, the Riverside for the grand hotels and boats to the temples, Silom and Sathorn for Lumphini Park, the Old Town for the Grand Palace. Five areas and 12 hotels.

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Asok that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

Bangkok was the world’s most visited city in 2025, with 30.3 million international arrivals by Euromonitor International’s count, and it is a city of traffic. Where you sleep decides how much of the trip you spend in it. A hotel a few minutes’ walk from a Skytrain station, or one with its own boat across the river, keeps you out of most of it.

This guide compares five areas, then names twelve hotels across them, with the reason to pick each and, where there is one, the catch. They are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating at the start of October 2026. One of them, the Dusit Thani, is a new building under an old name, and booking sites still list the old one too; we say which is which. No hotel pays to appear here.

Sukhumvit: the first-trip base

Sukhumvit Road runs east from the centre under the Skytrain’s Sukhumvit Line, and the stretch from Nana through Asok to Phrom Phong is where most first-time visitors end up, for good reason. Asok is where the Skytrain meets the MRT Blue Line, at Sukhumvit station, and its exits lead into the Terminal 21 mall. Phrom Phong, one stop east, has Benjasiri Park and the EmQuartier and Emporium malls. Nana, one stop west of Asok, is known for its nightlife. The hotels and restaurants line the sois, the side streets numbered off the main road, with odd numbers on one side and even numbers on the other.

Where to stay on Sukhumvit

  • Hyatt Regency Bangkok Sukhumvit, comfortable to splurge. On Sukhumvit Soi 13, linked to Nana station by a covered skybridge, so you reach the Skytrain without crossing the road. Choose it for the train at your door. Watch out: Nana is Sukhumvit’s nightlife end. Check rates on Booking.com →

Siam and Ratchaprasong: the centre of the Skytrain

Siam is where the Skytrain’s two lines cross, at a cross-platform interchange, and skybridges link the station to Siam Paragon, Siam Center, Siam Square One and Centerpoint of Siam Square. An elevated walkway, the Sky Walk, runs on to CentralWorld at Ratchaprasong, one stop east at Chit Lom. If the trip is about shopping, or you want every ride to be short, this is the middle of the map.

Where to stay in Siam and Ratchaprasong

  • Siam Kempinski Hotel Bangkok, splurge. A resort-style hotel with pools in landscaped grounds and direct access to the Siam Paragon mall, which is linked to Siam station. Choose it for luxury at the centre of the network. Watch out: price. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Pathumwan Princess Hotel, comfortable. 451 rooms and suites, family suites among them, connected to the MBK Center and across the road from Siam Square. Choose it for a mid-range base among the malls. Watch out: you are inside a busy shopping complex. Check rates on Booking.com →

The Riverside: grand hotels and boats to the temples

Bangkok’s oldest grand hotel is on the Chao Phraya, and the river is still the easiest way to the Grand Palace, Wat Pho and Wat Arun. From Sathorn Pier, beside Saphan Taksin station, the Chao Phraya Express Boat runs upriver to Tha Tien (N8) for Wat Pho and the ferry to Wat Arun, and to Tha Chang (N9) for the Grand Palace. Several riverside hotels run their own free boats to Sathorn Pier, which makes a hotel on the far bank more practical than it looks on a map. The trade-off is distance from the shopping districts, and evenings that run to the boat timetable.

Where to stay on the Riverside

  • Mandarin Oriental, Bangkok, splurge. It opened as The Oriental in 1876, the first hotel built in Thailand, and its original Authors’ Wing still stands by the river; hotel boats run from Sathorn Pier. Choose it for history and service on the water. Watch out: price. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Capella Bangkok, splurge. Opened in October 2020 on Charoen Krung Road, with 101 rooms, every one of them facing the river. Choose it for a smaller, newer riverside hotel. Watch out: price. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Avani+ Riverside Bangkok Hotel, comfortable. All 250 rooms look over the river from the Thonburi bank, there is a rooftop infinity pool, and a free shuttle boat runs to Sathorn Pier and Saphan Taksin station from 08:00 to 23:15. Choose it for river views at a mid-range price. Watch out: every trip into town starts with the boat. Check rates on Booking.com →

Silom and Sathorn: Lumphini Park and the business district

Silom and Sathorn are Bangkok’s business district: two parallel roads of offices and hotels running south-west from Lumphini Park. The Skytrain’s Silom Line and the MRT Blue Line meet at Sala Daeng and Si Lom stations, joined by a skywalk, and the bars of Patpong and Thaniya are a short walk away. It suits a base with the park for a morning walk, or a trip split between work and sightseeing.

Where to stay in Silom and Sathorn

  • The Sukhothai Bangkok, splurge. Completed in 1991 on South Sathorn Road to a design by Kerry Hill and Ed Tuttle, it sits among gardens and lotus ponds across about six acres. Choose it for calm in the middle of the business district. Watch out: price. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Dusit Thani Bangkok, splurge. The original Dusit Thani opened in 1970 and closed in January 2019 to be rebuilt; the new hotel opened on the same site on 27 September 2024, with 257 rooms, all looking out over Lumphini Park. Choose it for the park view across from Silom. Watch out: booking sites still list the old hotel as well; our rates link goes to the new one. Check rates on Booking.com →

The Old Town: the Grand Palace on foot

The Old Town is Rattanakosin, the island of temples and palaces around the Grand Palace and Wat Pho, and Banglamphu just to the north, home of Khao San Road. Staying here puts you at the palace gates when they open, and Wat Arun is a short ferry ride from Tha Tien. There is no Skytrain: the MRT Blue Line reaches Sanam Chai, near Wat Pho, and the river boats stop at Tha Chang, Tha Tien and Phra Arthit (N13) for Khao San Road. How your evenings feel depends on how close you are to Khao San.

Where to stay in the Old Town

  • Riva Surya Bangkok, comfortable. On Phra Arthit Road beside the river, about five minutes’ walk from Khao San Road, with two pools, one of them for children, and the Phra Arthit pier on the same road. Choose it for the Old Town on the water. Watch out: there is no Skytrain here, so you will use the boat and taxis. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Lamphu Tree House, good value. A small hotel overlooking the Bang Lamphu canal, with a pool and a wall of antique golden teak from old Thai houses in every room, five to ten minutes’ walk from Khao San Road. Choose it for character on a budget. Watch out: it is a walk to the river piers, and further to the MRT. Check rates on Booking.com →

Before you book

  • Read the “++”. Hotel rates and restaurant bills marked “++” add a 10% service charge and then 7% VAT on top, about 17.7% in all. Booking sites differ in whether the first price you see includes them, so compare totals, not headline rates.
  • Have your hotel’s address ready before you fly. The Thailand Digital Arrival Card, which is free and filed online in the 72 hours before you arrive, asks where you are staying (the official TDAC site).
  • Choose the station before the hotel. On Sukhumvit and in Silom, a five-minute walk to the Skytrain is worth more than a nicer room fifteen minutes away. If you are still deciding which booking site to use, our hotel booking sites compared goes through them.

Getting around from your hotel

  • From Suvarnabhumi Airport, the Airport Rail Link’s City Line runs to Phaya Thai, where it meets the Skytrain, in about 27 minutes, for 15 to 45 baht depending on distance, from 05:30 to midnight. For the MRT, change at Makkasan for Phetchaburi station.
  • From Don Mueang Airport, the SRT Dark Red Line runs to Krung Thep Aphiwat Central Terminal for 33 baht; from there the MRT Blue Line at Bang Sue continues into the city.
  • On the river, the Chao Phraya Express Boat runs from Sathorn Pier (CEN), beside Saphan Taksin station, to Tha Tien (N8) for Wat Pho and the Wat Arun ferry, Tha Chang (N9) for the Grand Palace and Phra Arthit (N13) for Khao San Road. For an evening on the water, see our guides to Bangkok river cruises and where to book a dinner cruise.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Bangkok for a first trip?

Sukhumvit, between Nana and Phrom Phong stations: the Skytrain runs to Siam and to the river at Saphan Taksin, the MRT Blue Line meets it at Asok, and the choice of hotels and food is wide. Choose Siam instead if the trip is mostly shopping.

Is it better to stay in Sukhumvit or Siam?

Sukhumvit for a wider choice of hotels and restaurants and the MRT at Asok; Siam for shopping, since the Skytrain's two lines meet there and skybridges link the station to Siam Paragon, Siam Center and, by the Sky Walk, CentralWorld.

Is the Riverside a good place to stay in Bangkok?

Yes, for the grand hotels and for the temples, which are a boat ride upriver: the Chao Phraya Express Boat runs from Sathorn Pier, beside Saphan Taksin station, to Tha Tien for Wat Pho and Tha Chang for the Grand Palace. The trade-off is distance from the shopping districts.

Where should I stay near the Grand Palace?

In the Old Town: Rattanakosin, around the Grand Palace and Wat Pho, or Banglamphu, around Khao San Road. There is no Skytrain there; the MRT Blue Line stops at Sanam Chai near Wat Pho, and the river boats stop at Tha Chang, Tha Tien and Phra Arthit.

How do I get from Suvarnabhumi Airport to central Bangkok?

The Airport Rail Link's City Line runs to Phaya Thai, where it meets the Skytrain, in about 27 minutes, for 15 to 45 baht depending on distance, from 05:30 to midnight. Change at Makkasan for the MRT.

What does "++" mean on hotel prices in Thailand?

A 10% service charge, then 7% VAT on top, about 17.7% in all. Booking sites differ in whether the first price you see includes them, so compare totals.
