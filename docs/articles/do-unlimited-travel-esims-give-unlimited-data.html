Do "Unlimited" Travel eSIMs Really Give You Unlimited Data?

By Casey, Gently Yonder editor

"Unlimited" travel eSIMs give unlimited data but not unlimited speed: a daily high-speed cap (often 500MB-5GB) then throttling to 64Kbps-1Mbps. What the Fair Usage Policy really means, and when unlimited is smart vs a rip-off.

Updated 2026-09-12 · 8 min read

Short answer: no — not in the way the word makes you think. Almost every “unlimited” travel eSIM gives you unlimited data but not unlimited speed. You get a daily bucket of full-speed data — commonly somewhere between 500MB and 5GB — and once you cross it, your connection is throttled to a slower floor for the rest of the day, then reset. The data never runs out. The speed does. Whether that’s a great deal or a quiet bait-and-switch depends entirely on what you actually do with your phone abroad, and almost no one reads the one document that tells you.

The document that decides everything: the Fair Usage Policy

The word doing the heavy lifting is buried in a page called the Fair Usage Policy (FUP). Virtually every unlimited travel eSIM has one, and it defines what “unlimited” actually behaves like once you use a lot. There is no truly infinite, full-speed plan on the market — the network capacity simply doesn’t exist to promise that to everyone at once.

Take the most-bought example. Airalo’s unlimited plans, by their published policy, give you full speed up to 3GB per day; use more than that and your speed drops to around 1 Mbps for the rest of the day. Your allowance then resets every 24 hours — counted from the moment you activated the eSIM, not at midnight. Crucially, there’s no total cap: you can’t run out of data, you just spend part of the day at the slower floor if you’re heavy that day. Airalo happens to state this before you buy; the threshold can differ by plan, and where it does, it’s shown in the package details. (I’m naming Airalo because it publishes clear numbers — not every brand is as upfront, which is rather the point.)

That 3GB-then-1Mbps shape is representative, not universal. Across the market the high-speed bucket usually lands between 500MB and 5GB a day, and the throttled floor between 64 Kbps and 1 Mbps. Two plans both stamped “unlimited” can therefore be wildly different products.

Two different things are called “throttling”

It’s worth separating two mechanisms, because travellers blame the wrong one.

  • Fair-use throttling is the daily-cap behaviour above: a deliberate, predictable speed reduction after you cross your high-speed allowance. It’s written down. You can plan around it.
  • Deprioritisation is softer and situational. On a shared network your traffic is given lower priority than other users on the same cell tower during congestion. When the tower is quiet you’ll barely notice; in a packed station at rush hour, even a plan you haven’t “used up” can crawl. This isn’t in your daily total — it’s about the tower, not your allowance.

Most “my unlimited eSIM was slow!” complaints are one of these two, and knowing which saves you a pointless support ticket. If it’s slow all afternoon after heavy morning use, that’s fair-use throttling. If it’s slow only in crowds and fine an hour later, that’s deprioritisation.

What can you actually do at the throttled speed?

This is the question that matters, and it’s more reassuring than the word “throttled” sounds. Once you’re at a ~1 Mbps floor:

  • Comfortably fine: maps and live navigation, messaging (WhatsApp, LINE, iMessage), email, calling a taxi, mobile boarding passes, music streaming, and standard-definition social video. The things you most need abroad barely care.
  • Frustrating: HD/4K video streaming, large photo and video uploads to the cloud, big app downloads, and smooth video calls.

At the lower floors some brands use (256 Kbps or less), even social feeds get sticky. So the honest test isn’t “is it unlimited?” — it’s “what’s the throttle floor, and can I live at it for a few hours?” For most sightseeing days, 3GB of full speed never gets exhausted in the first place.

So is “unlimited” a rip-off or a bargain?

Both, depending on you — and this is where a little honesty pays off.

Unlimited is smart when your usage is uncertain or heavy: you tether a laptop, stream, hop between cities, or simply don’t want to ration data or watch a counter. You’re buying peace of mind — the freedom to stop thinking about it — and a daily reset means one heavy day never wrecks the trip. That’s a genuine product, not a trick.

Unlimited is a poor deal when you’re a light, predictable user. If you sip 1–2GB a day — maps, messaging, the odd search — a fixed data plan (say 5–10GB for the week) is often cheaper and never throttles, because you never come close to the cap. Paying an unlimited premium to use a fraction of a daily bucket is spending for a reassurance you don’t need.

There’s also a quieter catch worth checking: hotspot/tethering. Some unlimited plans limit or exclude it, so if your plan is to run a laptop off your phone all day, read that line specifically before you rely on it.

How to buy without being surprised

You don’t need to distrust the category — you need to read one page. Before you buy an unlimited eSIM:

  1. Find the daily high-speed cap. 1GB and 5GB are very different trips. If it isn’t stated, treat that as a warning.
  2. Find the throttle floor. ~1 Mbps is livable; 128–256 Kbps is a different experience once you cross the cap.
  3. Check the reset clock. Most reset every 24h from activation, not at local midnight — which changes when your “fresh” data actually arrives.
  4. Check hotspot rules if you tether.
  5. Match it to your usage, not to the biggest-sounding word.

The honest verdict

“Unlimited” travel eSIMs really do give you unlimited data — you cannot run out — but not unlimited full speed. What you’re actually buying is a generous daily high-speed allowance plus a slower floor you fall back to if you exceed it, refreshed every day. For the ordinary rhythm of a trip — maps, messages, a few videos, uncertain days — that’s a genuinely good deal and worth paying a little for. For a light, predictable user, a fixed data plan is often cheaper and never slows down. The rip-off isn’t the product; it’s buying it without reading the one page that says how it behaves. Read the Fair Usage Policy, match it to how you travel, and “unlimited” stops being a marketing word and becomes a sensible purchase.

Frequently asked questions

Does “unlimited” travel eSIM data ever run out? No. The data itself doesn’t run out — you can’t hit a total cap. What changes is speed: after a daily high-speed allowance (commonly 500MB–5GB), your connection is throttled to a slower floor for the rest of that day, then reset the next day.

What speed do you get after the daily cap? It varies by provider, typically between 64 Kbps and 1 Mbps. Airalo’s published policy, for example, throttles to around 1 Mbps after 3GB in a day — enough for maps, messaging, and standard-definition video, but not smooth HD streaming or large uploads.

When does the daily allowance reset? On most plans, every 24 hours counted from when you activated the eSIM — not at local midnight. That’s why activating your eSIM at the right time matters.

Is an unlimited eSIM better than a fixed-data plan? Only if you’re a heavy or unpredictable user, or you want to stop thinking about data. If you use only 1–2GB a day, a fixed data plan is usually cheaper and won’t throttle you, because you never approach the cap.

Does unlimited data include hotspot/tethering? Not always. Some plans limit or exclude hotspot use, so check the specific plan’s terms before relying on tethering a laptop or tablet.
