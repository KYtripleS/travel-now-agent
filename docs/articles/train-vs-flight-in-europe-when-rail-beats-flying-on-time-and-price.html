Train vs Flight in Europe: When Rail Beats Flying on Time and Price

By Casey, Gently Yonder editor

When does the train beat the plane in Europe? Door-to-door timing, rail times on 8 routes, luggage rules, EU delay compensation and pass reservation fees.

Last updated 1 Nov 2026 · 13 min read

Gently Yonder’s verdict: Take the train between cities with a fast service of under about 3.5 hours: you arrive in the centre and turn up about 20 minutes before, not two hours. At 4 to 5 hours, work out your own door-to-door time; beyond about 5 hours, fly or take a night train.

On most city pairs where the train takes up to about four hours, rail is as fast as flying or faster once you count the whole journey, because a train leaves from the centre of one city and arrives in the centre of the next. Between about four and five hours it depends on where the airports sit. Beyond that, the plane usually wins on time. That is our arithmetic, not a rule from any operator, and this guide shows the working so you can redo it for your own trip. We also cover luggage, what you are owed if a train is late, France’s ban on some short domestic flights, and the reservation fees that catch Interrail and Eurail pass holders out.

    How to compare: door to door, not airport to airport

    A flight’s headline time is the part in the air; a train’s is almost the whole journey. So compare door to door. The overheads below are our own planning assumptions, not operator figures; adjust them for your cities.

    By plane - Getting to the airport: 30 to 60 minutes - Arriving early for check-in and security: about 90 minutes to 2 hours - Landing, bags, and getting to the centre at the far end: 50 to 100 minutes - Total overhead, before you count the time in the air: roughly 3.5 to 4 hours

    By train - Getting to the station: 15 to 30 minutes - Arriving before departure: 20 minutes to an hour (see below) - Walking out and onward: 15 to 20 minutes - Total overhead, before you count the time on board: roughly 1 to 1.5 hours

    The difference is about two and a half to three hours in the train’s favour. That is why the rule of thumb works: a train beats a flight when its time on board is less than the flight’s time in the air plus roughly two and a half to three hours. For a flight of one to two hours, that puts the break-even at about 3.5 to 5 hours of train. It sorts options quickly; it is not a promise. An airport close to both ends narrows the gap and one on the far edge of a city widens it.

    Two facts from the operators help calibrate the overhead:

    • Rail check-in is short on some routes. Eurostar asks passengers on its Paris to Brussels service to arrive 20 minutes before departure. London departures involve border and security checks at St Pancras, and Eurostar tells passengers to arrive at its recommended time to allow for them, so allow longer there.
    • Airline bag drop is stricter than it looks. Ryanair’s help centre says its check-in and bag drop desks open at the latest two hours before departure and close 40 minutes before. A Ryanair press release dated 27 October 2026 says that from 10 November 2026 bag drop at all its airports closes 60 minutes before departure. Passengers with only a small cabin bag who have checked in online go straight to security.

    Luggage: where the train is generous

    Luggage is where rail is most often kinder than a budget flight.

    • Eurostar includes two pieces of luggage and one small hand item on a standard ticket. There is no strict weight limit per bag: if you can carry it safely yourself, you can take it. The length limit is 85 cm on routes to and from London and 75 cm on its other routes.
    • Ryanair says every fare includes a small personal bag that must fit under the seat in front of you. For anything more you choose an option and pay for it, and prices vary by route and date. Check the live booking page for the amounts on your flight.

    Other train operators have their own rules and some limit the number or size of bags, so check the operator for your route. With a suitcase, a pair of bags or a pushchair, the bag fees can outweigh a small difference in ticket price, and there is no bag-drop deadline on a train. Eurostar also says there is no volume limit on liquids on its London to Paris service, so you can pack toiletries without airport-style limits; if you will fly on other legs of the trip, see our airport security liquids guide.

    Price: compare the whole bill

    We have left fares out of the table because both rail and air prices move with the date and how early you book. Price the same date on both, then add what a headline fare leaves out. For the flight: the bag, any seat charge, and the transfers at both ends. For the train: any compulsory seat reservation and the restrictions of the ticket type.

    You can compare train and coach options for most European routes on Omio, and flights on Kiwi.com or Trip.com. They are comparison tools: confirm the final price and conditions at checkout. We may earn a commission if you book through them, at no cost to you; it does not change what we write.

    If the train is late: EU rail passenger rights

    Regulation (EU) 2021/782 sets passenger rights across EU rail services. The EU’s Your Europe page summarises them. The key numbers:

    Delay at your final destination What you can claim
    60 minutes or more Refund, or rerouting at the earliest opportunity or a later date at no extra cost; meals and refreshments in proportion to the wait; a hotel if you need a night, where possible
    60 to 119 minutes Compensation of 25% of the ticket price
    120 minutes or more Compensation of 50% of the ticket price

    A few details from the same page:

    • The railway must pay compensation within one month of your request.
    • If the railway has not told you about rerouting options within 100 minutes of the scheduled departure, you can book other public transport yourself, and the railway must reimburse the necessary, appropriate and reasonable cost.
    • There is no compensation if you were told about the delay before you bought your ticket, if you chose a refund, or if the cause was extraordinary circumstances such as extreme weather. The page says strikes by railway staff do not count as extraordinary circumstances, and refund, rerouting and assistance still apply in those cases.
    • A missed connection counts on the same thresholds only if you hold a through-ticket, and some national rules or railway terms may be more favourable.

    The page is about rail services under EU rules. If your journey starts or ends outside the EU, such as London to Paris, check the operator’s own terms and the rules that apply to your ticket. Keep your ticket and note the actual arrival time.

    France’s ban on short domestic flights

    France has a rule that blocks scheduled domestic flights when the same journey can be made by train in under two and a half hours. It came out of the work of France’s Citizens’ Convention for Climate and was put into effect by decree No. 2023-385 of 22 May 2023, according to the transport ministry’s press release. The transport minister’s press release of 23 May 2023 said the immediate effect was to ban flights between Nantes, Bordeaux, Lyon and Paris-Orly, because a train service makes those trips in under two and a half hours.

    The French ecology ministry maintains a page listing the routes that may be banned each season. Its entry, updated on 14 April 2026, names these routes for the summer 2026 season (31 March to 30 October 2026): Nantes, Lyon, Bordeaux, Rennes, Strasbourg, Lille and Reims to Paris-Orly, and Lille to Paris-Charles de Gaulle. The ministry calls these routes liable to be banned, so check its page for the current season’s list.

    For a visitor the practical effect is small: the rule covers domestic French routes, not international ones, and the list is short. What it shows is that the French government treats a train journey of under two and a half hours as a reasonable substitute for a short flight.

    Interrail and Eurail passes: the reservation-fee watch-out

    A rail pass does not always cover the full cost of using a fast train.

    • Interrail says seat reservations are mandatory for most high-speed trains and all night trains in Europe, and that they are charged by the railway companies and not included in the pass.
    • Eurail publishes an indicative table of international reservation fees and says the figures are subject to change. On the day we read it (1 November 2026) it listed Paris to London on Eurostar as mandatory at €35 in second class and €40 in first, and Frecciarossa on Paris to Turin or Milan as mandatory at €13.

    Whether a pass saves money depends on how many reservation-only trains you take; our Europe rail pass guide works through when it pays back.

    When the plane still wins

    • Long legs. Beyond roughly five hours of train time, the overhead you save no longer covers the extra hours on board.
    • Airports close to both ends. An airport 15 minutes from your door and another 15 minutes from your destination shrinks the plane’s overhead.
    • No fast train. Some routes have no direct high-speed service, and a slow regional train with changes does not compete.
    • Price on the day. Sometimes the flight is cheaper even after the bag. We say so when it is.
    • Onward flights on one booking. If the short leg is part of a longer journey on a single airline booking, check the airline’s conditions before splitting it into separate tickets.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is the train faster than flying in Europe?

    Often, when the train takes up to about four hours. Counting the whole journey, a flight carries roughly 3.5 to 4 hours of overhead (getting to the airport, arriving early, landing, getting to the centre) against about 1 to 1.5 hours for a train. That is our own arithmetic, not an operator figure. Between about 4 and 5 hours of train time the answer depends on how far your start and end points are from the airports; beyond that, flying usually wins on time.

    How long do the main European high-speed routes take by train?

    Scheduled times we read on operator sites: Paris to Brussels 1 hour 22 minutes (Eurostar), Frankfurt to Cologne 1 hour 5 minutes (Deutsche Bahn), Paris to Lyon 1 hour 56 minutes on the TGV INOUI services listed for 2 November 2026, London to Paris 2 hours 16 minutes (Eurostar), Milan to Rome 2 hours 50 minutes non-stop (Trenitalia), Frankfurt to Amsterdam 3 hours 55 minutes and Vienna to Munich about 4 hours 15 minutes on a direct Railjet Express. Madrid to Barcelona is roughly 2.5 to just over 3 hours depending on the train. Check a live timetable for your date.

    Is luggage easier on a train than a budget flight?

    Usually. Eurostar includes two pieces of luggage and one small hand item with no strict weight limit; the length limit is 85 cm on London routes and 75 cm on its others. Ryanair says every fare includes a small personal bag that must fit under the seat, and larger cabin or hold bags are paid options whose prices vary by route and date. Other rail operators have their own rules, so check yours.

    What compensation do I get if a European train is late?

    Under Regulation (EU) 2021/782, as summarised on the EU's Your Europe site, a delay of 1 to 2 hours at your final destination earns 25% of the ticket price and 2 hours or more earns 50%. From 60 minutes you can also have a refund or rerouting and meals or accommodation in proportion to the wait. There is no compensation for extraordinary circumstances such as extreme weather, although refund, rerouting and assistance still apply. Check the operator's terms for journeys that start or end outside the EU.

    Does France ban short domestic flights?

    France bans scheduled domestic flights where a train makes the same journey in under 2 hours 30 minutes, under decree No. 2023-385 of 22 May 2023. The French ecology ministry publishes the list of routes liable to be banned each season; for summer 2026 it names Nantes, Lyon, Bordeaux, Rennes, Strasbourg, Lille and Reims to Paris-Orly, and Lille to Paris-Charles de Gaulle. The rule covers domestic routes, not international ones.

    Do Interrail and Eurail passes cover seat reservations?

    No. Interrail says reservations are mandatory on most high-speed trains and all night trains in Europe, are charged by the railway companies and are not included in the pass. Eurail's indicative table, read on 1 November 2026, lists Paris to London on Eurostar as mandatory at 35 euros in second class and 40 in first, and Frecciarossa on Paris to Turin or Milan at 13 euros. Fees change, so check before you travel.
