Hotels around Innere Stadt, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Innere Stadt that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

Vienna’s hotel bill changed this summer. Since 1 July 2026 the city tax, the Ortstaxe, is 5% of the price of the stay, up from 3.2% of a reduced price, and on 1 July 2027 it rises again, to 8%. The way in from the airport has changed too: a central stretch of the city’s S-Bahn line is closed until the end of October 2027, so the airport express now runs as a bus. Where you stay decides how much of the city you see on foot and how often you need the U-Bahn.

This guide covers five areas, from the Innere Stadt to Neubau, 12 hotels, the airport links and the tax. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

Vienna’s city tax in 2026

Every place to stay in the city, from a hotel to a holiday flat or a campsite, collects the Ortstaxe and passes it to the city. It is a percentage of what you pay for the stay itself, not counting VAT and breakfast, and it does not depend on the number of guests or the star rating:

Period Ortstaxe
Until 30 June 2026 3.2% of the price of the stay, after a flat 11% deduction
1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027 5% of the price of the stay
From 1 July 2027 8% of the price of the stay

VAT on accommodation in Austria is 10%, and it is normally already in the rate you see. The exemptions are narrow: they cover minors staying for school or training or in youth hostels, students at Vienna’s universities and colleges, and stays of more than three months.

Our arithmetic: a room priced at €220 a night including VAT, room only, is €200 before VAT, so the tax is €10 a night, or €30 for three nights. Book the same stay for July 2027 and the tax is €16 a night. Many hotels quote it on top; Hotel Daniel Vienna, for one, publishes its rates with the city tax excluded.

Getting in from the airport, and around

The S-Bahn’s central section between Wien Praterstern and Wien Hauptbahnhof is closed for modernisation until the end of October 2027, and trains do not stop at Rennweg or Wien Mitte/Landstraße. That changes the usual routes:

  • CAT by bus. The City Airport Train runs as a non-stop premium bus between the airport and Wien Mitte/Landstraße, 21 minutes, every 12 minutes between 7 am and 7 pm and every 15 minutes at other times. A single costs €14.90 and a return €24.90; CAT tickets bought online are valid for six months. In town the stop is at Marxergasse 1b.
  • ÖBB Railjet and InterCity. Twice an hour, non-stop from the airport to Wien Hauptbahnhof in about 15 minutes, or to Wien Meidling in just under 30.
  • REX 7 and S7. The REX 7 runs to Wien Hauptbahnhof in around 20 minutes. The S7 now stops short at St. Marx, outside the centre, where trams 18 and 71 and bus 74A continue.
  • The train fare for the Railjet, REX 7 or S7 is €5.50: €2.30 from the airport to the city boundary plus €3.20 within Vienna. With a Vienna City Card or a multi-day ticket, you need only the €2.30 ticket.
  • In town, a single ticket is €3.20 and is valid for 80 minutes from validation, with changes; a 24-hour ticket is €10.20, and a 7-day ticket €25.20 digital or €28.90 on paper. Validate a paper ticket before you travel, in the blue machine at the station entrance or on board trams and buses. For 48 or 72 hours, the Vienna City Card includes public transport, at €31 and €37 in its digital version.

The Innere Stadt: the first-trip base

The 1st district is the old city inside the Ringstraße: St Stephen’s Cathedral, the Graben and Kärntner Straße, the Hofburg, the State Opera and the Albertina. Most of what a first visit is for is a walk away, and Stephansplatz is on the U1 and U3. It is also where rooms cost most, and the streets around the cathedral are busy from morning to late.

Where to stay in the Innere Stadt

Rooms start with the Deluxe category, 27 to 39 m². Choose it for the classic Vienna hotel at the opera’s door. Doubles start at €135 a night with breakfast, on the hotel’s own published rates. Choose it for the old town without the old-town price. Leopoldstadt: across the canal

Leopoldstadt sits between the Danube Canal and the Danube, across the water from the old town. Its near end, around Taborstraße and Praterstraße, is a few minutes’ walk from Schwedenplatz and the cathedral; further out are the Karmelitermarkt, the Augarten and the Prater. It is quieter at night than the 1st district and usually cheaper.

Where to stay in Leopoldstadt

  • SO/ Vienna, splurge. A tower by the architect Jean Nouvel at the start of Praterstraße, with 182 rooms and suites on floors 6 to 17, every one with a view over the rooftops, the LOFT restaurant and bar with 360° views, and a spa with sauna, steam bath and gym included. Choose it for Vienna seen from above. Watch out: the entry Classic room, 29 m², faces the Ferris wheel; the cathedral view starts with the Deluxe room. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Stefanie, comfortable. On Taborstraße, it calls itself Vienna’s oldest hotel, dating back to 1600, with 111 renovated rooms and suites, a restaurant and a courtyard garden. Schwedenplatz is about 300 metres away and the Danube Canal three minutes’ walk. Choose it for history a short walk from the old town. Watch out: St Stephen’s is a 10 to 12-minute walk and Kärntner Straße about 20; parking in the hotel garage is €31 a night. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • MEININGER Hotel Vienna Downtown Franz, budget. Between the Augarten and the Danube Canal, with 131 rooms and 487 beds in dormitories and private rooms, a guest kitchen and a bar. Choose it for a bed near the centre at hostel prices. Watch out: St Stephen’s Cathedral and the Hofburg are about 20 minutes on foot. Check rates on Booking.com →

Landstraße: the Belvedere and the main station

The 3rd district runs from the Stadtpark south to the Belvedere, the baroque palace that holds the Klimt collection, and its gardens beside the Botanical Garden. The embassy streets around Rennweg are calm, and the southern edge is close to Wien Hauptbahnhof, for the Railjet to the airport and trains across Europe.

Where to stay in Landstraße

  • Austria Trend Hotel Savoyen Vienna, comfortable. On Rennweg, in the revitalised building of the former imperial state and court print works behind a protected façade, with a six-floor atrium, a gym and a wellness area. Superior rooms are 32 to 37 m², and the Belvedere and the Botanical Garden are close by. Choose it for space near the palace. Watch out: it is also a big conference hotel, with what it calls the largest hotel ballroom in Vienna, 1,056 m². Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Daniel Vienna, good value. On Landstraßer Gürtel, with the Belvedere as its neighbour and Wien Hauptbahnhof a 10-minute walk away; 116 rooms plus a garden trailer, a bakery, beehives and an urban garden on the 7th floor. Smart rooms, with a 160 cm bed, start at €114 a night, city tax excluded. Choose it for the airport train and the palace at a fair price. Watch out: the Belvedere room, 40 m² with the palace view, starts at €260, more than twice the Smart room. Check rates on Booking.com →

Wieden and the Naschmarkt: markets and theatres

Just south of the Opera, the Naschmarkt runs along the Wienzeile between Wieden in the 4th district and Mariahilf in the 6th, with Karlsplatz, St Charles Church, the Secession and the Theater an der Wien at its northern end. It is a lively base for eating out, the U4 along it runs straight to Schönbrunn, and the Ringstraße is a short walk.

Where to stay by the Naschmarkt

  • Hotel Beethoven Wien, comfortable. A family-run four-star on Papagenogasse, opposite the Papageno Gate of the Theater an der Wien, with 47 rooms in four categories, the Lvdwig bar, and free coffee and tea all day in the lounge, with Viennese pastries from 2 to 5 pm. Salon rooms of about 30 m² look at the theatre or the Naschmarkt. Choose it for the theatres and the market at the door. Watch out: the smallest category, Papageno, is about 12 m². Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Wombat’s City Hostel Vienna, budget. On Rechte Wienzeile next to the Naschmarkt and the U4 station Kettenbrückengasse, with dormitories and private rooms, guest kitchens and its own bar. Choose it for the cheapest bed close to the centre. Watch out: the minimum age is 18 unless a guardian books a private room, and breakfast costs extra.

Neubau and the MuseumsQuartier: museums and bars

The 7th district starts at the MuseumsQuartier, home to the Leopold Museum, the mumok and the Kunsthalle Wien, and runs west through the cobbled lanes of the Spittelberg, with Mariahilfer Straße, the city’s long shopping street, along its southern edge. It has the independent shops, cafés and bars, and the Ringstraße museums are across the road.

Where to stay in Neubau

  • 25hours Hotel Vienna at MuseumsQuartier, comfortable. A circus-themed hotel by the Weghuberpark, with the Dachboden bar and its roof terrace view, RIBELLI for pizza, and a 250 m² sauna and fitness area. Choose it for a lively hotel a few steps from the museums. Watch out: Medium rooms are 17 to 22 m² and face the courtyard or Lerchenfelder Straße, and the balcony bathtubs of the Extra Large rooms are not in use in winter. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Altstadt Vienna, comfortable. A boutique hotel on the Spittelberg, opened in 1991 by Otto E. Wiesenthal in an old patrician house, with 62 rooms and suites and a collection of contemporary art that includes Warhol. Choose it for character and art in the 7th. Watch out: no two rooms are alike, so check the category you are booking, not only the gallery. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel am Brillantengrund, good value. A family hotel with 34 rooms, each furnished from the 1950s to the 1970s, around a palm-lined courtyard, with a Filipino kitchen and the MuseumsQuartier five minutes’ walk away. Doubles start at €109 with an à la carte breakfast. Choose it for design and a fair price. Watch out: the compact rooms are about 14 to 16 m². Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Vienna

  • Count the tax. Add 5% of the room price before VAT, breakfast not included: €10 a night on a €220 room. For stays from 1 July 2027 it is 8%, so price a trip next summer at the higher rate.
  • Plan around the S-Bahn closure. Until the end of October 2027, the CAT bus ends at Wien Mitte/Landstraße and the Railjet and REX 7 at Wien Hauptbahnhof; a hotel near one of them saves a change with luggage.
  • Check the room size. Old-city rooms can be small: Hotel Beethoven’s Papageno rooms are about 12 m², Kärntnerhof’s cheapest doubles 15 m². If you need space, Hotel Savoyen’s Superior rooms are 32 to 37 m².
  • Book the view by name. At Hotel Sacher choose a room “with View”, at SO/ Vienna a Deluxe room for the cathedral, and at Hotel Daniel the Belvedere room.
  • Compare totals, and check direct rates. Hotel booking sites compared and booking direct or through a booking site go through where to book.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Vienna for a first trip?

The Innere Stadt, the 1st district inside the Ringstraße: St Stephen's Cathedral, the State Opera and the Hofburg are a walk away. Choose Leopoldstadt for a calmer base across the Danube Canal, Landstraße for the Belvedere and the main station, the Naschmarkt for markets and theatres, and Neubau for the MuseumsQuartier and bars.

How much is the city tax in Vienna?

From 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027 the Ortstaxe is 5% of the price of the stay, not counting VAT and breakfast; from 1 July 2027 it is 8%. On a room priced at €220 a night including VAT, room only, that is €10 a night now and €16 a night from July 2027.

Is the City Airport Train running in 2026?

It runs as a bus. While the central S-Bahn section through Vienna is closed, until the end of October 2027, the CAT runs non-stop premium buses between the airport and Wien Mitte/Landstraße in 21 minutes, every 12 minutes between 7 am and 7 pm and every 15 minutes at other times. A single costs €14.90 and a return €24.90.

What is the cheapest way from Vienna airport to the city centre?

The train. An ÖBB Railjet runs non-stop to Wien Hauptbahnhof in about 15 minutes and the REX 7 in around 20; the fare is €5.50, made up of €2.30 to the city boundary and €3.20 within Vienna. The S7 currently ends at St. Marx, outside the centre.

How much is a public transport ticket in Vienna?

A single ticket is €3.20 and is valid for 80 minutes from validation, with changes. A 24-hour ticket is €10.20, and a 7-day ticket €25.20 digital or €28.90 on paper. For 48 or 72 hours, the digital Vienna City Card includes public transport, at €31 and €37.

Is the Vienna city tax included in hotel prices?

Often not. The tax is worked out on the price of the stay before VAT and breakfast, and many hotels add it on top: Hotel Daniel Vienna, for one, publishes its rates with the city tax excluded. Compare totals before you book.
