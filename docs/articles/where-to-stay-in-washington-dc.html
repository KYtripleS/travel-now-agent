The map shows the hotels and apartments around Penn Quarter that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

Washington’s hotel tax is 15.95% of the room rate, set by the city’s Office of Tax and Revenue, and on a $300 room that is $47.85 a night before you have left the lobby. The museums that fill most visits cost far less: the Smithsonian states that admission is free at all its locations except one in New York. So the room, and how far it is from a Metro station, is where the money goes.

This guide covers five areas, from Penn Quarter to Capitol Hill, 12 hotels, the airport links and the tax. Washington’s hotel tax

The city’s Office of Tax and Revenue lists one rate for rooms, lodgings and accommodations, and it is well above the general sales tax:

Charge Rate
Sales and use tax on hotel rooms and other transient lodging 15.95% of the room charge
General sales tax, for comparison 6%
Parking or storing a vehicle, if you drive 18%

On a $300 room, 15.95% is $47.85, so the room costs $347.85 a night. That is our arithmetic, not a figure from the city. We found no separate per-night city charge on the Office of Tax and Revenue’s pages, but a hotel can add its own fees, so compare the total.

One date matters if you book far ahead. The Office of Tax and Revenue’s notice sets the 15.95% rate for April 1, 2023 to March 30, 2027, raised from 14.95%. For a stay after March 2027, check the office’s current rate before you rely on this number.

Getting in from the airports, and around

  • From Reagan National (DCA), the airport’s Metrorail station is on the Yellow and Blue lines. Terminal 2 connects to it by two enclosed bridges on the concourse level; from Terminal 1, take an airport shuttle bus to the bridges. Weekday fares run from $2.25 to $6.75 depending on distance, and WMATA’s trip planner gives the exact figure.
  • From Dulles (IAD), the Silver Line station is about a 600-foot walk from the terminal. The airport states a weekday-daytime fare to Metro Center of $6.75, and a ride of roughly 50 to 55 minutes to Rosslyn, in Virginia.
  • From BWI, a free shuttle runs from the terminal to the BWI Rail Station, and from there a MARC Penn Line or Amtrak train goes to Washington Union Station, about 30 minutes by Amtrak and 35 by MARC, according to the airport. The MARC one-way fare from BWI Airport to Washington is $8 on the Maryland Transit Administration’s Penn Line chart, dated June 2021, which is the chart the agency still links. Union Station has a Red Line Metro station.
  • In town, Metrorail fares are $2.25 to $6.75 on weekdays between 5 am and 9:30 pm, and $2.25 to $2.50 after 9:30 pm and at weekends. Buses are $2.25, with a $2.25 discount on a transfer between bus and rail within two hours. Tap a SmarTrip card or a contactless card at the gate.

Downtown and Penn Quarter: the first-trip base

Downtown, Penn Quarter and Chinatown are the part of the city closest to the White House, Capital One Arena and the National Mall; the hotels below describe themselves as steps or a walk away, and Moxy names a Metro station within walking distance. The Smithsonian’s museums charge no admission, although three locations need a free pass or ticket: the National Museum of African American History and Culture, the National Air and Space Museum in Washington, and the National Zoo. For a first trip it is the most convenient base.

The Wharf and Southwest: evenings on the water

The Wharf is a waterfront district on the Southwest shoreline, with restaurants, a mile of riverside walk, a free SW Shuttle and a water taxi to Georgetown, which the Georgetown business district says takes 25 minutes, with up to six departures a day. citizenM, just inland, says the L’Enfant Plaza Metro station is right by its door and that the Wharf and the National Mall are minutes away.

Dupont Circle and Foggy Bottom: restaurants and the Metro

Dupont Circle is a neighbourhood that one local hotel describes as plugged into the city’s diplomatic, business and academic life, with the Dupont Circle Metro station at its centre. Foggy Bottom, to the south-west, is the district around George Washington University, with its own station, Foggy Bottom-GWU, on the Blue, Orange and Silver lines. Between them they suit visitors who want a Metro station close and a choice of restaurants on foot.

Georgetown: historic streets and the river

Georgetown is Washington’s historic waterfront neighbourhood, with M Street’s shops and restaurants, Washington Harbour, Georgetown University and the Kennedy Center nearby. It has no Metro station of its own: the Georgetown business district points visitors to Foggy Bottom-GWU, a 15-minute walk, and to Rosslyn, across Key Bridge, and says several Metrobus lines serve the area. Choose it if you would rather walk the streets than ride the train.

Where to stay in Georgetown

  • Rosewood Washington, D.C., splurge. In Georgetown, with CUT by Wolfgang Puck, the CUT Above rooftop bar and historic townhouse suites next to the hotel. Choose it for a luxury stay in a historic neighbourhood. Watch out: the rooftop bar has DJs on Friday nights. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • The Graham Georgetown, comfortable. A boutique hotel where M Street is steps from the door, with a rooftop bar and The Alex Craft Cocktail Cellar & Speakeasy. Choose it for a hotel in the middle of Georgetown’s shopping streets. Watch out: the nearest Metro station, Foggy Bottom-GWU, is a 15-minute walk. Check rates on Booking.com →

Capitol Hill and Navy Yard: the Capitol and a quieter evening

Capitol Hill is the district around the Capitol and the Supreme Court, where the Capitol Hill Hotel occupies 19th-century row houses. Farther south, Cambria describes its address as the Navy Yard Riverfront and points guests to Nationals baseball and to soccer at Audi Field. Both suit visitors who plan to spend their days at the Capitol and on the Mall.

Where to stay in Capitol Hill and Navy Yard

  • Capitol Hill Hotel, comfortable. At 200 C Street SE, in 19th-century row houses, steps from the U.S. Capitol and the Supreme Court, and newly reimagined. Choose it to be on Capitol Hill itself. Watch out: parking is extra; the hotel’s Park & Stay package values daily parking at $50. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Cambria Hotel Washington D.C. Navy Yard Riverfront, good value. At 69 Q Street SW, with 154 accommodations, the RowHouse restaurant and a rooftop lounge; the hotel points to Nationals baseball and Audi Field. Choose it for a modern hotel at a lower price near the ballparks. Watch out: valet parking is $58 a night. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Washington

  • Count the tax. Add 15.95% to every room rate: a $300 room costs $347.85. A hotel’s own fees come on top, so check the total.
  • Count the parking. If you drive, the hotels here charge for it and the city adds 18% tax on parking: $78 a night at the Willard, $58 at Cambria and up to $47.20 at Hotel Rendale. citizenM has none on site.
  • Check the Metro. Georgetown has no station of its own. Look at the walk from a hotel to a station before you pay for the location.
  • Check the room size. Hotel Hive’s rooms start at 125 sq ft, and The Dupont Circle’s start at 300. If you need space, book the category above the cheapest.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Washington, DC for a first trip?

Downtown and Penn Quarter. The hotels there describe themselves as steps or a walk from the White House, Capital One Arena and the National Mall, and the Smithsonian's museums charge no admission. Choose the Wharf for the waterfront, Dupont Circle and Foggy Bottom for a Metro station close by, Georgetown for historic streets and Capitol Hill for the Capitol.

How much is the hotel tax in Washington, DC?

15.95% of the room rate, according to the city's Office of Tax and Revenue: on a $300 room that is $47.85 a night, our arithmetic. The office's notice sets that rate for April 1, 2023 to March 30, 2027, so check its current rate for a later stay. Parking carries a separate 18% tax.

How do you get from Dulles Airport to downtown Washington?

Take the Metro Silver Line from the station, about a 600-foot walk from the terminal. The airport states a weekday-daytime fare to Metro Center of $6.75, and a ride of roughly 50 to 55 minutes to Rosslyn, in Virginia.

Are the Smithsonian museums free?

Yes. The Smithsonian states that admission is free at all its locations except Cooper Hewitt in New York City. Three locations need a free pass or ticket: the National Museum of African American History and Culture, the National Air and Space Museum in Washington, and the National Zoo.

Is Georgetown on the Metro?

No station of its own. The Georgetown business district points visitors to Foggy Bottom-GWU, a 15-minute walk, and to Rosslyn, across Key Bridge, and says several Metrobus lines serve the area. A water taxi also runs between the Wharf and Georgetown, 25 minutes, with up to six departures a day.

How do you get from BWI to Washington, DC?

Take the free shuttle from the terminal to the BWI Rail Station, then a MARC Penn Line or Amtrak train to Washington Union Station: about 30 minutes by Amtrak and 35 by MARC, according to the airport. The MARC one-way fare is $8 on the Maryland Transit Administration's Penn Line chart, dated June 2021. Union Station has a Red Line Metro station.
