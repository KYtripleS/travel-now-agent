How to read it:

  • The reseller listings are packages, not the bare ticket. Each standard listing bundles the official entry with its own audio guide app and booking fees, and is non-refundable.
  • What the package adds is the audio guide app, not a different entry. Neither listing says it shortens a queue at the gate, and the Colosseum’s own rules (below) set the entry time for everyone.
  • Tiqets showed two prices for the same day. The headline read “From €29.00”, but once the timeslot list opened, slots were priced from €42 (12:00 to 13:30) up to €70 (12:45). We could not tell from the page what separates them, so treat €29.00 as a starting point and check the total at checkout.
  • GetYourGuide’s price is a “from” price too. It read “From €26 per person” for one adult on 14 December. Its listing says the price shown includes the audio guide and booking fees.
  • Viator was read but not priced. Its Rome listings are a mix of guided tours and packages shown in US dollars on the page, and the one self-guided listing we opened is worded ambiguously about whether the Colosseum ticket is included. Rather than print a number we could not match to the official ticket, we left it out.
  • The Tiqets arena listing is not the official Full Experience. It sends you to a Touristation office to redeem a voucher and watch a video, lists the arena floor, and says it does not include access to the Colosseum’s first and second levels. At €52 against €24 official, treat it as a different product.

What each seller’s listing says

Official: Parco archeologico del Colosseo

The official ticketing site says you may buy tickets online exclusively on its own page, and it invites reports of “secondary ticketing”, the sale of tickets on unauthorised channels at increased prices. The standard ticket (listed at €18) includes admission to the Colosseum at the booked time, the Roman Forum and Palatine area, and any current exhibitions. The park’s ticket page says the Colosseum visit lasts 75 minutes on this ticket type, that you should arrive 15 minutes before your slot, and that you may use the Forum and Palatine “within 24 hours before or after the booking time at the Colosseum”.

The listing we read, “Colosseum, Forum, Palatine Hill Entry & Audioguide App”, is badged “Official ticket” and carries a “Likely to sell out” tag. It says: “This activity is non-refundable”. Its small print says access to the Colosseum is only at your scheduled time and “rescheduling is not possible”, that tickets are nominative so you must enter each participant’s name or you will not get in, and that bookings must be made 3 days before the visit because of the name requirement. The Colosseum’s own rules that change what you buy

These come from the Parco’s own pages, read on 16 November 2026.

  • Timed entry is compulsory. The park says ticket sales, “with compulsory reservation of the time slot for entry to the Colosseum”, open 30 days before the date of the visit. On 16 November the calendar for December ended on 16 December. So you cannot buy a date further out than a month, from any seller that sells the official entry.
  • Tickets are in the holder’s name. The park’s notice: “Tickets are issued in the holder’s name”, and an identification document must be kept visible at the entrances with the ticket.
  • Name changes are tightly limited. For visits from 9 May, the holder’s name may be changed only once and only by midnight on the seventh day before the visit, and only for a clerical error, a wrong ticket type within the same booking, or serious documented reasons. This is why the non-refundable reseller listings matter: if your plans move, you may lose the ticket.
  • The Forum and Palatine are flexible, the Colosseum is not. You choose one Colosseum time and may visit the Forum and Palatine at the time you prefer within 24 hours before or after.
  • Opening hours. From 25 October 2026 to 28 February 2027 the park is open 8:30 to 16:30, with last admission at 15:30. It is closed on 25 December 2026 and 1 January 2027.
  • Free admission days. The park lists free admission on the first Sunday of the month, and on 25 April, 2 June and 4 November. The Forum Pass SUPER open-date ticket is not valid on those free days.
  • Sell-outs. On the day we looked, two early slots on 14 December were shown as closed or sold out and one afternoon slot had 7 places left, so the popular times fill first. We have no longer-run data, so we do not state a pattern.
  • Child ages. We could not read the official child and reduced-price rules. GetYourGuide’s listing says the ticket for visitors under 18 is free but its own price is for the package with the audio guide and booking fees. We therefore state no child rules here.

Which seller for which trip

  • Self-guided, with an audio guide app: GetYourGuide. Its listing is badged “Official ticket”, starts from €26 and bundles the app and booking fees. Tiqets is the alternative, from €29.00, also with an app.
  • A guided tour: GetYourGuide or Viator. Guides are sold by the partners, often with free cancellation on the listings we saw on Viator.
  • Plans that may move: a guided tour with free cancellation. The self-guided listings we read on GetYourGuide, Tiqets and Viator are non-refundable and cannot be rescheduled.

Five checks before you pay

  1. Date inside 30 days. The official sales window is one month.
  2. Name and ID. Enter every traveller’s name exactly as on their ID.
  3. Which ticket is it? Standard, arena, underground and attic are different tickets.
  4. Refund terms. The reseller listings we read were non-refundable.
  5. The total at checkout, in your own currency.

Frequently asked questions

Where should I buy Colosseum tickets?

GetYourGuide's "Colosseum, Forum, Palatine Hill Entry & Audioguide App" listing, which is badged "Official ticket" and was "from €26" on 16 November 2026 for a Monday 14 December visit, with an audio guide app included and non-refundable. Tiqets showed "from €29.00" on the same terms. For a guided tour use GetYourGuide or Viator; the Viator guided tours we saw had free cancellation. Viator's self-guided listing was not priced and Klook was not checked. Prices change daily.

How far ahead can I buy?

The park says ticket sales open 30 days before the date of the visit, with a compulsory reservation of the Colosseum time slot. On 16 November the December calendar ended on 16 December.

Do I need to give names and ID?

Yes. The park says tickets are issued in the holder's name and that an identification document must be kept visible with the ticket at the entrances. GetYourGuide says to enter each participant's name or entry is refused.

Can I change the name or the date?

The park allows one name change, by midnight on the seventh day before the visit for visits from 9 May, in limited cases. The GetYourGuide, Tiqets and Viator self-guided listings we read say rescheduling is not possible or that the ticket is non-refundable; the Viator guided tours we saw offered free cancellation.

Is the Colosseum ever free?

The park lists free admission on the first Sunday of the month, and on 25 April, 2 June and 4 November. We did not read how free days are booked.

Do I need to visit the Forum and Palatine on the same day?

No. The standard ticket lets you visit the Forum and Palatine within 24 hours before or after your Colosseum time.
