Hotels around Queenstown town centre, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Queenstown town centre that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

A Queenstown hotel bill is simple: New Zealand’s 15% goods and services tax (GST) and, in October 2026, no local visitor levy. The charge that visitors do pay is not on the hotel bill at all: the NZ$100 International Visitor Conservation and Tourism Levy, collected with your travel authority before you fly. What decides the cost of a stay is the season, because the town fills in the southern summer and again in ski season, and the area, because the hotels sit in several places around the lake.

This guide covers four areas, from the lakefront to Arrowtown, 12 hotels, the airport bus and the charges. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

What a Queenstown hotel bill adds

Charge Amount Where it appears
Goods and services tax (GST) 15% On the hotel bill
Queenstown visitor levy None charged in October 2026 Not on the bill
International Visitor Conservation and Tourism Levy (IVL) NZ$100 a person Paid with your NZeTA or visa, before you travel

GST. Inland Revenue sets the rate at 15%. Check whether a rate is shown with GST included or added. If a NZ$300 room is quoted without GST, the bill is NZ$345. If the NZ$300 already includes GST, Inland Revenue’s method puts the GST at 3/23 of the price, NZ$39.13 of the NZ$300. This is our arithmetic, not a hotel’s quote.

The visitor levy. Queenstown Lakes District Council has proposed a levy on short-term visitor accommodation, backed by 81.37% of those who voted in a 2019 postal poll. The council lists the project as on hold, it has not been introduced and nothing is added to a hotel bill for it. In August 2026 the National Party said it would not introduce a bed-night levy.

The IVL. Most international visitors pay NZ$100 a person when they apply for a visa or an NZeTA, according to the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment. Australian citizens and permanent residents, and people from most Pacific islands, are among those exempt.

Getting in from Queenstown Airport, and around

  • From the airport, the Orbus public bus stops outside the terminal and runs to Frankton and the town centre. The airport says buses generally run every 15 minutes in the day, and every 30 minutes in the evening. The Otago Regional Council fare is NZ$2.50 for an adult with a registered Bee Card or NZ$10 in cash; children and youths pay NZ$1.50 with a Bee Card or NZ$8 in cash.
  • The Bee Card costs NZ$5, with a minimum top-up of NZ$5, and is sold online, at the PaperPlus store in the terminal and at the council’s Frankton office. One free transfer is allowed within 45 minutes if you tag on and off both buses. Buy a card in advance if your flight lands late.
  • By taxi, rideshare or shuttle, the airport lists taxis and Uber at pick-up zones outside the terminal, and pre-booked shared and private shuttles to Queenstown, Wānaka and elsewhere. It publishes no fares, and says times and fares depend on traffic and destination.
  • Distance: the airport is 8 km from the town centre, a drive of 10 to 15 minutes along Frankton Road, according to Queenstown’s regional tourism organisation.
  • In town, any bus journey in the Queenstown network is one zone: NZ$2.50 for an adult with a Bee Card, NZ$4 in cash. Frankton’s bus hub links to other routes, including Jacks Point and Arrowtown.
  • To Arrowtown, a bus runs every half hour, seven days a week, according to the same tourism organisation.

The town centre: the first-trip base

Most hotels, attractions and restaurants in the centre are within about ten minutes on foot of the lakefront, and it is where the adventure operators and the nightlife are. It is also the busiest and, on weekends, the noisiest part of town.

Fernhill: views from above town

The slope above the centre, around Fernhill Road and Sainsbury Road, trades the walk for height. Hotels here look over Lake Wakatipu and The Remarkables, and the tourism organisation describes the area as one of elevated views and peaceful surroundings.

Where to stay in Fernhill

  • Kamana Lakehouse, splurge. At 139 Fernhill Road, with Nest Restaurant, six room categories from the Kamana King to the Lakeview Luxe, soak tubs, complimentary parking and a ski storage and dry room. Choose it for lake views and a restaurant in the building. Watch out: the hotel says neighbouring development work began in April 2024, with noise from Monday to Saturday, 8am to 6pm, and that the Lakeview Luxe and Tahuna Lakeview views may be affected at times. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Mövenpick Queenstown, comfortable. At 21 Sainsbury Road, with 148 rooms, each with a lake or garden outlook, a buffet breakfast restaurant, South Eatery, a lounge bar with an open fire, and a seasonal pool, spa pools and sauna. Choose it for a large hotel with views. Watch out: parking is paid, and the Garden View rooms are 24 m² against 28 m² for the Lake View rooms. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Rendezvous Heritage Hotel Queenstown, good value. At 91 Fernhill Road, on the slopes of Ben Lomond, with rooms from 26 m² to 88 m², apartments, balconies in many rooms and Mackenzies Restaurant & Bar. Choose it for apartment space at a lower price. Watch out: the pool, spa pool, sauna and gym are closed from 23 November to 7 December 2026 for maintenance, and parking costs NZ$15 a night. Check rates on Booking.com →

Frankton and Kelvin Heights: the airport side

Frankton, on the Frankton Arm of Lake Wakatipu, is where the airport, the Remarkables Park shops and the bus hub meet. Queenstown’s tourism organisation recommends it for airport access and family stays, and the centre is a drive or a bus ride away.

Where to stay in Frankton and Kelvin Heights

  • Hilton Queenstown Resort & Spa, splurge. At Kawarau Village, 79 Peninsula Road, on the shore of the Frankton Arm, three kilometres from the airport, with a heated indoor pool, a spa, Wakatipu Grill and a water taxi across the lake. Choose it for a lakeside resort near the airport. Watch out: the hotel puts the town centre 10 kilometres away. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Ramada Suites by Wyndham Queenstown Remarkables Park, comfortable. At 24 Hawthorne Drive, about two minutes’ drive from the airport, with the Remarkables Park Town Centre next door and apartments from studios to three bedrooms, most with kitchens and a washer and dryer. Choose it for self-catering near the airport. Watch out: the Loft Room has a staircase and a low ceiling, which the hotel says may not suit everyone, and the centre is about twelve minutes away by car. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Sudima Queenstown Five Mile, good value. At 20 Grant Road, in Frankton’s Five Mile retail precinct, with free parking during your stay, Podium Restaurant and Bar, and seven room types including the Remarkables View King and the Deluxe Family room for up to five. Choose it for easy parking and a family room. Watch out: it is in a retail precinct, not by the lake. Check rates on Booking.com →

Arrowtown: a gold-rush village

Arrowtown is a historic town, a legacy of the 1860s Otago gold rush, set apart from Queenstown, and a stay here means evenings in a small town rather than in the centre. Without a car, rely on the half-hourly bus.

Where to stay in Arrowtown

  • Millbrook Resort, splurge. On Malaghans Road, with two championship 18-hole golf courses, a day spa, five onsite dining venues and rooms from Deluxe Studios to Resort Homes and Luxury Retreats. Choose it for golf and a resort stay outside town. Watch out: it is on Malaghans Road, not in the village; the hotel does not give the distance to Arrowtown or Queenstown. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Arrowtown House Boutique Hotel, comfortable. At 10 Caernarvon Street, with five rooms, each with a continental breakfast served in the house, a coffee machine, a cheese platter and underfloor heating in the bathroom. Choose it for a small house in the village. Watch out: with only five rooms there is little choice of room, and the hotel’s site gives no parking or check-in details. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Arrowfield Apartments, good value. At 115 Essex Avenue, with one-, two- and three-bedroom apartments, each with a full kitchen, a gas fireplace and a garage, and a heated 22-metre indoor pool, gym and tennis court shared by guests. Choose it for space for a family or a group. Watch out: the hotel says the walk to the town is ten minutes, and guests put it at 10 to 15. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Queenstown

  • Count the GST. Check whether the rate includes it: a NZ$300 room shown without GST costs NZ$345.
  • Watch the season. Prices rise in winter, June to September, and in summer, December to February, according to Queenstown’s tourism organisation, which recommends booking three to six months ahead for those periods. It names October and November, and March to May, as the best-value months.
  • Check the parking and the facilities. Parking is paid at Mövenpick Queenstown and Rendezvous Heritage, and free at Kamana Lakehouse and Sudima Queenstown Five Mile. Ask about closures: the Rendezvous pool and gym close from 23 November to 7 December 2026.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Queenstown for a first trip?

The town centre: most hotels, attractions and restaurants are within about ten minutes on foot of the lakefront. Choose Fernhill for views over Lake Wakatipu and The Remarkables, Frankton and Kelvin Heights for the airport and self-catering, and Arrowtown for a quieter historic village.

What taxes are added to a Queenstown hotel bill?

New Zealand's 15% GST. If a NZ$300 room is quoted without GST, the bill is NZ$345; if the NZ$300 already includes GST, NZ$39.13 of it is GST. No Queenstown visitor levy is charged in October 2026.

Is there a visitor levy or bed tax in Queenstown?

Not in October 2026. Queenstown Lakes District Council has proposed a levy on short-term accommodation, but the project is listed as on hold and nothing is added to hotel bills. In August 2026 the National Party said it would not introduce a bed-night levy.

What is the International Visitor Levy?

The International Visitor Conservation and Tourism Levy is NZ$100 a person for most international visitors, paid with the NZeTA or visa before you travel, not on the hotel bill. Australian citizens and permanent residents, and people from most Pacific islands, are among those exempt.

How do you get from Queenstown Airport to the town?

Take the Orbus public bus from outside the terminal to Frankton and the town centre. It generally runs every 15 minutes in the day and every 30 in the evening, and costs NZ$2.50 for an adult with a registered Bee Card or NZ$10 in cash. Taxis, Uber and shuttles also serve the airport; the airport is 8 km from the centre, a 10 to 15 minute drive.

When are Queenstown hotels cheapest?

Prices rise in winter, June to September, and in summer, December to February. The regional tourism organisation names October and November, and March to May, as the best-value months, and recommends booking three to six months ahead for the busy seasons.
