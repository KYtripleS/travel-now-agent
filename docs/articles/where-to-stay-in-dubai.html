Hotels around Downtown Dubai, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Downtown Dubai that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

A Dubai hotel bill is rarely the price you first see. Every room carries the Tourism Dirham, a flat fee of AED 7 to AED 20 a room a night set by the hotel’s category, and hotels’ own rate terms add a 7% municipality fee, a 10% service charge and 5% VAT on top. Where you stay decides the rest: Dubai is long and thin, strung along the coast, and the metro, the tram and taxis join it up.

This guide covers five areas, from the Burj Khalifa to the Palm, 12 hotels, the airport links and the fees. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

Before you book: entry and the regional situation

  • Visas. Visit Dubai, the Department of Economy and Tourism’s site, says citizens of more than 80 countries can get a visa on arrival; check your own passport on its visa page.
  • Check the travel advice. The conflict in the Middle East still shapes travel. Australia’s Smartraveller, updated on 8 October 2026, advises a high degree of caution in the UAE and warns that its airspace may open or close at short notice, affecting flights at Abu Dhabi and Dubai International. The UK Foreign Office says there have been strikes and retaliatory attacks by Iran across the region since 8 July, and tells British nationals in the Middle East to be prepared for flight cancellations and periodic airspace closures. Read your own government’s advice before booking, and again before you fly.

Dubai’s hotel fees in 2026

The Tourism Dirham is set by Dubai’s Executive Council and charged per occupied room, per night, by the hotel’s classification:

Hotel category Per room, per night
Five-star or higher hotel or resort AED 20
Four-star hotel or resort AED 15
Three-star hotel or resort AED 10
Two-star hotel AED 10
One-star or budget hotel AED 7
Luxury hotel apartment AED 20
Deluxe hotel apartment AED 15
Standard hotel apartment AED 10
Guest house AED 7

It is charged for a maximum of 30 consecutive nights, to the main guest in the room. It is per room, not per person, so two people sharing pay it once. A couple spending four nights at Canopy by Hilton Dubai Al Seef, a four-star hotel that lists AED 15 a room a night, pays AED 60 on top of the room, by our arithmetic.

The percentages are the larger part. A Dubai government decree of 2019 lowered the municipality fee on hotel sales from 10% to 7%, the UAE’s VAT is 5%, and hotels’ own rate terms list both, with a 10% service charge, on each room night. Together that is at least 22% of the room rate: on an AED 800 room, at least AED 176 a night before the Tourism Dirham, by our arithmetic.

A 2026 change to watch. In May 2026 Dubai’s government approved a second package of economic incentives. It exempts establishments registered with the Department of Economy and Tourism from collecting the Tourism Dirham and the sales fees on hotel rooms. The package’s measures run for three to 12 months, and we could not find a published end date for this one. Hotels’ own terms in October 2026 still list the fees: Millennium Airport Hotel Dubai’s offer terms, valid to 31 December 2026, and Canopy by Hilton Dubai Al Seef both do. Ask the hotel what will be on your bill. Hotel taxes around the world compares the fees in other cities.

Getting in from the airports, and around

  • From Dubai International (DXB), the metro’s Red Line runs from Terminals 1 and 3: 5am to midnight Monday to Thursday and on Saturday, 5am to 1am on Friday, and 8am to midnight on Sunday. You may take up to two pieces of luggage, hand luggage included. Red Line passes are sold at the ticket offices and machines in Terminals 1 and 3.
  • By bus, services leave from stands at Arrivals in Terminals 1, 2 and 3. You need a Nol card: cash is not accepted.
  • By taxi, Dubai Taxi cars wait at ranks in each terminal, 24 hours a day, from a starting fare of AED 25 plus distance.
  • From Al Maktoum International (DWC), the airport’s own page lists two buses, each every 30 minutes: the F55 to Expo metro station and the N55 to Ibn Battuta bus and metro stations.
  • In town, metro, tram and bus fares follow zones; Dubai’s map is divided into seven. The metro’s Red Line serves both the airport and Burj Khalifa / Dubai Mall station.
Journey Nol Silver card Nol Red ticket

  • Which card. The Silver card costs AED 25, of which AED 19 is credit to travel on. The paper Red ticket costs AED 2 and holds up to 10 single trips. Children aged five and under ride free.

Downtown Dubai: the first-trip base

Downtown is the Dubai of the postcards: the Burj Khalifa, the Dubai Mall and the Dubai Fountain, with its own stop, Burj Khalifa / Dubai Mall, on the metro’s Red Line. It is busy, polished and expensive, and the easiest place to start.

Old Dubai: Al Fahidi, Bur Dubai and the Creek

Before the towers, Dubai was a trading town on its Creek. The Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood, in Bur Dubai, is the oldest community in the city, with wind towers and maze-like alleyways, and the Al Seef waterfront runs along the Creek beside it. Abra, the wooden boats in the photograph, cross the water.

Where to stay in old Dubai

  • XVA Art Hotel, good value. In the former home of the Seddiqi family in the Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood, open since 2003, with 15 rooms around three shaded courtyards, a café and the XVA Gallery. Choose it to sleep inside the old town, among the wind towers. Watch out: the Standard rooms measure 13 to 15 m². Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Canopy by Hilton Dubai Al Seef, comfortable. On the Creek at Al Seef, with 198 rooms, a rooftop pool, a spa and a riverside lounge, 1.5 km from the Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood by the hotel’s count. When we checked, a night on 21 October started at AED 273 on Hilton’s own site. Choose it for the Creek with a pool. Watch out: breakfast is available for a fee. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Arabian Courtyard Hotel & Spa, good value. A four-star hotel with an Arabian theme opposite the Dubai Museum in Bur Dubai, near Meena Bazaar, the Spice Market, the Gold Souk and the Al Fahidi historical area; Sharaf DG and Al Ghubaiba metro stations are 500 metres away on foot, by the hotel’s count. Choose it for old Dubai on foot at a modest price. Watch out: only the Deluxe Museum View rooms are sold with a view. Check rates on Booking.com →

Jumeirah: the beach road

Jumeirah is the long beach district between the old town and the Marina: villas, public beaches and, at Umm Suqeim, the sail of the Burj Al Arab. The Burj Al Arab itself is undergoing a phased restoration programme, and Jumeirah offers guests an alternative reservation at one of its other Dubai hotels.

Where to stay in Jumeirah

  • Jumeirah Beach Hotel, splurge. A resort on Jumeirah Road in Umm Suqeim 3, designed for families, with a private beach and pools, the Jumeirah Circle kids club and views from every room over the Burj Al Arab and the Gulf. Choose it for a family beach stay with the city’s best-known view. Watch out: it is designed for families, so expect children at the pools. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Rove La Mer Beach, good value. A beachfront hotel at J1 Beach, the former La Mer, in Jumeirah, with Downtown Dubai, the Dubai Mall and City Walk about 15 minutes away by the hotel’s count. Choose it for the beach at a Rove price. Watch out: rooms take at most two adults and one child aged 4 to 16. Check rates on Booking.com →

Dubai Marina and JBR: the beach and the tram

Dubai Marina is a canal city of residential towers and yachts, and Jumeirah Beach Residence, JBR, sits between it and the sea, with The Walk, a promenade of restaurants and shops. The Dubai Tram, which links the metro with the Palm Monorail, runs along Al Sufouh Road and Jumeirah Beach Road and stops at Jumeirah Beach Residence. It is a long way from the old town, but it is the liveliest base with a beach.

Where to stay in Dubai Marina and JBR

  • JA Ocean View Hotel, comfortable. A five-star hotel on The Walk at JBR, with 346 rooms and suites that all face the sea, and an infinity pool. Choose it for the beach and The Walk at the door. Watch out: the hotel describes a private balcony for its Superior Sea View rooms, not the entry Sea View Room; book by name if you want one. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Rove Dubai Marina, budget. A 26 m² Rover Room, an outdoor pool and bikes to hire for a small fee; the hotel puts JBR beach, Dubai Marina Mall and the Dubai Tram ten minutes away on foot. Choose it for the Marina at the lowest price here. Watch out: the beach is a ten-minute walk, only the Marina View rooms are sold with a view, and rooms take two adults and one child at most. Check rates on Booking.com →

Palm Jumeirah: the resort island

The Palm Jumeirah is the palm-shaped island off the Marina, with villas on its fronds and the big resorts on the outer crescent. It is a place to stay put, with the rest of the city a taxi or a monorail-and-tram ride away.

Where to stay on the Palm

  • Atlantis, The Palm, splurge. The resort on the Palm’s crescent, with King and Queen Rooms of 45 to 47 m². Every Deluxe Room includes unlimited access to the Aquaventure World waterpark and the Lost World Aquarium, a daily session at the Atlantis Explorers Club kids’ club, and the beach and pools. Choose it for children, and for the waterpark at no extra charge. Watch out: the hotel puts a taxi from Dubai International at about 40 to 60 minutes and about AED 120. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Sofitel Dubai The Palm, comfortable. A resort on the East Crescent with a private beach, six pools, 12 restaurants and bars, and family apartments with fully equipped kitchens. Choose it for space and a kitchen on a family stay. Watch out: the hotel sells day passes to its beach and pools, so you share them with day visitors. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Dubai

  • Count the fees. Add the Tourism Dirham, AED 7 to AED 20 a room a night, and at least 22% in municipality fee, service charge and VAT. Then ask whether the 2026 exemption applies to your stay.
  • Price your own dates. We found no official statement of Dubai’s high-season dates, so we make no claim about them; Atlantis was advertising up to 35% off stays when we checked, so compare the nights you want.
  • Book a rate you can cancel. With airspace able to close at short notice, the flexibility is worth paying for.
  • Check the room size and who it sleeps. Rove’s rooms are 26 m² and take two adults and one child; XVA’s Standard rooms are 13 to 15 m². Families do better with Atlantis’s Queen Rooms or the Sofitel’s apartments.
  • Compare totals. Hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Dubai for a first trip?

Downtown Dubai: the Burj Khalifa, the Dubai Mall and the Dubai Fountain are there, with Burj Khalifa / Dubai Mall station on the metro's Red Line. Choose old Dubai, around Al Fahidi and the Creek, for heritage, Dubai Marina and JBR for the beach and the tram, Jumeirah for a family beach hotel and Palm Jumeirah for a resort.

How much is the Tourism Dirham in Dubai?

It is a flat fee per room, per night, set by the hotel's category: AED 20 at a five-star hotel, AED 15 at a four-star, AED 10 at a three- or two-star and AED 7 at a one-star or budget hotel or a guest house. It is charged for a maximum of 30 consecutive nights, and per room, not per person.

What else is added to a Dubai hotel bill?

Hotels' own rate terms list a 7% municipality fee, a 10% service charge and 5% VAT on each room night, on top of the Tourism Dirham: at least 22% of the room rate. In May 2026 Dubai approved an exemption from collecting the Tourism Dirham and the sales fees on hotel rooms for registered establishments, for three to 12 months, so ask the hotel what will be on your bill.

How do you get from Dubai airport to the city?

The metro's Red Line runs from DXB Terminals 1 and 3, from 5am to midnight on most days, to 1am on Friday and from 8am on Sunday, with up to two pieces of luggage. Buses leave from Terminals 1, 2 and 3 and need a Nol card, and Dubai Taxi cars start at AED 25 plus distance.

How much is the Dubai Metro?

With a Nol Silver card, AED 3 within one zone, AED 5 across two adjacent zones and AED 7.50 for more than two; the paper Red ticket costs AED 4, AED 6 and AED 8.50. The Silver card costs AED 25, including AED 19 of travel credit, and children aged five and under ride free.

Is it safe to travel to Dubai in 2026?

Check your own government's advice. Australia's Smartraveller, updated on 8 October 2026, advises a high degree of caution in the UAE and warns that its airspace may open or close at short notice. The UK Foreign Office says there have been strikes and retaliatory attacks by Iran across the region since 8 July, and tells British nationals in the Middle East to be prepared for flight cancellations and periodic airspace closures.
