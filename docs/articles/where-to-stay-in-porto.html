Hotels around Ribeira, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Ribeira that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

Porto adds a tourist tax of €3 a person a night to every hotel bill, for up to seven nights, and the city is split in two by the river: cross to Vila Nova de Gaia and a second tax, also €3 since late summer 2026, applies instead. Where you sleep decides which tax you pay, and it decides the hills too, because the old town drops steeply to the water.

This guide covers six areas, from Ribeira to the Atlantic coast, 12 hotels, the airport link and both taxes. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

Porto’s tourist tax, and Gaia’s

Two councils charge a tax on the night, and the rules differ:

Porto Vila Nova de Gaia
Rate €3 a person a night €3 a person a night
Limit 7 consecutive nights, a maximum of €21 7 consecutive nights a person a stay
Who pays Guests aged 13 and over Guests aged 16 and over
Exempt Children aged 12 and under; stays for medical treatment, and two companions; guests with a disability of 60% or more, and two companions; some other cases, such as displaced people and the first night in a pilgrim hostel Stays for medical treatment, and one companion; guests with a disability of 60% or more; groups travelling for work, study or sport pay half
In force €3 since 1 December 2024 €3 from 30 days after its publication on 22 July 2026; it was €2.50 before

Exemptions need proof. The hotel collects the tax and may ask for it at the start, during or at the end of the stay. On top of the tax, hotel accommodation in Portugal carries VAT at the reduced rate of 6%, under item 2.17 of the VAT code’s List I, according to a ruling from the Portuguese tax authority.

Here is our arithmetic, not an official figure:

  • Two adults, four nights in Porto: 2 × 4 × €3 = €24. The same stay in Gaia is also €24.
  • Two adults and a 14-year-old, four nights: in Porto the teenager pays, so 3 × 4 × €3 = €36; in Gaia, under 16, the teenager does not, so the total stays €24.
  • Two adults, nine nights: only seven nights count, so 2 × 7 × €3 = €42, not €54.

One caution for 2027. On 25 September Lusa reported that Porto’s council would vote the following Tuesday on starting the procedure to revise its tax regulation; the proposal named no figure, and in July the mayor had said the tax could rise from three to four euros. We found no decision, so €3 is the rate for a 2026 trip; check before booking later.

Getting in from the airport, and around

  • From Francisco Sá Carneiro airport (OPO), Metro do Porto’s line E, the violet line, runs to Trindade station in the city centre. The airport’s own page says it runs every 20 or 30 minutes, depending on the time and day.
  • The fare is a Z4. Andante, the Porto area’s public-transport ticket, lists a Z4 trip at €2.30 when you tap a contactless bank card at the Aeroporto or Trindade stations, among others, and a trip is valid for 1 hour 15 minutes on the metro and on STCP buses. From four trips in a day the charge is capped at €7.75. We have not quoted a journey time: Metro do Porto’s own timetable pages were closed to us, so check it for first and last trains.
  • From Trindade, change to line D for São Bento in the old town, as the Pestana Vintage Porto’s own directions say; Rosa et Al, in Cedofeita, gives a walking route from Trindade.
  • By bus or taxi, the airport’s page lists STCP and other operators for buses and says taxis circulate 24 hours a day and wait at ranks, and it quotes no fare.
  • On the slope: Porto climbs from the river to the old town. The Guindais funicular, which links the Ribeira quay with Batalha above, has track gradients of up to 36.1%, according to Visit Porto. STCP suspended it in July 2026 for a certification renewal and we found no reopening date, so plan to climb on foot.

Ribeira and the Historic Centre: the river and the old town

Visit Porto groups the Historic District around Ribeira, the cathedral, São Bento station, the Palácio da Bolsa, the Clérigos Tower and the Dom Luís I bridge, and describes the historic centres of Porto and Gaia as the choice for UNESCO World Heritage. Stay here for the first trip: everything is on foot, though every route out of the quay climbs.

Baixa and Aliados: shops and the main avenue

Visit Porto’s Downtown district covers Avenida dos Aliados, the Bolhão market, Rua de Santa Catarina, the Coliseu and the Clérigos area, and says it is at its liveliest at night. It is a good base for shopping and for the cafés, a short climb above the river.

Where to stay in Baixa

  • Infante Sagres, splurge. On Praça D. Filipa de Lencastre, with 85 rooms and suites, fully renovated in 2024, and a summer terrace with a plunge pool. Choose it for a renovated five-star in the middle of the city. Watch out: the hotel’s terrace and pool close for the season from 6 October. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Grande Hotel do Porto, good value. A hotel of 1880 on the pedestrian Rua de Santa Catarina, with 94 rooms, a gym and a terrace, five minutes from São Bento station by the hotel’s count. Choose it for a classic hotel on the main shopping street at a lower price. Watch out: the standard rooms are 14 to 17 m², and the street is pedestrian, so a car must be registered with the hotel in advance. Check rates on Booking.com →

Cedofeita and Bombarda: galleries and design shops

Visit Porto lists Bombarda and Rua de Cedofeita within its Downtown district. Rosa et Al, which stands here, calls the area the Bombarda art district and describes galleries, artists’ studios and concept stores around it.

Where to stay in Cedofeita and Bombarda

  • Gallery Hostel, budget. On Rua Miguel Bombarda in a restored 19th-century building that opened as a hostel in 2011, with every room named after a local artist, an art gallery, a games room, complimentary bike rental and daily walking tours. Choose it to keep the budget low on the art street. Watch out: the main building has seven dorms and only two private rooms; the 18 m² doubles are in a garden wing behind. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Rosa et Al Townhouse, comfortable. On Rua do Rosário, in a building from the 18th century, with seven suites and a brunch room. The Queen Deluxe City Heritage suite is 33 m², with its original plasterwork ceiling. Choose it for a boutique stay close to the galleries. Watch out: the Queen Deluxe suites face Rua do Rosário, and the hotel’s own text mentions its street life; the Garden suites are a separate type. Check rates on Booking.com →

Vila Nova de Gaia: the Port wine lodges

Gaia lies on the south bank, facing Ribeira, and is a separate municipality: its own tax applies, and its rules differ from Porto’s. It is where the Port lodges are: the Pestana Vintage Porto’s own page puts the leading Port wine houses across the river, opposite Ribeira.

Where to stay in Gaia

  • The Yeatman, splurge. A Relais & Châteaux wine hotel on a hill amid the Port lodges, with all rooms and suites having a terrace or balcony facing Porto, indoor and outdoor infinity pools, a wine spa and a restaurant with two Michelin stars. Choose it for the view of Porto from the south bank. Watch out: you cross the river for the old town, and the hotel is on a hill; the stay is also taxed by Gaia, not Porto. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Porto River Soul Hotel, comfortable. A boutique hotel with 65 rooms in six types, near the Port wine cellars, the Dom Luís I bridge and Ribeira, with a river-view restaurant, Rabelo, and a wellness space, Azure. Choose it for the river and the cellars at the door. Watch out: only some rooms face the river; the River View double is 22 m², and the Interior View room faces inwards. Check rates on Booking.com →

Boavista: concerts and gardens

Visit Porto’s Boavista district brings together Casa da Música, the Palácio de Cristal and its gardens, the Botanical Garden and the Bom Sucesso market, and describes a mix of modern and traditional architecture and many international companies. It is a modern base, away from the old town.

Where to stay in Boavista

  • Hotel da Música, good value. Inside the Mercado do Bom Sucesso, with 85 rooms, a gym and paid parking at €12 a day; when we checked, its own site listed standard rooms from €85. The hotel puts the Crystal Palace Gardens 1 km away. Choose it for a simple base for the gardens and Casa da Música. Watch out: it has no pool, and it puts the Dom Luís I bridge 3.5 km away. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Porto Palácio Hotel by The Editory, comfortable. On Avenida da Boavista, with the Auge restaurant and a 360-degree view on the 19th floor, and a spa of 1,500 m² with an indoor pool. Choose it for the view and the spa. Watch out: the hotel itself describes the avenue as Porto’s business and financial centre, not the old town. Check rates on Booking.com →

Foz do Douro: the sea

Visit Porto’s Atlantic district takes in Foz, the beaches, the seafront promenades, the Pérgola da Foz and the Parque da Cidade, which it calls the largest urban park in the country, at over 85 hectares and beside the sea. It is a seaside neighbourhood, and Duas Portas puts the city centre ten minutes away.

Where to stay in Foz

  • Vila Foz Hotel & Spa, splurge. A restored 19th-century manor house with a modern building beside it, 66 rooms and suites, a spa, and two restaurants, Vila Foz and Flor de Lis. Choose it for a seaside luxury stay. Watch out: the hotel’s page says Vila Foz won a Michelin star in the 2022 guide; check the current guide if the restaurant matters to you. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Duas Portas, comfortable. A family-run house of eight rooms, in front of the mouth of the Douro, with a garden, a wine bar, homemade breakfast and bicycles, ten minutes from the centre by its own count. Choose it for a small, quiet stay by the river mouth. Watch out: four of the eight rooms face the river and sea and the rest face the garden; airport transfers and tours cost extra. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Porto

  • Count both taxes. Add €3 a person a night, up to seven nights, in Porto, and the same in Gaia, where only guests aged 16 and over pay. Four nights for two adults is €24 either way.
  • Check which side of the river. The Yeatman and Porto River Soul are in Gaia; the other ten are in Porto. The rules and the exemptions differ, so look at the hotel’s address.
  • Watch the season. Infante Sagres closes its terrace and pool from 6 October, and Hotel da Música listed an autumn offer of up to 15% off when we checked. We did not compare prices across dates.
  • Check room size. Several hotels here are small by design: Porto A.S. 1829’s smallest rooms are 14 m² and the Grande Hotel do Porto’s standard rooms are 14 to 17 m². If you need space, book the category above the cheapest.
  • Compare totals. Hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book, and hotel taxes around the world puts Porto’s tax in context.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Porto for a first trip?

Ribeira and the Historic Centre. Visit Porto's Historic District groups Ribeira, the cathedral, São Bento station, the Palácio da Bolsa, the Clérigos Tower and the Dom Luís I bridge, so the old town is on foot. Choose Baixa for shops and Aliados, Cedofeita and Bombarda for galleries, Gaia for the Port lodges, Boavista for Casa da Música and Foz for the sea.

How much is the tourist tax in Porto?

€3 a person a night, up to seven consecutive nights, a maximum of €21, since 1 December 2024. Guests aged 13 and over pay; children aged 12 and under are exempt, with proof. Two adults for four nights pay 2 × 4 × €3 = €24, our arithmetic. In late September the council was due to vote on starting a revision, with no figure named, so €3 stands for a 2026 trip.

Does Vila Nova de Gaia have its own tourist tax?

Yes. Gaia, on the south bank, charges a city tax of €3 a person a night, up to seven nights a stay, from 30 days after its publication on 22 July 2026; it was €2.50 before. Guests aged 16 and over pay, so a 14-year-old pays in Porto but not in Gaia. The Yeatman and Porto River Soul Hotel are in Gaia.

How do you get from Porto airport to the city centre?

Take Metro do Porto's line E, the violet line, from the airport to Trindade station; the airport's page says it runs every 20 or 30 minutes. The fare is a Z4: Andante lists €2.30 with a contactless bank card, valid for 1 hour 15 minutes on the metro and STCP buses. From Trindade, line D goes to São Bento. We quote no journey time because we could not confirm one at Metro do Porto.

Is Porto hilly, and does that affect where to stay?

Yes. The old town climbs steeply from the river: the Guindais funicular, which links Ribeira with Batalha, has track gradients of up to 36.1%, according to Visit Porto. STCP suspended it in July 2026 for a certification renewal, and we found no reopening date, so plan to climb on foot. Baixa sits above the river, and Boavista and Foz are away from the old town.

Is VAT added to hotel rooms in Porto?

Hotel accommodation in Portugal carries VAT at the reduced rate of 6%, under item 2.17 of the VAT code's List I, according to a ruling from the Portuguese tax authority. The tourist tax is separate: €3 a person a night in Porto or Gaia, collected by the hotel, so compare the total with both.
