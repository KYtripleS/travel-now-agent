Vatican Museums Tickets (2026-27): Official Site vs GetYourGuide, Tiqets, Viator, Klook and KKday

The Vatican Museums sell one entry ticket, and the sellers around it differ in three ways: the price, how many dates and time slots they can actually offer, and what happens if your plans change. For a Wednesday 9 December 2026 visit, read on 17 November, the official skip-the-line ticket was €25 (€20 for entry plus a €5 “Skip the Line” charge). The one reseller we could price for that date, GetYourGuide, asked €32.50 for a self-guided ticket, and every self-guided ticket we read was non-refundable.

The official site also carries a warning that matters more than the price gap, and we quote it below: look-alike websites that charge much more. We may earn a commission from GetYourGuide, Tiqets, Viator, Klook and KKday, all of which are partners. The Vatican Museums’ own ticket site pays us nothing, and we say below when it is the better buy, which on our check it was.

What the official site says about other sellers

The Vatican Museums’ ticket pages carry a notice. It reads: “The only official site for purchasing tickets online is https://tickets.museivaticani.va”. It continues: “Please beware of potential scams and websites that use domains similar to the official one, which may charge significantly higher prices.”

Read plainly, that is a warning about imitation websites, not a statement that tickets bought elsewhere will be refused. We found no wording on the official pages saying that entry is denied for tickets bought from a reseller, and no wording approving named platforms either. The prices page links to a list of “Accredited Tour Operators” (a PDF), which we did not read, so we cannot say whether any seller in this guide is on it.

Practically: a search for the museums’ tickets returns many look-alike sites with “official” in their pitch, and we priced none of them. If you buy from a reseller, choose one with an established listing and a clear cancellation line. If you want no doubt about who issued the ticket, the official portal is the only place the museums themselves name.

How to read it:

  • On the comparable row, the official site had the lower price. GetYourGuide’s listing says all taxes and fees are included in its €32.50; the official €5 is the “Skip the Line” charge the museums list beside the €20 entry price. A reseller earns its place when the official slot you want is gone, and on our date most official morning slots were sold out or nearly so (see below).
  • The €20 on its own is not a skip-the-line ticket. The prices page lists “Full entry ticket” at €20.00 without online booking, and a separate “Skip the Line” ticket at €20.00 plus €5.00 when booked on the official site. Comparing a reseller’s price to €20 would flatter the official site, so we use €25.
  • Tiqets did not sell its plain ticket for 9 December. Its fast-track ticket page said “This experience is not available on Wednesday, Dec 9”, while offering a guided version from €64.00 on that date. Its calendar showed prices only for dates up to 30 November when we looked. For dates we could see, the plain ticket was €28 to €38.
  • The official morning slots were nearly gone. On the official portal, the slots from 08:00 to 14:00 showed eight as sold out (“POSTI ESAURITI”) and five as nearly sold out (“IN ESAURIMENTO”). We did not read the later afternoon slots.
  • Closed days. On the official calendar, 6, 8, 13, 20, 25 and 26 December were closed; 9 December was open.

What each seller’s listing says

Official: tickets.museivaticani.va

The official portal sells entry for a date and a time slot. For one adult it listed “Biglietto Intero” (full ticket) at €20.00 plus “Diritti di Prevendita” (advance-sale fee) of €5.00, and the cart carried the line “I biglietti acquistati non sono rimborsabili” (purchased tickets are not refundable). The prices page says the same in English: “Tickets cannot be refunded”, and that the ticket entitles the holder to the Museums and Sistine Chapel “solely on the day on which the ticket is issued”. We did not go to checkout, and the pages we read do not say whether a ticket carries the holder’s name or needs ID.

The same portal sells official guided visits and “closed areas” visits. For the private-group guided product, the cart for one visitor showed the €20 ticket, the €5 advance-sale fee, €1.50 for a headset and a guide service of €300.00 per booking, a total of €306.50. It is a flat fee for a group, not comparable with a per-person ticket.

Its terms: “This activity is non-refundable”, and the ticket is “valid for the date and time slot chosen”. It says: “Please do not arrive outside your assigned time slot”. The museums’ own rules that change the decision

  • Closed days and hours. Monday to Saturday 8:00 to 20:00 with the last entry at 18:00. On the last Sunday of each month the museums open 9:00 to 14:00 with free entry, with last entry at 12:30. The hours page excludes dates that coincide with Easter Sunday, 29 June, 25 December, 26 December and 31 December from that Sunday opening. Other Sundays are closed.
  • Same-day validity, no refunds. The ticket is for the day it is issued, and the official site says tickets cannot be refunded. Buy once your date is firm.
  • Free entry. Children under 7 enter free; free tickets for disabled visitors “cannot be booked online” and are issued on site.
  • Dress code. “Sleeveless and / or low-cut garments, shorts above the knee, miniskirts and hats are not permitted.”

When the official site is the better buy

  • You want the lowest price for a plain ticket. €25 against €32.50 on 9 December.

When a reseller is the better buy

  • The official slot you want is gone. Reseller stock is separate: on 9 December GetYourGuide still showed an 08:00 start while the official morning slots were mostly full. We cannot say how many slots it held.
  • You want an audio guide waiting inside. GetYourGuide’s option was €39.50, with a pick-up desk.
  • You want a live guide at a set price per person. Tiqets’s guided listing showed from €64, and a group of up to 15 is a different thing from a €300 private-group fee.

Frequently asked questions

Is it cheaper to buy Vatican Museums tickets from the official site?

On 17 November 2026, for a Wednesday 9 December visit, the official skip-the-line ticket was €25 (€20 plus a €5 "Skip the Line" charge), against €32.50 on GetYourGuide. Tiqets did not sell its plain ticket for that date. Viator, Klook and KKday were not priced. Prices change daily.

Does the Vatican Museums' site refuse tickets bought from resellers?

We found no such wording. The official notice says "The only official site for purchasing tickets online is https://tickets.museivaticani.va" and warns against look-alike domains that may charge significantly higher prices. It does not name or approve any reseller.

Can I get a refund on a Vatican Museums ticket?

The official site says "Tickets cannot be refunded", and the GetYourGuide, Tiqets and Viator listings we read were non-refundable. KKday's skip-the-line listing says free cancellation up to 3 days in advance.

Are the Vatican Museums closed on Sundays?

Yes, except the last Sunday of the month, when entry is free from 9:00 to 14:00, with some December and holiday exceptions listed on the official hours page. On the official calendar, 6, 13 and 20 December 2026 were closed, and so was Tuesday 8 December.

What is the difference between the €20 and €25 official price?

The prices page lists the full entry ticket at €20.00 without online booking and the "Skip the Line" ticket at €20.00 plus €5.00 when booked on the official site. For a dated slot, expect the €25 figure.

Do I need to arrive at my time slot?

GetYourGuide's listing says "Please do not arrive outside your assigned time slot", and tickets are valid for the date and slot chosen. The official ticket is valid only on the day it is issued.
