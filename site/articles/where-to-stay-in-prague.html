Prague is a city of spires, bridges, and layers of history. For travelers, this means there is no single "center." Where you sleep determines whether you wake up to Gothic towers, baroque courtyards, or quiet residential streets — and how many tram or metro rides you take to reach the next wonder. Choosing the right neighbourhood is the single most important logistical decision of your trip.

First, choose the area

Prague's central districts are compact but distinct. To make your sightseeing stress-free, prioritize hotels near these key zones:

  1. Staré Město (Old Town): The historic core, home to Old Town Square, the Astronomical Clock, and Týn Church.
  2. Malá Strana (Lesser Town): The baroque district beneath Prague Castle, known for Nerudova Street and embassy buildings.
  3. Nové Město (New Town): The area founded by Charles IV, featuring Wenceslas Square and Art Nouveau architecture.
  4. Žižkov: The quirky district east of the centre, famous for the TV Tower, independent venues, and a multicultural atmosphere.
  5. Vinohrady: The upscale residential district southeast of the centre, known for tree-lined streets and local markets.

Being within a ten-minute walk of a metro stop (A) or tram stop (22, 23) will save you hours over a week-long trip.

Staré Město: the historic heart

Staré Město is where you find the Astronomical Clock, Týn Church, St. Nicholas Church, and the Charles Bridge (a short walk away). It is one of Prague's busiest neighbourhoods, filled with hotels, souvenir shops, and cafes that have served visitors for centuries.

Malá Strana: Lesser Town quiet

Malá Strana is where you find Prague Castle, the St. Nicholas Church, and the charming lanes that lead down to the Vltava River. It's the best area for those who want to be near the castle and enjoy panoramic views while avoiding the crowds of Old Town.
