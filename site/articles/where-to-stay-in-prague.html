Hotels around Old Town, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Old Town that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

Prague’s hotel bill has one line that is easy to miss: the city’s local fee for staying overnight, the poplatek z pobytu. It is 50 CZK a person a night, the most Czech law allows, and it is set in koruna even when your hotel quotes its rates in euros. The bigger cost is where you sleep: the old centre is compact but steep in places, and the right area saves both walking and fares.

This guide covers six areas, from the Old Town to Holešovice, 12 hotels, the airport links and the fee. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

Christmas markets 2026: where to stay

Prague’s two biggest Christmas markets, on Old Town Square and Wenceslas Square, run from 28 November 2026 to 6 January 2027, according to Visit Czechia, the national tourist board. Smaller ones open earlier and close on Christmas Eve: Peace Square in Vinohrady from 20 November, Republic Square from 25 November. The Old Town puts the main market on your doorstep; the New Town is close to both; Vinohrady has its own.

If your trip falls in the market season, book a rate you can cancel as soon as you know your dates, and put them into the hotel map above to see live prices and what is still free.

Prague’s city fee in 2026

Every place that rents beds by the night collects the fee for the city, hotels, hostels and short-term rentals alike, under a city-wide ordinance.

Charge Rate
Local fee for a stay (poplatek z pobytu) 50 CZK a person, for every day of the stay except the day you arrive
Who pays Guests on a paid stay of up to 60 nights in a row with one provider
Who does not Under-18s, and a few other groups listed by law, such as blind people and holders of the Czech ZTP/P disability card with a companion

In practice the fee is charged per person per night. By our arithmetic, a couple staying four nights pays 400 CZK, roughly €16 at an approximate exchange rate, on top of the room. Under-18s add nothing.

Many hotels quote their rates in euros, while the fee is set in koruna, so check whether a quoted price already includes it before you compare.

Getting in from the airport, and around

Václav Havel Airport has two terminals: Terminal 1 for flights from outside the Schengen area, such as the UK, the US and Japan, and Terminal 2 for flights within it. The Airport Express boards at Terminal 1. City buses have been boarding at the Terminal 2 stop during road repairs, so check PID’s airport page for the current stop and follow the signs.

  • Line 59 and metro line A is the cheapest way in. Line 59 runs from the terminals to Nádraží Veleslavín, on metro line A, every 3 to 10 minutes and in 17 minutes; PID, the city’s integrated transport system, puts the whole trip to the centre at about 35 minutes. One 90-minute ticket covers the bus and the metro: 46 CZK in the PID Lítačka app, 50 CZK on paper.
  • Line 100 runs to Zličín, on metro line B, every 10 to 20 minutes, about 45 minutes to the centre in all, on the same ticket.
  • The Airport Express, line AE, runs to the main railway station, Hlavní nádraží, on metro line C, in about 45 minutes to the centre by PID’s estimate. It has its own fare: 200 CZK, or 100 CZK for children aged 6 to 15, and you can buy the ticket from the driver. Ordinary PID tickets and the 24- and 72-hour passes are not valid on it.
  • At night, lines 907 and 910 leave from Terminal 2 for the night network, on the ordinary 90-minute ticket.
  • In town, one PID ticket works on the metro, trams, buses and ferries. A 30-minute ticket is 36 CZK in the app or 39 CZK on paper, 90 minutes 46 or 50 CZK, 24 hours 140 or 150 CZK, and 72 hours 340 or 350 CZK. These are full fares, for passengers aged 15 to 60.
  • How to pay: buy in the app, from machines at stops and metro stations, or with a contactless bank card on the payment terminals in every vehicle. App and contactless tickets start automatically after 60 seconds; stamp a paper ticket once, in a yellow validator, on your first ride. Children under 10 and passengers over 65 ride free in Prague, the over-65s with an ID showing their date of birth.

By our arithmetic, the 72-hour ticket pays for itself from the eighth ride: seven 90-minute app tickets cost 322 CZK and eight cost 368 CZK, against 340 CZK for the pass.

The Old Town: the first-trip base

The Old Town, Staré Město, is the medieval core on the right bank of the Vltava: Old Town Square with the Church of Our Lady before Týn, the Astronomical Clock, and Charles Bridge at its western edge. Inside it is the Jewish Quarter, Josefov, whose synagogues and Old Jewish Cemetery can be visited on one Jewish Museum ticket; the Pinkas Synagogue holds the memorial to the Shoah victims from the Czech lands. It is the most walkable base and the most crowded by day.

Choose it for modern design in the oldest part of the city. Watch out: the Mini rooms are, by the hotel’s own description, compact; book a Castle View room for the view. Check rates on Booking.com →

Malá Strana: the castle side of the river

Malá Strana, the Lesser Town, climbs from the far end of Charles Bridge towards Prague Castle: Baroque palaces and gardens, the green dome of St Nicholas Church, Kampa island on the river, and Petřín hill above. Trams do most of the work here, and Malostranská metro station sits at its northern edge. Day visitors fill the main streets; the side streets quieten by evening.

Where to stay in Malá Strana

  • Mandarin Oriental, Prague, splurge. In a group of historic buildings that were once a monastery, on Nebovidská, opened in 2006, with rooms that keep vaulted ceilings and parquet floors, a spa in a former Renaissance chapel, and Monastiq, a restaurant of Czech cooking. By the hotel’s count Charles Bridge, Kampa and Prague Castle are each a five-minute walk. Choose it for a quiet hotel on the castle side. Watch out: Old Town Square is a 20- to 25-minute walk.   • Aria Hotel Prague, splurge. A hotel of 51 rooms and suites on four floors themed on contemporary, classical, opera and jazz music, each room named after a musician, next to the Vrtba Garden, a Baroque garden; it has a private cinema, a music library and CODA, a rooftop restaurant. Breakfast is included in the room rate. Choose it for music and a garden view. Watch out: the 30 m² Deluxe rooms look over either the Vrtba Garden or the hotel’s Winter Garden atrium, so ask which.   • Lokál Inn, good value. Rooms above a Czech pub-restaurant in the house U Bílé kuželky on Míšeňská, a Baroque building designed by Kryštof Dientzenhofer and built between 1708 and 1714, a short walk from Charles Bridge. The restaurant below serves Czech cooking and tank Pilsner, and breakfast is included. Choose it for a historic house at a pub-and-rooms price. Watch out: breakfast is served only from 8 to 10am.

The New Town: Wenceslas Square and the metro

The New Town, Nové Město, was laid out by Emperor Charles IV, and its medieval street plan survives under later buildings. Its centre is Wenceslas Square, a long boulevard with the National Museum at the top. It is the most practical base: Muzeum and Můstek stations connect all three metro lines between them, and the main railway station, where the airport bus stops, is a short walk away.

Where to stay in the New Town

  • Jalta Boutique Hotel, comfortable. On Wenceslas Square at number 45, in a building of the post-war socialist-realist period, with 1950s-style rooms, the original marble floors, and a spiral staircase to a terrace that looks over the square and the National Museum. Choose it for a piece of 1950s Prague with the metro at the door. Watch out: it is on the square itself, which stays busy late. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Motel One Prague-Florentinum, good value. A few minutes’ walk from Republic Square, with a lobby of marionettes and a lounge under Bohemian crystal chandeliers. Choose it for a central room at a chain price. Watch out: the smaller rooms are 16 m². Check rates on Booking.com →

Vinohrady: cafés, parks and a local base

Vinohrady, in Prague 2, rises east of the New Town: long streets of grand apartment houses, náměstí Míru with its twin-spired church, the Vinohrady Theatre, and two hilltop parks, Riegrovy sady and Havlíčkovy sady. Metro line A stops at Náměstí Míru and Jiřího z Poděbrad. Stay here to come home to cafés rather than crowds; Wenceslas Square is within walking distance.

Where to stay in Vinohrady

  • Le Palais Art Hotel Prague, comfortable. A Belle Époque building on U Zvonařky, where the painter Luděk Marold lived and worked, with his original ceilings, 72 rooms and suites, Artista, a fine-dining restaurant with a view of the city, a lobby bar and a spa. Choose it for a grand hotel in a residential district. Watch out: the Economy rooms are attic rooms of the hotel’s “old standard”, with skylights; the Deluxe rooms are on the first to third floors. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Czech Inn, budget. A design hostel in a restored 19th-century building on Francouzská, just over the edge of Vinohrady in Prague 10, with dorm beds behind curtains, ensuite and premium dorms, private rooms and apartments, breakfast, a bar and a guest kitchen. Choose it for the lowest price in this guide. Watch out: the standard dorms share bathrooms; book an ensuite dorm or a private room if that matters. Check rates on Booking.com →

Karlín: the newer side

Karlín, in Prague 8, is a grid of 19th-century streets east of Florenc, between the river and Vítkov hill, that has filled with offices, restaurants and cafés. Metro line B stops at Florenc and Křižíkova.

  • Botanique Hotel Prague, comfortable. On Sokolovská, where Karlín meets Florenc, next to the metro, bus and tram stops there and, by the hotel’s count, a 15-minute walk from the heart of the Old Town. Its rooms have been renovated in a nature-inspired style, it has a bistro and bar with vegan and gluten-free dishes, and it holds Green Key certification. Choose it for a renovated hotel by a transport hub. Watch out: free breakfast comes with direct-booking rates; check what a third-party rate includes. Check rates on Booking.com →

Holešovice: galleries and Letná

Holešovice, in Prague 7, sits in the bend of the Vltava north of the Old Town, below Letná park. The National Gallery’s Trade Fair Palace holds its modern and contemporary collection, and the DOX centre for contemporary art is further north. Metro line C stops at Vltavská and Nádraží Holešovice, and trams run to the Old Town.

  • Mama Shelter Prague, good value. A hotel of 238 rooms over ten floors on Veletržní, designed by Jalil Amor, with a restaurant of long communal tables and an eighth-floor rooftop terrace over the city’s roofs; by the hotel’s count the Old Town is a five-minute tram ride. Choose it for a lively hotel outside the tourist crush. Watch out: the rooftop takes no reservations. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Prague

  • Count the fee. Add 50 CZK a person a night for everyone 18 or over: a couple’s four nights adds 400 CZK. If the hotel quotes in euros, ask whether the fee is included and in which currency it is charged.
  • Watch the season. Compare two or three sets of dates, and weeknights against weekends, before you settle.
  • Check the room size. Rooms vary widely: Motel One’s smaller rooms are 16 m² and Hotel Paris’s Deluxe rooms 19 to 24 m². If you need space, the Four Seasons starts at 33 m² and Aria’s Deluxe rooms are 30 m².
  • Book the view by name. At the Four Seasons, choose a river category; at Hotel Josef, a Castle View room; at Aria, ask for the Vrtba Garden side.
  • Compare totals, and check direct rates. Hotel Paris offers an upgrade and Lokál Inn a discount code for booking direct, and Botanique adds breakfast. Hotel booking sites compared and booking direct or through a booking site go through where to book.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the best area to stay in Prague for a first trip?

    The Old Town (Staré Město): Old Town Square, the Jewish Quarter and Charles Bridge are within a few streets. Choose Malá Strana for the castle side of the river, the New Town for Wenceslas Square, the metro and the airport bus, and Vinohrady for a residential base with cafés and parks.

    How much is the tourist tax in Prague?

    Prague's local fee for a stay, the poplatek z pobytu, is 50 CZK a person for every day of the stay except the day you arrive, so in practice per person per night. Under-18s are exempt, and it applies to paid stays of up to 60 nights in a row with one provider. A couple staying four nights pays 400 CZK.

    How do you get from Prague Airport to the city centre?

    The cheapest way is line 59 to Nádraží Veleslavín and then metro line A, about 35 minutes to the centre on one 90-minute ticket: 46 CZK in the PID Lítačka app or 50 CZK on paper. The Airport Express, line AE, runs to the main railway station for 200 CZK.

    Can you use a 24-hour or 72-hour ticket on the Prague Airport Express?

    No. The Airport Express, line AE, has its own fare of 200 CZK, or 100 CZK for children aged 6 to 15, and ordinary PID tickets and the 24- and 72-hour passes are not valid on it. Lines 59 and 100 do accept ordinary PID tickets.

    How much is a public transport ticket in Prague?

    In 2026 a 30-minute ticket is 36 CZK in the PID Lítačka app or 39 CZK on paper, a 90-minute ticket 46 or 50 CZK, a 24-hour ticket 140 or 150 CZK, and a 72-hour ticket 340 or 350 CZK. These are full fares, for passengers aged 15 to 60.

    Can you pay for Prague trams and buses with a contactless card?

    Yes. You can buy a ticket with a contactless bank card on the payment terminals in every vehicle, or in the PID Lítačka app; these tickets start automatically after 60 seconds. A paper ticket must be stamped once, in a yellow validator, on your first ride.
