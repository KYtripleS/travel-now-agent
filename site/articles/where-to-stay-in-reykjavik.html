Hotels around Laugavegur, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Laugavegur that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

Reykjavík’s hotel bill carries two Icelandic taxes. The first is the accommodation tax, gistináttaskattur: 800 ISK for every room, apartment or separately rented bed, every night, whatever the room costs. The second is 11% VAT on the room itself. The tax is flat, so it adds more, as a share, to a cheap room than to a dear one. Two other costs shape a stay as much as the room rate: the 50 km from Keflavík Airport, and which side of the small city centre you sleep on.

This guide covers five areas, from Laugavegur and the hill of Hallgrímskirkja to the Old Harbour, Grandi and Hlemmur, 12 hotels, the airport links and the tax. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

Reykjavík’s hotel tax: 800 ISK a room a night, plus 11% VAT

Iceland’s accommodation tax is set nationally and collected by the hotel. Skatturinn, the Icelandic tax authority, charges it per sold accommodation unit, a let of up to one day and night. What counts as one unit is set out in its rules: a hotel room is one unit, each apartment a hotel lets is one unit, and where a guesthouse or hostel lets each bed separately, each bed is one unit. So a couple in a double room pay it once, and two friends in two dorm beds pay it twice.

Charge Rate in Reykjavík, 2026
Accommodation tax at hotels, guesthouses and other licensed lodgings (licence categories II–IV) 800 ISK per room, apartment or separately let bed, per night
Accommodation tax at campsites and pitches for campervans and caravans 400 ISK per unit, per night
VAT on hotel and guest-room rental 11%

Iceland’s official guidance on VAT in tourism, on island.is, says hotel and guest-room rental is always charged at the 11% rate, whatever the length of stay, and that the accommodation tax is not part of the amount VAT is charged on. Reykjavík adds no city tax of its own.

Our arithmetic: a room at 30,000 ISK a night, VAT included, for three nights costs 90,000 ISK, and that holds 8,918.92 ISK of VAT (30,000 × 11 ÷ 111 = 2,972.97 ISK a night). The accommodation tax adds 3 × 800 = 2,400 ISK, for 92,400 ISK in all. Some hotels quote the 800 ISK inside the rate and some show it as a separate line, so compare final totals. Getting in from the airport, and around

Keflavík Airport is about 50 km from Reykjavík, on the Reykjanes peninsula. Two buses run into town.

  • Flybus. The coach from Keflavík to the BSÍ bus terminal in central Reykjavík takes about 45 minutes. One way costs 3,999 ISK for adults and 1,999 ISK for ages 6 to 15, and children aged 1 to 5 ride free. Flybus to hotel, at 5,299 ISK for adults and 2,649 ISK for ages 6 to 15, adds a connecting shuttle from BSÍ to hotels, guesthouses and official bus stops across the city. Departures from the airport are timed to every arriving flight, and if your flight is late your ticket stays valid for the next coach. Going back, coaches leave BSÍ every 30 to 60 minutes between 04:00 and 22:30. The Airport Direct website now forwards to Flybus’s own site, so in practice Flybus is the airport coach to plan around.
  • Strætó route 55. The public bus runs between the airport and the capital area every day, with twelve daily departures. An adult pays 2,400 ISK to Reykjavík or 1,800 ISK to Fjörður in Hafnarfjörður; ages 12 to 17 and over-67s pay half, and children 11 and under ride free. Pay on board by debit or credit card or in cash. Not every trip runs to BSÍ: some start or end at Fjörður, so check the timetable. Since 1 October 2026 the bus stops at the terminal itself, with arriving passengers boarding at the KEF Arrivals stop in the Bus B pick-up area.

Our arithmetic for two adults one way into town: 7,998 ISK on Flybus to BSÍ, 10,598 ISK on Flybus to hotel and 4,800 ISK on route 55. By taxi, Isavia, which runs the airport, put a typical fare to the capital area at about 20,000 ISK for a standard car in 2024; Visit Reykjavík lists Hreyfill, with meters and an optional fixed price, and the app-based Hopp.

In town, a Strætó city bus ride costs 690 ISK for an adult and 345 ISK for ages 12 to 17 and over-67s; children 11 and under ride free. Tap a contactless card on board or buy tickets in the Klappið app. With fare capping, an adult never pays for more than three rides a day or nine a week on the same payment method: 2,070 ISK a day at most, by our arithmetic. Most of the centre is walkable, and from Hlemmur or Grandi the walk to Laugavegur is part of the day.

Downtown 101: Laugavegur, Austurvöllur and the pond

Downtown Reykjavík is the old town between the harbour and Tjörnin, the pond where City Hall stands. Laugavegur, the main shopping street, runs east from Bankastræti; at the western end are Austurvöllur, the square in front of the Parliament House, and Lækjartorg. Restaurants, bars, bookshops and the tour pick-up points are all within a few minutes. For a first trip, especially in winter, this is the easiest base: everything is on foot.

It has Art Deco interiors, 99 rooms from singles to the Tower Suite, with views over downtown, and the Borg Spa, with hot tubs and steam baths. Choose it for history on the city’s central square. Skólavörðustígur and Hallgrímskirkja: the hill

Skólavörðustígur climbs from Laugavegur to Hallgrímskirkja, the concrete church on the hill of Skólavörðuholt whose tower is visible across the city. The street is lined with galleries, craft shops and cafés, and the side streets around the church are residential and quieter after dark than Laugavegur. Downtown is a short walk downhill, which matters less on the way out than on the way back.

Where to stay near Hallgrímskirkja

  • Hotel Leifur Eiríksson, good value. At Skólavörðustígur 45, by Hallgrímskirkja, run by the same family since 1999, with 47 rooms, each with its own layout and design, a bar open to guests and locals, and a continental breakfast. Choose it for the church at the door and a family-run welcome. Watch out: there is no lift; the hotel says so itself, and offers help with luggage instead. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Holt, comfortable. A four-star hotel at Bergstaðastræti 37, open since 1965, with 42 classically decorated rooms, many with views of Hallgrímskirkja, a bar, and what the hotel calls Iceland’s largest private art collection. Choose it for art and an older Reykjavík. Watch out: the décor is traditional on purpose; if you want Nordic minimalism, look elsewhere. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hótel Óðinsvé, comfortable. A 50-room four-star hotel at Þórsgata 1, open since 1984, in a residential corner of the centre where the streets are named after the Norse gods. By the hotel’s count it is a 6-minute walk from Hallgrímskirkja, 10 minutes from Harpa and 15 from the Old Harbour, and it has its own restaurant, Snaps. Choose it for a quiet street between the hill and the old town. Watch out: the hotel and Óðinsvé Hotel Apartments are booked separately; check which one you are booking. Check rates on Booking.com →

The Old Harbour: Harpa and the waterfront

The Old Harbour runs along the north edge of downtown, from Harpa, the glass concert hall, west towards Grandi. Whale-watching and tour boats leave from its piers, and the restaurants along the quays are a few minutes from Austurvöllur.

Where to stay by the Old Harbour

  • The Reykjavik EDITION, splurge. At Austurbakki 2, by the Old Harbour and steps from Harpa, with 253 rooms and suites, most with floor-to-ceiling windows and some with terraces, looking over the harbour, Mount Esja or towards the Snæfellsjökull glacier. It has the TIDES restaurant, The Roof, the Tolt cocktail bar and a spa, and calls itself Reykjavík’s first five-star modern luxury hotel. Choose it for the harbour and Harpa at the door. Watch out: price; this is the five-star end of the city. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Exeter Hotel, comfortable. At Tryggvagata 12, on the Old Harbour, with a sauna, a greenhouse lounge, jazz on Monday and Friday, and in the building Le Kock for burgers and DEIG for doughnuts, coffee and bagels. Rooms run from Urban Standard to Harbour Suites. Choose it for food and life downstairs. Watch out: the hotel’s own line is “We’re not a quiet hotel”. Check rates on Booking.com →

Grandi: the harbour district

West of the Old Harbour, Grandi is the harbour district where working fish sheds now sit beside restaurants, shops and museums. It feels more open and windswept than downtown, and the walk back to Laugavegur along the quays is part of staying here.

Where to stay in Grandi

  • Grandi by Center Hotels, comfortable. An industrial-style hotel in Grandi, near the Old Harbour, with a restaurant open every day for dinner, a bar and the Grandi Spa, whose two hot tubs, at different temperatures, sit under an open skylight. Rooms run from standard to deluxe and a suite, and Center Hotels guests get 15% off the restaurant’s à la carte menu. It holds Green Key certification. Choose it for a spa and the harbour district. Watch out: Laugavegur and the hill are a walk back through the harbour, and longer in wind and sleet. Check rates on Booking.com →

Hlemmur and Borgartún: the east side

Hlemmur, at the eastern end of Laugavegur, is the old city bus terminus, now a food hall, and still a stop for many city bus routes. North of it lies Borgartún, Reykjavík’s business district, with taller buildings, and the shore path with the Sun Voyager sculpture on the sea side. The hotels here are larger and further from the bars than those downtown, and one of them includes breakfast and free parking.

Where to stay around Hlemmur

  • Storm Hotel, good value. A 93-room Keahotels hotel at Þórunnartún 4, with three room types, each with a walk-in shower, breakfast included, a bar and lounge, and limited free parking. Choose it for breakfast in the rate and somewhere to leave a car. Watch out: it is in postcode 105, outside 101, so Laugavegur is a walk rather than a step. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Fosshotel Reykjavík, comfortable. Iceland’s largest hotel, with 320 rooms over 16 floors in the business district, a short walk from the main shopping area, many rooms with views over the city and the bay, seven suites on the top floor, the Haust bistro and the Beer Garden. Choose it for a high-floor view. Watch out: it is built for conferences and groups as well as travellers, and feels like it. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • KEX Hostel, budget. A hostel and hotel in a former biscuit factory at Skúlagata 28, on the downtown side of Hlemmur; KEX means “biscuit” in Icelandic. It has dorms, guesthouse-style doubles and twins, family rooms for four or six, and a floor of hotel rooms with private bathrooms, plus shared kitchens on the guest floors, a bar open until 23:00 and a breakfast buffet at 2,700 ISK. Choose it for a social base with a kitchen. Watch out: the hostel doubles and family rooms share hallway bathrooms; book a hotel-floor room for your own. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Reykjavík

  • Count the tax. Add 800 ISK a room a night if the rate does not already show it; a dorm bed pays it too. The 11% VAT is already in the price.
  • Read what breakfast costs. Sand Hotel and Storm Hotel include it; at KEX the buffet is 2,700 ISK. In a city where eating out is dear, a rate with breakfast can be the cheaper one.
  • Check the room and the stairs. Hotel Leifur Eiríksson has no lift, and at KEX the hostel rooms share bathrooms. If either matters, book the category above the cheapest or another hotel.
  • Price the airport run as a group. Flybus discounts return bookings and groups of four or more automatically; route 55 is the cheapest for one or two.
  • Compare totals. Hotel booking sites compared and booking direct or through a booking site go through where to book; some hotels here, Hotel Borg among them, give extras only on direct bookings.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Reykjavik for a first trip?

Downtown 101, around Laugavegur and Austurvöllur: the main shopping street, the Parliament square and the pond, Tjörnin, are all on foot. Choose the streets around Hallgrímskirkja for a quieter hill, the Old Harbour for Harpa and the waterfront, Grandi for a spa hotel in the harbour district, and Hlemmur and Borgartún for breakfast in the rate and parking.

How much is the accommodation tax in Iceland?

800 ISK per room, apartment or separately let bed, per night, at hotels, guesthouses and other licensed lodgings, and 400 ISK per unit at campsites. Skatturinn, the Icelandic tax authority, sets it per unit, not per person, so a couple in one room pay it once.

What is the VAT on hotel rooms in Iceland?

11%. Iceland's official guidance says hotel and guest-room rental is always charged at the 11% rate, whatever the length of stay, and the accommodation tax is not part of the amount VAT is charged on.

How do you get from Keflavík Airport to Reykjavik?

Flybus takes about 45 minutes to the BSÍ bus terminal for 3,999 ISK one way for adults, or 5,299 ISK with the connecting shuttle to hotels, and its departures are timed to every arriving flight. Strætó route 55 costs 2,400 ISK for an adult to Reykjavík, paid on board by card or in cash, with twelve departures a day; not every trip runs to BSÍ.

How far is Keflavík Airport from Reykjavik?

About 50 km. Flybus puts the coach journey to the BSÍ bus terminal at about 45 minutes, depending on weather and traffic.

How much is the bus in Reykjavik?

A Strætó city bus ride costs 690 ISK for an adult and 345 ISK for ages 12 to 17 and over-67s, and children 11 and under ride free. Tap a contactless card on board or use the Klappið app; with fare capping an adult never pays for more than three rides a day or nine a week.
