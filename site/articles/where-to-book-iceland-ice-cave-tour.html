Two things decide an ice cave booking more than the headline price: where the tour starts and what happens if the cave cannot be entered that day. Iceland’s natural ice caves are in different places. The blue caves of Vatnajökull start at Jökulsárlón or Skaftafell in the far south-east, while the Katla cave starts at Vík on the south coast, and only a few listings run a day trip with pickup from Reykjavik. The same operator’s tour is also sold on several sites, at close but not identical prices, with different cancellation windows. For a first blue ice cave, it is the GetYourGuide Jökulsárlón Crystal Blue Ice Cave tour. What decides whether you get in

  • Season. Visit Iceland says the ice cave season “typically extends from November until May”. Our date, 12 December, is inside that window.
  • A guide is part of the product. Visit Iceland says to “always go with professional glacier guides and the right equipment”. Every listing above includes a guide and the equipment.
  • The cave changes. Each listing says the cave visited is chosen on the day for conditions and safety, so the cave you see may differ from the photos.
  • Closures. Only some listings say what happens if the operator cancels. Where the wording is in the listing (GetYourGuide and KKday for Jökulsárlón), it is a reschedule or full refund. Where we did not capture it, we say so above.
  • Age. Minimum ages run from 6 to 8 on the listings we read, and the Skaftafell hike takes no one under 8.

What to wear and what is not included

Wear thermal layers, a waterproof shell, gloves and sturdy hiking boots. The GetYourGuide Jökulsárlón listing says to bring warm clothing, snacks and rain gear, and the Skaftafell listing makes boots with ankle support mandatory. Viator’s Troll Vík listing lists boot rental at 1,750 ISK. Food and hotel pickup are not included on the Viator Jökulsárlón listing, and the Jökulsárlón and Vík tours are meet-on-location, so plan how you reach the meeting point.
