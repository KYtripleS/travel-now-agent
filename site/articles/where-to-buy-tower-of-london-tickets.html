    What the venue’s terms say about tickets

    Historic Royal Palaces runs the Tower of London. Its ticketing terms, read on 25 November 2026 at https://www.hrp.org.uk/terms-of-use/, say: “Tickets must not be offered for re-sale via a third party. To do so will invalidate the ticket.” They also say tickets cannot be resold or refunded, and that altering or defacing a ticket renders it void. Those clauses govern what a ticket holder may do with a ticket. The terms name no authorised sellers, and they do not say that tickets bought from a booking platform are refused: they say that tickets purchased through a third party cannot be amended by the venue and that you should contact the company used for booking.

    In practice that means two things. Buy for the date you will use, and do not buy from people reselling tickets. We have also applied a test of our own to each partner below: we include a partner only if its own listing issues a dated admission to the buyer, using words such as “Official ticket” or “Show your smartphone ticket”. GetYourGuide, Tiqets and Viator each passed. Klook is not checked and KKday did not show a plain entry ticket, so neither is in the price table with a figure.

    The venue’s own rules that change the buying decision

    These come from hrp.org.uk only, read on 25 November 2026.

    • What a ticket includes. The Tower ticket page says it covers “All open public areas of the Tower”, including the Crown Jewels, the White Tower, the Battlements, the Bloody Tower, the Torture at the Tower exhibition, the Fusiliers Museum and the Royal Mint exhibition, plus the Yeoman Warder Tour (subject to availability).
    • Resale and changes. Tickets must not be offered for re-sale via a third party, and tickets bought through a third party cannot be amended by the venue. If you need to change a booking made through a seller, the seller’s own cancellation rule is the one that applies.
    • No fast-track entry. The venue’s FAQ says there is no fast-track entry option at the Tower, so a listing that sells a standard admission is the ticket you want.
    • Release dates. The FAQ says admission tickets are released a few months in advance.
    • Children. Children under 16 must be accompanied by an adult at all times. The ticket page lists ages 0-4 as free, 5-15 at £19.00 and 16-17 at £19.00.
    • Bags. Back packs, large bags and suitcases are not permitted inside the buildings, and limited luggage storage is at the venue’s discretion.
    • Opening hours. We did not read the venue’s own hours for 12 December, so we give none; the listings differ (see above).

    Four checks before you pay

    1. The ticket type. £38 is adult; children aged 5-15 are £19.00.
    2. The cancellation cut-off. GetYourGuide and Viator: 11 December. Tiqets: 24 hours before.
    3. The meeting point. A guided listing may start somewhere other than the gate.
    4. Who you buy from. Use a booking page that issues a ticket for your date, and do not buy from people reselling tickets.

    Frequently asked questions

    Where should I buy Tower of London tickets?

    GetYourGuide. On 25 November 2026, for Saturday 12 December, it showed £38.00 for one adult, with free cancellation until 11 December and a score of 4.6 from 33,624 reviews. Tiqets and Viator also showed £38 for an adult. Klook was not checked.

    Is there a difference in price between sellers?

    Not on the day we looked. GetYourGuide, Tiqets and Viator each showed £38 for an adult on 12 December, so the choice comes down to cancellation terms and what the page shows.

    How much is a child ticket?

    The venue's ticket page lists ages 0-4 as free, ages 5-15 at £19.00 and ages 16-17 at £19.00. Tiqets showed Child (5-15) at £19.00. GetYourGuide says children under 5 go free; we did not read a child price on it or on Viator.

    Can I cancel a Tower of London ticket?

    It depends on the listing. GetYourGuide said Cancel for free until Dec 11 for a 12 December visit, Viator said free cancellation before 11 December, and Tiqets said free cancellation until 24 hours before the visit date.

    Can I resell or give away a Tower of London ticket?

    No. The venue's terms say tickets must not be offered for re-sale via a third party, and that doing so invalidates the ticket. Buy for the date you will use, and do not buy from people reselling tickets.

    Is there fast-track entry?

    The venue's FAQ says there is no fast-track entry option at the Tower. The tickets we compared are standard admission.
