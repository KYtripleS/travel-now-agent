    What a Cape Town hotel bill adds

    Two charges can appear on a hotel bill in Cape Town. We found no hotel occupancy tax of the New York kind, and no separate City of Cape Town accommodation charge, in the official sources listed below.

    Charge What it is
    Value-added tax (VAT) 15%, the standard rate. The South African Revenue Service treats hotel accommodation as “commercial accommodation”, a taxable supply
    Tourism levy (TOMSA) 1% of the room rate, collected only by hotels that take part

    The levy is TOMSA, Tourism Levy South Africa, administered by the Tourism Business Council of South Africa, which hands the money to South African Tourism for destination marketing. It is voluntary for hotels, but its own FAQ says it is compulsory for guests at every place that collects it. TOMSA says it is not a tax.

    Our arithmetic, not an official example: on a room at R3,000 a night, 15% VAT is R450 and a 1% levy is R30, so the bill is R3,480, about 16% more. TOMSA does not say whether its 1% is worked out before or after VAT; we have assumed before. Whether a quoted rate already includes VAT and the levy varies by hotel and booking site, so compare the final total, which the invoice shows line by line.

    Getting in from the airport, and around

    • From Cape Town International (CPT), the MyCiTi A01 airport bus is not running. The City announced its suspension from 1 December 2022, citing weak demand and diesel costs, and MyCiTi’s own airport page still carries a notice that the service is temporarily suspended. Its text still describes the route, so check before relying on it.
    • At the airport, the operator, Airports Company South Africa, lists authorised airport taxis, walk-in from the transport kiosks and the domestic arrivals desk from 5am to midnight, plus the Uber and Bolt apps and two shuttle companies, Citi Hopper and Centurion Tours. It advises arriving passengers to use only authorised airport taxis, and not to accept rides from unsolicited drivers.
    • Around town, MyCiTi buses run on a network that includes the Waterfront, Sea Point and Camps Bay. A myconnect card costs R40. From 1 July 2026 a Mover fare for 0 to 5 km is R19.50 at peak times, weekdays 06:45 to 08:00 and 16:15 to 17:30, and R15.00 at other times, with a One Day Pass at R130, a Three Day Pass at R290 and a Seven Day Pass at R420.
    • The useful routes on MyCiTi’s timetable list are the 104, Oranjezicht to Gardens and Adderley to the Waterfront; the 105, Sea Point and Fresnaye to Civic Centre; the 106 and 107, Camps Bay to Civic Centre; and the T01, Dunoon and Table View to Civic Centre and the Waterfront.

    The V&A Waterfront: the first-trip base

    The Waterfront is Cape Town’s old harbour turned into a district of hotels, restaurants and shops, with Table Mountain behind it. It is the easiest base for a first visit: the hotels sit inside it, the Robben Island ferries leave from the harbour, and MyCiTi runs from here to the city centre and the Atlantic coast.

    Where to stay at the Waterfront

    How the hotel links work: a hotel’s name goes to its own website. “Check rates” opens it on Booking.com through our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book, at no extra cost to you.

    • Cape Grace, A Fairmont Managed Hotel, splurge. On West Quay Road, on a private quay in the Waterfront, with rooms that look onto the harbour, the marina or Table Mountain, a pool, a Fairmont spa and the Heirloom restaurant. Choose it for the harbour at the door. Watch out: the view is part of the room category, Harbour View or Mountain View, so pick the one you want when you book. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Victoria & Alfred Hotel, comfortable. In a 1904 warehouse on the North Quay, at PierHead, with 24-hour room service and a complimentary drink on arrival. Choose it for a historic building on the water. Watch out: the pool, spa and gym are at The Dock House, not in the hotel itself. Check rates on Booking.com →

    The City Bowl: Long Street, Gardens and the Bo-Kaap edge

    The City Bowl is the amphitheatre of streets under Table Mountain. Long Street runs through the middle of it, Gardens climbs towards the mountain, and the Bo-Kaap, the hillside of painted houses, sits on its western edge. Bree Street’s cafés, Greenmarket Square and the Parliament buildings are all in the city centre. It is the base for wandering on foot, and the busiest part of town at night.

    Where to stay in the City Bowl

    • Mount Nelson, A Belmond Hotel, splurge. The pink hotel at 76 Orange Street in Gardens, open since 1899, with 196 rooms and suites, nine acres of gardens, seven restaurants and its afternoon tea. Choose it for a grand old hotel in a garden, in the middle of town. Watch out: space follows the category: rooms measure 32 to 63 m², and the Signature Suites 88 to 180 m². Check rates on Booking.com →
    • The Grand Daddy Boutique Hotel, comfortable. At 38 Long Street, with individually styled rooms and suites, and Rooftop Airstreams that the hotel says are the only authentic Airstreams on a rooftop. Choose it for a hotel with character on Cape Town’s best-known street. Watch out: you are on Long Street, whose energy the hotel itself advertises. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Ashanti Gardens Guesthouse, budget. At 7 Union Street, Gardens, in two restored Victorian houses a short stroll from Kloof Street, with en-suite doubles, twins and triples, a family room, a shared kitchen in each house and wrap-around balconies. Choose it for a low price with a private bathroom. Watch out: reception is open from 8am to 4pm, although a team member is on site 24 hours, and the former Ashanti Lodge backpackers has closed. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Cape Town Lodge Hotel, good value. A four-star at 101 Buitengracht Street, with 124 rooms on the higher floors, looking at Table Mountain, Lion’s Head, Signal Hill, the harbour and the Bo-Kaap, a rooftop pool and a free fitness centre. Choose it for views and a pool at a middle price. Watch out: the Superior Rooms have a shower only, no bath. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Sea Point and Green Point: the promenade

    West of the Waterfront, Green Point and Sea Point run along the Atlantic: Cape Town Stadium, then the Sea Point Promenade, where locals walk and cycle above the water. Both are residential and less crowded than the Waterfront. The MyCiTi 105 serves Sea Point, and the Waterfront is a short ride or, from Green Point, a walk.

    Where to stay in Sea Point and Green Point

    • The Winchester Hotel, comfortable. A four-star at 221 Beach Road, in a 1920s Cape Dutch-style building on the edge of the Sea Point Promenade, with a courtyard, a pool, the Shoreditch restaurant and Harvey’s Bar. Choose it for the sea and the mountain from a classic building. Watch out: off-street parking is limited. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • The Greenhouse Guesthouse, good value. A Victorian guest house at 69 Somerset Road, Green Point, with seven bedrooms, each with an en-suite shower. By the guesthouse’s count, the Waterfront, the Zeitz Museum, the Bo-Kaap and the Robben Island ferries are 5 to 10 minutes on foot, Cape Town Stadium is 1 km away, and a MyCiTi stop is on the doorstep. Choose it for a walk to the Waterfront at a low price. Watch out: with seven rooms there is little choice of room. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Camps Bay: the beach and the mountains

    Camps Bay is a beachfront strip below the Twelve Apostles, the line of peaks along the back of Table Mountain, on the Atlantic side of the peninsula. Between December and February, Cape Town’s summer, it is where the beach is. The trade-off is distance: the city and the Waterfront are a drive or a MyCiTi ride away, on the 106 and 107, which run from Camps Bay to Civic Centre.

    Where to stay in Camps Bay

    • The Twelve Apostles Hotel and Spa, splurge. On Victoria Road, on the coastal road between Camps Bay and Hout Bay, with 70 rooms and suites, most with ocean or mountain views, and a spa. Choose it for a tranquil stay above the Atlantic. Watch out: the hotel puts the Camps Bay beachfront at an 8-minute drive and the Waterfront at 15. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • The Bay Hotel, comfortable. At 69 Victoria Road, on the Camps Bay beachfront, with four pools, a gym, indoor and outdoor padel courts, a spa and the Tides restaurant. Choose it for the beach at the door. Watch out: the Mountain View rooms face Table Mountain and the Twelve Apostles, so check the category if you want the sea. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Constantia: a winelands base

    Constantia, on the southern side of the mountain, is the historic wine valley that both hotels below call Cape Town’s Vineyard. It is quiet, green and residential, with gardens rather than a seafront. You will want a car or a driver for the city and the beaches, which is the cost of the calm.

    Where to stay in Constantia

    • The Cellars-Hohenort, splurge. With 51 rooms and suites in nine acres of gardens, views of Table Mountain, and the city centre 15 minutes away by car, by the hotel’s count. Choose it for gardens and the valley. Watch out: every trip into town is that drive. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • The Alphen Boutique Hotel & Spa, comfortable. On Alphen Drive, a Provincial Heritage site with 18 designer suites in the Mill Range and the Dower House, a heated pool, a spa and three restaurants, beside the Constantia Greenbelt. Choose it for a small heritage hotel in the valley. Watch out: with 18 suites there are few rooms to choose from. Check rates on Booking.com →

    How to book Cape Town

    • Count the bill. Add 15% VAT, and 1% more where the hotel collects the TOMSA levy: a R3,000 room comes to about R3,480.
    • Work out how you will move. Without the airport bus, the first ride is a taxi or app. After that, the Waterfront, Green Point, Sea Point and Camps Bay sit on MyCiTi routes, but Constantia is not on the routes listed above and suits a car or a driver.
    • Watch the summer. December to February is summer, and the season for the Atlantic beaches at Sea Point and Camps Bay. We have not compared rates across the year, so check your dates early and compare the total.
    • Check the room category. Views, size and bath or shower change by category: Mount Nelson, Cape Grace and The Bay Hotel all sell rooms by view or size.
    • Compare totals. Hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the best area to stay in Cape Town for a first trip?

    The V&A Waterfront: the hotels sit inside the harbour district, the Robben Island ferries leave from the harbour, and MyCiTi buses run from here to the city centre and the Atlantic coast. Choose the City Bowl for Long Street and walking, Sea Point and Green Point for the promenade, Camps Bay for the beach and Constantia for gardens and wine.

    What taxes are added to a Cape Town hotel bill?

    15% VAT, the standard South African rate, on the room, plus a 1% tourism levy (TOMSA) at hotels that take part. The levy is voluntary for hotels but compulsory for guests where it is collected. We found no separate city accommodation tax in the official sources we read. On a R3,000 room, our arithmetic gives R450 VAT plus R30 levy, R3,480 in all.

    Is there a bus from Cape Town airport to the city?

    Not at the moment. MyCiTi's A01 airport route has been suspended since 1 December 2022, and MyCiTi's own airport page still carries a notice that the service is temporarily suspended. Airports Company South Africa lists authorised airport taxis, walk-in from 5am to midnight, plus Uber, Bolt and two shuttle companies, and advises using only authorised airport taxis.

    How much is the MyCiTi bus in Cape Town?

    From 1 July 2026 a Mover fare for 0 to 5 km is R19.50 at peak times (weekdays 06:45 to 08:00 and 16:15 to 17:30) and R15.00 at other times. The myconnect card costs R40, and a One Day Pass is R130, a Three Day Pass R290 and a Seven Day Pass R420.

    Is Camps Bay a good base in Cape Town?

    It suits a beach stay, mainly between December and February, Cape Town's summer. The city and the Waterfront are a drive or a MyCiTi ride away on the 106 and 107, which run from Camps Bay to Civic Centre. The Twelve Apostles Hotel and Spa puts the Camps Bay beachfront at an 8-minute drive.

    What does UK government advice say about travelling in Cape Town?

    The UK Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office, which we read on 22 October 2026, says South Africa has a high crime rate, gives specific advice for the road to and from Cape Town airport, and lists the MyCiTi bus in central Cape Town and along the Atlantic Seaboard among the generally safer and more reliable ways to travel.
