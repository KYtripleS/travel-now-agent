A San Diego hotel bill depends on where the hotel stands. Since 1 May 2025 the City of San Diego has charged a hotel tax of 11.75%, 12.75% or 13.75%, set by a three-zone map that rises towards the Convention Center, and a hotel with 70 or more rooms can add a 2% marketing district assessment on top. Coronado, across the bay, is a separate city, and many beach and resort hotels add a daily charge of $39 to $50 a night.

This guide covers six areas, from the Gaslamp Quarter to Coronado, 12 hotels, the airport links and the tax. San Diego’s hotel tax in 2026

The City of San Diego’s Office of the City Treasurer collects the transient occupancy tax, the city’s hotel tax, on stays of less than a month. Since 1 May 2025 the rate depends on the zone the hotel is in:

Charge Rate
Hotel tax, Tax Zone 3, the downtown area 13.75% of the room rate
Hotel tax, Tax Zone 2, the rest of the city 12.75%
Hotel tax, Tax Zone 1, two outer areas of the city 11.75%
Tourism Marketing District assessment, hotels with 70 or more rooms 2.00%, which the operator may pass on to the guest

The city’s ordinance defines Zone 3 as a downtown area from Laurel Street south, which by our reading takes in the Gaslamp Quarter, Little Italy and the Convention Center, and Zone 1 as the parts of the city north of State Route 56 and south of State Route 54. Zone 2 is everything else, which by our reading covers Old Town, Mission Bay, Pacific Beach and La Jolla. The city publishes an interactive tax zone lookup map, so check the hotel’s address on it if the zone matters to you.

A worked example, our arithmetic: a $300 room in a downtown hotel with 70 or more rooms that passes on the assessment costs 13.75% ($41.25) plus 2% ($6.00) in tax, $347.25 in all. The same room in Zone 2 costs $300 plus 12.75% ($38.25) and 2% ($6.00): $344.25.

Coronado is a separate city with its own tax. The Hotel del Coronado’s own reservation policies list “Current Room Taxes & Assessments” at 11%, subject to change; on a $300 room that is $33, our arithmetic. Resort and amenity charges are separate and set by each hotel; the ones each hotel publishes are noted below.

Getting in from the airport, and around

  • By bus, Route 992 runs between San Diego International Airport and Downtown, stopping on the elevated platform at both terminals, every 15 minutes from 4:30 a.m. to 1 a.m. MTS, the transit system, says the bus takes about 15 minutes to Santa Fe Depot, and the fare is $3.00. You can tap a contactless card or phone on the validator.
  • The San Diego Flyer is a free shuttle between the airport terminals and the Old Town Transit Center, running about every 20 to 30 minutes from 4:45 a.m. to 12:30 a.m. It is for transit riders only.
  • The trolley does not stop at the airport. Take Route 992 to Santa Fe Depot, where the Blue Line and Green Line stop, or to Courthouse Station for the Orange Line.
  • In town, the MTS adult fare is $3 one way on the trolley, buses and Rapid routes, and $1.50 with a reduced-fare ID. A PRONTO card or app adds unlimited transfers for two hours and caps what you pay at the $7 day pass; a cash fare is a single ride with no transfers. Riders aged 18 and under ride free with a Youth PRONTO account.
  • The lines that matter to visitors are the Blue Line, from San Ysidro through Old Town, Little Italy and Downtown to UTC, and the Green Line, which stops at Seaport Village, the Convention Center, the Gaslamp Quarter, Santa Fe Depot and Old Town on its way to Mission Valley.
  • To Coronado, the Hotel del Coronado says the resort is about 15 minutes from the airport and reached by the Coronado Bridge or the ferry.

The Gaslamp Quarter and Downtown: the first-trip base

The Gaslamp Quarter is a grid of restaurants and bars a few blocks from the waterfront, with the Convention Center and Petco Park, the Padres’ ballpark, at its edge. The Green Line stops at the Gaslamp Quarter and Convention Center stations, and the Blue and Green Lines meet at Santa Fe Depot. It is busy at night, which suits some travellers and not others.

Little Italy: a walkable neighbourhood

Little Italy sits just north of the Gaslamp Quarter, with restaurants and cafés along its streets, and the County Center / Little Italy station on the Blue and Green Lines at 1550 California Street. It suits travellers who want a neighbourhood to walk in rather than a nightlife strip.

Where to stay in Little Italy

  • Porto Vista Hotel, good value. At 1835 Columbia Street, with mid-century-style rooms from queens and kings to suites, original murals and art installations, and the GlassDoor Restaurant on the fourth floor, which looks over the downtown skyline and the harbour. Choose it for an art-filled hotel at a lower price. Watch out: parking is valet only, $55 a night for a standard vehicle. Check rates on Booking.com →

Old Town: history and an easy airport link

Old Town is the historic district north of Downtown, a cluster of restaurants, galleries and museums around the Old Town Transit Center, where the Blue and Green Lines stop and the free Flyer shuttle from the airport arrives. The hotels sit on a hillside above it.

Where to stay in Old Town

  • Hacienda Hotel Old Town, good value. A hacienda-style hotel above Old Town with a heated pool and spa, a complimentary hot breakfast buffet, and a complimentary shuttle to the airport, the Old Town Transit station and the cruise terminal, running from 6:30 a.m. to 9 p.m. Ten of its king rooms have full kitchens. Choose it for the airport link and the price. Watch out: parking is $30 a night, and the airport shuttle needs a call 45 minutes ahead. Check rates on Booking.com →

Mission Bay and Pacific Beach: sand, surf and a bay

North of Downtown, Pacific Beach faces the ocean along a boardwalk and Mission Bay holds the calmer water behind it. This is the area for a beach holiday, but the trolley does not reach the beachfront, so plan on a bus, a taxi or a car.

Where to stay in Mission Bay and Pacific Beach

  • Paradise Point Resort & Spa, comfortable. A 44-acre island resort on Mission Bay with a mile of sandy beach, five pools, a marina and bungalow-style rooms. Choose it for a resort with a beach and pools. Watch out: a Resort Amenities Fee of $46 a room a night is included in the room rate, and parking is $49 a night. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Tower23 Hotel, splurge. An oceanfront boutique hotel in Pacific Beach, with Oceanfront King rooms, Oceanfront Suites and the JRDN restaurant. Choose it for rooms over the boardwalk and the surf. Watch out: valet parking is $35 a night. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • The Beach Cottages, good value. At 4255 Ocean Boulevard, on Pacific Beach, with a courtyard firepit, guest laundry, gas grills, a ping pong table and shuffleboard. Choose it for a simple base on the beach at a lower price. Watch out: the property has several types of accommodation, so check which one you are booking. Check rates on Booking.com →

La Jolla: coast and cliffs

La Jolla is a village of shops and restaurants on a cliff above La Jolla Cove, with sea caves, tidepools and seals along the coast. The trolley does not reach it: the Blue Line’s nearest stations are at the university and UTC, so plan on a bus, taxi or car from Downtown.

Where to stay in La Jolla

  • La Valencia Hotel, splurge. At 1132 Prospect Street, a pink landmark that opened on 15 December 1926, with rooms from the 250 sq ft Vintage to the 800 sq ft Icon Suites, the Mediterranean Room and an ocean-view pool. Choose it for a historic hotel in the village. Watch out: the nightly resort fee is $39 plus taxes. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Pantai Inn, comfortable. A boutique inn with Balinese décor, steps from the beach and the village, with a complimentary breakfast, onsite parking and fireplaces in many rooms. Choose it for a quieter stay by the water. Watch out: rooms range from studios to multi-bedroom villas, so check the room type you are booking. Check rates on Booking.com →

Coronado: the resort on the sand

Coronado is a city on a peninsula across the bay from Downtown, reached by the Coronado Bridge or the ferry. It is a place for a beach resort and the long sandy shore.

Where to stay in Coronado

  • Hotel del Coronado, splurge. At 1500 Orange Avenue, open since 1888 and, by its own account, fresh from a six-year revitalisation, with Victorian rooms, cottages and villas. Choose it for the landmark and the beach. Watch out: a mandatory $50 a room a night is added, made up of a $35 resort charge and a $15 Historic Preservation Fund charge, and self-parking is $59 a night, valet $79. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Glorietta Bay Inn, comfortable. Across the street from the Hotel del Coronado, in the former Spreckels Mansion, with a complimentary hot breakfast and a heated outdoor pool. Choose it for a historic inn across the street from the Del. Watch out: the resort fee is $40 a night, and second-floor rooms are reached by stairs. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book San Diego

  • Count the tax, and check the zone. A downtown room carries up to 15.75% with the assessment, a Zone 2 room up to 14.75%: $300 becomes $347.25 or $344.25.
  • Add the daily charges. At the Hotel del Coronado a $300 room starts at $350 once the $50 charge is added, before tax; La Valencia adds $39, Glorietta Bay Inn $40 and Paradise Point $46 (included in its rate).
  • Price the parking. Three nights of self-parking at Paradise Point is $147, and Pendry’s valet is $70 a night. Hotels on the trolley or by the airport shuttle can save this.
  • Choose by how you will move. Downtown, Little Italy and Old Town work without a car; the beaches and La Jolla work better with one.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in San Diego for a first trip?

The Gaslamp Quarter: its restaurants, Petco Park and the Convention Center are within walking distance, and the Green Line trolley stops at the Gaslamp Quarter and Convention Center stations. Choose Little Italy for a walkable neighbourhood, Old Town for an easy airport link, the beaches for a beach holiday, La Jolla for the coast and Coronado for a grand resort.

How much is the hotel tax in San Diego?

In the City of San Diego it is 11.75%, 12.75% or 13.75% of the room rate, depending on the tax zone, with 13.75% in the downtown zone. Hotels with 70 or more rooms may also pass on a 2.00% Tourism Marketing District assessment. On a $300 downtown room that is $41.25 plus $6.00, $347.25 in all, by our arithmetic.

Is the hotel tax different in Coronado?

Yes. Coronado is a separate city. The Hotel del Coronado's own reservation policies list its current room taxes and assessments at 11%, subject to change, and the hotel adds a mandatory $50 a room a night in charges.

How do you get from San Diego Airport to Downtown?

Take MTS Route 992 from either terminal, every 15 minutes from 4:30 a.m. to 1 a.m., about 15 minutes to Santa Fe Depot, for $3.00. The free San Diego Flyer shuttle runs to Old Town Transit Center instead. The trolley does not stop at the airport.

How much is the trolley in San Diego?

$3 one way on the MTS trolley, buses and Rapid routes, or $1.50 with a reduced-fare ID. A PRONTO card or app adds unlimited transfers for two hours and caps what you pay at the $7 day pass; a cash fare is a single ride with no transfers.

Do San Diego hotels charge resort fees?

Some do. Among the hotels in this guide, La Valencia charges $39 a night plus taxes, Glorietta Bay Inn $40, Paradise Point $46 (included in its room rate) and the Hotel del Coronado $50 in mandatory daily charges. Pendry San Diego has a nightly amenity fee whose amount its FAQ does not give.
