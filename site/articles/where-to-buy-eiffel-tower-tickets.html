What differs between sellers is not the tower, it is the product. What the tower itself says about resellers

  • Only bundles. The page on buying tickets says: “Our resellers are only authorized to sell offers that combine a ticket and an additional service (such as a guided tour).” The scam-warning page adds that for such offers “the prices include the public service rate plus the price of the additional service”.
  • Lift-only tickets. The same page says only the tower’s own ticket site is authorised to sell lift-only tickets. None of the products below is one.
  • Wording. The tower says it “does not operate any skip-the-line system” and that resellers may use only the term “reserved access”. Listings word this differently, so judge a product by what it includes, not by its headline.
  • Personal tickets. The visit page says “Each e-ticket is personal” and asks you to bring a valid ID for each member of your group, including children.

Nothing we read says a reseller bundle will be refused at the gate; each listing’s operator is noted below.

How to read it:

  • Tiqets and GetYourGuide are the same tour at the same price. €139.90 against €140 is a rounding difference. Both listings name Memories France as the provider.
  • The €79 Viator product is a different thing. Its overview says a guide meets you, tells you about the tower, and then you “discover the monument on your own”. Its included list names second-floor and summit tickets and an English guided tour, and it says “Non-refundable”. Choose it if your date is firm.
  • The GetYourGuide climb price is a “from” figure for the 2nd floor by stairs; we did not read the summit upgrade.
  • The Tiqets cruise bundle is not like for like. Its page says a “From €73.00” list price, shown as €69.35 after a 5% bundle discount, and the included guided tour goes to the 2nd floor, with “Summit access (if selected at checkout)”.
  • The KKday cruise combo costs €109 and does not cancel. The calendar read 109.00 for 16 December and the page says “No cancellation”.
  • Official fares change on 11 January 2027. The tower’s page says new 2027 fares apply for visits from that date; our table uses the 2026 adult fares.

The tower’s rules that change what you buy

These are the tower’s own statements, and they apply to every ticket above.

  • Timed slot. “If you purchased a timed-entry e-ticket, the time shown is when you’re expected on the forecourt under the Tower.” The visit page asks you to “Allow 15 to 20 minutes to go through the security checks at the entrance”. Guided tours add their own meeting time.
  • How far ahead. Elevator tickets can be booked up to 90 days ahead, stairs tickets up to 60. The tower says Christmas-holiday tickets to the top “sell out very quickly”, and that tickets are released again at intervals.
  • Summit closures. The opening-times page says “Access to the summit may be restricted during severe weather”, and the tips page says tickets to the top “can be unavailable during busy times of day due to crowd limits”. Seller terms for a closure differ: GetYourGuide’s small-group tour refunds the price difference and its cruise listing offers a refund, while a non-refundable listing says nothing about it that we read.
  • Stairs versus lift. Stairs tickets reach the second floor; the summit then needs the lift. The tower says the top and the stairs are not accessible to people with reduced mobility or on crutches, for evacuation reasons.
  • Ages. On the tower’s own tickets, under-4s need a free ticket, 4 to 11 pay the child rate and 12 to 24 the youth rate, with proof of age. Bundles set their own child terms.

Which partner to book

  • You want a guide to the summit and an easy refund: Tiqets or GetYourGuide. The same operator, about €140, free cancellation until 24 hours before. GetYourGuide adds the automatic price-difference refund if the summit is closed.
  • You want to reach the summit for the least money and your date is fixed: Viator. €79 for 16 December, non-refundable.
  • You want a cruise included: GetYourGuide for the summit version at €85 with free cancellation.
  • You want to climb: GetYourGuide’s stairs tour, from €49 for the second floor.

Four checks before you pay

  1. What the product is: a guide to the summit, a host who hands over tickets, or a guide to the second floor only.
  2. The meeting point. Pont d’Iéna and an agency on Avenue de la Bourdonnais both appear in listings.
  3. The cancellation line and the late rule. Several listings refuse refunds to latecomers.
  4. Name and ID for each traveller, children included, and the total in euros at checkout.

Frequently asked questions

Where should I buy Eiffel Tower tickets?

For a guide who stays with you to the summit, Tiqets or GetYourGuide: on 21 November 2026, for a 16 December visit, they showed €139.90 and €140 with free cancellation until 24 hours before. For the least money to reach the summit, Viator showed €79, non-refundable. Prices change daily.

Can I buy a lift-only ticket from a reseller?

The tower's pages say its resellers may sell lift tickets only with an additional service such as a guided tour, and that only its own ticket site sells lift-only tickets. Every product we priced includes a guide, a host or a cruise.

Is there a skip-the-line ticket?

The tower says it does not operate a skip-the-line system and that resellers may use only the term "reserved access". You still pass security.

How far ahead can I book?

The tower releases elevator tickets 90 days ahead and stairs tickets 60 days ahead, and says December holiday tickets to the top sell out quickly.

What if the summit is closed?

The tower says summit access may be restricted in severe weather. GetYourGuide's small-group listing refunds the price difference automatically and its cruise listing offers a refund; check the listing you book.

Are children charged?

On the tower's own tickets, under-4s are free, 4 to 11 pay the child rate and 12 to 24 the youth rate. Reseller bundles set their own terms, so read the listing.
