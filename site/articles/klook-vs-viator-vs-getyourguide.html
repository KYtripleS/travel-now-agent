Is Viator cheaper than Klook?

Not reliably, and anyone who tells you one platform always wins is guessing.

The platforms are resellers. An operator lists the same tour on several of them and agrees terms with each separately, so the price you see depends on that operator’s deal with that site — and it moves with demand. The same tour can be cheaper on Klook one week and on Viator the next.

There are tendencies, though:

  • In Asia, Klook is usually cheaper. It holds direct partnerships with local operators and runs frequent sales, and its catalogue there is deeper.
  • In Europe and the Americas, Viator’s sheer volume creates more competition between operators on the same routes, which tends to keep its prices sharp there.

Our own checks in September 2026 fit the pattern, though they compare entry prices in the same category rather than identical tours: the standard Mount Fuji day tour sat at about ¥7,800 on Klook and KKday while Viator’s guided full days started around US$66, and Chao Phraya dinner cruises started from about ฿880 on KKday against roughly US$31 on Viator.

The only reliable method: find the operator’s name on any listing, search that name on both platforms, and compare the same date — then read the inclusions line before the price. A “cheaper” tour that leaves out an entrance ticket is not cheaper.

Does Viator or Klook have more activities?

Viator, by a wide margin — more than 400,000 experiences, and it is a Tripadvisor company, so its reach follows Tripadvisor’s. It also advertises round-the-clock customer support.

Klook was founded in Hong Kong in 2014 and works with more than 100,000 operators across a few thousand destinations, concentrated in Asia-Pacific. Its catalogue is smaller overall, but in Asia it carries categories Viator barely touches: Japan Rail and regional passes, airport transfers, SIM cards and eSIMs, and attraction tickets bundled with transport.

So “more” depends on the question. More activities in total: Viator. More of what you will actually need for a trip to Japan, Korea, Taiwan or Southeast Asia: usually Klook. Our Klook vs KKday comparison covers the other Asia specialist.

Which is better for families with kids?

In Asia, the big family days are theme parks and attractions, and that is Klook’s home ground — the tickets are listed with child prices, often bundled with the transport to get there.

For moving a family around, look at private car charters, which Klook lists across Asia and sizes by vehicle rather than by seat:

Viator’s family strength is different: many of its private tours are priced per group, not per person. For a family of five or six, divide the group price by heads before dismissing a private tour as extravagant — it can come close to the per-person price of a public one, without strangers or a fixed pace.

On any platform, check the child age bands and minimum ages on the listing itself. They differ by operator, not by site.

Which is better for groups?

It depends on the format you want:

  • A guided tour just for your group: Viator’s Private & Custom Tours category is the widest, and the per-group pricing is what makes it work.
  • Your own itinerary with a driver: Klook’s private car charters in Asia — pick the vehicle for your headcount and plan the stops.
  • A public tour with friends: compare the same listing on both. Group size does not change the platform maths; the operator’s price does.

Which is better for couples?

Neither, honestly. The experiences couples book — sunset cruises, dinner cruises, food tours, a day trip somewhere scenic — are usually resold by the same operators on all three sites. The operator decides whether the evening is good; the platform only decides what you pay and what happens if you cancel. Compare the specific listing, then choose on price and cancellation terms. Neither programme is a reason to choose a platform for a single trip. They matter only if you book several activities a year.

Where GetYourGuide fits

GetYourGuide is strongest in Europe, where its skip-the-line tickets and guided tours are dense, and it is the platform whose free-cancellation policy is applied most consistently. On dinner cruises it also lays out menu tiers clearly, which makes a three-course and a five-course easy to compare. If your trip is European and the booking is weather-sensitive, it is often the calm choice.

How to decide in two minutes

  1. Decide the experience first — whale watching, a day trip, a theme park, a dinner cruise.
  2. Find the operator’s name on any listing. That is your real search term.
  3. Search that operator on two platforms for the same date.
  4. Read the inclusions and the cancellation line on both.
  5. Then take the lower price — and keep the booking where the cancellation terms suit your plans.

For the Asia side, you can check the same tour on Klook and cross-check KKday, which often lists smaller local operators. For standalone attraction tickets, Tiqets is worth a look.

Frequently asked questions

Is Viator cheaper than Klook for the same activities?

Not as a rule. The same tour is priced by each operator's agreement with each platform, and prices move with demand, so either can be cheaper on a given date. Klook tends to be cheaper in Asia, where it has direct operator partnerships and frequent sales; Viator's volume keeps its prices competitive in Europe and the Americas. Compare the same operator and date on both.

Does Viator or Klook have more activities?

Viator, by a wide margin, with more than 400,000 experiences worldwide. Klook's catalogue is smaller overall but deeper in Asia-Pacific, and it also sells rail passes, airport transfers and eSIMs that Viator largely doesn't.

Is Klook or Viator better for families with kids?

In Asia, Klook — theme-park and attraction tickets with child prices, and private car charters sized for a whole family, up to 13 passengers in Tokyo. Elsewhere, look at Viator's private tours, which are often priced per group and can come close to the per-person cost of a public tour for a family of five or six.

Is Viator or Klook better for groups?

For a private guided tour, Viator, whose private and custom tours are priced per group. For your own itinerary with a driver in Asia, Klook's private car charters, which you choose by vehicle size.

Is Klook or Viator better for couples?

Neither has an edge. The same operators resell sunset cruises, dinner cruises and food tours across all three platforms, so compare the specific listing on price and cancellation terms.

Which has the best cancellation policy?

GetYourGuide applies its 24-hour free cancellation most consistently. Viator commonly offers a full refund up to 24 hours before, but not on every listing, and Klook's terms vary by product — some are non-refundable. Read the line on the listing itself.

Who owns Viator?

Viator is a Tripadvisor company. Klook is an independent company founded in Hong Kong in 2014.

Should I book on the platform or direct with the operator?

Check both. Direct is sometimes cheaper, because no commission is built into the price; a platform gives you card protection, clear cancellation terms and reviews in one place. For boat trips in particular, see where to book a Halong Bay cruise.
