August is the month when Edinburgh’s hotel bill stops being ordinary. The Fringe’s 80th edition is announced for 6 to 30 August 2027, the Royal Edinburgh Military Tattoo runs from 6 to 28 August, and rooms near the centre are in demand. Since 24 July 2026 the bill also carries a Visitor Levy: 5% of the cost of the room, charged on the first five nights only.

This guide covers five areas, from the Old Town on the Royal Mile to Leith on the waterfront, plus Glasgow as a train base, 12 hotels, the levy with a worked example, and how to get in from the airport. What is published for 2027, and what is not

The Fringe Society’s homepage says the Fringe’s 80th edition will take place from 6 to 30 August 2027. The Tattoo’s own page says its 2027 show, The Same Heart Beat, runs from 6 to 28 August 2027, and the Edinburgh Festivals site says tickets are on sale now, after an almost sold-out audience every night in 2026.

We could not find 2027 dates for the Edinburgh International Festival: its website showed only 2026 when we checked, so we give none. The Fringe’s venue list for 2027 had not loaded on its venues page either, so we do not tie any venue to an area beyond what the Fringe says about the Old Town. Check a show’s address in the Fringe programme before you choose a hotel for it.

The Fringe and the Tattoo both open on 6 August 2027, so the first week is busy from the first day. The Edinburgh Festivals site advises planning as far in advance as possible during the busy August festival season, and says early booking gives the best rates and city-centre locations.

Edinburgh’s Visitor Levy: 5% on the first five nights

Charge Rate in 2026-27
Edinburgh Visitor Levy 5% of the paid overnight accommodation cost, charged before VAT, for the first 5 nights of a stay
Start Stays from 24 July 2026
Not charged on Extras such as parking, meals, drinks or transport

The City of Edinburgh Council describes the levy as a 5% payment on the cost of the paid, overnight accommodation. It says the levy is charged before VAT, is only charged on the first 5 nights’ stay, applies at the same rate every day of the year and covers stays from 24 July 2026 onwards. The council’s announcement describes the cap as five nights in a row. The existing where to stay in Edinburgh guide goes through how VAT applies to the levy in more detail.

Our arithmetic: a room at £300 a night including 20% VAT is £250 before VAT, so the levy at 5% is £12.50 a night, or £62.50 over five nights. A nine-night stay still pays the levy on five nights only. Hotels display the levy in different ways, so compare the final total at the payment step, not the headline rate.

Getting in from the airport, and around

  • Tram. Edinburgh Trams lists an adult airport single at £7.90 and an adult airport open return at £9.50, and says trams run full route from every 7 minutes. Edinburgh Airport says the journey between the airport and the city centre takes 30 minutes, with a tram every 7 minutes between 6:30am and 7pm and every 10 minutes early in the morning and later in the evening. The tram runs through Haymarket, West End, Princes Street and St Andrew Square on its way to The Shore, Port of Leith, Ocean Terminal and Newhaven.
  • Airlink 100. The Fringe’s visiting page gives the Airlink 100 express bus as £6 for a single and £8.50 for a return. Lothian Buses describes it as a 24/7 express service between Edinburgh Airport and the city centre, leaving from Waverley Bridge in town.
  • On foot. The Fringe says Edinburgh is compact and that, during August, the quickest way to get between venues can often be on foot.
  • Trains. Waverley station is, in the Fringe’s words, in the heart of the city and close to most festival destinations, and Princes Street is served by many bus routes.

    Haymarket and the West End: lower prices and the tram

    The west side of the centre is where the tram from the airport arrives first, and where the Haymarket tram stop sits. It is not on the Royal Mile, so expect to take the tram, a bus or a longer walk to shows in the Old Town.

    The Old Town and Royal Mile: walk to the shows

    The Fringe says that in the Old Town you would not need more than five to ten minutes to walk from the Royal Mile to many Old Town venues. That makes the Royal Mile the easiest place to stay if you plan several shows a day, and the hardest place to find a bargain.

    Where to stay in the Old Town

    • Motel One Edinburgh-Royal, budget. At 18-21 Market Street, with a buffet breakfast served from 6:00 to 10:00 on weekdays and 7:00 to 11:00 at weekends and on public holidays. Choose it for the lowest price near the Royal Mile and Waverley. Watch out: there is no on-site parking; the hotel points to a public car park about a 15-minute walk away. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Radisson Blu Hotel, Edinburgh City Centre, comfortable. At 80 High Street on the Royal Mile, with an indoor pool and spa, check-in from 3:00pm and check-out at 12:00. Its Standard Room is listed at 27 m². Choose it for a full-service hotel on the Royal Mile itself. Watch out: Superior rooms are listed at 26 m², a little smaller than the Standard, so check the category you book. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • The Witchery, splurge. At 352 Castlehill on the Royal Mile, with nine named suites designed for two adults sharing a UK king-sized bed; booking direct gets the best rate and a complimentary bottle of Champagne in the suite. Choose it for a romantic splurge beside the castle gates. Watch out: the suites are for two adults, and the hotel cannot take reservations for more than six people. Check rates on Booking.com →

    The New Town and Princes Street: Georgian streets, a ten-minute walk to the Royal Mile

    The Fringe describes the city as having two distinct areas, both UNESCO listed: the Old Town, dominated by Edinburgh Castle, and the neoclassical New Town. A New Town hotel gives you wider pavements, a night away from the Royal Mile crowds and the tram on Princes Street, with the Old Town a walk across the gardens.

    Where to stay in the New Town and Princes Street

    • Kimpton Charlotte Square, splurge. At 38 Charlotte Square, in seven interconnected Georgian townhouses at the edge of a private garden, with a spa and gym. Choose it for the most elegant New Town setting. Watch out: the pages we read gave no room count or sizes, so ask before you book. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • The Balmoral, splurge. At 1 Princes Street, with its landmark clock tower, Number One Brasserie, Prince Bar and the Irene Forte Spa. Choose it for the best-known address between the Old and New Towns. Watch out: its main page gave no room rates, so check the total for your dates. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • The Caledonian Edinburgh, Curio Collection by Hilton, comfortable. On Princes Street, with views of Edinburgh Castle, a spa, check-in from 3:00pm and check-out at 12:00. The hotel says Princes Street Gardens, the Royal Mile and the Old Town are within 10 minutes. Choose it for castle views at a lower price than the Balmoral. Watch out: ten minutes is a walk, not a step, to a late show. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Southside and Newington: quieter, by Arthur’s Seat

    South of the Old Town, Ten Hill Place stands in a university quarter near Nicolson Street, and Prestonfield House sits further out in parkland. We could not load the Fringe’s venue list, so we cannot say which 2027 venues stand nearest; check your show’s address.

    The University of Edinburgh’s Residences, on the Pollock Halls campus, are open to the public in summer, but the university page we read does not mention festival visitors, so we have not counted them among the 12 hotels. They are described as 2-star accommodation with shared bathrooms.

    Where to stay in Southside and Newington

    • Ten Hill Place, comfortable. At 10 Hill Place, with 129 bedrooms in Classic and Superior categories and accessible rooms, run by the Royal College of Surgeons of Edinburgh, with discounted tickets for Surgeons’ Hall Museums. Choose it for a hotel with a quiet, university-quarter address. Watch out: Superior-room extras such as robes and slippers are offered only when you book direct. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Prestonfield House, splurge. On Priestfield Road, which the hotel describes as just five minutes from the Old Town, with 23 bedrooms and suites in 20 acres of gardens and parkland and the Rhubarb restaurant. Choose it for a country-house night after the festival crowds. Watch out: the hotel does not say how its five minutes are travelled, and with only 23 rooms it is likely to fill early. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Leith: the waterfront, a tram ride from Princes Street

    Edinburgh Trams runs from the airport to Newhaven by way of Princes Street, St Andrew Square and Leith, with stops at The Shore, Port of Leith and Ocean Terminal. Leith is where to stay for restaurants and water rather than for walking to shows.

    Where to stay in Leith

    • Malmaison Edinburgh, comfortable. At 1 Tower Place on the Leith waterfront, which the hotel calls only 10 minutes by taxi from the city centre. It has Chez Mal Brasserie & Bar and a 24/7 reception. Choose it for a waterfront base with a brasserie in the building. Watch out: the hotel has a cashless payment policy. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Fingal, splurge. At Alexandra Dock, a former Northern Lighthouse Board ship, open since 2019, with 23 cabins, AA five-star status and the Lighthouse Restaurant & Bar. Choose it for a night on the water. Watch out: tables at the restaurant must be booked in advance, so book a table with your cabin. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Glasgow as a train base

    Glasgow is an option only if you accept an evening commute. The Edinburgh Festivals site says Glasgow is only a 50-minute train journey and that trains leave there every 15 minutes for the capital. The council says the Edinburgh scheme covers the whole of the City of Edinburgh Council area; we have not checked whether Glasgow charges its own levy.

    What we could not confirm is the last train home. ScotRail’s timetable pages were behind a security check when we tried to read them, and we have no official 2027 timetable. The Fringe says ScotRail’s festival timetable is normally available in August, so look for it before you commit to a Glasgow base, and leave time for the Fringe’s late shows. We name no Glasgow hotels here; the 12 below are all in Edinburgh.

    How to book Edinburgh for the Fringe

    • Count the levy. The 5% applies to the first five nights, so a long stay costs proportionally less per night. Compare totals, not rates.
    • Watch the season. In our earlier check, linked from where to stay in Edinburgh, rooms for two weeknights in mid-August 2027 cost about twice as much as the same nights in November 2026, or more.
    • Plan for the Tattoo. The Tattoo runs from 6 to 28 August and is bought separately, so a stay that includes a Tattoo night has to be fitted to its performance dates.
    • Check room size and breakfast. Motel One, Radisson Blu and the Hampton each publish room or breakfast terms on their own sites; the New Town hotels publish fewer details.
    • Compare totals. Our hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book, and hotel taxes around the world sets Edinburgh’s levy beside other cities.

    Frequently asked questions

    When is the Edinburgh Fringe in 2027?

    The Fringe Society says the Fringe's 80th edition will take place from 6 to 30 August 2027. The Royal Edinburgh Military Tattoo runs from 6 to 28 August 2027. We found no 2027 dates for the Edinburgh International Festival on its website.

    Where is the best place to stay for the Fringe?

    The Old Town and Royal Mile are the easiest for walking to shows: the Fringe says many Old Town venues are five to ten minutes' walk from the Royal Mile. The New Town is calmer, Haymarket and the West End have the tram, Southside and Newington are quieter, and Leith is on the waterfront.

    Does Edinburgh charge a tourist tax?

    Yes. The Edinburgh Visitor Levy is 5% of the cost of paid overnight accommodation, charged before VAT, for the first five nights of a stay, for stays from 24 July 2026. It is not charged on extras such as parking, meals, drinks or transport.

    How do I get from Edinburgh Airport to the city centre?

    Edinburgh Trams lists an adult airport single at £7.90 and an open return at £9.50, and Edinburgh Airport says the journey to the city centre takes 30 minutes. The Fringe's visiting page gives the Airlink 100 express bus as £6 for a single and £8.50 for a return.

    Can I stay in Glasgow for the Fringe?

    It is possible: Edinburgh Festivals says Glasgow is a 50-minute train journey with trains every 15 minutes. We could not confirm the last train home because ScotRail's timetable pages were not readable; the Fringe says ScotRail's festival timetable is normally available in August.

    When should I book a hotel for the Fringe?

    As early as you can. Edinburgh Festivals advises planning as far in advance as possible for the August festival season, and says early booking gives the best rates and city-centre locations.
