Hong Kong’s New Year’s Eve is not the fixed fireworks show many visitors expect. For the 2025 countdown the Government said the event would forgo the fireworks display and take place in an alternative format, and the Hong Kong Tourism Board (HKTB) held the countdown at Chater Road in Central. The 2026 plan was not public when we checked, so the right hotel depends on arrangements that usually appear in December. Hong Kong hotels also add a 3% Hotel Accommodation Tax to the room charge, on top of a service charge that is not taxed.

This guide covers what the authorities published for 31 December 2025, how to read it for Thursday 31 December 2026, six areas, 12 hotels, and the terms that catch people out. What was published for 31 December 2025, and what we do not yet know for 2026

We could not find an announcement for 31 December 2026 when we checked in November, so everything below about arrangements is last year’s, and we label it as such. Last year the Government announced the plan in a notice of 17 December 2025, HKTB issued its programme on 24 December, and the Transport Department published its traffic notice on 30 December. If the pattern holds, expect the 2026 details in the second half of December; check discoverhongkong.com and the Transport Department notices before you commit to a district.

  • No fireworks, an alternative format. The Government’s notice said the countdown would forgo the fireworks display and take place in an alternative format, with HKTB to announce the details separately. We do not know whether the 2026 event will return to the harbour, so do not book a room for a fireworks view.
  • The venue was Chater Road. HKTB’s release placed the 2025 countdown at the Chater Road Pedestrian Precinct in Central, with music from 11:30pm on New Year’s Eve to 12:10am on New Year’s Day and a three-minute light performance on Central landmarks at midnight.
  • Other places joined in. Large screens carried the show at Chater Garden and Edinburgh Place, an outdoor live projection was shown at the Hong Kong Cultural Centre in Tsim Sha Tsui, and a giant countdown clock was shown on the Hong Kong Convention and Exhibition Centre in Wan Chai. Hong Kong Disneyland and, for the first time, the West Kowloon Cultural District ran their own countdown events.
  • Crowd control was the Police’s call. The Government said the Police would arrange sufficient manpower for crowd management and special traffic arrangements as necessary. The Transport Department said road closures and diversions in the vicinity of Central would be implemented in phases from 2pm, with the Police free to extend the closed area, and that the Police might introduce ad-hoc measures in the Tsim Sha Tsui area at short notice. Its notice did not list closures for Wan Chai or Causeway Bay.

Getting around on the night

  • The MTR ran overnight last year. The Transport Department said train frequencies on all MTR lines except the Airport Express would be enhanced in phases from 3pm on 31 December, and the Island, Tsuen Wan and Kwun Tong lines, among others, would run overnight. The East Rail line ran overnight between Admiralty and Sheung Shui; the last train to Lo Wu left Admiralty at 12:56am. The Airport Express’s hours were extended.
  • Buses and minibuses may be diverted. The notice said routes in affected areas could be diverted, suspended or have stops changed, and listed eleven special overnight bus services.
  • The Mid-Levels Escalator ran later. Its hours were extended to 3am on 1 January.
  • Check before you travel. The Police can change closures on the night, and the Transport Department asked drivers to avoid the affected areas and the public to use public transport. The notice did not mention the Star Ferry, so check its timetable separately.

    Central and Admiralty: closest to last year’s countdown

    Central is where the 2025 countdown was staged, so a hotel here puts you near the event if it returns to Chater Road, and also inside the area the Transport Department said would close from 2pm. Admiralty sits next door on the same Island Line, and last year’s overnight East Rail service ran to it. Ask any hotel in this area in writing how guests get through the closed streets on the night; none of the hotels below publishes that.

    Wan Chai: the Convention Centre side

    Wan Chai has the Convention and Exhibition Centre on the harbour, where last year’s giant countdown clock was shown, and Lockhart Road’s bars and restaurants behind it. The Transport Department’s notice listed no Wan Chai closures last year, which makes it easier to come and go than Central, though that can change.

    Where to stay in Wan Chai

    • The Hari Hong Kong, comfortable. At 330 Lockhart Road, with 210 guest rooms and suites that have a terrace. Its own page gives the Convention and Exhibition Centre as 8 minutes’ walk, Exhibition Centre MTR station 7 and Causeway Bay MTR station 6. It describes rooms with views over Wan Chai, Causeway Bay, the Peak and Victoria Harbour. Choose it for a design hotel between the two districts. Watch out: the page does not say which rooms have which view, so ask. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Wharney Hotel, good value. At 57-73 Lockhart Road, Wanchai, with free Wi-Fi in all guest rooms and complimentary standard mini-bar refreshments, by its own account. Choose it for Wan Chai at a modest price. Watch out: its page lists no New Year’s Eve terms, so confirm the rate and any minimum stay for 31 December. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Causeway Bay: shopping streets and trams

    Causeway Bay is a retail and dining district next to Wan Chai on the Island Line, and the Hong Kong Island trams run through it. It is not among the areas the Transport Department named for closures last year, but expect crowds on the night.

    Where to stay in Causeway Bay

    • Regal Hongkong Hotel, comfortable. At 88 Yee Wo Street, a 35-floor hotel with 481 guestrooms including 33 suites, and a three-minute walk to Causeway Bay MTR station by its own count. Choose it for a large hotel on the shopping streets. Watch out: the pages we read gave no New Year’s Eve terms, so confirm them. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Lanson Place Causeway Bay, comfortable. A small luxury hotel that its page says was recently redesigned by Pierre-Yves Rochon. Choose it for a quieter, boutique feel in a busy district. Watch out: its page publishes no New Year’s Eve package, and we could not confirm a walking time to the station from its own site. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Tsim Sha Tsui: Nathan Road and the Kowloon waterfront

    Tsim Sha Tsui faces the island’s skyline across the harbour, and last year HKTB projected a live show on the Hong Kong Cultural Centre here. The Transport Department said the Police might close roads, divert traffic and change public transport stops in the area at short notice, so allow extra time, and ask your hotel how its entrance is reached if streets close.

    Where to stay in Tsim Sha Tsui

    • The Langham, Hong Kong, splurge. At 8 Peking Road, with Palm Court, T’ang Court and The Food Gallery among its dining rooms. Choose it for a five-star Kowloon base. Watch out: its site published no New Year’s Eve package when we looked, so ask for the terms for 31 December. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Kowloon Shangri-La, splurge. At 64 Mody Road, Tsim Sha Tsui East, about 35 minutes from the airport by its own count. Its offers page lists festive dining offers to 1 January 2027 but no New Year’s Eve room package. Choose it for a large hotel at the east end of the district. Watch out: it is in Tsim Sha Tsui East, so check the walk to the waterfront and the nearest station on a map. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • The Mira Hong Kong, comfortable. At Mira Place, 118-130 Nathan Road, with 489 designer guestrooms including 56 suites in four colour themes. Choose it for a design hotel on Nathan Road.     Mong Kok and Jordan: lower prices, the MTR on both sides

    North of Tsim Sha Tsui, Nathan Road gets cheaper and busier, and the Tsuen Wan Line carries you back across the harbour to Central. It is a ride to the Central closures, not a walk, and last year’s overnight service covered the Tsuen Wan Line.

    Where to stay in Mong Kok and Jordan

    • Nathan Hotel, good value. At 378 Nathan Road, which its page puts a five-minute walk from Jordan MTR station, Exit B2. Choose it for a Nathan Road base at a modest price. Watch out: its page lists no New Year’s Eve terms, so confirm the rate for 31 December. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Royal Plaza Hotel, comfortable. At 193 Prince Edward Road West, sitting on top of Mong Kok East station, with an 11-minute train ride to Exhibition Centre station by its own count. Choose it for the East Rail line at your door. Watch out: Mong Kok East is on the East Rail line, a different station from Mong Kok on the Tsuen Wan Line, so for the Island Line you change trains. Check rates on Booking.com →

    North Point and Quarry Bay: the island’s eastern harbour

    East of Causeway Bay, North Point and Quarry Bay sit away from the Central closures and face the harbour’s eastern side. They are on the Island Line, and Harbour Plaza puts Causeway Bay ten minutes away, so it suits you if you want a harbour-facing room without paying for Central.

    Where to stay in North Point and Quarry Bay

    • Harbour Grand Hong Kong, comfortable. At 23 Oil Street, North Point, by MTR Fortress Hill station, Exit A, with the 188 Restaurant & Lounge, which its page says has a 188-degree harbour view. Choose it for a harbour-side hotel on the east. Watch out: the restaurant’s view is not a promise about your room, so ask which rooms face the water. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Harbour Plaza North Point, good value. At 665 King’s Road, a two-minute walk from Quarry Bay MTR station, Exit C, by its own count, with most rooms looking at the eastern side of Victoria Harbour. Choose it for a harbour view without Central prices. Watch out: its page lists city, hill and harbour views as separate options, so check which you are booking. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Hong Kong’s hotel tax: 3% on the room, not on the service charge

    Charge Rate in 2026
    Hotel Accommodation Tax (HAT) 3% of the accommodation charge, charged again since 1 January 2025
    Service charge Normally 10% of the room charge; exempt from HAT

    The tax is on the room, and the Inland Revenue Department says service charges are exempt, so a bill runs about 13% above the room rate. On a HK$2,000 room, the service charge is HK$200 and the tax HK$60, a total of HK$2,260 (our arithmetic from the published rates). Check whether a price already includes both, and compare the final total across sites; our hotel taxes around the world sets Hong Kong beside other cities.

    Getting in from the airport

    • Airport Express. The MTR says the 35.3km journey between the airport and the city takes as little as 24 minutes. An adult single costs HK$120 to Hong Kong station or HK$105 to Kowloon station with an Octopus card, a contactless bank card or a QR code, and HK$130 or HK$115 with a smart ticket.
    • On the night. Last year the Airport Express’s hours were extended on 31 December; check the MTR’s notice for 2026 before you plan a late arrival.

    How to book New Year’s Eve in Hong Kong

    • Wait for the plan, but not too long. The 2026 arrangements had not been announced when we checked, and last year’s came in the second half of December. If your dates are fixed, pick a district for its MTR links rather than for a view.
    • Do not book for fireworks. Last year’s countdown had no fireworks. If a hotel promises a fireworks view for 2026, ask what the promise rests on.
    • Count the tax and the service charge. Compare the final total, not the headline rate.
    • Ask about the closures. None of the 12 hotels publishes how guests get through closed streets, or a New Year’s Eve minimum stay, on the pages we read.
    • Check the cancellation terms. Compare them across sites; our hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book.
    • Book as early as your plans allow. We cannot tell you when each hotel sells out.

    Frequently asked questions

    Does Hong Kong have New Year's Eve fireworks in 2026?

    We do not know yet. The 2026 plan was not public when we checked in November 2026. For 31 December 2025 the Government said the countdown would forgo the fireworks display and take place in an alternative format, and the Hong Kong Tourism Board held it at Chater Road in Central. Do not book a room for a fireworks view unless the 2026 plan confirms one.

    Where was the Hong Kong New Year countdown held last year?

    The Hong Kong Tourism Board placed the 2025 countdown at the Chater Road Pedestrian Precinct in Central, with music from 11:30pm to 12:10am. Screens were set up at Chater Garden and Edinburgh Place, a live projection was shown at the Hong Kong Cultural Centre in Tsim Sha Tsui, and a countdown clock was shown on the Hong Kong Convention and Exhibition Centre in Wan Chai.

    Where is the best place to stay in Hong Kong for New Year's Eve?

    It depends on the 2026 plan, which usually appears in December. Last year's countdown was in Central, with road closures from 2pm, so Central, Admiralty and Wan Chai suit people who want to be near it, while Causeway Bay, North Point and Quarry Bay sit east of the closures. Tsim Sha Tsui, Mong Kok and Jordan are on the Kowloon side, with the MTR across the harbour.

    Is the MTR running all night on New Year's Eve in Hong Kong?

    Last year it was. The Transport Department said MTR train frequencies, except on the Airport Express, would be enhanced in phases from 3pm on 31 December and that most lines would run overnight, with the Airport Express hours extended. The 2026 notice had not been published when we checked, so confirm it in late December.

    Is there a tax on hotel rooms in Hong Kong?

    Yes. Hotel Accommodation Tax has been charged again since 1 January 2025, at 3% of the room charge. The service charge, normally 10% of the room charge, is exempt from the tax, so a bill runs about 13% above the room rate. On a HK$2,000 room that is HK$200 service charge and HK$60 tax, our arithmetic from the published rates.

    How do I get from Hong Kong airport to my hotel on New Year's Eve?

    The Airport Express takes as little as 24 minutes over 35.3km. An adult single costs HK$120 to Hong Kong station or HK$105 to Kowloon station with Octopus, a contactless bank card or QR code, and HK$130 or HK$115 with a smart ticket. Last year its hours were extended on 31 December; check the MTR's 2026 notice.
