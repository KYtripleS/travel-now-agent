Hotels around Taipei Main Station, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Taipei Main Station that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

Taipei is an easy city to stay in well, because the MRT reaches almost everything you will want to see. The choice is about what is outside your door: the station that takes you to the airport and the high-speed rail, a night market, Taipei 101, a quiet street of cafés, or a hot spring. The city’s trains meet at Taipei Main Station, and the rest follows from how far from it you want to be.

This guide compares five areas, then names twelve hotels across them, with the reason to pick each and, where there is one, the catch. They are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating at the start of October 2026. Two of the twelve are in Beitou, the hot-spring district on a branch of the MRT’s Red Line, for a night in a room with its own spring-water bath. No hotel pays to appear here.

Taipei Main Station and Zhongshan: the first-trip base

Taipei Main Station is where the city’s trains meet: the Red and Blue MRT lines, the high-speed rail, the conventional railway and, at its own station next door, the Airport MRT, whose express trains make only two stops before the airport. Zhongshan, one stop north on the Red Line, is the pleasanter place to walk out of, with department stores on Nanjing West Road, cafés and small shops in the lanes behind them, and Dihua Street, in old Dadaocheng, a walk to the west. Both stations are on the Red Line, so Taipei 101 is one train away.

Ximending: food, shopping and value

Ximending is Taipei’s youth-culture quarter, a grid of pedestrian streets around Ximen station, where the Blue and Green lines meet, full of street food, sneaker shops, cinemas and crowds until late. The Red House, an octagonal market hall built in 1908, stands at its edge, and Longshan Temple is one stop west. It has many of central Taipei’s lower-priced hotels, and Main Station is one stop east.

Where to stay in Ximending

  • amba Taipei Ximending, good value. A design-led hotel from the Ambassador group in the middle of Ximending, about seven minutes’ walk from the Red House. Choose it for the neighbourhood at your door. Watch out: Ximending is loud and busy late into the night. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Just Sleep Taipei Ximending, good value. A simple, well-reviewed hotel about two minutes’ walk from Ximen station, renovated in 2019, with self-service laundry and a breakfast buffet. Choose it for a low-fuss base by the MRT. Watch out: there is little beyond the room itself. Check rates on Booking.com →

Xinyi: Taipei 101 and the big hotels

Xinyi is Taipei’s modern business district, laid out around Taipei 101, the city’s tallest building, with department stores and malls joined by skybridges. Taipei City Hall station on the Blue Line and Taipei 101/World Trade Center station on the Red Line serve it, and Elephant Mountain, where the photograph at the top of this guide was taken, is a short walk from Xiangshan station. It suits a trip built around shopping, dining and the night view, and it costs more than the older districts.

Where to stay in Xinyi

  • Grand Hyatt Taipei, splurge. Open since 1990 on Songshou Road beside Taipei 101, with about 850 rooms and an outdoor pool. Choose it for Xinyi at your door. Watch out: its size. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • W Taipei, splurge. 405 rooms on the 8th to 31st floors of the building above Taipei City Hall station, opened in 2011, with an outdoor pool. Choose it for nightlife and the MRT in the same building. Watch out: price, and a lively bar scene at night. Check rates on Booking.com →

Da’an and the East District: cafés and quieter streets

Da’an is residential Taipei at its most pleasant: Yongkang Street, by Dongmen station, where Din Tai Fung began; Da’an Forest Park; the Shida night market near National Taiwan Normal University; and, on its northern edge, the East District’s department stores and side streets around Zhongxiao Fuxing and Zhongxiao Dunhua stations. It is quieter at night than Ximending and more central than Xinyi.

Where to stay in Da’an and the East District

  • Shangri-La Far Eastern, Taipei, splurge. A tower on Dunhua South Road with views to Taipei 101 from the upper floors and a rooftop pool. Choose it for the view and a full-service hotel between the East District and Xinyi. Watch out: the nearest MRT station is a walk away. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Proverbs Taipei, splurge. A design hotel on Da’an Road, a member of Design Hotels, next to Zhongxiao Fuxing station, where the Blue and Brown lines meet, with a rooftop pool. Choose it for a boutique stay in the shopping district. Watch out: price. Check rates on Booking.com →

Beitou: a night in the hot springs

Beitou, on the city’s northern edge, has been a hot-spring resort since the early twentieth century. Its public bathhouse of 1913 is now the Beitou Hot Spring Museum, free to enter, and the steaming Thermal Valley is a short walk up the stream. Take the Red Line to Beitou and change for the one-stop branch to Xinbeitou. A night here, in a room with its own spring-water bath, pairs well with a few nights in the centre.

Where to stay in Beitou

  • Grand View Resort Beitou, splurge. Every room has its own hot- and cold-spring baths and a balcony, and there is an outdoor pool; it is a short drive from Xinbeitou station. Choose it for a resort night with the spring in your room. Watch out: children under 12 are not allowed in the public hot spring. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Royal Beitou, comfortable. Opposite Xinbeitou station, with Beitou’s white-sulphur spring water, about 45 to 50°C, piped to a bath in every room. Choose it for a hot-spring night without a taxi. Watch out: sulphur water tarnishes silver, so take jewellery off before you bathe. Check rates on Booking.com →

Before you book

  • Read the rate. Many Taiwanese hotels add a 10% service charge to the room rate, and tipping is not customary; check whether the price you see includes the charge and the tax, and compare totals (Taiwan Tourism Administration).
  • Fill in the Taiwan Arrival Card online. Since 1 October 2025 the paper arrival card has been replaced by the online Taiwan Arrival Card, which is free, is submitted on the National Immigration Agency’s site in the week before you arrive, and asks where you are staying (National Immigration Agency).
  • Choose the station before the hotel. A five-minute walk to the MRT matters more than a bigger room further out. If you are still choosing a booking site, our hotel booking sites compared goes through them.

Getting around from your hotel

  • From Taoyuan Airport, the Airport MRT’s express trains run to Taipei Main Station with only two stops on the way, and several airlines let you check in your bags at Taipei Main Station before you head to the airport; the Taoyuan Metro site lists which (Taoyuan Metro).
  • Around town, an EasyCard pays for the MRT, buses and convenience-store purchases. For trains beyond Taipei, see getting around Taiwan and whether the high-speed rail pass is worth it.
  • Before you fly, sort out mobile data (the best eSIM for Taiwan), and read Taipei for first-timers, things to do in Taipei and where to book a Taipei day tour.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Taipei for a first trip?

Around Taipei Main Station or Zhongshan, one stop north: the Airport MRT, the high-speed rail and the Red and Blue MRT lines meet at Main Station, and Zhongshan has department stores and cafés on the Red and Green lines.

Is Ximending a good place to stay in Taipei?

Yes, for food, shopping and lower-priced hotels: it is a grid of pedestrian streets around Ximen station, one stop from Taipei Main Station. The catch is noise; it is busy late into the night.

Should I stay in Xinyi near Taipei 101?

If your trip is about shopping, dining and the night view, yes, but Xinyi costs more than the older districts and is further from the airport train. Grand Hyatt Taipei and W Taipei are both there.

Can I stay in a hot-spring hotel in Taipei?

Yes, in Beitou, on a branch of the MRT's Red Line. Grand View Resort Beitou and Hotel Royal Beitou both have a hot-spring bath in every room.

Do Taipei hotels add a service charge?

Many add a 10% service charge to the room rate, and tipping is not customary. Check whether the price you see includes the charge and the tax before comparing.

Do I need an arrival card for Taiwan?

Yes. Since 1 October 2025 the paper card has been replaced by the online Taiwan Arrival Card, which is free and is submitted on the National Immigration Agency's site in the week before you arrive.
