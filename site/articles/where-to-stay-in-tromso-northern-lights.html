    The season: what the official sources say

    Aurora months. Visit Tromsø says the northern lights may be visible from late August to early April, and that clear skies and darkness are essential. It adds that Tromsø lies within the auroral zone. That is a description of conditions, not a forecast: cloud can hide the lights even when they are active, and no hotel or tour can promise a sighting.

    Polar night. Visit Tromsø gives the polar night as lasting approximately from 27 November to 15 January. From mid-January the light gradually returns, and because the surrounding mountains block the low sun, it is usually not visible from central Tromsø until later in January.

    What that means for where you sleep. Darkness is the condition a hotel can influence least. A room in the town sits among streetlights and ships’ lights; Malangen Resort says its wilderness camp, a short walk from the resort, has minimal light pollution, which is the resort’s own claim. In town, a window facing the water or a rooftop bar is a convenience, not a guarantee. Most visitors in the season also take an evening tour or drive out; check each hotel’s pick-up arrangements with the hotel.

    Norway’s VAT and the planned visitor fee

    VAT. The Norwegian Tax Administration’s rates page lists letting of rooms, with accommodation in the footnote, at 12%, against a normal rate of 25%.

    The 3% visitor fee (besøksbidrag). The Storting’s law on visitor contributions, passed on 5 June 2025, lets a municipality charge an overnight fee calculated as a 3% surcharge on the payment for the stay, excluding VAT. It applies to stays of under 30 consecutive days, covers the whole municipality, and must be shown separately in the sales documents. The duty to pay arises when the stay begins, and the municipality may set which months of the year the fee applies.

    Tromsø’s status, as of 28 October 2026:

    Step Date Source
    Municipal council (kommunestyret) decides to introduce the overnight fee 24 June 2026 City of Tromsø
    Planned start of the fee 1 January 2027 City of Tromsø
    Draft local regulation on public consultation Responses due 30 October 2026 City of Tromsø
    Ministry approval of Tromsø’s plan Required before the fee can take effect; we found no published approval Storting law, § 2-2

    The city says the consultation does not cover the rate, which is set in the law at 3%. So treat 1 January 2027 as planned, not final. A stay that begins before that date falls before the planned start; one beginning in January 2027 or later falls after it, if the plan is approved and the final regulation sets no different months.

    Our arithmetic, for a quoted rate that includes VAT: five nights at NOK 2,400 a night is NOK 12,000. Without 12% VAT that is about NOK 10,714, and 3% of that is about NOK 321, which would bring the bill to about NOK 12,321 if the fee is added on top. Check whether a quote includes VAT and the fee, and compare final totals.

    Getting in from the airport, and around

    • Airport Express (Flybussen). Run by Bussring, it stops at the airport and at several hotels. The operator’s stop list includes The Dock Hotel, Scandic Ishavshotel, Havneterminalen (Prostneset), Storgata 44, Polaria on Strandveien and Moxy Hotel. Its price list shows NOK 125 one way and NOK 200 return for an adult, dated from 1 November 2024, so confirm the current fare when you book. Bussring says the trip to the airport takes about 15 minutes; Visit Tromsø says about 20. Svipper warns that Flybussen tickets cannot be bought in the Svipper app, and Visit Tromsø says the bus has dedicated luggage space.
    • City buses. Routes 24, 26, 40 and 42 stop at Tromsø Lufthavn Langnes, with limited luggage space. Tickets are sold in the Svipper app, at ticket machines, or by Visa or Mastercard on board; cash is not accepted. Svipper’s adult single ticket costs NOK 50 for one zone from 1 February 2026, with an off-peak single in Tromsø at NOK 30 on weekdays from 9:00 to 14:00 and from 17:00 to 1:00.
    • Taxi. Avinor says the rank is to the left outside the arrivals hall, the ride to the centre takes about 10 minutes, and drivers must tell you the price in advance. Visit Tromsø says prices vary between operators.
    • Out of town. Malangen Resort runs a shuttle bus from Tromsø city centre at fixed times from 1 December to 19 April, and a flexible shuttle from 20 April to 30 November. Tromsø Lodge & Camping says the centre is 5 minutes away by car or 30 minutes on foot.

    Sentrum: shops, restaurants and a short walk

    Sentrum is the practical base for a short stay. Four hotels sit in the streets around Storgata, Grønnegata and Vestregata, and the Airport Express stops on Storgata. The trade-off is light: you are in the middle of the town’s glow.

Choose it for an affordable central bed. Watch out: the hotel describes itself as having no unnecessary extras, and parking is from NOK 42 an hour. Check rates on Booking.com →

    The harbour: the quay and the Hurtigruten

    The harbour streets (Fredrik Langes gate, Kaigata, Skansegata and Sjøgata) put you beside the water, with ships at the quay and mountains across the strait. Two of the hotels below are Airport Express stops.

    Where to stay at the harbour

    • Scandic Ishavshotel, comfortable. At Fredrik Langesgate 2, by the quayside and 50 metres from the Hurtigruten ferry, with a restaurant called Roast, a breakfast buffet and a 24-hour shop. Standard rooms are 19 to 21 m². Choose it for the quay and an Airport Express stop on the doorstep. Watch out: entry-level single rooms are 16 to 20 m². Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Clarion Hotel The Edge, comfortable. At Kaigata 6, right by the water, about 10 minutes from the airport by the hotel’s own account, with views of the fjords and mountains, a lobby bar and a gym with a sauna included for guests. Superior twin rooms are 16 to 22 m². Choose it for a large, central hotel on the water. Watch out: breakfast is included only with some rates, and parking is from NOK 40 an hour. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • The Dock 69°39 by Scandic, splurge. At Skansegata 7, 0.5 km from the city centre, with a rooftop bar, an outdoor terrace, a jacuzzi and a wellness area. Standard rooms are 16 to 18 m² and superior rooms 20 to 21 m². Choose it for a premium harbour stay with a wellness area. Watch out: Scandic lists wellness and sauna as an entrance-fee amenity for guests aged 16 and over. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Home Hotel With, good value. At Sjøgata 35-37 in the harbour area, with breakfast, fika and dinner included in the room rate, a sauna and a gym. Choose it for a quayside base where meals are already paid for. Watch out: rooms are small, with the compact double at 13 m² and the standard twin at 15 m². Check rates on Booking.com →

    Tromsøya’s quieter ends: a spa, and the airport side

    Away from the harbour, the island has two options that suit different trips: a spa hotel in a quiet street, and a modern hotel at the airport.

    Where to stay on quieter Tromsøya

    • St. Elisabeth Hotel & Spa, splurge. At Mellomvegen 50, which the hotel describes as a peaceful location 10 minutes from the city centre. It runs daily spa sessions (morning 9:00 to 12:00 with children aged 10 and over, a night spa from 18:00 to 21:00 Tuesday to Sunday), has a top-floor restaurant and includes a gourmet breakfast. Choose it for a spa evening between aurora trips. Watch out: the spa is adults-only after 13:00 and pass-based, and Booking.com also lists a separate suites property at the same address, so check that you are booking the hotel rooms. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Moxy Tromsø, good value. At Huldervegen 2, by Tromsø Airport according to Visit Tromsø, with an airport shuttle run with the Airport Express, a sky bar, a breakfast buffet and a 24-hour grab-and-go. Rooms are compact by design, and the hotel holds Green Key and BREEAM certificates. Choose it for an early flight or a late arrival. Watch out: it is on the airport side rather than by the harbour, and parking is NOK 25 an hour or NOK 170 a day. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Out of town: Tromsdalen and Malangen

    The two out-of-town options below are reached by road. One is five minutes from the centre by car, the other is more than an hour away.

    Where to stay out of town

    • Tromsø Lodge & Camping, good value. In Tromsdalen, with 58 cabins in five categories, from small modern cabins to large ones for families and groups, plus space for tents and motorhomes. It is open all year, with free parking and a breakfast buffet; by car the centre is 5 minutes away, or 30 minutes on foot. Choose it for cabin space at a fair price. Watch out: cabin types differ widely, and the walk into town is long on a winter night, so plan a bus or taxi. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Malangen Resort, splurge. At Skutvik, 70 minutes’ drive south of Tromsø, with 10 hotel rooms, cabins and 20 waterfront apartments, and a wilderness camp it says has minimal light pollution and hosts dog sledding and snowshoeing. The resort says it sits in one of the best northern lights hotspots in the Tromsø area. Choose it for a night-sky stay away from town. Watch out: it is 70 minutes each way, the 1 December to 19 April shuttle runs at fixed times, and the hotspot line is the resort’s claim, not a promise. Check rates on Booking.com →

    How to book Tromsø for the northern lights

    • Book with flexibility. A tour or a night in the cold is weather-dependent, so choose a rate you can change, and compare the cancellation terms across sites.
    • Pick the base by how you will move. Without a car, Sentrum and the harbour give you buses and the Airport Express; Malangen means the shuttle timetable.
    • Count the tax. VAT is 12%. The 3% fee is planned from 1 January 2027 and needs approval, so check the final regulation, the months it covers and whether the quote includes it.
    • Check room size. Several city rooms are 9 to 16 m², so check the figure before you book if you will spend long hours inside in the polar night.
    • Compare totals. Hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book, and direct booking versus online agencies covers when to go direct.

    Frequently asked questions

    When is the northern lights season in Tromsø?

    Visit Tromsø says the northern lights may be visible from late August to early April, but that clear skies and darkness are essential. That describes conditions, not a forecast: no hotel or tour can promise a sighting.

    When is the polar night in Tromsø?

    Visit Tromsø gives the polar night as lasting approximately from 27 November to 15 January, with only a few hours of twilight a day. The light gradually returns from mid-January, and the sun is usually not visible from central Tromsø until later in January because of the surrounding mountains.

    What is the best area to stay in Tromsø for the northern lights?

    Sentrum is the practical base for shops, restaurants and the Airport Express stop on Storgata, and the harbour puts you by the Hurtigruten quay. For more distance from the town's lights, Malangen Resort is a 70-minute drive south of Tromsø and says its wilderness camp has minimal light pollution, though that is the resort's own claim, not a promise of a sighting.

    Does Tromsø charge a tourist tax on hotel stays?

    Not yet, but one is planned. Tromsø's council decided on 24 June 2026 to introduce an overnight fee from 1 January 2027, set in the national law at 3% of the payment for the stay excluding VAT. The ministry must approve the city's plan first, we found no published approval as of 28 October 2026, and the draft local regulation was on consultation until 30 October 2026, so the start date is planned, not final.

    What VAT applies to hotels in Norway?

    The Norwegian Tax Administration's rates page lists letting of rooms, with accommodation in the footnote, at the 12% low rate, against a normal rate of 25%. Check whether a quoted rate includes VAT and compare final totals.

    How do I get from Tromsø Airport to the city centre?

    The Airport Express (Flybussen), run by Bussring, stops at several hotels; its price list shows NOK 125 one way and NOK 200 return for an adult, dated from 1 November 2024, so confirm the fare. City bus routes 24, 26, 40 and 42 also stop at the airport, with tickets in the Svipper app. Avinor says taxis leave from the rank outside the arrivals hall, the ride takes about 10 minutes, and drivers must give the price in advance.
