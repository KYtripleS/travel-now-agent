Where to Book a Halong Bay Cruise: Klook, Viator, or Direct?

By Casey, Gently Yonder editor

Booking direct with the boat operator is often cheaper, because the platform commission is inside the price. When the platform is still worth paying for, what a day trip and an overnight actually cost, and which bay you are really entering.

Last updated 16 Sep 2026 · 7 min read

Gently Yonder’s verdict: Compare the same boat on a platform and on the operator’s own site — direct is often cheaper. Pay the platform premium when you want card protection and clear cancellation terms: Klook for day trips from Hanoi, Viator for the widest range of overnights.

Check Halong Bay cruises on Klook →

This is the one tour on our list where the honest answer is often not a booking platform at all.

Halong Bay cruises are sold by the boat operators themselves, and most of those operators run their own websites. When you book through Klook, Viator or GetYourGuide, the platform’s commission is inside the price you pay. Book the same cabin on the same boat directly with the operator and you will frequently pay less.

Settle these two questions before you open any platform

1. Day trip or overnight? They are different products at different prices:

Format Typical price What you get
Basic day trip from about US$13 Transport and a short cruise; often more bus than boat
Premium day trip around US$29–50 Kayaking, lunch, a longer time on the water
Overnight, 1 night about US$99–130 A cabin, the evening and the early morning on the bay
2 nights from about US$365 Further into Lan Ha or Bai Tu Long, away from the day fleet

The day trip is around four hours of road for a few hours afloat. It works if Halong is the only thing you want from a spare day. If you can spare the night, the difference is not marginal — the bay after the day boats leave is a genuinely different place.

2. Which bay? Cruises depart into Halong Bay, Lan Ha Bay or Bai Tu Long Bay. Lan Ha and Bai Tu Long carry the same limestone seascape with noticeably fewer boats in the anchorage. Our guide to Halong Bay cruises from Hanoi works through the routes and the road journey in full.

Only once you’ve fixed the format and the bay does the platform question mean anything — because then you are comparing the same boat rather than two different holidays.

When the platform is worth the commission

Booking direct is cheaper. It is not always better. The case for paying the platform:

  • Payment. Vietnamese operators commonly ask for a bank transfer or an advance deposit. A platform booking stays on your card, with the recourse a card gives you.
  • Cancellation. Halong is weather-dependent, and sailings do get cancelled. Platform terms are written in plain English and enforceable from another country. A direct booking’s terms may be neither.
  • Reviews you can weigh. Boats are refitted, renamed and resold constantly. A platform’s recent review volume is the fastest way to see what a boat is like now rather than what its own website says.
  • One place to argue from. If the boat isn’t the boat in the photographs, a platform gives you somewhere to take that.

The practical approach most experienced travellers land on: use a platform to find the boat and read the reviews, check the operator’s own site for the same dates, then decide whether the gap is worth giving up the card protection. Often it is not, and you book on the platform anyway with your eyes open.

What to check on the listing

  1. Which bay? Halong, Lan Ha and Bai Tu Long are sold under the same headline.
  2. How many boats at anchor? Recent reviews will tell you; the listing won’t.
  3. Is the transfer included, and is it a limousine van or a coach? This is four hours each way.
  4. What’s the cancellation term if the operator cancels for weather? Refund or credit?
  5. Then compare the same boat on a platform and on the operator’s own site.

Frequently asked questions

Is it cheaper to book a Halong Bay cruise direct or on Klook?

Direct with the operator is frequently cheaper, because the platform's commission is inside the price you pay on Klook, Viator or GetYourGuide. The trade-off is that operators often want a bank transfer or deposit, while a platform booking stays on your card with clearer cancellation terms.

How much does a Halong Bay cruise cost?

Basic day trips start around US$13 and premium day cruises with kayaking and lunch run about US$29 to 50. One-night overnight cruises typically cost US$99 to 130, and two-night itineraries start around US$365.

Is a Halong Bay day trip worth it?

It depends on your time. A day trip is roughly four hours of road for a few hours on the water. If you can give it a night, the bay after the day fleet has gone is a substantially different experience: quieter water, and the early morning to yourself.

Halong Bay, Lan Ha Bay or Bai Tu Long Bay, which should I book?

Lan Ha and Bai Tu Long offer much the same limestone seascape as Halong Bay with fewer boats in the anchorage. Halong proper is the busiest and the most heavily sold. Check which bay a listing actually enters, because all three are advertised under the same headline.

Which platform has the most Halong Bay cruises?

Viator carries the largest catalogue by a clear margin, including multi-day and less common itineraries. Klook is strong on the Hanoi-pickup day-cruise format that most first-time visitors book.
