Kyoto’s New Year is quiet and ritual rather than a party. There is no countdown crowd to book around. What the city has is the midnight temple bells, hatsumode (the first shrine visit of the year) and a fire at Yasaka Shrine that burns through the night. The trains matter more here than in most cities, because last year the city subway, the buses, JR West and Hankyu did not run overnight on New Year’s Eve, and only Keihan did. Since 1 March 2026 the city’s accommodation tax has also been higher in its middle and upper bands, so the room rate changes the bill.

This guide covers what each temple, shrine and railway had published when we looked on 29 and 30 October 2026, five areas matched to the kind of night you want, 12 hotels, the accommodation tax and what to book first. For the rest of your trip, where to stay in Kyoto covers the city in general, and our Tokyo New Year guide does the same job for the capital. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

31 December 2026 to 3 January 2027: what is published

Temple bells

  • Chion-in. The bell weighs about 70 tonnes and is rung by 17 monks. The Tourism Association’s 2026 list says ringing starts at 10:20pm, visitors may watch only, application is in advance, and it costs 3,000 yen, free for elementary school children and under. The temple’s own page still carried last year’s terms: online applications from 2pm on 1 December for four groups of 500, a separate cash sale of 400 tickets at its Yuzen-en office from 8 to 24 December, about 30 minutes inside per group, no re-entry and no applications on the day. We found no 2026/27 sale dates, so check from 1 December. The free alternative is the trial ringing on Sunday 27 December at 2pm in the bell tower, which the temple has posted. Last year’s page said it needed no application, and the city’s list says numbers may be limited if it is crowded.
  • Kodai-ji. The list gives 11:45pm for the ringing, with numbered tickets for the first 108 groups handed out from 10pm, and says visitors can also do hatsumode there from 10pm to 2am. The temple’s own pages showed only its autumn night opening when we looked.
  • Where you can ring. The Tourism Association lists, for 31 December, Hyakumanben Chion-ji (11:30pm to 12:30am, after a 10:30pm service), Eikan-do (11pm to 12:30am, for people in the queue), Shinnyo-do (11:45pm, four or five people per strike) and Mibu-dera (11:30pm, about ten people per strike). Tenryu-ji hands out numbers for the first 108 groups from 11:30pm and does not have parking. Myoman-ji has moved its bell to 3pm to 5pm. These pages show what each temple told the city, so confirm on the temple’s own site in December.
  • Kiyomizu-dera. Its annual calendar for 2026 lists 108 bells on 31 December and gives no times or ticket terms. Its ordinary hours are 6am to 6pm, so we do not know whether it opens late that night.

The first shrine visit

  • Yasaka Shrine. Its New Year page says the okera-mairi runs from about 7:30pm on 31 December to about 5am on 1 January. The sacred fire is lit at the shrine at 7pm after the year-end ceremony and transferred to lanterns in the grounds, and visitors carry some of it home on a rope. The shrine says it is open 24 hours for worship. The page is not dated, so treat the timings as the usual pattern. On 1 January the Okera ceremony is at 5am, and on 3 January there is a Noh performance at 9am and a poetry-card ceremony at 1pm.
  • Fushimi Inari Taisha. Its homepage still showed last year’s arrangements: prayers on 31 December from 8:30am to 2:30pm, and on 1 January from midnight to 5:30pm, paused from 5am to 7am for the ceremony. Stalls open from 31 December to 5 January, the shrine’s car parks close from 30 December to 6 January, and it asks visitors to check public transport first. The shrine’s December rites page lists a year-end purification at 3pm on 31 December, followed by a service as the year turns.
  • Heian Jingu. Its annual rites list the year-end purification at 3pm and a service at 4pm on 31 December, and a ceremony at 6am on 1 January. We found no special New Year hours, and its ordinary hours from November to February are 6am to 5pm, so we do not expect an all-night opening.
  • Crowds. Kyoto City says the busiest times are just after the gates open and the morning of 1 January, and asks people to spread their visits beyond the first three days. Its comfort-forecast pages and live cameras cover Fushimi Inari and the Yasaka area. They were broadcasting from 26 December to 12 January last year.

Trains on the night

Nothing for 2026/27 had been posted by Kyoto City Transportation Bureau or Keihan on 30 October, so this is last year’s, taken from the Tourism Association’s summary of each operator’s announcement.

  • Keihan. Last year it ran all night from 6pm on 31 December to 7am on 1 January on its main line, Oto line, Nakanoshima line, Katano line and Uji line, but not the Otsu lines. That main line serves Gion-Shijo, Sanjo and Fushimi-Inari.
  • Not overnight. The city subway and buses, JR West, Hankyu, Eizan and Randen did not run overnight, and the subway and buses did not push back their last trains. The subway ran Saturday and holiday timetables from 27 December to 4 January, and the buses ran holiday timetables from 28 December to 4 January. The two tourist express buses ran daily from 27 December to 4 January.
  • Closures. We found no official list of Kyoto shop or restaurant closures for this year, so check each place before you plan a meal around it.

The rules that decide where you sleep

  • Walk, or Keihan. If last year’s pattern holds, only Keihan runs after midnight, so Gion, Sanjo, Shijo and Fushimi Inari are the places where getting home is easy. Kyoto Station is JR and subway, and neither ran overnight.
  • Apply early for Chion-in. Last year its applications opened on 1 December. The bell is viewing only, so if you want to strike a bell, pick one from the queue-based list above.
  • Numbered tickets come late. Kodai-ji and Tenryu-ji hand out numbers on the night, so the hotel’s distance matters more than the date you book.
  • Late entry. Ask each hotel how it admits guests after midnight. Ryokan Yachiyo says it has no curfew and asks you to tell the front desk. Len’s reception closes at 10pm.
  • First visit, then sleep. Fushimi Inari’s first hours and the morning of 1 January are the crowded ones, so a visit after the first rush is calmer.

Kawaramachi and Shijo: the all-night line

Kawaramachi, Sanjo and Shijo put you between the Kamo river and the shops, with Keihan’s Sanjo and Gion-Shijo stations and Hankyu’s Kawaramachi all close. It is the practical base if you want a bell, a late walk and a bed without a taxi, and Yasaka is across the river. Where to stay in Kyoto covers the same streets for the rest of the year.

Kyoto Station: the easy arrival

Kyoto Station is where the Shinkansen and the airport trains arrive. It is not on Keihan, and the Karasuma subway and JR did not run overnight last year. Fushimi Inari is two stops and five minutes away on the JR Nara line, according to the shrine, so the first visit is easy from here after the rush. Fushimi Inari: the first visit

Fushimi Inari Taisha is next to JR Inari station and five minutes’ walk from Keihan Fushimi-Inari, and the Keihan main line ran all night last year. The shrine’s car parks close from 30 December to 6 January, so a hotel within walking distance spares you that. There are few hotels in the immediate area, so the choice is a small one.

Okazaki and Nanzen-ji: a calm base

Okazaki and Nanzen-ji sit at the eastern edge of the city, near Heian Jingu and the Keage station on the Tozai line. It is a residential area with gardens and few late restaurants, which suits a night when you are not in the crowds. The subway did not run overnight last year, so plan on a taxi or a walk for the late hours.

Kyoto’s accommodation tax

The tax is charged per person per night, by the room-only price, which the city defines as the room and service charge, excluding meals and consumption tax. It applies to hotels, ryokan, simple lodgings and unlicensed lets. It is collected by the accommodation, so ask whether a booking site’s total already includes it.

Room price per person, per night Tax from 1 March 2026
Under 6,000 yen 200 yen
6,000 to 19,999 yen 400 yen
20,000 to 49,999 yen 1,000 yen
50,000 to 99,999 yen 4,000 yen
100,000 yen and over 10,000 yen

As our arithmetic, a room-only rate of 40,000 yen a night for two is 20,000 yen per person, so 1,000 yen each, 2,000 yen a night and 4,000 yen for the two nights of 31 December and 1 January. Before March the same room would have been in a 500-yen band. Pupils on school trips and their escorts are exempt.     Frequently asked questions

    Which Kyoto temples ring the New Year's Eve bells in 2026?

    Kyoto City Tourism Association lists bells on 31 December 2026 at Chion-in (from 10:20pm, viewing only), Kodai-ji (11:45pm), Hyakumanben Chion-ji, Eikan-do, Shinnyo-do, Mibu-dera, Tenryu-ji and others. At several of them visitors can ring. Chion-in's trial ringing is on Sunday 27 December at 2pm.

    Do you need a reservation for Chion-in's bell in 2026?

    The Tourism Association's list says application is in advance and costs 3,000 yen, free for elementary school children and under, and that visitors only watch. Chion-in's own page still showed last year's terms: online applications from 1 December and a cash sale at its Yuzen-en office from 8 to 24 December. We found no 2026/27 sale dates.

    What is the okera-mairi at Yasaka Shrine?

    Yasaka Shrine lights a sacred fire in its grounds after a year-end service at 7pm on 31 December, and visitors carry some of it home on a rope. Its New Year page gives about 7:30pm to about 5am, says worship is possible 24 hours, and is not dated, so treat the times as the usual pattern.

    Do Kyoto trains run all night on New Year's Eve?

    Last year only Keihan did, from 6pm on 31 December to 7am on 1 January on its main line and four others. The city subway and buses, JR West, Hankyu, Eizan and Randen did not. No 2026/27 notices were out on 30 October 2026.

    Where is the best place to stay in Kyoto for New Year?

    Gion and Higashiyama suit Yasaka Shrine's fires on foot. Kawaramachi and Shijo suit the all-night Keihan line, Fushimi Inari suits a first visit to the shrine, Kyoto Station is the easiest arrival, and Okazaki and Nanzen-ji are the calm option. The hotels here are in those five areas.

    How much is Kyoto's accommodation tax over New Year?

    Since 1 March 2026, per person per night on the room-only price: 200 yen under 6,000 yen, 400 yen from 6,000, 1,000 yen from 20,000, 4,000 yen from 50,000 and 10,000 yen from 100,000. A room at 40,000 yen for two carries 1,000 yen each, so 4,000 yen over two nights.
