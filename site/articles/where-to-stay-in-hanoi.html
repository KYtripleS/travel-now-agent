Hanoi’s hotel bill is less simple than the price on the screen. Some hotels quote a “net” rate that already includes tax and service charge, and others add both afterwards. The tax that matters is VAT, which is 8% on most goods and services until 31 December 2026 and 10% after that unless the reduction is extended. Where you stay decides the rest: the Old Quarter and the lake are walkable, while West Lake and Ba Dinh mean a ride into the centre.

VAT and the price on your bill

Vietnam’s VAT is normally 10%. Under Resolution 204/2025/QH15 the National Assembly cut it by two points, to 8%, for goods and services that are otherwise taxed at 10%, from 1 July 2025 to 31 December 2026. The reduction does not apply to telecommunications, finance, banking, securities, insurance, real estate, metal products, mining products other than coal, or goods that carry excise tax. Accommodation is not among the exclusions in those reports, but we could not read the decree’s appendices, so check the rate on your own bill.

Hotels print rates in two ways. La Siesta’s offers pages quote packages “Net… including tax and service charge”, which means the price is all-in. Others quote the room rate before tax and service charge, which is what “++” after a price means. Read the line at the booking step, because the difference is not small.

Here is the arithmetic, which is ours and not a rule. Take a room at 2,000,000 VND a night. VAT at 8% adds 160,000. If the hotel also adds a service charge of 5% of the room rate, which we have assumed for the example because hotels set their own, that is another 100,000. The bill comes to 2,260,000 VND, 13% above the quoted rate. If VAT returns to 10% on 1 January 2027, the same room costs 2,300,000 VND.

Getting in from the airport, and around

Hanoi’s airport is Noi Bai International, code HAN. The airport’s own page lists these public bus routes into the city:

  • Route 86 runs to Hanoi Railway Station for 50,000 VND a trip, with departures from 7:00 to 22:15.
  • Route 17 runs to Long Bien and route 90 to Kim Ma; each costs 15,000 VND a trip. Route 17 departs from 5:10 to 22:00 and route 90 from 5:00 to 22:30.
  • By taxi, the airport says taxis serving passengers must have working, calibrated meters, and it publishes a fare table that applies from 1 March 2025. Ask for the meter and compare the total with that table.
  • Journey times are not given on the airport’s page, so allow generous time and ask your hotel how long a transfer takes at your hour.
  • In town, the Old Quarter, the lake and the French Quarter are close enough to walk between. West Lake and Ba Dinh are rides: Fraser Suites on Xuan Dieu Street describes itself as a 10-minute drive from the Old Quarter.

The Old Quarter: the first-trip base

The Old Quarter is a dense web of narrow streets, still organised by trade: Hang Gai is known as Silk Street, and Dong Xuan Market, Hanoi’s largest traditional market by one hotel’s account, is a walk away. It is loud, close-packed and busy with motorbikes, and it puts food, shops and the lake within a short walk.

Hoan Kiem Lake and the Cathedral: a walk around the water

Hoan Kiem Lake sits at the edge of the Old Quarter, with Ngoc Son Temple on its shore and the Thang Long Water Puppet Theatre nearby. St Joseph’s Cathedral stands a few streets to the west, on Nha Tho. It is the easiest area to leave on foot in any direction, and the streets around the lake are calmer than the Old Quarter’s.

  • Apricot Hotel, splurge. At 136 Hang Trong, with 123 rooms hung with original paintings and sculptures by Vietnamese artists, and the rooftop bar One36 over the lake. Rooms run from the 25 to 30 m² Sketch and the 30 to 32 m² Canvas to the 50 m² Gallery and 80 m² Masterpiece. Choose it for art and a lake view. Watch out: a press release on the hotel’s own site warns of fake websites and accounts that impersonate it and ask for deposits; book only on apricothotels.com or a booking site you know. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hanoi Pearl Hotel, comfortable. On Bao Khanh Lane, which the hotel calls “famous animated”, with a rooftop Pearl Lounge looking over the lake and the cathedral, The Candles Restaurant and the P-spa. The hotel puts the lake a minute’s walk away and calls itself a four-star. Choose it for the lake at a middle price. Watch out: a lively lane; ask for an upper floor if you sleep lightly. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • La Siesta Premium Lakeside, splurge. On the banks of Hoan Kiem Lake, with an outdoor pool that looks over the lake, Link Bistro Cafe and a spa. Choose it for the lake from the water’s edge. Watch out: the hotel’s lake-facing rooms are its Suites, so a standard room may not face the water. Check rates on Booking.com →

The French Quarter: grand hotels and the Opera House

South of the lake, the French Quarter is the area of colonial-era boulevards, with the Opera House at its centre and Trang Tien Street running towards the lake. The hotels here are the city’s old names, and the area is a few minutes’ walk from the Old Quarter.

  • Sofitel Legend Metropole Hanoi, splurge. At 15 Ngo Quyen Street, steps from the Opera House, open since 1901, with 358 rooms and suites and a historic Heritage Wing. The hotel says it was awarded two MICHELIN Keys in 2026. Choose it for the city’s best-known address. Watch out: at 358 rooms it is a large hotel, and the Heritage Wing is only part of it; ask which wing you are booking. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel de l’Opera Hanoi – MGallery, splurge. At 29 Trang Tien Street, next to the Opera House, with an operatic theme through the interior and rooms from Superior and Deluxe to suites. Choose it for the Opera House at the door. Watch out: the Opera House views belong to the Junior Suite and above, so a standard room may look elsewhere.

Ba Dinh: the Mausoleum and wide boulevards

Ba Dinh holds the Ho Chi Minh complex, with the mausoleum and the presidential palace. It is spacious and calmer than the Old Quarter, but the hotels here are a ride from the lake and the shops.

  • Pan Pacific Hanoi, splurge. At 1 Thanh Nien Road, with twin views of West Lake and Truc Bach Lake, and the restaurants Song Hong and The Summit. Choose it for a lake view from a tower. Watch out: the hotel describes itself as on the fringe of the city centre, so budget for rides into the Old Quarter. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • FTE Ba Dinh Hotel, good value. At 268 Buoi Street, with rooms that include a private bath, wi-fi, cable television and full breakfast, a pool and gym on the top floor, the Tequila rooftop bar and a 24-hour contactless concierge. Choose it for breakfast and a pool at a lower price. Watch out: it is outside the walkable centre; check the map before you book. Check rates on Booking.com →

West Lake (Tay Ho): lake views and a quieter evening

West Lake is Hanoi’s largest lake, and Tay Ho, on its shore, is the district of lakeside hotels, cafés and the Xuan Dieu and Quang An dining strip. Tran Quoc Pagoda, which Fraser calls the oldest Buddhist pagoda in Hanoi, stands on a small peninsula in the lake. It suits a longer stay, with the Old Quarter a ride away.

  • The Hanoi Club Hotel & Residences, comfortable. At 76 Yen Phu Street, on the shore of West Lake, with 88 hotel rooms, an outdoor pool and The Cove restaurant; hotel guests get access to the club’s facilities. One of its lake-view rooms measures 35 to 40 m². Choose it for a lakefront hotel with a pool. Watch out: it is a private members’ club with a hotel inside, and some rooms look over the city rather than the lake. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Fraser Suites Hanoi, comfortable. In Syrena Tower at 51 Xuan Dieu Street, with 280 serviced residences from studios to duplexes, kitchens, a rooftop pool and the restaurant Mizumi on level 13. The Xuan Dieu and Quang An dining strip is a seven-minute walk. Choose it for a family or a longer stay with a kitchen. Watch out: a coffee machine comes only in the Executive categories. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Hanoi

  • Read the tax line. Decide whether the price is net or “++”. On a 2,000,000 VND room, VAT at 8% and an assumed 5% service charge make the bill 2,260,000 VND.
  • Watch the 2027 date. The 8% rate runs to 31 December 2026; from 1 January 2027 it is 10% unless a new decision extends it.
  • Check the property. La Siesta runs four hotels in Hanoi and Church Boutique several, so confirm the address. Book only on a hotel’s own site or a booking site you know: Apricot warns of impersonators asking for deposits.
  • Check room size. Apricot’s Sketch rooms are 25 to 30 m² against 50 m² for a Gallery; one of the Hanoi Club’s lake-view rooms is 35 to 40 m². If you need space, book the category above the cheapest.
  • Compare totals. Hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the best area to stay in Hanoi for a first trip?

    The Old Quarter: narrow trading streets, with Hang Gai's silk shops and Dong Xuan Market, and Hoan Kiem Lake a short walk away. Choose the French Quarter for grand hotels near the Opera House, Ba Dinh for the Ho Chi Minh complex and West Lake for lake views and a quieter evening.

    How much VAT is added to a hotel bill in Hanoi?

    Vietnam's VAT is normally 10%, cut to 8% for goods and services otherwise taxed at 10% from 1 July 2025 to 31 December 2026. Accommodation is not among the exclusions in the reports we read, but check the rate on your bill. Some hotels add a service charge, and each hotel sets its own.

    Do Hanoi hotel prices include tax?

    It depends on the hotel. La Siesta's offers pages quote 'Net' rates that include tax and service charge; other hotels quote the room rate before both. Read the line at the booking step. On a 2,000,000 VND room, VAT at 8% and an assumed 5% service charge make 2,260,000 VND.

    How do you get from Noi Bai Airport to central Hanoi?

    By public bus or taxi. The airport lists route 86 to Hanoi Railway Station at 50,000 VND, departing 7:00 to 22:15, and routes 17 (Long Bien) and 90 (Kim Ma) at 15,000 VND each. Taxis must have working meters, and the airport publishes a fare table that applies from 1 March 2025.

    Is the Old Quarter noisy?

    It is busy, with motorbikes and market streets, so light sleepers should ask for a quiet room. Hoan Kiem Lake and the French Quarter are calmer and still walkable; Hanoi Pearl Hotel calls its own Bao Khanh Lane animated, so ask for an upper floor there.

    Where can you stay by West Lake in Hanoi?

    Tay Ho on West Lake has The Hanoi Club Hotel & Residences at 76 Yen Phu Street, with 88 hotel rooms, and Fraser Suites Hanoi at 51 Xuan Dieu Street, with 280 serviced residences. Fraser describes the Old Quarter as a 10-minute drive away.
