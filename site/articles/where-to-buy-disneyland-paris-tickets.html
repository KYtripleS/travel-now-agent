The ticket is the same gate, the same parks and, on the listings we read, the same three-day cancellation window. What changes is the price, and on the day we checked it changed by up to £10.41 on a two-day adult ticket, in favour of the resellers.

That is a small gap against a trip bill, so this guide is about the other differences: which tickets have a date and which do not, who refunds what, and what a reseller ticket does not give you. How to read it:

  • Tiqets and GetYourGuide were level. The gap was 6p, 8p and 15p across the three rows, which is a rounding difference, not a saving.
  • Viator sat between the resellers and Disney on the two-park rows and level with Disney on one park. Viator also has a separate one-park listing, which showed £61.18 for the same date, so it pays to open both.
  • Disney was highest on every row. Our arithmetic, from the table: £3.84 more than Tiqets for one park, £5.59 more for two parks in a day, and £10.41 more for two days.
  • Disney’s figures are whole pounds from its price calendar; the resellers show pence. Tiqets says its calendar prices may be rounded.
  • The date matters more than the seller. Disney’s own November calendar shows a one-day, two-park adult ticket from £87 to £119 depending on the day, and £56 to £88 for one park. On Saturday 21 November the one-park price read £85 on Disney and £79 on Tiqets.

What each seller’s listing says

Disneyland Paris, direct

Disney sells dated and undated tickets. Dated tickets of one to four days are free to cancel up to 3 days before the visit date; Disney’s own example is that a 4 June ticket can be cancelled until midnight on 1 June. You cancel online in “My Bookings” in a Disney account, and Disney says that to change your date you cancel and buy again, subject to availability on the new date.

The 1-day undated ticket is a different product. Disney lists it as valid for one year from the day after purchase (except 31 October and 31 December), cancellable only under the 14-day right of withdrawal, and you “must register your visit date in advance”, subject to park capacity. It is the one to buy as a gift, or if you cannot fix a date, but it is not the cheap one and it is not a walk-up ticket. Disney also says park tickets generally cannot be bought at the entrance.

Disney’s other terms on the ticket page: children aged 3 to 11 pay the child rate, and under-3s go free. The one-day dated ticket can cover one or two parks; Disney’s configurator offered no one-park choice for two days. Disney also runs a Price Match Promise: if you find a lower price for the exact same ticket within 24 hours, you can claim the difference, but the lower price must be in the same currency and country (£, United Kingdom for us), have the same cancellation conditions, and be claimed within 72 hours of booking. Its page does not say whether resellers count, so do not rely on it to match one.

The listing is a dated ticket you choose a date for. The one-day, two-park option said: “Cancel for free until 72 hours before your visit date and get a full refund”, and that rescheduling is possible until 72 hours before. The listing’s “not included” list reads: skip-the-line at the attractions, food and drinks, and parking. The multi-day ticket is described as covering both parks “within the same day for 2 consecutive days” (and three or four).

GetYourGuide

The 1-day listing is labelled “Official ticket” and describes a ticket for one park, or both, “on a pre-booked date”. Its terms: “Cancel up to 3 days in advance for a full refund”, and for our 18 November visit the options said “Cancel for free until Nov 15”. The two-park option states entry to both parks “on the same pre-booked date”. The 2, 3 and 4-day listing is separate, covers both parks across consecutive days from the date booked, and had the same 15 November cancellation date. The 1-day listing also carries a “likely to sell out” warning. GetYourGuide has other flexible-date and transport-bundle listings; we did not price them.

Viator

The listing we used lets you choose one or two parks and one to four days. For two parks it says “full park-hopping”; for one park, access to one of the two parks. On the page we read: “Free cancellation before 15 Nov (local time)” for the 18 November visit, and “no amendments can be made, including name changes”, with ID that may be asked for at the gate. It also says to sync Viator tickets in the Disneyland Paris app to add Disney Premier Access. Viator sells a separate 1-Day/1-Park listing, which is where the £61.18 came from.

“Park hopper”, Extra Magic Time and what a ticket does not include

Disneyland Paris does not use the word “hopper” on the pages we read. The idea is a two-park ticket: Disney’s ticket page offers a “2 Parks experience” ticket covering Disneyland Park and Disney Adventure World, and Viator’s listing says “park-hopping” for its two-park option. If you want to switch parks in a day, buy two parks; a one-park ticket is one park. Tiqets and GetYourGuide both describe the two-park day as entry to both on the same date.

Extra Magic Time is the early entry before the official opening time. Disney’s page says: “Guests staying at a Disney Hotel, Les Villages Nature Paris, or with a Disneyland Pass Gold can enter one or both Disney Parks before the official opening time.” That is the whole list. A day ticket bought from any seller is not on it, and the page adds that the opening time, duration and parks can vary, are shown up to three months ahead, and can change until the day of your visit. If early entry is the reason you are comparing sellers, you are comparing the wrong thing: it comes from where you sleep, which is covered in where to stay for Disneyland Paris.

When the official site is the better buy

  • You want the undated ticket. Only Disney’s own page showed the one-year ticket in our checks.
  • You are booking a hotel too. A Disney Hotel plus tickets package comes from Disney, and Disney Hotel guests are the ones named for Extra Magic Time.
  • You may change your date. Resellers’ dated tickets in our checks could be cancelled up to about three days out, the same window as Disney. With Disney you cancel online in “My Bookings” and buy again; with a reseller you go through its own system.
  • You want one party to deal with. Disney’s refunds go back to the original payment method through its own system; a reseller adds its own support desk on top.

When a reseller is the better buy

  • You have a fixed date and want the lowest price. Three resellers beat Disney on all three rows that day.
  • You already have an account with a reseller and want the booking beside your other tours. Our Klook, Viator and GetYourGuide comparison explains how they differ.
  • You want to see the price in another currency before paying. Tiqets and Viator let you switch the displayed currency on the page, but Tiqets says the currency you pay in may differ, so check the total at checkout.

Five checks before you pay

  1. Same date, same park count, same days. A one-park price is not comparable with a two-park one.
  2. The cancellation date. For an 18 November visit, the resellers we read said 15 November. Put it in your diary.
  3. Name and ID. Tickets are personal, and Viator says no name changes are possible.
  4. Is it dated or undated? An undated ticket still needs a registered visit date.
  5. Compare the total in your own currency, after any fee shown at checkout.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is it cheaper to buy Disneyland Paris tickets from a reseller than from the official site?

    On 31 October 2026, for a Wednesday 18 November visit, Tiqets, GetYourGuide and Viator were all below Disney's own price on the three ticket types we compared. For a one-day, two-park adult ticket Disney showed £95 against £89.41 on Tiqets, £89.49 on GetYourGuide and £90.91 on Viator. Klook was not checked. Prices change daily, so compare your own date.

    Can I cancel a Disneyland Paris ticket?

    Disney's dated 1 to 4-day tickets can be cancelled and refunded up to 3 days before the chosen date. Tiqets said free cancellation until 72 hours before, and GetYourGuide and Viator said until 15 November for an 18 November visit. The undated ticket can be cancelled only under the 14-day right of withdrawal, on Disney's page.

    What is the difference between a dated and an undated ticket?

    A dated ticket is for the day or days you choose when you buy. Disney's undated 1-day ticket is valid for one year from the day after purchase, except 31 October and 31 December, and you must register your visit date in advance, subject to park capacity.

    Does a ticket from Klook, Tiqets, GetYourGuide or Viator give Extra Magic Time?

    Disney's Extra Magic Time page names three groups: guests of a Disney Hotel, guests of Les Villages Nature Paris, and Disneyland Pass Gold holders. A day ticket bought from any seller is not on that list, and the times can change until the day of your visit.

    Do I need a two-park ticket to see both parks in one day?

    Yes. A one-park ticket covers one park, either Disneyland Park or Disney Adventure World. The two-park ticket covers both on the same date, and Viator's listing calls this park-hopping.

    Does Disneyland Paris match a lower price?

    Disney's Price Match Promise refunds the difference if you find a lower price for the exact same ticket within 24 hours of booking, in the same currency and country and with the same cancellation conditions, and you claim within 72 hours. Its page does not say whether resellers count, so check before you rely on it.
