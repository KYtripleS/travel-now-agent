Hotels around Duomo, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Duomo that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

Florence adds its own charge to every hotel bill: the imposta di soggiorno, a city tax per person and per night that rises with the hotel’s star rating, from €3.50 at a one-star to €8 at a five-star. A couple spending four nights in a four-star hotel pays €56 on top of the room. The tax stops after seven nights. Where you stay decides the rest: the old centre is small enough to cross on foot, so the choice is about what is outside your door at night, the cathedral, the river, a market or a neighbourhood square.

This guide covers six areas, from the Duomo to the Oltrarno, 12 hotels, the airport links and the tax. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

Florence’s city tax

Every hotel collects the imposta di soggiorno for the Comune di Firenze, per person and per night. The rates below were approved in December 2024 and have applied since 1 February 2025; the city still lists them as current in October 2026:

Where you stay Per person, per night
Five-star hotel €8
Four-star hotel €7
Three-star hotel €6
Two-star hotel €4.50
One-star hotel €3.50
Residenze d’epoca (historic residences) €7
B&Bs, guest rooms, holiday flats and short-term rentals €6
Youth hostels €4

Two rules soften it. Children pay nothing until their 12th birthday. And the tax is charged for at most seven consecutive nights, so a long stay costs no more in tax than a week.

By our arithmetic, a couple staying four nights pays €56 at a four-star, €48 at a three-star and €64 at a five-star. Ten nights in one four-star hotel cost the couple €98, not €140, because only the first seven count. A family of four with two children under 12 pays for two.

If you were planning a holiday flat: since 30 July 2024 the city has barred new short-term rentals in the UNESCO historic core, and since 21 June 2026 no new short-term rentals may open in further parts of the central Zone A. Flats already registered can carry on, and every short-term rental in the city needs a municipal authorisation. Their guests pay the same €6 a night as at a B&B.

Getting in from the airports, and around

  • From Florence airport (Peretola), tram line T2 leaves from the Peretola Aeroporto stop, a two-minute walk from arrivals, and reaches the centre in about 20 minutes, stopping at Unità beside Santa Maria Novella station. A ticket costs €2 and lasts 90 minutes; buy it from the platform machine or tap a contactless card or phone on board. The first tram leaves the airport at 05:00, the last at 23:58 (01:15 on Friday and Saturday).
  • Mind the luggage rule. One bag of up to 10 kg and 55 × 40 × 20 cm travels free. Each larger bag, up to 80 × 45 × 25 cm and 20 kg, needs an extra ticket, two at most per person, and anything bigger is not allowed on board. With a full-size suitcase, take a taxi.
  • From Pisa airport, the PisaMover shuttle runs to Pisa Centrale station in about five minutes, for €6.50 single or €13 return, every day from 06:00 to midnight (to 01:00 from June to September). Regional trains then run to Firenze Santa Maria Novella: on Trenitalia’s journey planner, €9.80 and 52 to 82 minutes on a November weekday.
  • Firenze Santa Maria Novella is the city’s main station, at the western edge of the centre. Frecciarossa high-speed trains reach Roma Termini in about 1 hour 35 minutes and Venezia Santa Lucia in about 2 hours 15 minutes.
  • In town, you will mostly walk; the same €2 ticket covers trams and city buses.

Duomo and Signoria: the first-trip base

The streets between the cathedral and the river hold most of what a first visit covers: Brunelleschi’s dome, the Baptistery, Piazza della Signoria, the Uffizi and the Ponte Vecchio. It is also the busiest part of the city. If you want the sights before breakfast and after the day-trippers leave, sleep here.

Where to stay around the Duomo

  • Hotel Brunelleschi, comfortable. On Piazza Sant’Elisabetta, a few steps from the Duomo, built around the Torre della Pagliazza, a round tower the hotel dates to the sixth or seventh century. Excavations beneath it found part of a Roman bath, now shown in the hotel’s private museum; there is a Tower Bar and a restaurant, Osteria della Pagliazza. Choose it for Florence’s oldest layers, by the cathedral. Watch out: only two rooms are in the tower itself. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Portrait Firenze, splurge. A five-star of the Lungarno Collection on the Arno, steps from the Ponte Vecchio, with 37 rooms and suites, many facing the river, and a restaurant, Caffè dell’Oro, beside the bridge; it is pet friendly. Choose it for a suite over the Arno. Watch out: price, and children aged four and over are booked as adults. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Pendini, good value. A historic hotel between Palazzo Strozzi and Piazza della Repubblica, in the building with the great arch over the square, taking in travellers since 1879; rooms keep their parquet floors and wallpaper, and there are triples, quadruples and a family suite. The hotel parks your car for €28 a day (€37 for larger cars), registering the plate for the limited traffic zone. Choose it for the very centre at a three-star price. Watch out: Classic rooms face either the busy Via Strozzi or the quiet inner courtyards; ask for a courtyard. Check rates on Booking.com →

Santa Croce and Borgo Pinti: the quieter east side

East of the Duomo, the city loosens. Santa Croce has its Franciscan basilica and a square lined with cafés; Sant’Ambrogio has a covered food market, and Borgo Pinti is a long street of palaces with gardens behind their walls. The evenings are calmer than in the centre.

Where to stay in Santa Croce and Borgo Pinti

  • Four Seasons Hotel Firenze, splurge. A five-star at Borgo Pinti 99, set in two Renaissance palaces beside one of the city’s largest private gardens. Palazzo della Gherardesca, built in 1473, has the Michelin-starred Il Palagio and a spa overlooking the pool and gardens; Palazzo del Nero adds 36 rooms with views of the Duomo. Choose it for a garden, a pool and a spa in the city. Watch out: the two palaces offer different things, so check which one your room is in. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Palazzo Monna Lisa, comfortable. A four-star historic residence on Borgo Pinti with a private Italian garden laid out in the 15th century; no two rooms are alike, some have furnished balconies over the garden, and the Contessa Grand Room is a 75 m² suite. Choose it for a quiet garden a short walk from the Duomo. Watch out: rooms vary in size and furnishing, and only some overlook the garden. Check rates on Booking.com →

Santo Spirito: evenings in the Oltrarno

The Oltrarno, “beyond the Arno”, is the left bank: Palazzo Pitti, the Boboli Gardens and, around Piazza Santo Spirito, trattorias, antique dealers and artisan workshops. The square fills up in the evening, so expect some noise on summer nights; the centre is a few minutes away over the bridges.

Where to stay in Santo Spirito

  • Hotel Palazzo Guadagni, comfortable. A Renaissance palazzo on Piazza Santo Spirito, built in 1505 for a silk merchant, with a third-floor loggia, the Loggia Roof Bar, looking over the city; some rooms have frescoed ceilings, the Deluxe Rooms with Balcony overlook the square, and Family Rooms join two doubles. Choose it for a roof loggia on the Oltrarno’s liveliest square. Watch out: as an old building, some rooms, including the Superior rooms, are reached only by an internal staircase. San Niccolò: the river and the hill

San Niccolò is the eastern end of the Oltrarno, under the old walls at the foot of the hill up to Piazzale Michelangelo. It is small and residential, and the Ponte alle Grazie leads straight across to Santa Croce.

Where to stay in San Niccolò

  • Hotel Silla, good value. A three-star on the left bank of the Arno, run by the Belotti family since 1964, with a terrace over the river for breakfast in spring and summer. By the hotel’s account it is one of the last hotels in the historic centre reachable by car without crossing the limited traffic zone, with a private garage. Choose it if you are driving, or for a river terrace at a three-star price. Watch out: garage spaces are limited enough that the hotel advises booking one in advance. Check rates on Booking.com →

Santa Maria Novella: the station quarter

Santa Maria Novella takes its name from the Dominican basilica on its square, open and green, a few minutes from the station. It is the base for day trips by rail, early trains and the airport tram. The streets right by the station are busier and plainer than the centre.

Where to stay near Santa Maria Novella

  • The Place Firenze, splurge. A small luxury house on Piazza Santa Maria Novella, by its own account five minutes’ walk from the station and seven from the Duomo. There is no check-in desk: you ring the doorbell and are offered a drink. The minibar is complimentary, breakfast is served outdoors on the piazza, and children up to 12 stay free. Choose it for quiet luxury close to the trains. Watch out: the hotel has no accessible rooms, though its restaurant and terrace are accessible. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel L’Orologio, comfortable. A four-star on Piazza Santa Maria Novella, within 300 metres of the station by the hotel’s measure, themed on a collection of luxury watches, with a gym and a sauna; the Deluxe rooms are 28 m², and a top-floor Honeymoon Suite has a private terrace facing the Duomo. Choose it for the square outside your window. Watch out: only some Deluxe rooms face the piazza or the Duomo; book the view by name.
  • Hostel Archi Rossi, budget. A family-run hostel on Via Faenza, a short walk from the station and, by its count, eight minutes from the cathedral, with private rooms for one to five and dorms of four, six and nine beds, all with their own bathroom and air conditioning, a 24-hour reception and a garden. Choose it for the lowest price in this guide. Watch out: the largest dorm sleeps nine. Check rates on Booking.com →

San Lorenzo and San Marco: the market and the David

North of the Duomo, San Lorenzo is the Medici quarter: the basilica with its unfinished facade, the Medici Chapels and the Central Market. A few blocks on, San Marco has the Accademia Gallery, home of Michelangelo’s David. It is central, a little less polished than the riverside, and close to both the Duomo and the station.

Where to stay in San Lorenzo and San Marco

  • Hotel Orto de’ Medici, comfortable. A four-star at Via San Gallo 30, on the site of the Medici garden of San Marco, where, by the hotel’s account, Lorenzo the Magnificent met the young Michelangelo in 1489. The garden and a terrace bar, open from May to October, are the draw; Deluxe rooms, 215 sq ft (20 m²), open onto the garden, and standard bookings can be cancelled free until 18:00 the day before. Choose it for a garden near the David. Watch out: the Basic rooms are 140 sq ft (13 m²), and pets are not accepted.
  • Il Guelfo Bianco, good value. A three-star in a restored 15th-century building near the Accademia, the Duomo and the San Lorenzo market, hung with contemporary and antique art, with a buffet breakfast and an adjoining restaurant, Il Desco Bistrot, cooking produce from the owners’ organic farm in Reggello. Choose it for the Accademia at a three-star price. Watch out: it is inside the limited traffic zone; if you drive, give the front desk your plate number on arrival to avoid a fine. How to book Florence

  • Count the tax. It is per person and set by the star rating: €7 a night at a four-star, €6 at a three-star, nothing for children under 12, and nothing after the seventh consecutive night.
  • Check the room size. Old palazzi mean small rooms: in this guide they run from 13 m² for the Basic rooms at Hotel Orto de’ Medici to 28 m² for the Deluxe rooms at Hotel L’Orologio. If you need space, book the category above the cheapest.
  • Book the view by name. Only the rooms sold with it are sure to have it: the river-view studios and suites at Portrait Firenze, the piazza-view Deluxe rooms at Hotel L’Orologio, the balcony rooms at Palazzo Guadagni.
  • Leave the car. The historic centre is a limited traffic zone; hotels inside it, such as Hotel Pendini, register your plate, and Hotel Silla can be reached without entering it.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Florence for a first trip?

Around the Duomo and Piazza della Signoria: the cathedral, the Uffizi and the Ponte Vecchio are all a short walk apart. Choose Santo Spirito in the Oltrarno for evenings in a neighbourhood square, San Lorenzo and San Marco for the market and the David, and Santa Maria Novella for the trains and the airport tram.

How much is the tourist tax in Florence?

Since 1 February 2025 the imposta di soggiorno has been €3.50 a person a night at a one-star hotel, €4.50 at a two-star, €6 at a three-star, €7 at a four-star and €8 at a five-star. B&Bs and short-term rentals charge €6, and youth hostels €4. A couple spending four nights in a four-star hotel pays €56.

Is there a maximum number of nights for Florence's city tax?

Yes. The tax is charged for at most seven consecutive nights, and children pay nothing until their 12th birthday. Ten nights in one four-star hotel cost a couple €98 in tax, not €140.

How do you get from Florence airport to the city centre?

Take tram line T2 from the Peretola Aeroporto stop, a two-minute walk from arrivals. It reaches the centre in about 20 minutes, stopping at Unità beside Santa Maria Novella station. A ticket costs €2 and lasts 90 minutes. Each bag larger than 55 × 40 × 20 cm needs an extra ticket, and bags over 80 × 45 × 25 cm are not allowed on board.

How do you get from Pisa airport to Florence?

Take the PisaMover shuttle to Pisa Centrale, about five minutes for €6.50, then a regional train to Firenze Santa Maria Novella. When we checked Trenitalia's journey planner for a November weekday, the train cost €9.80 and took 52 to 82 minutes.

Can you still rent a holiday flat in central Florence?

Flats already registered can still be let, but since 30 July 2024 the city has barred new short-term rentals in the UNESCO historic core, and since 21 June 2026 no new ones may open in further parts of the central Zone A. Every short-term rental needs a municipal authorisation, and guests pay a city tax of €6 a night.
