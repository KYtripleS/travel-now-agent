The map shows the hotels and apartments around Okazaki that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

Kyoto’s cherry trees open, on average, on 26 March and reach full bloom on 4 April, by the Kyoto observatory’s normals for 1991 to 2020. They do not keep to the calendar. In 2025 they opened on 27 March and were full on 5 April; in 2026 they opened on 23 March and were full by 30 March. Hotels fill for that fortnight long before anyone knows which week it will be, so the trick is to book early, book flexibly, and sleep near the trees you most want to see.

This guide covers the dates to aim for, five areas matched to the blossom you can reach from each, twelve hotels to start with, and how to book so that an early or late spring does not cost you. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating at the start of October 2026. When to go: the dates to aim for

The averages are a guide, not a promise. By the Kyoto Local Meteorological Office’s count:

Year First flowers Full bloom
Normal (1991–2020) 26 March 4 April
2025 27 March 5 April
2026 23 March 30 March

Full bloom came seven to nine days after the first flowers in each of these, and the blossom does not hold for long after that. Aim for the last days of March and the first days of April, and stay four or five nights rather than two, so that an early or a late year still overlaps with your stay.

Forecasts for 2027 will come from Japan’s private weather companies. For the 2026 season, the first ones came from the Japan Meteorological Corporation on 18 December 2025, from Weathernews on 8 January and from the Japan Weather Association, which runs tenki.jp, on 29 January, and all three were updated as spring approached.

Not every tree is a Somei Yoshino, the variety the forecasts follow. Nijo Castle has about 300 cherry trees of some 50 varieties, from early to late, and in 2026 its cherry festival ran from 19 March to 19 April, with the castle open in the evenings; weeping cherries open earlier and double-flowered ones later, which stretches the season at either end.

Okazaki and Nanzen-ji: the classic blossom walks

Okazaki is the cultural quarter around Heian Shrine, where the Lake Biwa Canal turns past the museums, and it puts Kyoto’s best-known blossom walks on foot. The Philosopher’s Path runs for about 2 kilometres beside a canal from Ginkaku-ji to Nyakuoji Shrine, under some 400 cherry trees. The Keage Incline, the 582-metre slope where canal boats were once hauled up and down on rails, has about 90 more along its old tracks. Nanzen-ji sits between them. Keage and Higashiyama stations, on the Tozai subway line, take you downtown in a few minutes.

Gion and Higashiyama: the old city at dawn

Gion and Higashiyama are the Kyoto of the postcards, and in blossom season they are also the most crowded. Maruyama Park, behind Yasaka Shrine, is built around a great weeping cherry that is lit up at night in season; the Shirakawa canal in Gion runs under cherry branches past old teahouses; and the slopes of Ninenzaka and Sannenzaka climb to Kiyomizu-dera. Staying here lets you walk out at dawn, before the tour groups, and come back to the lanes at dusk. The Keihan line at Gion-Shijo and the Tozai line at Higashiyama are the nearest stations.

Downtown: the river and the most choice

Downtown, between Karasuma and the Kamo River, has the widest choice of hotels and restaurants, and blossom of its own: the banks of the Kamo River, and the Takase canal along Kiyamachi, lined with cherry trees. Nijo Castle, with its month-long cherry festival, is a few stops west on the Tozai line, and the Keihan line runs from Sanjo to Fushimi Inari and beyond. If the areas beside the famous spots are full, this is where to look first.

Kyoto Station: the trains and plenty of rooms

Kyoto Station has a large cluster of hotels around it, and in blossom season that matters: when the old city is full, there are often still rooms here. JR lines run to Arashiyama and to Nara, the Karasuma subway line runs north through downtown, and Daigo-ji, where Toyotomi Hideyoshi held a famous cherry-viewing party in 1598, is a short ride south-east by JR to Yamashina and the Tozai line. The trade-off is that the blossom is always a ride away.

Arashiyama: the river and the hills

Arashiyama, on the city’s western edge, is where the river leaves the hills, and in spring the slopes above Togetsukyo Bridge are dotted with mountain cherries. Nakanoshima Park, on the island below the bridge, and Kameyama Park, above the bamboo grove, are the places to sit. Day-trippers fill it by late morning; staying the night gives you the riverbank early and late. JR’s Sagano line, the Hankyu line and the Randen tram all reach it.

Where to stay in Arashiyama

  • Suiran, a Luxury Collection Hotel, Kyoto, splurge. 39 rooms built around a Meiji-era summer residence on the river, 17 of them with their own hot-spring bath, a three-minute walk from Tenryu-ji and five from Togetsukyo Bridge. Choose it for the river at dusk once the crowds have gone. Watch out: price, and a 15-minute walk from Saga-Arashiyama station. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • HOSHINOYA Kyoto, splurge, ryokan. 25 rooms upriver, every one facing the water; you check in near Togetsukyo Bridge and arrive by the hotel’s own boat. Choose it for a ryokan night reached by river. Watch out: it is not on Booking.com, so book with the hotel.

How to book for a season that moves

  • Book a refundable rate now for the nights you want, then adjust when the forecasts firm up in January and February. A booking you can cancel costs nothing if the trees come early or late; check each rate’s cancellation deadline before you pay.
  • Stay longer, not later. Four or five nights across the end of March and the start of April give you better odds than two perfectly timed ones.
  • Mind the tax. Since 1 March 2026, Kyoto has charged an accommodation tax per person per night, set by the room rate, and blossom-season rates can push a room into a higher band:
Room rate per person per night Tax per person per night
Under ¥6,000 ¥200
¥6,000 to under ¥20,000 ¥400
¥20,000 to under ¥50,000 ¥1,000
¥50,000 to under ¥100,000 ¥4,000
¥100,000 or more ¥10,000
  • If Kyoto is full, look at Kyoto Station and downtown first, then at Osaka, under half an hour away by JR Special Rapid; our guide to where to stay in Osaka covers it. For which booking site to use, see hotel booking sites compared, and for the taxes in full, Japan’s tourist taxes in 2026.

Getting around in blossom season

  • Take the train when you can. City buses to Ginkaku-ji, Kiyomizu-dera and Arashiyama fill up in blossom season; the Tozai subway line (Nijojo-mae, Sanjo Keihan, Higashiyama, Keage) and the Keihan line along the Kamo River are steadier.
  • Go early. The Philosopher’s Path and Maruyama Park are quietest at first light, and far busier by midday.

Frequently asked questions

When will the cherry blossoms bloom in Kyoto in 2027?

No one knows yet. The Kyoto observatory's normals are first flowers on 26 March and full bloom on 4 April; in 2026 the trees opened on 23 March and were full on 30 March. For the 2026 season the first forecasts came out between 18 December and 29 January, so expect the 2027 ones on a similar timetable.

What is the best area to stay in Kyoto for cherry blossom?

Okazaki, near Nanzen-ji, puts the Philosopher's Path, the Keage Incline and the canal by Heian Shrine on foot. Gion and Higashiyama suit dawn walks to Maruyama Park and Kiyomizu-dera; downtown has the Kamo River and the widest choice of hotels.

How far ahead should I book a Kyoto hotel for cherry blossom season?

As soon as your dates are firm. Book a refundable rate for the last days of March and the first days of April, then adjust when the forecasts firm up in January and February, checking each rate's cancellation deadline.

How long do the cherry blossoms last in Kyoto?

Full bloom has come seven to nine days after the first flowers in recent years, and the blossom does not hold for long after that. Earlier weeping cherries and later double-flowered ones stretch the season, and Nijo Castle's 2026 cherry festival ran from 19 March to 19 April.

How much is Kyoto's accommodation tax?

Since 1 March 2026 it is charged per person per night by room rate: ¥200 under ¥6,000, ¥400 from ¥6,000, ¥1,000 from ¥20,000, ¥4,000 from ¥50,000 and ¥10,000 from ¥100,000.

Is Kyoto Station a good base in cherry blossom season?

Yes, for the trains and the choice of rooms: there are often still rooms there when the old city is full, and Arashiyama, Nara and Daigo-ji are a train ride away. The trade-off is that every blossom spot is a ride, not a walk.
