Kuala Lumpur rewards a good base more than most cities. The heat makes long walks tiring and the traffic makes short taxi rides slow, while the trains, the LRT, the MRT, the monorail and the KLIA Ekspres, are quick if you stay near a station. Each of the four areas below is built around a few stations, and each suits a different trip.

This guide compares four areas, then names twelve hotels across them, with the reason to pick each and, where there is one, the catch. They are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating at the start of October 2026. One of them, Park Hyatt Kuala Lumpur, opened in August 2025 high up in Merdeka 118, Malaysia’s tallest building. No hotel pays to appear here.

KLCC: the first-trip base

KLCC, the Kuala Lumpur City Centre development, is laid out around the Petronas Twin Towers: the Suria KLCC mall at their feet, KLCC Park behind them with its fountains and paths, and the convention centre beyond. The LRT’s Kelana Jaya line stops at KLCC station, and a covered, air-conditioned walkway runs from the convention centre to Bukit Bintang, so you can reach the shops without crossing a road.

Bukit Bintang: food and shopping

Bukit Bintang is Kuala Lumpur’s shopping and entertainment district: the Pavilion, Starhill and Lot 10 malls along Jalan Bukit Bintang, and, a block behind, Jalan Alor, a street of hawker stalls and open-fronted restaurants that is busiest after dark. The MRT’s Kajang line and the KL Monorail both stop at Bukit Bintang, and the covered walkway to KLCC starts at Pavilion.

The old city and Chinatown: Merdeka Square and Merdeka 118

Kuala Lumpur began where the Klang and Gombak rivers meet, by Masjid Jamek, and the old city around that spot holds Merdeka Square, where the flag of independent Malaya was first raised in 1957, the Moorish-style Sultan Abdul Samad Building, the Central Market and, south of it, Petaling Street in Chinatown. Merdeka 118, Malaysia’s tallest building, rises over the area beside Merdeka Stadium. The LRT and MRT meet at Pasar Seni, and Masjid Jamek station links two LRT lines. Hotels here are fewer and often cheaper, with one big exception.

Where to stay in the old city and Chinatown

  • The Majestic Hotel Kuala Lumpur, Autograph Collection, comfortable.   • AnCasa Hotel Kuala Lumpur, Chinatown, good value. 294 rooms on Jalan Tun Tan Cheng Lock, beside Chinatown and near Plaza Rakyat LRT station, with an outdoor pool. Choose it for Petaling Street and Central Market on foot at a low price. Watch out: guests rate it below the other hotels here; expect simple rooms. Check rates on Booking.com →

KL Sentral: the airport train

KL Sentral is the city’s main rail hub: the KLIA Ekspres runs from here to the main airport terminal in 28 minutes and to Terminal 2 in 33, and the LRT and KTM Komuter trains stop here, with the MRT and the monorail a short walk away. Some airlines let you check in your bags at KL Sentral before you take the train. Brickfields, Kuala Lumpur’s Little India, is next door. It suits short stays and early flights more than sightseeing.

Where to stay at KL Sentral

  • Hilton Kuala Lumpur, comfortable. A 35-floor tower beside KL Sentral, linked to the station by an air-conditioned walkway and to the NU Sentral mall. Choose it for the airport train a few minutes from your room. Watch out: the sights are a train ride away. Check rates on Booking.com →

Before you book

  • Read the bill. Malaysian hotel rates are subject to an 8% service tax, many hotels add a service charge, usually 10%, and foreign guests pay a tourism tax of RM10 per room per night, which the hotel collects. Booking sites differ in what they include, so compare totals (Royal Malaysian Customs on the service tax and the tourism tax).
  • Fill in the Malaysia Digital Arrival Card. It is free and is done online in the three days before you arrive, on the Immigration Department’s own site; copycat sites charge for it, so use the official one (Immigration Department of Malaysia).
  • Choose the station before the hotel. In this heat, five minutes from a station is worth more than a bigger room. If you are still choosing a booking site, our hotel booking sites compared goes through them.

Getting around from your hotel

  • From the airport, the KLIA Ekspres runs nonstop to KL Sentral in 28 minutes from Terminal 1 and 33 minutes from Terminal 2 (KLIA Ekspres).
  • Around town, a Touch ‘n Go card pays for the LRT, the MRT and the monorail, and the covered walkway between KLCC and Bukit Bintang saves a hot walk.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Kuala Lumpur for a first trip?

KLCC: the Petronas Twin Towers, KLCC Park and the Suria KLCC mall are at your door, the LRT stops at KLCC station, and a covered walkway runs to Bukit Bintang.

Is Bukit Bintang or KLCC better?

KLCC for the towers, the park and quieter evenings; Bukit Bintang for Jalan Alor's food stalls, the malls and nightlife. They are joined by a covered, air-conditioned walkway, so either works for both.

Where should I stay in Kuala Lumpur for the airport?

KL Sentral: the KLIA Ekspres runs nonstop to the main terminal in 28 minutes and to Terminal 2 in 33, and Hilton Kuala Lumpur and Aloft Kuala Lumpur Sentral are linked to the station.

What taxes do Malaysian hotels add?

An 8% service tax, often a 10% service charge, and, for foreign guests, a tourism tax of RM10 per room per night collected by the hotel. Compare totals, since booking sites show them differently.

Do I need an arrival card for Malaysia?

Yes, the Malaysia Digital Arrival Card. It is free and is submitted on the Immigration Department's site in the three days before you arrive; avoid copycat sites that charge for it.

What is the Park Hyatt in Merdeka 118?

Park Hyatt Kuala Lumpur opened on 7 August 2025 on levels 75 to 114 of Merdeka 118, Malaysia's tallest building, with 252 rooms and a spa on the 99th floor.
