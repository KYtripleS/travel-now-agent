Munich’s hotel bill has an unusual feature for a big European city: no city tax. The city council voted in March 2023 for a 5% levy on room prices, but Bavarian law forbids it, and the courts have so far upheld the ban. What you pay on top of the room rate is German VAT, 7% on the room itself. The real cost of Oktoberfest week is timing, because the best rooms near the grounds go first and at least one hotel group was already selling 2027 festival rooms in autumn 2026.

Coming another time of year? Where to stay in Munich compares the Altstadt, Maxvorstadt, Schwabing and Haidhausen for an ordinary visit.

This guide covers five areas, from the streets beside the Theresienwiese to a calmer base in Augsburg, 12 hotels, the dates and tent rules the city publishes, and how to reach the grounds. Oktoberfest 2027: dates, hours and tables

The official Oktoberfest site says the 192nd Oktoberfest will take place from 18 September to 3 October 2027 on the Theresienwiese, so it opens on a Saturday and ends on a Sunday. Its opening-times page says the tents open at 9 a.m. on the first Saturday, non-alcoholic drinks are served from 10 a.m., and at 12 noon it is “O’zapft is” and the tapping begins. Cocoon, a hotel group, describes the pattern as the Saturday after 15 September to the first Sunday in October, but not before 3 October; that is the hotel’s wording, not the city’s.

The same page lists the festival site’s hours: Monday to Thursday 10 a.m. to 11.30 p.m., Friday 10 a.m. to midnight, Saturday 9 a.m. to midnight and Sunday 9 a.m. to 11.30 p.m. In the large beer tents it says the last beer and the music are at 10.30 p.m. The page also mentions the 2026 festival in places, so confirm the 2027 times nearer the date.

What the official site says about tent tables:

  • You do not have to reserve. Entry to the beer tents is free of charge, and a quarter of the seats in the big tents may not be reserved. At weekends and on public holidays, 40 percent of seats are exempt until 3 p.m., then 25 percent.
  • Reservations go through each tent. The site says there is no central booking desk, and reservations are made with the tent’s host.
  • Timing. For most beer tents the reservation option for 2027 will be activated by spring, at the latest in April or May.
  • Cost. The reservation itself is free of charge, but you must buy vouchers in advance, which the site says are tokens for items such as two litres of beer and half a chicken per person.
  • Crowds. The site warns that in the evenings and at weekends a tent can close because it is full.

Getting to the Theresienwiese, and what the transport operator says

The city’s site says there are no designated parking spaces for cars anywhere in the Oktoberfest area, and cars parked in prohibited areas may be towed, so plan to arrive by public transport or on foot. The MVG, the city’s transport operator and the festival’s official mobility partner, published its advice for the 2026 festival in a press release of 9 September 2026. We have not found the 2027 version, so treat the points below as last year’s advice.

  • The U4 and U5. These stop at Theresienwiese, the most direct station, and the release says the U-Bahn is sometimes overloaded and a platform may be closed briefly. It advises passengers on the U4 and U5 coming from the west to get off at Schwanthalerhöhe and walk to the grounds.
  • The U3 and U6. The MVG asks visitors to use these, because Goetheplatz and Poccistraße are only a few minutes’ walk from the grounds and the lines are usually less busy than the U4 and U5.
  • On foot from the Hauptbahnhof. The MVG says the walk via Hermann-Lingg-Straße is signposted and takes just under 15 minutes, and that on a crowded night it can be the calmer and sometimes faster option.
  • Trams and buses. The release names trams 16 and 17 to Hackerbrücke, 18 and 19 to Holzapfelstraße and Hermann-Lingg-Straße, and 20 and 21 to the Hauptbahnhof stops.
  • Going home. The release says there is a direct route from the grounds to the Theresienwiese U-Bahn station.

    Munich’s hotel tax: 7% VAT, and no city tax

    Charge Rate
    VAT on the room 7%, the reduced rate for short-term lodging
    VAT on breakfast and other extras 19%, the standard rate
    City or overnight tax None in force

    The German VAT law sets a reduced rate of 7% for letting rooms to guests for a short stay, and says that rate does not apply to services that do not directly serve the letting, even if the room price covers them. Our arithmetic: on a room that costs €200 including 7% VAT, the VAT is €13.08 (200 × 7 ÷ 107). A rate that includes breakfast is split across two rates, so look at the invoice, not the headline price.

    The city council decided in March 2023 on a 5% overnight tax, but the Bavarian Municipal Taxes Act says a municipality may not levy an Übernachtungsteuer. On 14 November 2025 the Bavarian Constitutional Court rejected the challenge by Munich and two other cities. On 26 February 2026 the city said its mayor and treasurer would propose a complaint to the Federal Constitutional Court. That court’s list of new cases for 2026 includes a municipal complaint against the Bavarian ban, but the list does not name the complainant, and we found no ruling. We found no tax in force in Munich, so none is added to the room rates here.

    Theresienwiese, Westend and Ludwigsvorstadt: the grounds on foot

    This is the area to pick if the walk home matters more than the sights. Cocoon’s Theresienwiese hotel is in Westend, Hotel Uhland and Hotel Mariandl are in Ludwigsvorstadt, and Cocoon’s Hauptbahnhof hotel is also in Ludwigsvorstadt, a short walk from the station. The trade-off is noise and crowds on your doorstep: Mariandl’s own page warns that during Oktoberfest the party runs every night and the street is louder than usual.

    Altstadt: sights, and a short trip to the tents

    The Altstadt is the old centre, around Marienplatz and the Viktualienmarkt, and it suits a trip where the festival is one part of the week. It is not beside the Theresienwiese, and none of these four hotels publishes a walking time to the grounds, so plan the journey with the MVG planner, bearing in mind the advice above on trams and the U3 and U6.

    Schwabing: quieter streets and the airport bus

    Schwabing is the university and café district north of the centre. Its hotels are further from the Theresienwiese, but they sit on the Lufthansa Express Bus route from the airport, and Flemings says the English Garden is about a ten-minute walk. Flemings names Münchner Freiheit (U3/U6) as about ten minutes away on foot, and the MVG asks festival visitors to use the U3 and U6; confirm your route in the MVG planner.

    Where to stay in Schwabing

    • Hotel Leopold, budget. At Leopoldstraße 119, a traditional house with breakfast included in every booking and parking at €8 a night or €10 in the garage. Choose it for the lowest price on this list that includes breakfast. Watch out: check-in runs from 15:00 to 23:30 and the hotel says arriving after 23:30 is no longer possible, which matters after a tent night. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Flemings Hotel München-Schwabing, comfortable. On Leopoldstraße, with a bus and tram stop directly in front and a garage with 36 spaces. Its rooms have a modern design with orange accents. Choose it for a bus and tram stop at the door. Watch out: it is a ten-minute walk from the nearest U-Bahn station by the hotel’s own figure. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Haidhausen: a residential base on the S-Bahn

    Haidhausen lies east of the Isar and is known as Munich’s artists’ quarter. The hotel below says the S-Bahn takes four stops (Ostbahnhof, Rosenheimer Platz, Isartor, Marienplatz) to reach Marienplatz in under ten minutes.

    Where to stay in Haidhausen

    • MOMA1890 Boutique Hotel, comfortable. A hotel owned by two brothers, with 51 individually designed rooms, fresh breakfast and free fast Wi-Fi. It says it is less than three kilometres from Marienplatz. Choose it for a design hotel away from the festival crowds. Watch out: it has no published walking time to the grounds, and you will change at the centre or take a tram or bus. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Augsburg: a calmer base outside the city

    Augsburg is the calm alternative. Deutsche Bahn’s timetable page for 10 November 2026, a sample day, shows direct ICE trains from München Hbf to Augsburg Hbf in as little as 25 minutes, with about 68 connections a day, and ICE fares from €17.98 on that day. For Oktoberfest, the trade-off is the last train home: check it before you commit.

    Where to stay in Augsburg

    • Hotel Maximilian’s, splurge. A five-star superior hotel in the centre of Augsburg with a rooftop pool heated all year, a spa, valet parking and a garage with charging for electric cars. Choose it for a quiet night after the festival, with a train ride between. Watch out: the hotel’s page gives no walking time to Augsburg Hbf, and Oktoberfest nights end late. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Getting in from the airport

    • S-Bahn (S1 and S8). Munich Airport says the trip to the main station takes about 40 minutes, and that the S1 and S8 run alternately, so there is a 10-minute interval. The S8 runs around the clock, with a train every 40 minutes from about 1 a.m. to 4 a.m.
    • Day ticket. The MVV’s Airport-City-Day-Ticket covers unlimited journeys between the airport and the city centre on one day, for one person or a group of up to five, from €17.50. We did not confirm a single-fare price, so check the MVV fare finder if you only need one trip.
    • Lufthansa Express Bus. It runs every 20 minutes from the Hauptbahnhof via Munich North (Schwabing) to the airport. The airport gives the journey as 45 minutes from the Hauptbahnhof and 25 minutes from Schwabing. An adult single is €12.50 online and €13.50 from the driver.

    How to book Munich for Oktoberfest

    • Book early, and watch the first window. Cocoon’s page says that from 19 October to 15 November 2026 its Oktoberfest rooms are available only on its own site, and that after that it opens the rooms for the official start of sales. Other hotels set their own dates, so ask each one.
    • Count the tax. Munich has no city tax in force, and the 7% VAT is normally in the quoted price. Compare the total, with breakfast, at the payment step.
    • Read cancellation terms and minimum stays. None of the 12 hotels here publishes an Oktoberfest minimum stay that we found, so ask in writing.
    • Do not let a tent table decide where you sleep. Each tent sets its own booking date, and although the reservation is free, the vouchers are paid in advance.

    Frequently asked questions

    When is Oktoberfest in 2027?

    The City of Munich's official site says the 192nd Oktoberfest takes place from 18 September to 3 October 2027 on the Theresienwiese. Its opening-times page says the tents open at 9 a.m. on the first Saturday and the tapping begins at 12 noon. The festival site is open Monday to Thursday 10 a.m. to 11.30 p.m., Friday 10 a.m. to midnight, Saturday 9 a.m. to midnight and Sunday 9 a.m. to 11.30 p.m.

    Do I need to reserve a table in an Oktoberfest tent?

    No. The official site says entry to the beer tents is free of charge, and a quarter of the seats in the big tents may not be reserved. There is no central reservation centre, so tables are reserved with each tent, and for most tents the 2027 reservation option will be activated by spring, at the latest in April or May. The reservation is free, but you must buy vouchers in advance.

    Does Munich have a hotel tax?

    We found no city tax in force in Munich. The city council decided on a 5% overnight tax in March 2023, but the Bavarian Municipal Taxes Act says a municipality may not levy an Übernachtungsteuer, and the Bavarian Constitutional Court rejected the challenge by Munich and two other cities on 14 November 2025. In February 2026 the city said it would propose a complaint to the Federal Constitutional Court, and we found no ruling.

    How much VAT is added to a Munich hotel room?

    German VAT on a short-term room let is the reduced rate of 7%, while breakfast and other extras that do not directly serve the letting fall under the standard 19% rate. Our arithmetic: on a room that costs €200 including 7% VAT, the VAT is €13.08. Look at the invoice if breakfast is included.

    How do I get to the Theresienwiese?

    The city's site says there are no designated parking spaces for cars in the Oktoberfest area. The MVG's advice for the 2026 festival, the latest we found, said the U4 and U5 stop at Theresienwiese but can be overloaded, asked visitors to use the U3 and U6 to Goetheplatz and Poccistraße, and said the walk from the Hauptbahnhof takes just under 15 minutes. The 2027 advice had not been published when we checked.

    Where is the best place to stay for Oktoberfest?

    For the shortest walk, stay in Theresienwiese, Westend or Ludwigsvorstadt; Cocoon says its Theresienwiese hotel is about a three-minute walk from the grounds. The Altstadt suits sightseeing, Schwabing is quieter and on the airport bus route, Haidhausen is a residential base on the S-Bahn, and Augsburg is 25 minutes from Munich by the fastest direct train on Deutsche Bahn's sample day. Book early: Cocoon said its 2027 festival rooms were available only on its own website from 19 October to 15 November 2026.
