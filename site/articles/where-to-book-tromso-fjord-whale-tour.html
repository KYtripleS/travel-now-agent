A Tromsø whale tour is really four different outings, and for Thursday 24 December 2026 the differences are bigger than the sellers’ price gaps. The boat is either a large catamaran with a heated lounge (the ones we read leave from the city harbour at 08:30 or 09:00) or an open RIB that needs a four-hour coach ride to Skjervøy first. The day runs from 6 to 8 hours up to 12. The weather terms differ: some operators promise a second sailing or a part refund, others state nothing. And the date matters: when we looked, the Brim Explorer hybrid-electric boat was not on sale for 24 December on GetYourGuide or Viator, and the one RIB seat on GetYourGuide was the last.

When whales are there, and what the listings promise

The Rødne listing says that from mid-October to the end of January humpbacks and orcas follow the herring into the fjords near Tromsø. The Cruise Service listing says December and January are peak whale-watching months. Both are the operators’ own statements. They also say the animals move with the herring and that no day is certain: the MS Gabriele listing says there is no 100% guarantee of seeing whales, and the Rødne listing says sightings cannot be guaranteed and may be affected by difficult weather.

What differs is what you get if it goes wrong. MS Gabriele (Cruise Service) offers a free ticket for a later date if no whales are seen, and a refund or another day if the tour is cancelled for weather. Rødne offers a free second sailing, and a 50 per cent refund if you cannot take it. The Brim and RIB listings we read did not state a second-chance or weather-refund term. Read the terms on the listing you choose, not only the price.

Which listing is the safe, quick buy

For 24 December it is KKday’s Tromsø Whale Safari with MS Gabriele. It had the lowest price we read for any whale sailing from Tromsø on the date (€158.64), it leaves from the city harbour at 09:00, you get a heated lounge and open deck, instant confirmation and free cancellation up to 2 days ahead. The operator’s own terms add a free later ticket if no whales are seen. It does not state a minimum age, and it carries no review score on the page we read.

If you want a larger review count, GetYourGuide’s Rødne listing has 4.6 from 3,858 reviews at €220.42 and a stated second-sailing promise. If you want to cancel as late as possible, GetYourGuide’s MS Gabriele listing allows it until 23 December, with reserve now and pay later, at €204.61.

The RIB is for a different reader. It is a long, cold, open-boat day with a 05:00 start, and it carries an age floor of 14. It had one place left on GetYourGuide when we looked.

Choosing between a catamaran and a RIB

  • Shelter. The catamarans have a heated lounge with large windows and open decks you can step out onto. The RIB is open to the sea and the weather for at least 3 hours, in a supplied thermal suit.
  • Time. The catamaran listings run 6 to 8 hours from the city harbour. The RIB is 12 hours, including about 4 hours on a coach each way to Skjervøy.
  • Children. The RIB takes no one under 14 or under 140 cm. The catamaran listings state no minimum age in the text we read; Viator and KKday list a child band from 6, and Viator lists under-6s as free. Confirm with the listing before booking for a young child.
  • Sea sickness. The Brim page says the trip can meet wavy conditions. The Rødne listing lists seasickness tablets as not included. A Viator reviewer reported rough seas on the MS Gabriele sailing in November.
