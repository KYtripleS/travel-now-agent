    12 February to 7 March 2027: the festival, Fair Day and the Parade

    Sydney Gay and Lesbian Mardi Gras publishes these 2027 dates:

    Date What Where
    Friday 12 February to Sunday 7 March The Mardi Gras festival Venues across the city
    Sunday 14 February, 10am to 9pm Fair Day, the festival’s kick-off, listed as free Victoria Park, corner of Parramatta Road and City Road
    Saturday 6 March The Parade, listed for 6pm to 11pm Oxford Street and Flinders Street, Darlinghurst and Surry Hills

    The festival calls this the 49th annual Parade, under the theme HYPERBLOOM, and says it will be led by Dykes on Bikes, LGBTQIA+ First Nations communities and the 78ers. It takes place on Gadigal land. Approved Mardi Gras+ events appear on the festival’s website and app from 6 November, so the calendar keeps growing after this guide was checked.

    The Parade route, and watching it

    The festival describes a route from Hyde Park to Moore Park along Oxford Street, Flinders Street and Anzac Parade, with more than 200 floats and more than 12,000 marchers, watched by hundreds of thousands of people in free and premium ticketed viewing areas.

    For 2027 the festival publishes this on viewing. A free Accessible Viewing Area has seating and an unobstructed view; you book free tickets, and it is an alcohol-free zone. One paid area, Glitter Club, has food, bars and its own accessible seating. A free shuttle runs from Foveaux Street Bus Stand F to Albion and Bourke Streets between 5pm and 8.30pm for registered guests, who must register by 5pm on Tuesday 26 January 2027. We found no map of general kerbside spots, so treat free street viewing as first come, first served and arrive early.

    Parade night: road closures and stations, as they were in 2026

    The 2027 transport plan was not published when we checked. The festival’s travel page still showed the Parade of Saturday 28 February 2026, and Transport for NSW’s Mardi Gras Parade event page redirected to its general events list. This is last year’s pattern, which 2027 may not match:

    • Roads: major roads from the city centre to Moore Park closed from 4pm to 2am. Oxford Street between College Street and Darlinghurst Road and the northbound lanes of Flinders Street closed from 5.30pm, along with sections of Bourke, Campbell and Commonwealth Streets, College and Liverpool Streets, and Wentworth Avenue.
    • Parking: strict no-parking clearways ran from 6am, residents included.
    • Trains: Museum Station closed from 5pm to 4am. The festival pointed the southern side of the route to Central, and the northern side to Town Hall, St James, Martin Place or Kings Cross. The closest Metro stations were Central, Gadigal and Martin Place.
    • Buses and light rail: extra buses ran from 3pm but diverted from 4pm, and the Moore Park light rail stop served Flinders Street.
    • Rideshare and on foot: rideshare could not take pick-ups inside the closed areas, and crossing points on Oxford Street, Flinders Street and Moore Park Road closed after 6.30pm.

    The lesson for a hotel search: pick a place you can walk to and from, or one on a station that stays open, and check the closure map on Live Traffic NSW closer to the date.

    Getting in from the airport, and around

    The train is the simple route: Sydney Airport says the city is 13 minutes away with trains about every 10 minutes, and Transport for NSW says the airport stations run from 5am to midnight. The fare has two parts. A station access fee of A$18.61 for adults is added to the ordinary train fare and goes to the Airport Link Company, which owns the stations; it is A$19.00 on a single ticket, the same on an Opal card or contactless card, and outside the Opal daily cap. Our arithmetic: if your trip falls in the 0 to 10 km band, A$18.61 plus the A$4.33 peak adult fare is A$22.94. The Opal daily cap is A$9.65 on a Saturday, and A$50 a week.     Darlinghurst and Surry Hills: on and beside the route

    The festival’s neighbourhood guide calls Darlinghurst the heart of Sydney’s LGBTQIA+ community, and says Oxford Street is where most revellers end up at midnight. Surry Hills lies on the other side of Oxford Street. Staying here means walking to the route, but also sleeping inside the closure zone, so check which of your hotel’s streets close, and when.

    Hyde Park and the city centre: the start of the route

    The Parade begins at Hyde Park, and the festival names the stations on this side: Town Hall, St James and Martin Place for the northern side of the route, Central for the southern. Museum Station, opposite one hotel below, was closed that night last year.

    Potts Point and Kings Cross: bars, clubs and a short walk

    Kings Cross is among the stations the festival names for the northern side of the route, and the festival’s guide names two Potts Point cafés among Sydney’s top. This is the base for staying local after the Parade.

    Where to stay in Potts Point and Kings Cross

    • Kirketon Hotel Sydney, comfortable, boutique. At 229 Darlinghurst Road, between Darlinghurst, Potts Point and Kings Cross, which the hotel says is a five-minute walk from Oxford Street and the city centre; free luggage storage and early check-in are subject to availability. Choose it for late nights on foot. Watch out: the hotel places itself in the middle of the bar and nightclub district, so ask for a quieter room if you want to sleep. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Newtown and Chippendale: the inner west

    Newtown is the community’s other hub. The festival’s guide points to King Street’s eating, a drag show at the Newtown Hotel, and the Imperial in Erskineville, made famous by The Adventures of Priscilla, Queen of the Desert, as an essential Mardi Gras stop. None of it is on the Parade route, so allow for the trip to Oxford Street on Parade night.

    Where to stay in Newtown and Chippendale

    • The Urban Newtown, good value. At 52-60 Enmore Road, a block from the Enmore Theatre and, the hotel says, a hop from King Street, with direct-booking perks listed on its site: check-in from 12.30pm, check-out at 11.30am and same-day cancellation. Choose it for a lower-priced base in the inner west. Watch out: plan the journey home before the night. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • The Old Clare Hotel, comfortable, heritage. At 1 Kensington Street, Chippendale, across two heritage-listed buildings, with a rooftop pool and bar and 24-hour reception; the festival’s guide says it straddles the inner west and the city centre. Choose it for character on the city side of the inner west. Watch out: parking carries a fee. Check rates on Booking.com →

    How to book Sydney Mardi Gras

    • Book early, and for the right night. The Parade is on Saturday 6 March, Fair Day on Sunday 14 February. Decide which you are coming for before you pick an area.
    • Check the closure zone for your street. Several hotels above sit on streets that closed on Parade night in 2026. Ask how the hotel handles arrivals, luggage and rideshare that evening.
    • Plan the way home. Roads stayed closed until 2am last year. A hotel you can walk back to is worth more than a nicer lobby.
    • Read the terms. We did not find festival-week minimum stays published for these hotels, so ask before you pay.
    • Compare totals. Our hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book; where to stay in Sydney has more hotels, and where to stay in Sydney for New Year’s Eve covers the other big night.

    Frequently asked questions

    When is Sydney Mardi Gras 2027?

    Sydney Gay and Lesbian Mardi Gras lists the 2027 festival as 12 February to 7 March, Fair Day at Victoria Park on Sunday 14 February from 10am to 9pm, and the Parade on Saturday 6 March, listed for 6pm to 11pm.

    Where does the Sydney Mardi Gras Parade go?

    The festival describes a route from Hyde Park to Moore Park along Oxford Street, Flinders Street and Anzac Parade, through Darlinghurst and Surry Hills, with more than 200 floats and more than 12,000 marchers.

    Where is the best place to stay for Sydney Mardi Gras?

    Darlinghurst and Surry Hills, if you want to walk to the route: the festival calls Darlinghurst the heart of the community. Hyde Park and the city centre suit the Parade's start and the stations, and Newtown is the community's other hub, a trip from the route.

    Is the Sydney Mardi Gras Parade free to watch?

    The festival lists free and premium ticketed viewing areas. For 2027 it publishes a free Accessible Viewing Area, where you book free tickets, and one paid area, Glitter Club. We found no map of general kerbside spots, so free street viewing is first come, first served.

    Do roads and stations close on Parade night?

    They did in 2026: major roads from the city centre to Moore Park closed from 4pm to 2am, and Museum Station closed from 5pm to 4am. The 2027 transport plan was not published when we checked, so confirm it with the festival and Transport for NSW.

    Is there a hotel tax in Sydney?

    We found none: Revenue NSW's list of state taxes, duties and levies includes no accommodation tax, and we found no city levy. GST of 10% applies to hotel stays of under 28 days. The airport train adds a separate station access fee of A$18.61 for adults.
