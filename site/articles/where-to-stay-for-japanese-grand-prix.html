The Formula 1 Japanese Grand Prix returns to Suzuka Circuit from Friday 9 to Sunday 11 April 2027, and the hotel question is really a question about the last 20 kilometres. We found no accommodation tax in force in Nagoya, Yokkaichi, Suzuka or Tsu, so the bill is shaped by distance, the train plan and how many nights you stay.

This guide covers the 2027 dates as the circuit and Formula 1 publish them, five bases weighed by how you reach the gates, 12 hotels, and the transport the circuit actually lists. The 2027 race-day timetables were not out in early November, so we label what ran in 2026. The 2027 dates: what is published

Formula 1’s 2027 calendar lists the Japanese Grand Prix as round 4, on 9 to 11 April, and Formula 1’s ticketing page says the race weekend takes place on those days at Suzuka. The circuit’s own Formula 1 page carries the 2027 branding and says tickets go on sale on Sunday 15 November 2026 at 11:00, so as of 3 November they were not yet on sale, and the ticketing page offered only a waitlist.

What When
The race weekend Friday 9 to Sunday 11 April 2027
Race day, per the circuit Sunday 11 April
Ticket sale, per the circuit Sunday 15 November 2026, 11:00
Applications for the circuit’s reserved direct buses From Monday 16 November 2026, 10:00

We have not seen a session timetable for 2027, so check it before you fix your nights. For comparison, the circuit’s 2026 busy-dates list put last year’s round on 27 to 29 March, so the 2027 date is about two weeks later in the spring.

Getting to the circuit: what the circuit says

The circuit’s Formula 1 access page is blunt. It names Kintetsu Shiroko Station and Kintetsu Hiratacho Station as the ways in from the Tokyo and Nagoya side, and it tells you not to use the Ise Railway’s Suzuka Circuit Ino Station. The reason it gives is that you may have to wait more than three hours to board, and that depending on conditions you may not get a train that day.

On ordinary days the circuit’s general access page gives these journeys, and race weekends will be slower.

Route What the circuit says
Kintetsu Nagoya to Shiroko About 50 minutes on the Kintetsu Nagoya Line
Shiroko to the circuit About 20 minutes by Mie Kotsu bus or taxi on ordinary days
JR Nagoya to Suzuka Circuit Ino JR Kansai Line to Yokkaichi, about 30 minutes, then Ise Railway, about 20 minutes, then about 20 minutes on foot
Kintetsu Osaka Namba to Shiroko About 100 minutes, then the same bus or taxi

Two cautions on that table. The 2027 event has its own page, which lists a walk from Kintetsu Shiroko Station of about 80 minutes to the Turn 1 Gate and about 60 minutes from Kintetsu Hiratacho Station to the Main Gate, so on race days the bus matters. And the circuit’s page says Ino station does not take transport IC cards and can run a train only every one to two hours at some times.

The circuit’s 2027 pages also list a no-reservation shuttle bus from Kintetsu Shiroko Station but give no fare or timetable for 2027, and we have found no Kintetsu or JR Central timetable for 2027 extra trains. News coverage of the 2026 round reported extra Kintetsu limited expresses between Kintetsu Nagoya and Shiroko and a JR Central limited express to Ino, but we could not read the operators’ own releases, so treat any 2027 train plan as unannounced.

The reserved direct buses. The circuit sells seat-reserved buses from a long list of stations, and the two that matter for this guide are priced as follows.

Service Days Price Notes
Nagoya Station (Taiko-dori Exit) to the circuit’s P9 car park Friday 9 to Sunday 11 April ¥10,000 return, ¥7,000 one way from the circuit One way about 2 hours 30 minutes to 3 hours 30 minutes; apply by 29 March
Kintetsu Shiroko Station, east exit Friday 9 April only ¥2,500 return Seven morning departures, returns at 17:30 and 19:30; apply by 24 March

The circuit’s list of direct-bus origins also includes Tsu Station on Saturday and Sunday, Kameyama Test Course as a park-and-ride, Kintetsu Nara, Kyoto and Shin-Osaka. By our arithmetic, the Nagoya return costs ¥7,500 more than the Shiroko Friday bus for the same day at the circuit, though one is for three days and one for a single Friday.

Driving. The circuit sells advance parking tickets. The main lots are three-day passes for 9 to 11 April, with lot P6, which is for electric vehicles, at ¥26,000 and lot P7 at ¥18,000, from which no car may leave between 15:00 and 19:00 each day. Ask each hotel about its own spaces before you rely on a car.

    What pushes the price up

    There is no accommodation tax table here. We found no accommodation tax in force in Nagoya, Yokkaichi, Suzuka or Tsu. Reports in 2026 say Nagoya City is setting up a committee to consider one, and Mie Prefecture has been running information seminars on accommodation taxes, but we found no decision. Suzuka City levies a bathing tax of ¥150 per person per night on staying guests of hot-spring baths, which is a different tax from an accommodation tax, and we did not find it printed on any hotel page here.

    • Event pricing. None of the hotel pages we read published event-weekend rates, so we have no number for how far prices rise. Book early, and compare the total.
    • Minimum stays. None of the pages named a minimum stay, but event terms can appear at the booking step, so read them before you pay.
    • The circuit hotel. The Suzuka Circuit Hotel’s booking page lists stays only up to 28 February 2027 and says later dates will be announced.
    • Parking. The circuit sells its own lots, and the hotel pages we read do not say whether spaces are held for event weekends, so ask each hotel.

    Nagoya Station: the most choice

    Nagoya is the biggest base here and the longest trip. Kintetsu’s Nagoya Line starts in the city, the circuit’s direct bus leaves from the Taiko-dori side of the station, and the city has Japan’s usual range of business hotels. The cost is time: about 50 minutes to Shiroko on ordinary days, then a bus.

Choose it for stepping from the platform to the lobby after a long day. Choose it for a full-service hotel in the city’s shopping district. Watch out: it is not at the station, and the hotel’s access page says it runs no free shuttle bus to Nagoya Station.     Yokkaichi: a shorter ride on the same lines

    Yokkaichi is the industrial port city between Nagoya and Suzuka. The circuit’s JR route runs Nagoya to Yokkaichi in about 30 minutes before the Ise Railway, and the hotels here are on the Kintetsu side. We have no official Yokkaichi-to-Shiroko journey time, so work it out on a journey planner.

    Where to stay in Yokkaichi

    • Platon Hotel Yokkaichi, comfortable. Three minutes’ walk from Kintetsu Yokkaichi Station, in what the hotel calls Mie’s busiest entertainment district, with 10 room types and a single room of 13.6 m². Choose it for a lively street at the door. Watch out: the entry rooms are small, and a busy district means evening noise.
    • Sanco Inn Yokkaichi Ekimae, budget. A business hotel one minute from the north exit of Kintetsu Yokkaichi Station, with a free Japanese and Western buffet breakfast. Choose it for the lowest-effort trip to the platform. Watch out: it is a business hotel, so the rooms are compact. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Yokkaichi Urban Hotel, budget. One minute from the north exit of Kintetsu Yokkaichi Station, with free breakfast and free entry to the group’s Manden no Yu bath. Choose it for breakfast and a bath included. Watch out: its page flags days when the free bath ticket does not apply, so check your dates.

    Shiroko and Suzuka city: closest to the track by rail

    Shiroko is the station the circuit recommends most strongly, and the hotels here are a minute or three from it. The circuit’s Friday reserved bus leaves from the east exit, and a no-reservation shuttle is planned for the rest of the weekend. The hotel choice is smaller, and event nights will fill first.

    Where to stay in Shiroko and Suzuka city

    • Shiroko Storia Hotel, good value. One minute from Kintetsu Shiroko Station, with a large bath, and single rooms that all have 140 cm double beds. Choose it for the shortest walk from the station. Watch out: the hotel says the circuit is about ten minutes by car, which is no help on closed roads.
    • Comfort Hotel Suzuka, budget. About three minutes from Kintetsu Shiroko Station, with a free breakfast. Choose it for a plain, predictable room by the station. Watch out: it is a chain business hotel, so expect small rooms and no event extras. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Around the circuit: the car and the track

    Two hotels sit close to the track. The Suzuka Circuit Hotel is the circuit’s own hotel, and the Suzuka Royal Hotel is a five-minute drive away in the city.

    Where to stay around the circuit

    • Suzuka Circuit Hotel, comfortable. The circuit’s own hotel, with eight room types listed on its booking page. Choose it for sleeping at the circuit. Watch out: its booking page lists stays up to 28 February 2027 only and says later dates will be announced, so there was no Grand Prix weekend on sale in early November.
    • Suzuka Royal Hotel, good value. A hotel at 3-2-21 Sumiyoshi that says it is five minutes by car from the circuit and 700 metres from the Honda Suzuka plant, with Japanese-style rooms of 14 mats that sleep three to six. Choose it for a group that wants a room with futons. Watch out: a five-minute drive is not a five-minute trip when the roads are closed, and the page gives its distances by car, not from a station. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Tsu: a quieter base to the south

    Tsu is the Mie prefectural capital, well down the line from Suzuka. The circuit’s direct bus from Tsu Station runs on Saturday and Sunday, which makes it a base for those days, not for Friday. We have no official train time from Tsu to Shiroko.

    Where to stay in Tsu

    • Dormy Inn Tsu, comfortable. One minute from the east exit of Tsu Station, with a natural hot-spring bath on the second floor and a Simmons bed in every room. Choose it for a bath after a long standing day. Watch out: it is the furthest base in this guide from the circuit. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Sanco Inn Tsu Ekimae, budget. One minute from the east exit of the Kintetsu and JR Tsu Station, with a communal bath. Choose it for the lowest price at the station. Watch out: it is a business hotel, so expect compact rooms. Check rates on Booking.com →

    How to book Suzuka for the Grand Prix

    • Wait for the tickets first. They go on sale on 15 November 2026, and the circuit’s direct bus applications open the next day, so you can fix your nights once you know your days.
    • Pick your last mile. The Nagoya bus is a fixed fare and a fixed seat but takes 2 hours 30 minutes to 3 hours 30 minutes one way. Shiroko is the shortest rail trip, but the 2027 shuttle details are not out.
    • Skip Ino. The circuit warns of waits of over three hours there.
    • Check the closures. Roads, bus lanes and car parks change for the race, so read the circuit’s access page again in March.
    • Compare totals. Our hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book, and where to stay in Japan covers the rest of the country, with where to stay in Osaka and where to stay in Kyoto if you are travelling on.

    Frequently asked questions

    When is the Japanese Grand Prix in 2027?

    Formula 1's calendar lists the Japanese Grand Prix as round 4, on 9 to 11 April 2027 at Suzuka, and the circuit's page names Sunday 11 April as race day. The circuit says tickets go on sale on 15 November 2026 at 11:00. We have not seen a 2027 session timetable.

    Where is the best place to stay for the Suzuka Grand Prix?

    Nagoya Station has the most hotels and the circuit's reserved direct bus, but the longest trip. Yokkaichi and Shiroko are shorter by rail, and Shiroko is the station the circuit recommends. The Suzuka Circuit Hotel's booking page lists stays only to 28 February 2027 and says later dates will be announced.

    How do you get to Suzuka Circuit for the Grand Prix?

    The circuit tells fans to use Kintetsu Shiroko or Hiratacho stations and warns that waits at the Ise Railway's Suzuka Circuit Ino Station can exceed three hours. It sells reserved direct buses from Nagoya Station, Tsu Station and others, and a Friday-only bus from Shiroko. We found no 2027 timetable for extra trains.

    How much is the direct bus from Nagoya to Suzuka Circuit?

    The circuit lists its reserved direct bus from Nagoya Station at 10,000 yen return, or 7,000 yen one way from the circuit, for 9 to 11 April, with a one-way time of about 2 hours 30 minutes to 3 hours 30 minutes. Applications open on 16 November 2026 at 10:00.

    Is there a hotel tax in Nagoya, Yokkaichi or Suzuka?

    We found no accommodation tax in force in Nagoya, Yokkaichi, Suzuka or Tsu. Nagoya City was reported to be setting up a committee to consider one, and Mie Prefecture has held information seminars. Suzuka City has a separate bathing tax of 150 yen per night for hot-spring bathers.

    Can you park at Suzuka Circuit during the Grand Prix?

    The circuit sells advance parking tickets. Its main lots are three-day passes for 9 to 11 April, with lot P6, for electric vehicles, at 26,000 yen and lot P7 at 18,000 yen, from which cars may not leave between 15:00 and 19:00 each day. Ask your hotel about its own parking.
