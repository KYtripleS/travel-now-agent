Hotels around Old Town, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Old Town that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

Edinburgh is the first place in Scotland to charge a visitor levy. Since 24 July 2026 every paid overnight stay in the city, from a five-star hotel to a hostel bed, carries a 5% charge on the room, on top of VAT, for up to five nights. On a £180 room at a hotel registered for VAT that is £9 a night. Where you stay decides the rest of the cost, in bus and tram fares and in how many hills you climb on foot.

This guide covers six areas, from the Old Town to Leith, 12 hotels, the airport links and the levy. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

Edinburgh’s hotel tax: the Visitor Levy and VAT

Two charges apply to a room in Edinburgh. VAT is normally already inside the price you see; the levy is new, and the City of Edinburgh Council sets its rules.

Charge Rate in 2026
Edinburgh Visitor Levy 5% of the accommodation-only price, before VAT
Nights charged The first 5 consecutive nights of a stay; nights 6 onwards are free of the levy
Where it applies Paid overnight stays from 24 July 2026: hotels, guest houses, B&Bs, hostels, aparthotels, short-term lets and campsites
Not charged on Extras such as parking, meals, drinks or transport
VAT on a hotel room 20%, the UK’s standard rate

VAT on the levy. The council’s guidance for accommodation providers says the levy counts as part of the business’s turnover, so a hotel registered for VAT adds 20% VAT to it as well. In practice that makes the levy 5% of the VAT-inclusive room price. A small guest house below the VAT threshold still charges the levy, but no VAT on it.

Our worked example. Two people book three nights at £180 a night, VAT included, at a VAT-registered hotel. The room before VAT is £150; 5% of that is £7.50, and VAT on the levy adds £1.50, so £9 a night and £27 for the stay. On a seven-night stay you pay the levy for five nights only, £45. That is our arithmetic from the council’s rules; how a hotel shows it on the bill can differ.

Older bookings. A stay that was booked and paid for, in part or in full, before 1 October 2025 is exempt, as long as the booking had fixed dates, a set number of rooms and a stated nightly price, and the payment was non-refundable. A refundable deposit does not count. A stay that began before 24 July 2026 is not charged, even if it ends after that date.

Refunds. The council refunds the levy, not the VAT on it, to guests who receive certain disability benefits, such as Personal Independence Payment or Disability Living Allowance. You claim after the stay, with the benefit award letter and the invoice.

Because the levy is a percentage, the total you are quoted should already include it. Hampton by Hilton Edinburgh West End, for example, says on its own site that the 5% Visitor Levy is included in the total price shown.

Getting in from the airport, and around

  • By tram, from the stop outside the terminal: £7.90 single or £9.50 open return to the city centre, with trams every 7 minutes for most of the day. The airport puts the journey into the city centre at 30 minutes and to Newhaven, beyond Leith, at 54 minutes. The tram runs through Haymarket, the West End, Princes Street and St Andrew Square to Leith.
  • By the Airlink 100 bus, Lothian’s 24-hour express to Waverley Bridge, next to Waverley station: £6 single or £8.50 open return, up to every 10 minutes from early morning to around 1am and up to every 20 minutes overnight.
  • In town, a single on Lothian buses or the city-zone tram costs £2.40, and a City DAY ticket £6.00, but it is not valid on the airport tram or the Airlink.
  • Tap a contactless card or phone on buses and trams and the fares cap at £5.70 a day in the city zone and £26.50 a week, Monday to Sunday. A Network DAY ticket, which covers the airport on both tram and bus, costs £14.00. Use the same card or phone all day, one per person.

The Old Town: the first-trip base

UNESCO describes Edinburgh as two distinct towns: the Old Town, “dominated by a medieval fortress”, and the planned New Town. The Old Town runs down the ridge of the Royal Mile from Edinburgh Castle to the Palace of Holyroodhouse, with closes and steep streets dropping off either side and Waverley station in the valley below. Almost everything a first visit needs is on foot. The price is crowds, above all in August, and a good deal of climbing.

Where to stay in the Old Town

On Castlehill, at the top of the Royal Mile, steps from the gates of Edinburgh Castle, with nine suites, among them the Library, with a book-filled bathroom, and the largest, with seven windows over the Royal Mile. Booking direct adds a bottle of Champagne in the suite each night. Choose it for a once-only night by the castle. Choose it to arrive by train or Airlink and walk in. Watch out: some guest reviews on the hotel’s own page mention rooms without a window; ask when you book. Check rates on Booking.com →

The New Town: Georgian squares and Princes Street

North of Princes Street Gardens, the New Town is the city laid out from the 18th century onwards: straight streets, squares and crescents of neoclassical stone, which UNESCO credits with a far-reaching influence on European town planning. The two towns were listed together as a World Heritage Site in 1995. It is flatter than the Old Town, the tram runs along Princes Street to St Andrew Square, and George Street has the restaurants and bars.

Where to stay in the New Town

  • The Balmoral, splurge. At 1 Princes Street, under the landmark clock tower, “where Old Town meets New” in the hotel’s words, with Castle View rooms and a Glamis Suite. Choose it for the grand hotel at the centre of everything. Watch out: price; when we checked, two November weeknights in an Executive Double cost £1,213. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Kimpton Charlotte Square, comfortable. Seven interconnected Georgian townhouses at the west end of George Street, around a glass-topped courtyard, with a spa, the BABA restaurant and a private townhouse, No. 33, to rent whole. Choose it for the New Town’s finest square. Watch out: the room count and sizes are not published on the hotel’s site; check the room type before you book. Check rates on Booking.com →

Stockbridge: a village feel

Stockbridge sits just below the New Town’s northern edge, on the Water of Leith, with shops, cafés and pubs around Raeburn Place and a riverside path upstream to Dean Village. It feels like a neighbourhood rather than a sight, and the Old Town is a walk away, uphill through the New Town. There are few hotels, which is part of the appeal.

Where to stay in Stockbridge

  • The Raeburn, comfortable. A Georgian family home built in 1832 on Raeburn Place, now a boutique hotel with ten bedrooms, a bar, a library and outdoor terraces. Choose it to live like a local for a few nights. Watch out: with only ten rooms it fills early, and room sizes are not published on its site. Check rates on Booking.com →

Leith: the waterfront and the end of the tram line

Edinburgh’s old port lies about two miles from the centre, on the Firth of Forth. The tram runs from the city centre through Leith to Newhaven, with stops at The Shore, Port of Leith and Ocean Terminal. Leith gives you the waterfront, with restaurants and bars along The Shore; the trade-off is a tram or bus ride to the castle.

Where to stay in Leith

  • Malmaison Edinburgh, comfortable. At 1 Tower Place on the Leith waterfront, with the Chez Mal Brasserie & Bar. Choose it for the waterfront and an easy tram ride in. Watch out: the hotel itself puts the city centre 10 minutes away by taxi. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Fingal, splurge. A former Northern Lighthouse Board ship moored in Alexandra Dock and open as a hotel since 2019, with 23 cabins, the Lighthouse Restaurant & Bar and an AA five-star rating. The Port of Leith tram stop is about two minutes’ walk away. Choose it for a night on a ship. Watch out: the hotel puts the city centre two miles away, so you will use the tram. Check rates on Booking.com →

The West End and Haymarket: the castle view and the trains west

The West End is the far end of Princes Street, under the castle rock, running on to Haymarket railway station. It has two tram stops, West End and Haymarket, on the line to the airport, and the Edinburgh International Conference Centre. It is a practical base: flat, well connected and quieter at night than the Old Town.

Where to stay in the West End

  • The Caledonian Edinburgh, Curio Collection by Hilton, splurge. On Princes Street for more than 120 years, with views of Edinburgh Castle, rooms inspired by the coats of arms in the stained-glass windows on the grand staircase, a spa and The Court restaurant. Choose it for the castle from your window. Watch out: when we checked on Booking.com, the entry Deluxe Queen rooms were 20 m² and breakfast cost extra; book a castle-view room by name. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hampton by Hilton Edinburgh West End, good value. At 166 Fountainbridge, a 10-minute walk from the conference centre and, by the hotel’s count, a mile from the castle, with a free hot breakfast. Choose it for a lower price with breakfast included. Watch out: pets are not allowed, and the Old Town is about a mile away, a walk or a bus ride. Check rates on Booking.com →

Southside and Newington: Arthur’s Seat and calmer streets

South of the Old Town, the Southside and Newington are the university quarter and the residential streets beyond it, with Holyrood Park and Arthur’s Seat to the east. The Old Town is a short walk north, and frequent buses run along the main roads. The streets are quieter than the Royal Mile’s, even in August.

Where to stay in Southside and Newington

  • Prestonfield House, splurge. A five-star house in 20 acres of gardens and parkland in the shadow of Arthur’s Seat, with 23 bedrooms and suites furnished with antiques, and the Rhubarb restaurant. Choose it for a country house five minutes from the Old Town, in the hotel’s words. Watch out: it is set apart in its own grounds; plan on taxis or buses into town. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Ten Hill Place, good value. A 129-bedroom hotel at 10 Hill Place, run by the Royal College of Surgeons of Edinburgh, on the edge of the Old Town by the hotel’s own description, with discounted tickets to the Surgeons’ Hall Museums. Choose it for a central, quieter base at a fair price. Watch out: the robes, slippers and chocolates in the Superior rooms come only when you book direct with the hotel. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Edinburgh

  • Count the levy. Make sure the total includes the 5% Visitor Levy for the first five nights. At a VAT-registered hotel it adds 5% of the room price: £9 a night on a £180 room.
  • Watch the season. The Edinburgh Festival Fringe runs from 6 to 30 August 2027, and prices rise with it. When we checked the same two weeknights for two adults, 11–13 August 2027 cost about twice as much as 18–20 November 2026, or more: Motel One Edinburgh-Royal £732 against £299, and Hampton by Hilton Edinburgh West End £671 against £355. For August, book early and favour free cancellation.
  • Check the room size. Some rooms here are small by design: Motel One’s are 16 m². If you need space, the Radisson Blu’s Standard rooms are 27 m².

Before you go

  • Check whether you need an ETA. If you are from Europe, the USA, Australia, Canada or certain other countries, you usually need an electronic travel authorisation to visit the UK, Scotland included, rather than a visa. It costs £20 on GOV.UK or the UK ETA app, lasts two years or until the passport expires, and a decision usually comes within a day, though it can take up to 3 working days. Each traveller needs one, children included; British and Irish passport holders do not. An ETA does not guarantee entry.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the best area to stay in Edinburgh for a first trip?

    The Old Town: the Royal Mile runs from Edinburgh Castle to the Palace of Holyroodhouse, with Waverley station below, so almost everything a first visit needs is on foot. Choose the New Town for Georgian squares and Princes Street, Stockbridge for a village feel, Leith for the waterfront, the West End for Haymarket station and the tram, and Southside for Arthur's Seat.

    How much is the Edinburgh tourist tax?

    The Edinburgh Visitor Levy is 5% of the accommodation-only price before VAT, charged on the first five consecutive nights of any paid overnight stay from 24 July 2026. At a VAT-registered hotel, VAT is added to the levy too, so it works out at 5% of the VAT-inclusive room price: £9 a night on a £180 room, by our arithmetic.

    Do I pay the Edinburgh Visitor Levy if I booked before it started?

    Not if the stay was booked and paid for, in part or in full, before 1 October 2025, with fixed dates, a set number of rooms, a stated nightly price and a non-refundable payment. A refundable deposit does not count. A stay that began before 24 July 2026 is not charged, even if it ends after that date.

    How do you get from Edinburgh Airport to the city centre?

    By tram, £7.90 single or £9.50 open return, about 30 minutes to the city centre, with trams every 7 minutes for most of the day; or by the 24-hour Airlink 100 bus to Waverley Bridge, £6 single or £8.50 open return.

    Is Edinburgh more expensive during the Fringe?

    Yes. The Fringe runs from 6 to 30 August 2027. When we checked the same two weeknights, 11 to 13 August 2027 cost about twice as much as 18 to 20 November 2026, or more: £732 against £299 at Motel One Edinburgh-Royal and £671 against £355 at Hampton by Hilton Edinburgh West End.

    Do I need an ETA to visit Edinburgh?

    If you are from Europe, the USA, Australia, Canada or certain other countries, you usually need a UK electronic travel authorisation rather than a visa. It costs £20, lasts two years or until the passport expires, and each traveller needs one, children included. British and Irish passport holders do not. An ETA does not guarantee entry.
