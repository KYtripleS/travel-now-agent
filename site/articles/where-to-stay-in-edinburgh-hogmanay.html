    Edinburgh’s Hogmanay Street Party is a ticketed night inside closed city-centre streets. The organisers say 45,000 people will party into 2027 there, with gates opening at 8pm on Thursday 31 December 2026 and last entry at 11pm.     29 December to 1 January: the official programme

It has not yet published stage times for the Street Party; the organisers say they will be released in the lead-up to the event.

Tickets run from £79, and there are Enclosure, Gardens and Premium tiers.

    The rules that decide where you sleep

    • Pick the gate first. The Street Party has three gates: the South Gate at Bank Street and the top of The Mound, the East Gate at the east end of Princes Street where it meets North Bridge, and the West Gate at the west end of Princes Street, off the bottom of Lothian Road. Each ticket names one gate, and admittance is strictly through it. The Gardens concert has two, West and South, with the same rule.
    • The two tickets are separate. A Street Party ticket does not get you into Hogmanay in the Gardens, and the other way round.
    • No re-entry. Once you leave the Street Party or the Gardens, you cannot go back in. Last entry is 11pm, so nobody nips back to the hotel at 11.30.
    • Ages. No one under 12 is admitted to the Street Party, the Gardens or the Night Afore Party. Anyone under 16 must hold a full-price ticket and be accompanied by an adult over 18.
    • Alcohol. You may bring alcohol into the Street Party only in plastic or metal containers under 500ml, up to four per person, and no glass. No alcohol may be brought into the Gardens concert or the Night Afore Party.
    • What to leave behind. Umbrellas, glass, drones, dogs (except registered assistance dogs), flares, Chinese lanterns and bags larger than a sheet of A3 paper.
    • Buy from the official sellers only. The organisers name edinburghshogmanay.com, Ticketmaster and tickets-scotland.com, say there is no official resale, and say tickets listed on Eventbrite or secondary sites are not valid. Tickets are e-tickets. A gifted ticket needs a signed letter of authority and the recipient’s photo ID.

    Last year’s closures, and what is not yet published

    The organisers’ page for residents and businesses still describes the 2025 edition, and the 2026 closure notices had not been posted when we looked. Last year’s pattern was this: closures began progressively from 00:01 on 29 December, the arena gates were in use from 17:00 on 30 December, and the main closure of Princes Street and its side streets began at 19:00 that evening. On 31 December most pedestrian restrictions started at 18:00 and lasted until about 04:00 on 1 January.

    Anyone with a property or business inside the arena was offered access passes, for staff and customers where applicable. The addresses on last year’s list were 30 to 134 Princes Street, 2 to 35 Castle Street, 1 to 19 Frederick Street, 1 to 33 Hanover Street, 17 Waverley Bridge and 18 to 21 Market Street. None of the hotels below published how it admits guests through the barriers, so ask in writing, and ask which side of the barrier your hotel’s front door falls on.

    The West End and Lothian Road: the West Gate

    The West Gate for the Street Party is off the bottom of Lothian Road, and West Princes Street Gardens, where the Underworld concert is held, lies below the Castle beside it. If your ticket is for the West Gate, or for the Gardens, a bed at this end means the walk home is short and flat.

    The east end of Princes Street: the East Gate

    The East Gate is the junction of Princes Street and North Bridge, and the three hotels here sit at that end of the street. None of them is on last year’s list of addresses that were offered access passes, which began at 30 Princes Street, but the organisers do not say exactly where the East Gate stands relative to them. Ask each hotel.

    The Old Town: Torchlight and the South Gate

    The Torchlight Procession on 29 December climbs through the Old Town, and the South Gate for the Street Party is at Bank Street, at the top of The Mound. The Old Town is where you stay if the procession is the point, or if you are happy to walk the Royal Mile to a South Gate ticket. The streets are steep and cobbled, and the walk back after midnight is the cost.

    Getting in from the airport, and around on the night

    • By tram from the airport: £7.90 single or £9.50 open return to the city centre, with trams every 7 minutes for most of the day. The airport puts the journey into the city centre at 30 minutes. Last year, Edinburgh Trams said that from after 6pm on 30 December trams would stop serving Princes Street and St Andrew Square, running a split service, Edinburgh Airport to West End and Newhaven to Picardy Place, through 31 December, with the road expected to re-open on the morning of New Year’s Day.
    • After midnight, last year’s pattern was all-night trams, free after midnight: up to every 10 minutes from midnight until 2am, then every 20 minutes until the start of service on 1 January. The 2026 notice was not yet posted when we looked.
    • By Airlink 100 bus: Lothian’s 24-hour express to Waverley Bridge, £6 single or £8.50 open return. Waverley Bridge is close to the arena, so check where it stops on the night.
    • Lothian’s city buses: last year’s Christmas and New Year guide said last buses would run earlier than normal on 31 December, with services curtailed or diverted, and several routes cut at West End or Waterloo Place all day. It listed special Hogmanay night services for the morning of 1 January. Lothian had not published its 2026 version when we checked.
    • In town, a single on Lothian buses or the city-zone tram costs £2.40, and fares cap at £5.70 a day in the city zone when you tap a contactless card or phone.
    • On foot. Princes Street is closed to traffic, and the organisers’ travel page, which still carries some of last year’s wording, says ScotRail services wind down from about 7pm on 31 December; check scotrail.co.uk.

    Edinburgh’s Visitor Levy

    Edinburgh is the first place in Scotland to charge a visitor levy. Since 24 July 2026 every paid overnight stay in the city, from a five-star hotel to a hostel bed, carries a 5% charge on the room, on top of VAT, for the first five nights.

    Charge Rate in 2026
    Edinburgh Visitor Levy 5% of the accommodation-only price, before VAT
    Nights charged The first 5 consecutive nights of a stay
    Where it applies Paid overnight stays from 24 July 2026: hotels, guest houses, B&Bs, hostels, aparthotels, short-term lets and camp sites
    Not charged on Extras such as parking, meals, drinks or transport
    VAT on a hotel room 20%, the UK’s standard rate

    A hotel registered for VAT adds VAT to the levy as well, which makes the levy 5% of the VAT-inclusive room price. As our arithmetic, a room at £450 a night including VAT costs £375 before VAT; 5% of that is £18.75, plus £3.75 of VAT on the levy, so £22.50 a night and £45 for the two nights of 30 and 31 December. A stay booked and paid for before 1 October 2025 is exempt, which no Hogmanay booking made now can be. Compare totals, not headline prices: Hampton by Hilton Edinburgh West End says on its own site that the levy is included in the total it shows.

    When to book Hogmanay in Edinburgh

    • Tickets first. Tickets for the Street Party and the Gardens concert are on sale, and the gate on your ticket is final. We could not see how many remain.
    • Check the minimum stay. The Balmoral’s Hogmanay Ball offer needs two nights, on 30 and 31 December only. The Caledonian sells Hogmanay Packages from £225. The other hotels publish no New Year’s Eve minimum on the pages we read, so ask.
    • Read what is a stay and what is a dinner. The Scotsman, Virgin Hotels Edinburgh, Apex Grassmarket and The Witchery sell Hogmanay events by the person. Check whether they need a room, and what the deposit terms are.
    • Check your dates before you fall for an offer. The Balmoral’s package covers 30 and 31 December only. Look at a booking site’s availability for your own nights, and keep free cancellation until your tickets are in hand.
    • Compare totals. Our hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book, and hotel taxes around the world shows how other cities charge theirs.

    Frequently asked questions

    When is Edinburgh's Hogmanay Street Party in 2026?

    Thursday 31 December 2026. The organisers say gates open at 8pm, last entry is at 11pm, the midnight countdown starts at 11:59pm and the event ends at about 12:50am. About 45,000 people are expected, and stage times had not been released when we checked.

    Do you need a ticket for the Hogmanay Street Party?

    Yes. Tickets start at £10, and each one names a single gate, east, west or south, which cannot be changed after purchase. The organisers name edinburghshogmanay.com, Ticketmaster and tickets-scotland.com as the only official sellers, and say there is no official resale.

    Where is the best place to stay for Hogmanay in Edinburgh?

    It depends on your gate. The East Gate is at the east end of Princes Street, by North Bridge, and the West Gate is off the bottom of Lothian Road. The South Gate is at Bank Street at the top of The Mound. The Old Town suits the Torchlight Procession and the South Gate, and the West End suits the West Gate and the Gardens concert.

    Do Edinburgh hotels have minimum stays at Hogmanay?

    Some do. The Balmoral's Hogmanay Ball offer needs a minimum of two nights and is available for stays on 30 and 31 December 2026 only. The Caledonian lists Hogmanay Packages from £225 without saying what they include. The other hotels here publish no New Year's Eve minimum on the pages we read, so ask.

    Can you leave the Street Party and come back in?

    No. The organisers say re-entry is not permitted, and last entry is at 11pm. Umbrellas, glass, drones, dogs other than registered assistance dogs and bags larger than a sheet of A3 paper are not allowed, and no one under 12 is admitted.

    Is there a visitor levy on Edinburgh hotels at Hogmanay?

    Yes. Since 24 July 2026 Edinburgh charges a Visitor Levy of 5% of the accommodation-only price, before VAT, for the first five consecutive nights of a stay. A VAT-registered hotel adds VAT to the levy, so it works out as 5% of the VAT-inclusive room price.
