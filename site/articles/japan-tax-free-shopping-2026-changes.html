Japan Tax-Free Shopping Changes 1 Nov 2026: What Tourists Need to Know

By Casey, Gently Yonder editor

From 1 November 2026 Japan switches to a pay-then-refund tax-free system: pay the full price in-store, claim the 10% back at the airport. The ¥5,000 minimum, the 90-day rule, the new refund steps, and what got simpler.

Updated 2026-09-09 · 5 min read

Build My Trip Checklist All guides

If you’re shopping in Japan from 1 November 2026, the tax-free system works differently. Instead of having the 10% consumption tax waived at the register — the way it works today — you’ll pay the full, tax-inclusive price in the store and claim the refund at the airport before you fly home. You still need to spend at least ¥5,000 (pre-tax) at one shop on one day, keep the goods with you, and complete the refund procedure — at a customs terminal or via Visit Japan Web in the departure lobby — with everything bought within 90 days of your departure. It’s a few more steps than before, but the rules on what actually qualifies get simpler.

Here’s the plain-English version, because this is the kind of change that quietly costs people money if they don’t know it’s coming.

What’s changing, exactly?

Today, tax-free stores deduct Japan’s 10% consumption tax at the point of sale when you show your passport — you walk out having paid the lower price. From 1 November 2026, Japan moves to a “pay first, refund later” system: you pay the full tax-included price at the register, then reclaim the 10% when you leave the country. There is no parallel transition period — the old point-of-sale exemption simply ends and the new method begins.

How the new refund process works

The flow at departure is straightforward once you know it:

  1. In the store: buy your goods and pay the full price, tax included. The shop records the purchase against your passport.
  2. At the airport, before check-in: go to a customs terminal or open Visit Japan Web in the departure lobby and submit your tax-free application.
  3. You’ll get a green result (proceed to check-in) or a red one (a customs inspection is required — have your goods and receipts ready).
  4. Claim your refund using the method the store told you about at purchase, after you’ve completed departure procedures.

Because it happens at departure, you’ll want to allow extra time at the airport and keep your purchases and receipts accessible — not buried in checked luggage.

Do the basic rules still apply?

Mostly yes, with the familiar thresholds:

  • Minimum spend: at least ¥5,000 before tax, at the same store on the same day.
  • Who qualifies: short-term foreign visitors (temporary-visitor status), with your passport.
  • Timing: goods must be purchased within 90 days of your departure, and they must leave Japan with you.

What actually gets simpler

This is the upside, and it’s real:

  • The old split between “general goods” and “consumables” is being eliminated — one set of rules.
  • The special “sealed packaging” requirement for consumables (cosmetics, snacks, medicines) is abolished, so you’re no longer handed a taped-up bag you can’t open until you leave.
  • The previous ¥500,000 upper limit on consumables is removed.
  • One condition remains: consumables still shouldn’t be used or consumed before you depart Japan.

What to do differently on the day

  • Budget for the 10% up front. Your cash or card needs to cover the full price now; the refund comes later. On a big electronics or luxury purchase, that’s real money to float.
  • Do Visit Japan Web ahead of time. The refund step ties into the same system used for a smooth arrival — see our Visit Japan Web guide to set it up before you fly.
  • Keep receipts and goods together and give yourself buffer time at Narita, Haneda, or Kansai.
  • Factor it into your trip budget — our guide to how much a Japan trip costs and how much cash to carry both help here.

One more, slightly older change worth knowing: since 1 April 2025, goods shipped home by international parcel are no longer eligible for the tax exemption — the refund is for items you carry out yourself.

Frequently asked questions

When does Japan’s new tax-free system start? 1 November 2026. There’s no overlap period — the point-of-sale exemption ends and the pay-then-refund system begins that day.

Will I still get the 10% back? Yes, on qualifying purchases — but as a refund claimed at the airport before departure, not as a discount at the register. You pay the full price first.

What’s the minimum I need to spend? At least ¥5,000 before tax at the same store on the same day, the same threshold as before.

Do I need Visit Japan Web? It’s one of the ways to process the refund application in the departure lobby (a customs terminal is the other). Setting up Visit Japan Web before you fly makes the airport step faster.

Can I still get consumables tax-free without the sealed bag? Yes — from 1 November 2026 the sealed-packaging requirement is abolished, though you still shouldn’t consume the items before leaving Japan.
