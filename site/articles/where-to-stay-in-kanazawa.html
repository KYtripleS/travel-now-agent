Hotels around Omicho, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Omicho that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

Kanazawa’s hotel bill carries a small city tax: ¥200 or ¥500 per person per night, and nothing at all on a room under ¥5,000 a person. It is a modest sum, but it is charged per guest, so it grows with the size of your party. The choice that matters more is where to base yourself. Kanazawa Station is the arrival point, while the sights sit a bus ride or a long walk to the east, so each area below trades convenience on arrival against distance from the old town.

This guide covers five areas, from Kanazawa Station to the Omicho market, 12 hotels, the tax and the airport and bus links. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here. Noto and the rest of Ishikawa Prefecture are separate trips and are not covered.

Kanazawa’s accommodation tax

Kanazawa City charges its own accommodation tax, in place since April 2019. Since 1 October 2024 stays under ¥5,000 per person per night are exempt, and the rest pay one of two flat amounts. As of 20 October 2026 the city’s tax page and ordinance show no later change.

Room charge per person per night, before consumption tax City tax
Under ¥5,000 None
¥5,000 to under ¥20,000 ¥200
¥20,000 or more ¥500

The charge is the room-only price with any service charge, but without meals, consumption tax or the bathing tax (nyuto-zei) a hot-spring hotel may add. It applies to every guest whatever their age, and to hotels, ryokan, simple lodgings and registered home-share rentals alike. You pay it to the property with the room charge, and the property passes it to the city.

For a worked example, which is our arithmetic: a couple pays ¥36,300 a night for a room only, with the 10% consumption tax included. Before that tax the room costs ¥33,000, or ¥16,500 each, so the city tax is ¥200 each, ¥400 a night and ¥1,200 over three nights. A solo traveller paying ¥22,000 including consumption tax is paying ¥20,000 before it, and so pays ¥500 a night. The band is set by each guest’s share of the room, not by the room’s total price.

Japan’s standard consumption tax rate is 10%, and Japanese hotel rates are normally quoted with it included.

Getting in, and around Kanazawa

  • By Shinkansen. The Hokuriku Shinkansen runs to Kanazawa Station. JR West gives the journey from Tokyo as 2 hours 28 minutes. For a pass, see is the JR Pass worth it in 2026.
  • From Komatsu Airport. Hokutetsu runs a limousine bus to the city. The adult fare is ¥1,400 (¥700 for a child) to Kanazawa Station’s west exit, Korinbo and the Musashigatsuji and Omicho Market stop, and the operator gives the journey as about 40 to 55 minutes. There is no reservation: you can board and pay when you get off, or buy a ticket in advance at the airport or the Hokutetsu ticket counters. The operator accepts the ICa card, contactless cards and digital tickets, but its bus change is only available for ¥1,000 notes, so carry coins. Its timetable changes on 25 October 2026.
  • The Kanazawa Loop Bus. The tourist loop runs from bay 7 at the station’s east exit, with stops for Higashi Chaya (Hashiba-cho), Kenrokuen and the castle, Korinbo and Omicho Market. An adult single costs ¥220 and a one-day pass ¥800, which is not sold on board; the operator lists IC cards and contactless cards as accepted. The right-hand route runs about every 15 minutes from 08:30 to 18:00 and the left-hand route about every 20 minutes from 08:38 to 17:58, so for dinner in Korinbo or Katamachi you will want a regular bus or a taxi.

Kanazawa Station: the train at your door

For a short stay, or an arrival late or early on the Shinkansen, the station is the easiest base. The hotels here are within a few minutes of the concourse, the loop bus starts at the east exit and the regular buses to Korinbo, Katamachi and Omicho leave from the same terminal. The old town is a bus ride away, so the area’s strength is convenience, not atmosphere.

Korinbo and Katamachi: dinner and a bus home

Korinbo and Katamachi are Kanazawa’s downtown districts, and OMO5’s own page calls Katamachi the city’s culinary heart. Staying here puts dinner close, while the loop bus stops at Korinbo for Kenrokuen and the castle by day. Buses from the station take about ten minutes, by the hotels’ own pages.

Where to stay in Korinbo and Katamachi

  • Kanazawa Tokyu Hotel, comfortable. At Korinbo 2-1-1, ten minutes by regular bus from the station to the Korinbo Atrio-mae stop and then two minutes on foot, or six minutes by taxi. Its luxury floors have Kutani-ware mugs, and its restaurant serves a breakfast buffet of local ingredients. Choose it for a classic downtown base. Watch out: it is not a station hotel, so you need the bus or a taxi with luggage. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • OMO5 Kanazawa Katamachi by Hoshino Resorts, good value. A 100-room hotel at Katamachi 1-4-23, ten minutes by bus from the station and four minutes on foot from the Korinbo stop, with Kenrokuen ten minutes’ walk away and the 21st Century Museum six. Choose it for a central base at a fair price. Watch out: its parking is by partner lots, first come, and cannot be reserved. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Murataya Ryokan, budget. A Japanese-style inn at Katamachi 1-5-2 with 11 tatami rooms, none with a private bath, a Japanese public bath and coin laundry. It is three minutes on foot from the Katamachi bus stop. Choose it for a plain, cheap and central night in a Japanese-style room. Watch out: it takes bookings by phone, fax or email on its own site, and its rates page still describes the 2019 tax, saying it is collected in cash, so confirm the current amount. Higashi Chaya: the teahouse streets

Higashi Chaya is one of Kanazawa’s old teahouse districts, a paved lane of wooden buildings with latticed fronts. The hotels inside the district are few. The loop bus reaches it from the station, at the Hashiba-cho stop, and the district is also about 12 minutes on foot from Omicho, by the Sanraku’s own count, so the pick here is a single machiya inn and not a row of hotels.

Where to stay in Higashi Chaya

  • UTAIMACHI, comfortable. A machiya inn at Higashiyama 1-16-6-1 with three rooms, Sen and Happyaku for up to four guests and Niju for up to three, among the district’s teahouses. Choose it for a night inside the old quarter. Watch out: with only three rooms there is little choice of room, and its reservation line keeps limited hours, 10:00 to 18:00 on weekdays and 11:00 to 17:00 at weekends. Check rates on Booking.com →

Kenrokuen and the castle: the garden at first light

The castle park and Kenrokuen Garden sit beside each other in the middle of the city, and the hotels near them can reach both on foot. Staying here suits a visitor who wants the gardens first thing and does not mind a bus ride to the station.

Where to stay near Kenrokuen and the castle

  • Kanazawa Hakuchoro Hotel Sanraku, splurge. A Taisho-period-themed hotel at Marunouchi 6-3, with a natural hot spring and rooms of 30 square metres (the Basic Twin) to 40 (the Executive Luxury). By its own page the castle park is three minutes on foot, Kenrokuen five and Omicho Market ten. Choose it for a bed five minutes from the garden and a hot-spring bath. Watch out: it is a taxi or bus ride from the station, and the nearest bus stop is five minutes’ walk away; the hotel runs a free shuttle from the station’s west exit, so check its times. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • KKR Hotel Kanazawa, budget. At Otemachi 2-32, next to the castle, with Kenrokuen, Omicho Market, Higashi Chaya and the 21st Century Museum all within 15 minutes’ walk by its own count. A large public bath opened on 15 August 2026. Choose it for a low price beside the castle park. Watch out: the hotel is run for a public-servants’ mutual-aid association, so the site separates member and general bookings, and it lists traffic restrictions and a shuttle bus for the Kanazawa Marathon on 25 October 2026. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Mitsui Garden Hotel Kanazawa, comfortable. A four-star hotel at Kamitsutsumicho 1-22, near Kenrokuen and Omicho Market, with a public bath on the 13th floor free to guests from 15:00 to 09:00. It is 17 minutes on foot from the station, or a bus to the Minamicho and Oyama Shrine stop and two minutes’ walk. Choose it for a bath with a view over the castle park. Watch out: the walk from the station is long with luggage, so use the bus or a taxi. Check rates on Booking.com →

Omicho: the market and the castle park on foot

Omicho Market is Kanazawa’s fish and produce market, and the streets around it are the most walkable base for a first visit: the castle park is three minutes from the Sanraku, Kenrokuen ten and Higashi Chaya 12, by that hotel’s own page. The Musashigatsuji bus stop beside the market serves both loop-bus routes and the airport limousine.

Where to stay in Omicho

  • The Hotel Sanraku Kanazawa, comfortable. At Owaricho 1-1-1, with 215 rooms, the superior rooms 32 to 35 square metres, and the market a one-minute walk away. Its restaurants use produce from Omicho. Choose it for a first trip on foot. Watch out: it is a different hotel from the Hakuchoro, though both carry the Sanraku name, and it changed its online booking system on 27 May 2026, so reservations made earlier follow the old steps. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Intergate Kanazawa, good value. A 166-room hotel at Takaokamachi 2-5, with a communal bath, a lounge and a gallery of Kanazawa crafts. The Minamicho and Oyama Shrine bus stop is at its door, reached from the station’s east exit. Choose it for a simple room and a bath at a moderate price. Watch out: it has no parking, and its communal bath supplies no towels, so take them from your room. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Sumiyoshiya Ryokan, budget. A small inn at Jukken-machi 54 that says it has run for 300 years, about 300 metres from the Omicho corner, with two shared baths, free bicycles and a washing machine and a dryer. Choose it for a cheap Japanese-style night near the market. Watch out: dinner service is suspended, and the inn closes on 27 to 29 October 2026 and 24 to 26 November 2026. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Kanazawa

  • Count the tax per person. The ¥200 and ¥500 bands apply to each guest’s share of the room before consumption tax, and nothing is charged under ¥5,000. A room that crosses ¥20,000 per person costs ¥300 a night more, per guest.
  • Check the date against events and closures. The marathon, a small inn’s closure days and planned hotel works all appear on the hotels’ own sites, so read the notices before you book.
  • Pick the arrival, then the evening. A station hotel makes the Shinkansen easy, but the old town is a bus ride away, and the loop bus stops running at about 18:00.
  • Compare totals. Hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book, and Where to stay in Japan puts Kanazawa beside Kyoto and Tokyo.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Kanazawa for a first trip?

Omicho, if you want to walk: from The Hotel Sanraku Kanazawa the market is a minute away, the castle park three and Kenrokuen ten, by the hotel's own page. Choose Kanazawa Station for the Shinkansen, Korinbo and Katamachi for dinners out, Higashi Chaya for the teahouse streets and the castle park edge for the garden first thing.

Does Kanazawa have an accommodation tax?

Yes. Kanazawa City charges ¥200 per person per night on a room charge of ¥5,000 up to ¥20,000, and ¥500 from ¥20,000, measured before consumption tax and without meals. Stays under ¥5,000 per person per night are exempt, and the tax applies to every guest whatever their age.

How much is the accommodation tax for two people in Kanazawa?

Our arithmetic: a couple paying ¥36,300 a night including the 10% consumption tax pay ¥33,000 before it, or ¥16,500 each, so the tax is ¥200 each, ¥400 a night and ¥1,200 over three nights. A solo traveller on a ¥22,000 room including consumption tax pays ¥500 a night.

How do you get from Komatsu Airport to Kanazawa?

Hokutetsu runs a limousine bus. The adult fare is ¥1,400 (¥700 for a child) to Kanazawa Station's west exit, Korinbo and the Musashigatsuji and Omicho Market stop, and the operator gives the journey as about 40 to 55 minutes. No reservation is needed: you pay when you get off or buy a ticket in advance.

How much is the Kanazawa Loop Bus?

An adult single costs ¥220 and a one-day pass ¥800, which is not sold on board, by the operator's page. The right-hand route runs about every 15 minutes from 08:30 to 18:00 and the left-hand route about every 20 minutes from 08:38 to 17:58, with stops for Higashi Chaya, Kenrokuen and the castle, Korinbo and Omicho Market.

How long does the Shinkansen take from Tokyo to Kanazawa?

JR West gives the Hokuriku Shinkansen journey from Kanazawa to Tokyo as 2 hours 28 minutes.
