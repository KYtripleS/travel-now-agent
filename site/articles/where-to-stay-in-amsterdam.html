Hotels around Canal Ring, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Canal Ring that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

Amsterdam’s hotel bill changed on 1 January 2026. VAT on a hotel room rose from 9% to 21%, and the city adds its tourist tax, the toeristenbelasting, at 12.5% of the room price before VAT. Together they add about a third to the base rate. The city is compact, so where you stay decides less about travel time than about what is outside the door at night: a canal, a market street, a museum lawn or the river.

This guide covers six areas, from the Canal Ring to Amsterdam-Noord, 12 hotels, the airport links and the tax. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

Amsterdam’s hotel taxes in 2026

Two charges apply to every hotel room in the city:

Charge Rate
VAT on hotel accommodation, from 1 January 2026 21% of the room price (it was 9%)
Amsterdam tourist tax (toeristenbelasting) 12.5% of the room price, excluding VAT

The tourist tax covers hotels, hostels, guesthouses, B&Bs, apartments, short-stay rentals and campsites. The city also charges a day tourist tax of €15 per passenger on cruise operators whose passengers visit for the day; it does not apply to hotel guests.

A worked example (our arithmetic): on a room priced at €200 a night before VAT, VAT at 21% adds €42 and the tourist tax at 12.5% adds €25, so the night costs €267. Three nights cost €801, of which €201 is tax. The rule is simple: a third on top of the base rate.

Getting in from Schiphol, and around

  • By train, the station is directly under the terminal, and trains run eight times an hour to Amsterdam Centraal, in about 17 minutes, by Schiphol’s count. The NS journey planner gives €5.50 for a single in second class; a single-use chip card bought at a machine adds a €1.60 surcharge. You can also tap a contactless bank card or phone on the card readers, in and out.
  • By bus, the Amsterdam Airport Express, line 397, leaves every 10 minutes from stop B17, outside Schiphol Plaza, for the Museumplein, the Rijksmuseum and Leidseplein; at night the Niteliner N97 runs once an hour. A single costs from €6.50 and a return from €11.75, online, from the driver by card (not cash), or by tapping in with a contactless card. Schiphol’s bus station is being renovated, and some buses are temporarily leaving from other stops.
  • The Amsterdam Travel Ticket covers travel to and from Schiphol and throughout Amsterdam: €20 for one day, €27 for two and €34 for three in 2026.
  • In town, GVB runs the trams, buses, metro and ferries. A GVB 1-hour ticket is €3.40. Pay by tapping a Maestro, V PAY, Mastercard or Visa card, or your phone, in and out with the same card, and GVB caps a day of its own trams, buses and metro at €10.50 (night buses excluded). You cannot buy a ticket on buses or on trams without a conductor.
  • Across the IJ, most GVB ferries are free for people on foot and on bikes.

The Canal Ring: the first-trip base

The Grachtengordel, the ring of the Herengracht, Keizersgracht and Prinsengracht, was built at the end of the 16th and the beginning of the 17th century as a new port city, on drained swampland, in concentric arcs to the west and south of the medieval town. UNESCO inscribed it as a World Heritage Site in 2010. Both hotels below occupy rows of joined canal houses, which is why their rooms vary so much in shape and size. It is the base to choose if you want to walk to most of your trip.

  • Pulitzer Amsterdam, splurge. A five-star in 25 restored canal houses from the 17th and 18th centuries on the Prinsengracht, with inner gardens, a restaurant, Jansz., and Pulitzer’s Bar. Choose it for a canal-house hotel at its most complete. Watch out: only the rooms sold as Canal View rooms face the water, and the Cosy Rooms, the smallest, suit one person. The Jordaan: village streets and markets

West of the Prinsengracht, the Jordaan grew up beside the grand canals as a working district of small houses and workshops. The narrow canals and lanes remain, now lined with cafés, galleries and small shops, and the Lindengracht has held a Saturday market for more than a century. The hotels here are small, and so are the rooms.

Where to stay in the Jordaan

  • Mr. Jordaan, good value. A small hotel on the Bloemgracht, a 17th-century canal just off the Prinsengracht, with single rooms of 11 m², doubles from 13 m², twins of 15 m² and a triple. Choose it for a canal in the Jordaan at a modest price. Watch out: the Standard and Small Doubles look over the courtyard or the back of the hotel; book a Canal View double if you want the water. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Linden Hotel, good value. A small hotel in a flatiron building on the Lindengracht, where the Saturday market sets up on the doorstep, with doubles and twins of 11 to 14 m², a triangle-shaped Superior Double, a triple and a family room. Choose it for the market and the village feel. Watch out: it cannot take extra beds, groups of more than eight or bookings of more than three rooms. Check rates on Booking.com →

The Museum Quarter: the museums and the Vondelpark

South of the canals, in Oud-Zuid, the Museumplein holds the Rijksmuseum, the Van Gogh Museum, the Stedelijk and the Concertgebouw, with the Vondelpark a few minutes away. It is quieter at night than the centre, and the airport bus stops here.

Where to stay in the Museum Quarter

  • Mandarin Oriental Conservatorium, splurge. A neo-Gothic building of the late 19th century, built as the national savings bank and later a music conservatory, redesigned by Piero Lissoni, with a spa, the restaurant Taiko and an Ottolenghi restaurant. By the hotel’s count, the Stedelijk is a minute away on foot, the Van Gogh Museum two and the Rijksmuseum six. Choose it for the museums at the door in a landmark building. Watch out: it is the most expensive kind of stay in this guide, and the 21% VAT and the 12.5% tax add a third to every night. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Fita, good value. An intimate hotel of 21 rooms on the Jan Luijkenstraat, a two-minute walk from the museums, with twins of 20 m² and Superior Doubles of 27 m²; the 397 airport bus stops nearby. Choose it for space near the museums at a modest price. Watch out: breakfast is €17.50 a person unless you book the bed-and-breakfast rate, and only some rooms have air conditioning, the rest a fan. Check rates on Booking.com →

De Pijp: the market street

South of the Singelgracht, De Pijp was built as dense housing for working families, and many of its streets carry the names of Dutch painters. Its centre is the Albert Cuyp Market, and the streets around it are dense with restaurants, cafés and bars. It is a neighbourhood people live in rather than a sight, and a short walk from the Museum Quarter.

Where to stay in De Pijp

  • Hotel Okura Amsterdam, splurge. A Japanese-owned hotel in a 23-storey building on the outskirts of De Pijp, with four restaurants holding three Michelin stars between them, a spa, and Panorama rooms over the city. Choose it for the food and the view. Watch out: it sits on the outskirts of the neighbourhood, so the canals are a tram or metro ride away rather than a stroll. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Sir Albert, comfortable. A former diamond factory on the Albert Cuypstraat, a few steps from the market, which keeps its high ceilings and large windows, with the IZAKAYA restaurant and bar. Choose it for design and De Pijp at the door. Watch out: the gym is not in the hotel but down the street, at Albert Cuypstraat 44. Check rates on Booking.com →

Centrum, near Centraal: the train at the door

The old centre runs south from Centraal to Dam Square and the Royal Palace. It is the busiest part of the city, but nothing beats it for an early train or a late arrival: Schiphol is one direct train away, and the ferries to Noord leave from behind the station.

Where to stay near Centraal

  • NH Collection Amsterdam Barbizon Palace, comfortable. 268 rooms across a row of 17th-century houses on the Prins Hendrikkade, opposite Centraal, with the Restaurant Vermeer, a spa and gym, and St Olof’s Chapel, from the 15th century, as its events hall. By the hotel’s account, the Royal Palace is five minutes on foot. Choose it for the station across the road. Watch out: the hotel notes that all rooms include stairs, and the room you get may differ from the photos. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Sint Nicolaas, good value. A family-owned hotel in a historic building on the Spuistraat, a short walk from Centraal, with a lift, a 24-hour reception, doubles of 21 m², twins of 19 m², and rooms for three and four; booked on its own site, the hotel lists breakfast and all-day drinks and snacks among the extras. Choose it for the centre at a fair price. Watch out: because of the building’s age it has no facilities for disabled guests, and check-in is online, with rooms from 14:00. Check rates on Booking.com →

Amsterdam-Noord: the river and the ferry

Across the IJ from Centraal, Noord was long the city’s working shore: the NDSM wharf was a shipyard, and the building that houses ClinkNOORD was a laboratory. Those buildings now hold studios, bars and hostels, and the free ferry makes the crossing a few minutes. Rooms here look back at the old city across the water.

Where to stay in Amsterdam-Noord

  • Sir Adam, comfortable. In the 22-storey A’DAM Tower, a free three-minute ferry ride from Centraal, by the hotel’s count, with a rooftop looking over the IJ and the city. Choose it for the view and a music-minded hotel. Watch out: every trip into the centre begins with the ferry. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • ClinkNOORD, budget. A large hostel in a 1920s Art Deco laboratory on the IJ, with private en-suite rooms for one to six, mixed dorms, female-only dorms, a bar and a café open 24 hours. Choose it for the lowest price in this guide. Watch out: dorms sleep up to 14, and check-out is by 10am. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Amsterdam

  • Count the tax. Add 21% VAT and the 12.5% tourist tax to any rate quoted before tax: a €200 room costs €267. Check whether a quoted price already includes the tourist tax before you compare.
  • Watch the season. Prices follow demand; compare two or three sets of dates, and weeknights against weekends, before you settle.
  • Check the room size. Canal-house hotels are small by design: the Linden Hotel’s doubles start at 11 m² and Mr. Jordaan’s singles are 11 m². If you need space, Hotel Fita’s Superior Doubles are 27 m².
  • Book the view by name. At the Pulitzer and Mr. Jordaan, only rooms sold with a canal view are sure to face the water.
  • Compare totals. Hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book, and whether a rail pass pays in Europe helps if Amsterdam is one stop of several.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the best area to stay in Amsterdam for a first trip?

    The Canal Ring, the 17th-century ring of the Herengracht, Keizersgracht and Prinsengracht, where most sights are on foot. Choose the Jordaan for village streets and markets, the Museum Quarter for the Rijksmuseum and the Van Gogh Museum, De Pijp for the Albert Cuyp Market, Centraal for the train, and Amsterdam-Noord for a river view for less.

    How much is the tourist tax in Amsterdam in 2026?

    12.5% of the room price, excluding VAT. On top of that, VAT on hotel rooms rose from 9% to 21% on 1 January 2026. On a room priced at €200 a night before VAT, VAT adds €42 and the tourist tax €25, so the night costs €267.

    How do you get from Schiphol to Amsterdam Centraal?

    By train from the station under the terminal: trains run eight times an hour and take about 17 minutes. The NS journey planner gives €5.50 for a single in second class; a single-use chip card from a machine adds €1.60. You can also tap a contactless bank card or phone in and out.

    How much is the 397 bus from Schiphol?

    The Amsterdam Airport Express, line 397, costs from €6.50 single and from €11.75 return. It leaves every 10 minutes from stop B17 outside Schiphol Plaza for the Museumplein, the Rijksmuseum and Leidseplein, and you can pay the driver by card or tap in with a contactless card; cash is not accepted.

    Can I use a contactless bank card on Amsterdam's trams and metro?

    Yes. Check in and out with the same Maestro, V PAY, Mastercard or Visa card, or your phone. GVB caps a day of travel on its own trams, buses and metro at €10.50, excluding night buses; a GVB 1-hour ticket is €3.40.

    What does the Amsterdam Travel Ticket cover?

    Travel to and from Schiphol and throughout Amsterdam, for €20 for one day, €27 for two and €34 for three in 2026.
