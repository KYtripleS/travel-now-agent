Hotels around Kata Beach, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Kata Beach that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

Phuket hotels often quote a price with “++” after it, and the plus signs are money: a 10% service charge, then 7% VAT on top of that, about 17.7% in all. On a room quoted at 3,000 baht that is 531 baht a night before you reach the lobby. There is no tourist entry fee to add yet: the plan for 300 baht on air arrivals has been replaced by a 450-baht proposal that is not in force. Where you sleep decides the rest, because Phuket’s beaches are strung along the west coast and the Old Town sits inland.

This guide covers six areas, from Kata to Nai Harn, 12 hotels, the airport buses and the bill. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

Phuket’s hotel bill: the “++”

A price quoted with “++” leaves out two charges, and Thai hotels add them in this order:

Charge Rate Applied to
Service charge, the hotel’s own usually 10% The room rate
VAT, the reduced rate, local tax included 7% until 30 September 2027 The room rate plus the service charge
Together about 17.7% The quoted room rate
Tourist entry fee Not in force Nothing yet

The Revenue Department announced on 27 July 2026 that the cabinet had approved extending the 7% VAT rate, which includes the local tax, from 1 October 2026 to 30 September 2027. Its VAT guide defines the tax base as the total value received for a service, fees included, which is why the VAT is worked out on the room rate plus the service charge. The service charge itself is the hotel’s own, so check the rate on your booking. Amari Phuket’s own site, for example, quotes rooms as “THB 4,160+++”, with three plus signs, so ask what each one covers.

Here is the arithmetic for a room quoted at “3,000++” (our arithmetic, not a hotel’s quote):

Line Baht
Room rate quoted as 3,000++ 3,000
Service charge, 10% 300
Subtotal 3,300
VAT, 7% of 3,300 231
Total for one night 3,531

Three nights come to 10,593 baht, not 9,000.

On the entry fee: Thailand’s plan for 300 baht on air arrivals and 150 baht by land and sea was replaced by a proposal, set out in a government notice of 17 August 2026, for a flat 450 baht a person for foreign visitors however they arrive. The Ministry of Tourism and Sports was then preparing a public hearing. On the ministry’s timetable, air arrivals would pay from 180 days after the announcement appears in the Royal Gazette, and land and sea arrivals about 360 days after that. We found no Gazette announcement by 19 October 2026, so no entry fee applies to an October 2026 trip. The only airport charge is the international departure charge, 1,120 baht since 20 June 2026, and it is already inside your airfare.

Getting in from the airport, and around

  • By Phuket Smart Bus, the airport line costs a flat 100 baht. Airports of Thailand lists its stops as Bann Khian, Cherngtalay, Surin Beach, Kamala, Patong, Karon, Kata and Rawai, and its airport stop is at Entrance 3 of the domestic terminal, on the third floor, listed as open until 11:30 pm. You can pay with a contactless Visa, Mastercard or JCB card, by QR code or in cash; children under six ride free with a paying adult. The operator posts its timetable as images, so check it before you fly.
  • To Phuket Town, the orange 8411 airport bus leaves from the public transport parking area: take Exit 3 and turn left. Fares are calculated on the distance, tickets are sold on the bus or online, and the service runs every day from 8:00 am to 9:00 pm, by the airport’s listing.
  • By taxi or limousine, Airports of Thailand lists the Phuket Airport Limousine, with counters at Entrance 1 in arrivals. Its page gives no fares, so ask for the price before you get in.
  • Around the island, the Smart Bus also runs from Bus Terminal 2 to Patong for a flat 50 baht, and a free Dragon Line shuttle circles Central Festival, the Old Town and the OTOP Market.

Kata and Kata Noi: the first-trip bay

Airports of Thailand lists the Smart Bus stops in order from the airport: Patong, then Karon, then Kata, then Rawai. That puts Kata between the biggest resort town and the south, on the line that costs 100 baht flat, which is why it suits a first trip. Kata Rocks overlooks Kata Beach. Kata Noi is a separate beach, and Katathani Phuket Beach Resort calls its stretch of sand a public beach.

Where to stay in Kata

  • Kata Rocks, splurge. All 34 villas have a private pool and sea views, and the one-bedroom Sky Pool Villa is 175 sq m with a seven-metre infinity pool. The resort overlooks Kata Beach and the Andaman Sea, and the villas face west. Choose it for a private pool and the sunset. Watch out: it is villas only, so there is no standard room, and the price follows. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Katathani Phuket Beach Resort, comfortable. On Kata Noi Beach, with an 850-metre stretch of sand and three wings: Thani, Bhuri and Vhari, the last with a new water park. The hotel says it is less than an hour from Phuket International Airport. Choose it for a full beach resort. Watch out: the wings are planned for different travellers, so choose the one that matches yours. Check rates on Booking.com →

Patong: nightlife and the widest choice

Patong is the lively end of the island. Amari Phuket’s site puts Bangla Road’s nightlife close by and Jungceylon beside it, and Lub d Phuket Patong lists Patong Beach 150 metres away, Bangla Walking Street 500 metres away and Phuket Old Town 15 kilometres away. The 50-baht Smart Bus from Bus Terminal 2 runs to Patong, and the airport line stops here too. It is the busy end, so choose it for the choice and not the quiet. Our Phuket first-timer’s guide compares the beaches in more detail.

Where to stay in Patong

  • Amari Phuket, comfortable. On Meun Ngern Road, with 380 rooms and suites on what the hotel calls a peaceful stretch of Patong Beach, and an Ocean Suites Wing. Choose it for a beachfront base with Bangla Road close by. Watch out: it is a large resort, and its offers quote rates with “+++”, so ask what the plus signs cover. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Lub d Phuket Patong, budget. A “social hotel” on Sawatdirak Road, with dormitories, including ladies-only ones, a co-working space and a restaurant and bar. Choose it to save money near the beach. Watch out: it runs social events, such as a night run in November, so it is not a quiet base. Check rates on Booking.com →

Kamala: above the beach

The Smart Bus lists Kamala between Surin Beach and Patong. Both hotels below sit above the sand rather than on it: Cape Sienna on a headland, Keemala in the rainforest. Kamala is a good choice if you want a view and a hotel pool more than a walk to the beach.

Where to stay in Kamala

  • Cape Sienna Phuket Gourmet Hotel & Villas, comfortable. On Nakalay Road, with 146 rooms and suites and a separate villa collection, all with sea views from private terraces; the Sea View Studio is 40 sq m. The hotel describes itself as on a west-coast headland. Choose it for a terrace over Kamala Bay. Watch out: the offers on its own site include non-refundable rates. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Keemala, splurge. A hilltop resort of 38 pool villas: 16 Clay Pool Cottages, 7 Tent Pool Villas, 7 Tree Pool Houses and 8 Bird’s Nest Pool Villas. Choose it for a villa in the trees. Watch out: it has no private beach; a free shuttle runs to Kamala Beach from 11:00 am to 4:00 pm. Check rates on Booking.com →

Bang Tao and Laguna: resorts and a lagoon

The Smart Bus lists Cherngtalay and Surin Beach on the way to Kamala, and this stretch holds Laguna Phuket, a resort estate whose hotels include Angsana, Banyan Tree, Cassia, Dusit Thani and SAii. It suits a stay in which the hotel is the destination.

Where to stay in Bang Tao and Laguna

  • Banyan Tree Phuket, splurge. All 217 villas have their own pool, set around a lagoon on an 89-acre estate in Cherngtalay. The hotel puts it about 16 kilometres and 25 to 30 minutes from the airport, with a dedicated exit to Bang Tao Beach. Choose it for a private pool and a beach club on the sand. Watch out: it is villas only, so there is no standard room. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Cassia Phuket, comfortable. One-bedroom suites and lofts of 40 to 52 sq m with a balcony, a full kitchen and a separate living area, and a shuttle boat across the lagoon to a private area of Bang Tao Beach. Choose it for self-catering space and for travelling with pets, since it calls itself the first pet-friendly hotel in Phuket. Watch out: the beach is across the lagoon by shuttle boat, not at the door. Check rates on Booking.com →

Phuket Old Town: shophouses, museums and food

Phuket Old Town is inland, with no beach: Lub d’s own page puts it 15 kilometres from Patong. In its place are Thalang Road, Soi Romanee, the Thai Hua Museum and the Sunday walking street. The 8411 airport bus runs to Phuket Town, and the free Dragon Line shuttle links the Old Town with Central Festival. Our things to do in Phuket covers what to see.

Where to stay in the Old Town

  • The Memory at On On Hotel, good value. On Phang-Nga Road, in a Sino-Portuguese building the hotel says opened in 1927 as the first hotel in Phuket Town. Rooms run from Superior to the Memory Suite, with free Wi-Fi, a complimentary minibar and rain showers. Choose it for the history. Watch out: the hotel’s site lists no room sizes, so ask before you book. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Sino House Phuket, good value. A boutique hotel at 1 Montree Road with 57 rooms, free parking and a spa, a few minutes’ walk from the Old Town by the hotel’s own account; the Superior Room is 31 sq m. Choose it if you are driving. Watch out: the hotel describes itself as near the Old Town, not in it. Check rates on Booking.com →

Nai Harn and Rawai: the far south

Rawai is the last stop on the Smart Bus line, and Nai Harn is not named in the airport’s list of stops, so ask your hotel how to cover the last stretch. Both hotels here are by Nai Harn Bay, which suits a stay that is mostly the beach and the hotel.

Where to stay in Nai Harn and Rawai

  • The Nai Harn, splurge. On Vises Road, with balconies over Nai Harn Bay; the Deluxe Ocean View Room is 47 sq m with breakfast included. The hotel won a MICHELIN Key in 2026, by its own site. Choose it for the bay. Watch out: the guest lift runs only to the sixth floor, and floors seven to nine are a short walk up, so ask for a lower room if stairs are a problem. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Sunsuri Phuket, comfortable. A hillside resort 500 metres from Nai Harn Beach, with rooms and pool villas. Choose it for a pool and a view a short way from the sand. Watch out: the hotel’s site warns of an external construction project nearby, expected to continue through 2027. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Phuket

  • Count the “++”. Add 17.7% to every quoted room rate: 3,000 baht becomes 3,531.
  • Watch the season. The wet season lasts about six months of the year, by the Tourism Authority’s account, and in an advisory of 17 May 2026 the Thai Meteorological Department forecast waves of 2 to 3 metres in the upper Andaman Sea, higher in thundershowers, and told small boats to stay ashore. Look at the flags on the beach: red means keep out of the water.
  • Check what the rate includes. The Nai Harn’s Deluxe Ocean View Room includes breakfast, and Cape Sienna’s offers page lists non-refundable rates. Compare like with like.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Phuket for a first trip?

Kata and Kata Noi. The Phuket Smart Bus airport line lists Patong, then Karon, then Kata, then Rawai, so Kata sits between the biggest resort town and the south, on a line that costs a flat 100 baht. Choose Patong for nightlife and shops, Kamala or Bang Tao for resorts, and the Old Town for streets, museums and food.

What does “++” mean on a Phuket hotel price?

It means two charges are still to be added: usually a 10% service charge, then 7% VAT on the room rate plus the service charge, about 17.7% in all. A room quoted at 3,000++ costs 3,531 baht a night: 3,000, plus 300 for service, plus 231 VAT. The service charge is the hotel's own, so check the rate on your booking.

Is there a tourist entry fee for Thailand in 2026?

Not yet. The plan for 300 baht on air arrivals was replaced by a proposal, in a government notice of 17 August 2026, for a flat 450 baht a person for foreign visitors. On the ministry's timetable air arrivals would pay from 180 days after a Royal Gazette announcement, and we found none by 19 October 2026.

How do you get from Phuket airport to the beaches?

The Phuket Smart Bus airport line costs a flat 100 baht and stops at Bann Khian, Cherngtalay, Surin Beach, Kamala, Patong, Karon, Kata and Rawai, by Airports of Thailand's list. Pay by contactless card, QR code or cash. Taxis and limousines are also available, and the Airport Limousine counters are at Entrance 1 in arrivals.

How do you get from Phuket airport to Phuket Old Town?

Take the orange 8411 airport bus to Phuket Town from the public transport parking area: leave by Exit 3 and turn left. It runs every day from 8:00 am to 9:00 pm, fares are calculated on the distance, and tickets are sold on the bus or online.

Is Phuket safe to swim in during the wet season?

Not always. The wet season lasts about six months of the year, and in an advisory of 17 May 2026 the Thai Meteorological Department forecast waves of 2 to 3 metres in the upper Andaman Sea and told small boats to stay ashore. A red flag with a no-swimming symbol means keep out of the water.
