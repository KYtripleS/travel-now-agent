Tokyo’s autumn comes late in the year and mostly in gold. By the Japan Meteorological Agency’s normals for 1991 to 2020, Tokyo’s sample ginkgo turns yellow on 23 November and its sample maple turns red on 28 November; in 2025 they turned on 22 and 23 November, and in 2024 both waited until 3 December. For 2026, the Japan Weather Association expects the Jingu Gaien ginkgo avenue at its best from late November into early December, and Rikugien’s maples from early to mid-December. Rikugien’s evening opening, with the garden lit, runs from 25 November to 6 December.

The city is large, so where you stay decides which of these you can reach before the crowds. This guide covers the dates, the 2026 openings, hours and fees, four areas matched to the parks and avenues near each, eleven hotels, and Tokyo’s hotel tax. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating at the start of October 2026. When to go: the dates to aim for

By the Japan Meteorological Agency’s count, for Tokyo’s sample trees:

Ginkgo turns yellow Maple turns red
Normal (1991–2020) 23 November 28 November
2025 22 November 23 November
2024 3 December 3 December

The agency’s date is the day its sample tree has turned almost entirely, and the gardens and avenues colour over several weeks around it. For 2026, the Japan Weather Association’s first forecast, on 1 October, expects a warm autumn to hold the colour back, and gives these against each spot’s usual best:

Spot 2026 forecast Usual best
Jingu Gaien ginkgo avenue Late November to early December Mid-November to early December
Rikugien Early to mid-December Late November to mid-December
Koishikawa Korakuen Early December Late November to early December

Aim for the last week of November and the first week of December, and stay four or five nights rather than two, so that an early or a late year still overlaps with your stay.

Shinjuku: Shinjuku Gyoen by the station

Shinjuku is built around one of the busiest stations in the world, and in autumn it has a garden at the door: the Shinjuku Gate of Shinjuku Gyoen is a ten-minute walk from the South Exit. The Jingu Gaien ginkgo avenue is a short ride away, and every other spot in this guide is reachable without a change of hotel. The trade-off is that Shinjuku never really lets up.

Shibuya and Aoyama: the ginkgo avenue

The Jingu Gaien ginkgo avenue runs for about 300 metres, four rows of trees deep, towards the Meiji Memorial Picture Gallery, and at its best it is one of the most crowded places in the city. Gaienmae Station, on the Ginza line, is two stops from Shibuya, so a Shibuya base lets you be there at first light and back for breakfast. Omotesando and Aoyama, on the way, are good for an afternoon on foot.

Ueno: gardens lit at night

Ueno pairs Ueno Park, whose trees are usually at their best from mid-November to early December, with a major station: Komagome, the station for Rikugien, a daimyo garden of the Edo period, is five stops round the Yamanote line, which makes Ueno the practical base for its evening opening. Yanaka, a short walk up the hill, is one of the few Tokyo neighbourhoods that came through the 1923 earthquake and the 1945 air raids largely intact.

Where to stay in Ueno and Yanaka

  • NOHGA HOTEL UENO TOKYO, comfortable. 130 rooms three minutes from Ueno Station, filled with work by local makers, with a bistro downstairs. Choose it for Ueno Park and an easy ride to Rikugien. Watch out: the western spots, Shinjuku Gyoen and the ginkgo avenue, are a longer ride. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Ryokan Sawanoya, budget, ryokan. A small inn in Yanaka, family-run for more than seventy years: futons, tatami, and the old quarter outside the door. Choose it for the simplest honest ryokan night in the city. Watch out: it is not on Booking.com, so book with the inn, and check the room type and bath arrangements.

Marunouchi: ginkgo by the station

The Marunouchi side of Tokyo Station turns gold in late autumn: ginkgo line the plaza in front of the red-brick station and Gyoko-dori, the avenue that runs from it towards the Imperial Palace. Hibiya Park is a walk south, and the Shinkansen leaves from downstairs, which makes this the base for a trip that goes on to Kyoto; see where to stay in Kyoto for the autumn leaves.

Where to stay in Marunouchi

  • The Tokyo Station Hotel, splurge, heritage. 150 rooms inside the red-brick station building itself, restored and reopened in 2012. Choose it to wake up above the ginkgo on the station plaza. Watch out: price. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • The Peninsula Tokyo, splurge. Opened in 2007 opposite Hibiya Park and the Imperial Palace, with 302 rooms and a direct underground link to Hibiya Station. Choose it for Hibiya Park across the road. Watch out: price. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Ryumeikan Tokyo, comfortable. Three minutes from the Yaesu North Exit, descended from a ryokan founded more than 120 years ago. Choose it for a gentler, Japanese-feeling base at the Shinkansen. Watch out: it is on the Yaesu side, across the station from the ginkgo plaza. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book for a season that moves

  • Book a refundable rate now for the nights you want, then adjust when the forecasts firm up in November. A booking you can cancel costs nothing if the leaves come early or late; check each rate’s cancellation deadline before you pay.
  • Stay longer, not later. Four or five nights across the end of November and the start of December give you better odds than two perfectly timed ones.
  • Buy Rikugien tickets in advance. They are ¥200 cheaper online, and the number sold each day is limited.
  • Mind the tax. Until 31 March 2027 Tokyo’s accommodation tax is ¥100 a person a night on rooms of ¥10,000 to ¥14,999 per person and ¥200 from ¥15,000; rooms under ¥10,000 are exempt. See Japan’s tourist taxes in 2026.

Frequently asked questions

When are the autumn leaves best in Tokyo in 2026?

Aim for the last week of November and the first week of December. The Japan Weather Association's first 2026 forecast expects the Jingu Gaien ginkgo avenue at its best from late November to early December and Rikugien from early to mid-December. By the Japan Meteorological Agency's normals, Tokyo's sample ginkgo turns yellow on 23 November and its maple red on 28 November.

Where should I stay in Tokyo for the autumn leaves?

In Shinjuku, where Shinjuku Gyoen is ten minutes from the South Exit and the Jingu Gaien ginkgo avenue is a short ride. Choose Shibuya for the ginkgo avenue, two stops away on the Ginza line, Ueno for Rikugien's evening opening and Ueno Park, and Marunouchi for the ginkgo outside Tokyo Station and the Shinkansen.

When is the Rikugien autumn light-up in 2026?

Rikugien's evening opening runs from 25 November to 6 December 2026, from 6pm to 8.30pm with last entry at 7.30pm. Tickets are ¥1,000 in advance online and ¥1,200 on the day, and the number sold each day is limited.

Is Shinjuku Gyoen open in late November?

Yes, from 9am to 4.30pm with last entry at 4pm, for ¥500. It closes on Mondays, or the next weekday when a Monday is a public holiday, except from 1 to 15 November, when it opens every day.

Is the Jingu Gaien ginkgo avenue affected by the redevelopment?

The district around it is being redeveloped. Tokyo's government has asked the developers in writing to take every care to preserve the four rows of ginkgo, and the avenue itself is open and free to walk. The Gaien had not announced a 2026 ginkgo festival at the start of October.

How much is Tokyo's hotel tax?

Until 31 March 2027 it is ¥100 a person a night on rooms of ¥10,000 to ¥14,999 per person and ¥200 from ¥15,000; rooms under ¥10,000 are exempt.
