Venice is a city of islands, canals, and centuries of history. For travelers, this means there is no single "center." Where you sleep determines which island you wake up on, how many vaporetto rides you take to reach the next wonder, and whether you hear church bells or local bacari at dawn. Choosing the right sestiere is the single most important logistical decision of your trip.

First, choose the sestiere

Venice is divided into six sestieri (neighborhoods), each with its own character. To make your sightseeing stress-free, prioritize hotels near these key zones:

  1. San Marco: The absolute core for St. Mark's Square, the Doge's Palace, and major sights.
  2. Cannaregio: The northern sestiere, known for the Jewish Ghetto, quieter canals, and authentic local life.
  3. Dorsoduro: The southern sestiere, home to art universities, museums, and lively squares.
  4. Castello: The largest and easternmost sestiere, with the Arsenale and green spaces.
  5. Santa Croce: The western gateway, where you arrive by car or bus at Piazzale Roma.
  6. San Polo: The small central sestiere, housing the Rialto Market and traditional craft workshops.

Being within a five-minute walk of a major vaporetto stop (lines 1, 2, or 5.1) will save you hours over a few-day trip.

San Marco: the heart of Venice

San Marco is where you find St. Mark's Basilica, the Doge's Palace, the Campanile, and the Rialto Bridge (a short walk away). It is one of Venice's busiest sestieri, filled with luxury hotels, souvenir shops, and cafes that have served visitors for centuries.

Cannaregio: local and authentic

Cannaregio is where you find the Jewish Ghetto, the Strada Nuova, and the Fondamente Nuova. It is one of Venice's most authentic sestieri, filled with local bacari, ateliers, and residential streets that feel lived-in.
