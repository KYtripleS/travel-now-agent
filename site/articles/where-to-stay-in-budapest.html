Hotels around Belváros-Lipótváros, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Belváros-Lipótváros that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

Budapest has no single hotel tax. Each of the city’s districts sets its own tourist tax, the idegenforgalmi adó, and in the districts where most visitors sleep it is 4% of the room price per guest night, collected by the hotel on top of the room. Under-18s are exempt. The airport is the other surprise: the express bus into the centre, the 100E, needs its own ticket, 2,500 forints, which is not valid on the ordinary airport bus, the 200E.

This guide covers six areas, from the Pest riverfront to Buda’s Castle Hill and Margaret Island, 12 hotels, the airport links and the tax. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

Budapest’s tourist tax in 2026

Hungarian law lets each municipality set the tax, with a ceiling of 4% of the room price where it uses a percentage. In Budapest the districts, not the city, set it. We read the published rules for the five districts in this guide:

District Area in this guide Rate
I Castle Hill and Víziváros 4%
V Belváros-Lipótváros 4%
VI Terézváros 4%
VII The Jewish Quarter 4%
VIII Józsefváros 4%

The base is the price of each guest night. District V’s and VII’s decrees define it as the room price less VAT, without compulsory breakfast and other services, and a district notice for hotel operators in VI says the same, while the district websites for I and V describe it as the gross room price. The difference is the VAT, so ask the hotel which it uses. Under-18s are exempt under the national local taxes act, which lists a few other exempt groups too, such as inpatients and people staying for study or for work at a business based in the district. The hotel collects the tax with the room rate. Margaret Island is not in one of these districts and we found no published rate for it; the Grand Margaret Island hotel says on its own FAQ that its published prices already include VAT and the local city tax.

Our arithmetic, not an official figure: two adults share one room at 60,000 forints a night, before VAT and without breakfast, for four nights. The base is 240,000 forints and the tax at 4% is 9,600 forints, or 2,400 a night, on top of the room.

Getting in from the airport, and around

  • By the 100E Airport Express. The 100E runs between Budapest Ferenc Liszt Airport and Deák Ferenc tér, with drop-offs at Kálvin tér, Astoria and Deák Ferenc tér. It runs around the clock: every 5 to 10 minutes by day, every 10 to 15 minutes in the morning and evening, and every 20 to 40 minutes on average at night, according to the airport. Since 1 April 2026 BKK, the city’s transport authority, has run it every 5 to 6 minutes in the morning and afternoon on the airport’s busiest days, Monday, Friday and Sunday, and every 6 minutes on Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday and Saturday.
  • The fare. It needs the special airport shuttle bus single ticket, 2,500 forints without a pass, up from 2,200 on 1 October 2025. Holders of a Budapest pass, a Pest County Pass or a Hungary Pass, other than a 15-day Budapest pass, can buy a 1,000-forint add-on instead. You can pay with a bank card on the bus, with Pay&GO, or in the BudapestGO app.
  • By bus 200E and metro M3. The 200E runs day and night from Terminal 2 to Kőbánya-Kispest, where you change to metro line M3. The 100E ticket is not accepted on it. BKK’s single ticket, 500 forints, covers one ride on one vehicle and allows changes only between metro lines, so by our reading a bus-plus-metro trip needs two singles, 1,000 forints, or one 90-minute ticket, 850 forints, sold on a phone only.
  • By taxi. The airport’s contracted operator is Főtaxi, booked at the stands at Terminals 2A and 2B. Hungarian regulation sets the airport fare at a base of 2,100 forints plus 520 forints a kilometre and 130 forints a minute, and Főtaxi prepares a fare calculation from the destination address before the trip starts.
  • In town, a single ticket costs 500 forints, or 700 if bought on the spot; a 24-hour travelcard is 2,750 forints and a 72-hour card is 5,750.

Belváros-Lipótváros: central Pest on foot

District V is the central part of Pest, along the river and around Deák Ferenc tér, which BKK calls one of the city’s major transport hubs and where the 100E ends. It is the first-trip choice because the airport bus drops you here and most of the headline sights are within walking distance.

The Jewish Quarter: Dohány Street and the Grand Boulevard

District VII is the Jewish Quarter, around the Dohány Street Synagogue and the restaurants and bars of the surrounding streets. It stays busy late and runs out to Blaha Lujza tér on the Grand Boulevard.

Where to stay in the Jewish Quarter

  • Anantara New York Palace Budapest, splurge. A Belle Époque building on Erzsébet körút with 185 suites and guestrooms of 30 to 50 sq m, a spa with an indoor pool, and the New York Café inside. Choose it for a grand room a short walk from the theatres and the Opera. Watch out: price; it is a five-star hotel. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Continental Hotel Budapest, comfortable. A four-star superior hotel on Dohány Street, close to the synagogue and Blaha Lujza tér, with 270 rooms with Art Deco features, rooftop wellness and indoor and seasonal outdoor pools. Choose it for pools in the middle of the quarter. Watch out: the Standard rooms measure 18 to 20 sq m, so book the category above if you want space. Check rates on Booking.com →

Terézváros: Andrássy Avenue and Király utca

District VI, Terézváros, holds Andrássy Avenue, a World Heritage street with the State Opera House, and Király utca, a long street of nightlife that starts near Deák Ferenc tér.

Where to stay in Terézváros

  • Hotel Moments Budapest, good value. A four-star hotel at Andrássy út 8 with 99 rooms in three categories, a few steps from the Opera and the Basilica, free coffee, tea and mineral water in the lounge bar around the clock, and a sauna and fitness room. Choose it for Andrássy Avenue at a fair price. Watch out: the Superior rooms measure 21 to 26 sq m and face the street. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Wombat’s City Hostel Budapest, budget. In a building from 1840 on Király utca, a former four-star hotel turned hostel, where each room has its own bathroom and air conditioning, with a bar and a café and free in-house events. Choose it for a low-cost base near Deák Ferenc tér. Watch out: the hostel bills Király utca as “the alley of party”, so expect noise; the minimum age depends on the season.

Castle Hill and Víziváros: the Buda side

District I is Buda’s riverside and its hill: Víziváros, the strip under the castle walls, and Castle Hill above it with the Fisherman’s Bastion. It is quieter at night than Pest, and the river views are the reason to come. You cross the Danube to reach most of Pest.

Where to stay on Castle Hill and in Víziváros

  • Carlton Hotel Buda Castle, comfortable. A four-star hotel on a quiet historic street at the foot of Castle Hill, a few steps from the Chain Bridge, with breakfast from 7 to 11 and lift access. Choose it for a calm base beside the bridge. Watch out: the Economy rooms are 14 sq m for a single and 17 sq m for a double. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Boutique Hotel Victoria, good value. A four-star boutique hotel at Bem rakpart 11, directly on the Buda riverside, with 27 Superior rooms of 22 to 28 sq m facing the Danube. Choose it for the river view. Watch out: the cheaper Budget rooms, 18 sq m, are in a separate building, reached by stairs only, and have no Danube view. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Castle Garden, good value. A four-star boutique hotel on Lovas út at the entrance of the Castle District, in a quiet street by the castle wall, a few minutes’ walk from Széll Kálmán tér, where the metro runs downtown, with the Riso restaurant and terrace. Choose it for Castle Hill at a lower price. Watch out: the Basic rooms are in the basement, overlooking the garden. Check rates on Booking.com →

Józsefváros: the Grand Boulevard and Bródy Sándor utca

District VIII, Józsefváros, lies along the Grand Boulevard. Blaha Lujza tér and József körút have the M2 metro and a tram that runs 24 hours, and the neighbourhood is less of a postcard than the river districts.

Where to stay in Józsefváros

  • Nemzeti Hotel Budapest, comfortable. A four-star hotel in the MGallery collection at József körút 4, in a 19th-century building near the M2 metro station and the 24-hour tram, with free Wi-Fi and rooms with a flat-screen TV and coffee and tea facilities. Choose it for a historic hotel on the boulevard. Watch out: the Classic room with a double bed is 15 sq m. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Brody House, comfortable. A heritage building on Bródy Sándor utca with eight rooms, each decorated with art from the Brody Collection, a double bed, an en-suite bathroom and air conditioning, and a bistro with a courtyard garden. Choose it for a small, artistic stay. Watch out: with eight rooms it fills up; the hotel asks you to email if you see no availability. Check rates on Booking.com →

Margaret Island: a park and a spa

Margaret Island lies in the Danube between Buda and Pest, a park with old trees and a spa hotel. It is a restful base rather than a sightseeing one, and you travel into the centre.

Where to stay on Margaret Island

  • Grand Margaret Island Ensana Health Spa Hotel, comfortable. A turn-of-the-century hotel with 164 rooms, views of the Danube and the island’s old trees, and a covered walkway to the medical spa and wellness facilities at Ensana Thermal Margaret Island. Choose it for a spa weekend. Watch out: the Deluxe Double is 20 sq m, the hotel’s rates are quoted per person per night, and it is on the island, not in the old centre. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Budapest

  • Count the tax. Add 4% of the room price, before VAT, to a room: a night priced at 60,000 forints before VAT adds 2,400 forints of tax. Some hotels, such as Grand Margaret Island, publish prices with the tax inside, so check which price you are comparing.
  • Check per person or per room. Ensana quotes its rates from €113 per person per night; most hotels quote the room. Compare the total for your party.
  • Check room size. Several hotels here have small entry-level rooms: 14 sq m for the Carlton’s Economy single, 15 sq m for the Nemzeti’s Classic double and 18 sq m for the Victoria’s Budget rooms. If you need space, book the category above.
  • Choose the side of the river. Pest has the nightlife and the metro; Buda has the views and the quiet. The 100E ends in Pest.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Budapest for a first trip?

Belváros-Lipótváros, District V, in central Pest: the airport's 100E bus ends at Deák Ferenc tér, a major transport hub, and most of the headline sights are within walking distance. Choose the Jewish Quarter for restaurants and nightlife, Terézváros for Andrássy Avenue and the Opera, and Castle Hill or Víziváros on the Buda side for the river views and quieter nights.

How much is the tourist tax in Budapest?

Each district sets its own. In the five districts covered here, I, V, VI, VII and VIII, it is 4% of the room price per guest night, which the hotel collects on top of the room. The district decrees we read define the base as the price less VAT, without compulsory breakfast and other services, while two district websites describe it as the gross price, so ask the hotel. Under-18s are exempt. Two adults paying 60,000 forints a night before VAT, for four nights, pay 9,600 forints of tax, by our arithmetic.

How do you get from Budapest Airport to the city centre?

The 100E Airport Express runs around the clock between the airport and Deák Ferenc tér, via Kálvin tér, on a special ticket costing 2,500 forints without a pass. Buses run every 5 to 10 minutes by day according to the airport, and since 1 April 2026 every 5 to 6 minutes in the morning and afternoon on the busiest days. The cheaper route is bus 200E to Kőbánya-Kispest and then metro line M3, on ordinary city tickets.

Can I use a normal public transport ticket on the airport bus?

Not on the 100E, which needs its own airport shuttle bus single ticket, 2,500 forints, or a 1,000-forint add-on if you hold a Budapest pass, a Pest County Pass or a Hungary Pass other than the 15-day Budapest pass. Bus 200E takes ordinary tickets and passes but not the airport ticket. A 500-forint single covers one ride on one vehicle, so by our reading bus 200E plus the metro needs two singles, 1,000 forints, or one 90-minute ticket, 850 forints, sold on a phone only.

How much is a taxi from Budapest Airport?

The airport's contracted taxi operator is Főtaxi, booked at the stands at Terminals 2A and 2B. Hungarian regulation sets the airport fare at a base of 2,100 forints plus 520 forints a kilometre and 130 forints a minute, and Főtaxi prepares a fare calculation from the destination address before the trip starts.

How much is public transport in Budapest?

A single ticket is 500 forints, or 700 forints if bought on the spot, valid for one ride on one vehicle with changes allowed only between metro lines. The 90-minute ticket is 850 forints, sold on a phone only, a 24-hour travelcard is 2,750 forints and a 72-hour travelcard is 5,750 forints, according to BKK.
