Toronto’s accommodation tax and HST

Two charges apply to a Toronto hotel room:

Charge Rate
City of Toronto Municipal Accommodation Tax (MAT) 6% of the room portion
Ontario Harmonized Sales Tax (HST) 13%, payable on the room and on the MAT

The City’s page says the MAT is charged on the purchase price of the room portion, and that other services, including food and beverage, are excluded if they are itemised separately on the bill. It also says HST is payable on the MAT portion. A temporary 8.5% rate ended on 1 August 2026, so any guide that still quotes 8.5% is out of date.

Our arithmetic, not an official figure: on a $300 room the MAT is $18.00, which makes $318.00; HST at 13% on that is $41.34, so the night costs $359.34, or about 19.8% more than the rate you were quoted.

Getting in from the airports, and around

  • From Pearson, by UP Express. The train runs from Toronto Pearson to downtown Toronto, ending at Union Station, in 28 minutes. The adult fare is $12.35 one way, or $9.25 when you pay with PRESTO or a contactless card. Children aged 12 and under travel free.
  • From Billy Bishop, by free shuttle. Complimentary shuttle buses connect Billy Bishop Toronto City Airport and Union Station every 15 minutes; the Union Station stop is on the west side of the Fairmont Royal York.
  • From Billy Bishop, on foot and by streetcar. The ferry to the mainland is free for pedestrians and runs every 15 minutes, from 5:15 am to 12:07 am. From the Bathurst and Queens Quay West stop, the 509 Harbourfront streetcar reaches Union Station in under 15 minutes.
  • In town, a TTC adult fare is $3.30 with PRESTO or a debit or credit card, and $3.35 in cash. Customers who pay cash get no two-hour transfer and no monthly fare capping, so tap a card or phone instead.

    Union Station and the Financial District: the first-trip base

    Union Station is where the UP Express ends and where the Billy Bishop shuttle stops, so a stay around it is the easiest first night in the city. The Financial District’s towers sit to the north and the lake to the south. It is busy on weekdays and quieter at weekends.

A landmark on Front Street West since its opening in 1929, with the REIGN restaurant, Fairmont Gold rooms and suites, and the Billy Bishop shuttle stop on its west side.     Downtown and the Entertainment District: theatres and King Street

    The Entertainment District, west of Yonge Street, is the area of King Street’s bars and restaurants and the theatres. Further east, around Victoria Street and Yonge, the downtown core meets the theatre district. It is loud on show nights and well served by streetcars and the subway.

    Where to stay in the Entertainment District and Downtown

    • Le Germain Hotel Toronto Mercer, comfortable. A boutique hotel on Mercer Street in the heart of the Entertainment District, within walking distance of King Street bars and Queen Street shops, with the PUNCH restaurant, a library-style Mercer Lounge and a rooftop terrace on the 11th floor. Choose it for a design hotel by the nightlife. Watch out: valet service is offered, at additional fees. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Pantages Hotel, good value. In the theatre district, with 106 suites, a fitness centre, in-room Keurig machines and pets welcome. Choose it for space downtown at a fair price. Watch out: check-in is at 3 pm and check-out at 11 am, so ask the front desk about storing bags if you arrive early. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Old Town and St Lawrence: a heritage hotel and a market

    Old Town is the older, east side of downtown, around King Street East and St. Lawrence Market. It is calmer in the evening than the Entertainment District and close to the Financial District on foot.

    • The Omni King Edward Hotel, comfortable. At 37 King Street East, with a heritage dating to 1903, 301 rooms, the Mokara Spa and traditional afternoon tea. Choose it for old-world character in the east of downtown. Watch out: the hotel describes itself as near a subway station, not at one; check the walk on a map. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Yorkville: shops and a quiet, upmarket base

    Yorkville is Toronto’s upmarket district, around Bloor Street and Yorkville Avenue, with boutiques, galleries and restaurants. It is a base for shopping and calm evenings rather than nightlife; the Financial District is a subway ride away.

    Where to stay in Yorkville

    • Four Seasons Hotel Toronto, splurge. At 60 Yorkville Avenue, near Bloor Street and Bay Street, with floor-to-ceiling windows, Café Boulud, a cocktail bar and a spa the hotel calls the city’s only Forbes 5-Star Spa. Choose it for full-service luxury in Yorkville. Watch out: price. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • The Hazelton Hotel, splurge. At 118 Yorkville Avenue, with Deluxe King rooms starting at a minimum of 465 sq ft, granite bathrooms with soaking tubs and Juliet or walk-out balconies. Choose it for generous rooms in Yorkville. Watch out: the Luxury Two Queen Room is at the back and overlooks a laneway. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Queen West and West Queen West: galleries and live music

    Queen Street West runs west from the downtown core, and West Queen West, further out, is the stretch of galleries, bars and independent shops. It is a neighbourhood for walking and eating, and the two hotels below are both on Queen Street West, at numbers 1150 and 1214.

    Where to stay on Queen West

    • The Drake Hotel, comfortable. At 1150 Queen Street West, a boutique hotel of 51 art-filled rooms in a Modern Wing and a Classic Wing, with live shows at the Drake Underground. Choose it for art and music downstairs. Watch out: live shows run in the building, so ask for a room away from the venue if you want a quiet night. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Gladstone House, good value. At 1214 Queen Street West, a hotel since 1889 that calls itself the city’s longest-operating, with artwork throughout. The Studio Queen is 180 sq ft, the Studio King 210 sq ft, the Loft King 310 sq ft and the Corner King 325 sq ft. Choose it for West Queen West at the door and a lower price. Watch out: the cheapest room, the Studio Queen, is 180 sq ft. Check rates on Booking.com →

    The Waterfront: the lake, the ferry and the CN Tower

    On the lake, Toronto has Harbourfront, the CN Tower and Ripley’s Aquarium, and the Billy Bishop ferry leaves from the foot of Bathurst Street. It suits visitors who want the water and the attractions close.

    • The Westin Harbour Castle, Toronto, comfortable. At 1 Harbour Square, with views of the Toronto Islands through the fitness studio’s windows, an indoor pool, a whirlpool and a tennis court. Choose it for the lake and the pool. Watch out: it is also a convention hotel, with 70,000 square feet of meeting space, so expect conference crowds. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Le Germain Hotel Toronto Maple Leaf Square, comfortable. At 75 Bremner Boulevard, a 167-room boutique hotel by the CN Tower, Ripley’s Aquarium and the waterfront, steps from Scotiabank Arena. Choose it for the attractions at the door. Watch out: on event nights at the arena, the area around it is busy. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Near Pearson Airport: an honest note

    Most visitors will not need an airport hotel, because the UP Express reaches downtown in 28 minutes. But for an early flight or a late arrival, one is worth a night.

    • Alt Hotel Toronto Airport, good value. A few steps from the flights, linked to the terminals and the economy car park by the free 24/7 LINK train, with a 24-hour gym on the 14th floor and breakfast in the lobby. Choose it for a short night before a flight. Watch out: it is in Mississauga, outside the City of Toronto, so the City’s 6% MAT table above is not necessarily the municipal tax on its bill; compare the final total. Check rates on Booking.com →

    How to book Toronto

    • Count the tax. Add the 6% MAT, then 13% HST on the room and the MAT together: a $300 room costs $359.34 a night.
    • Pick the airport first. Pearson means the UP Express to Union Station, from $9.25 with PRESTO. Billy Bishop means the free shuttle or the ferry, and the Entertainment District and the Waterfront are the closest bases.
    • Check the room size. Some hotels here publish small standard rooms: Gladstone House’s Studio Queen is 180 sq ft, while The Hazelton’s Deluxe King starts at 465 sq ft. If you need space, book the category above the cheapest.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the best area to stay in Toronto for a first trip?

    Around Union Station and the Financial District: the UP Express from Pearson ends there, the Billy Bishop shuttle stops there and the lake is close. Choose the Entertainment District for theatres and King Street, Yorkville for a quiet upmarket base, Queen West for galleries and live music, or the Waterfront for the CN Tower and the ferry.

    How much is the hotel tax in Toronto?

    The City of Toronto charges a 6% Municipal Accommodation Tax on the room portion, and Ontario's 13% HST is payable on the room and on that tax. By our arithmetic, a $300 room costs $359.34 a night, about 19.8% more than the quoted rate. A temporary 8.5% rate ended on 1 August 2026.

    How do you get from Pearson Airport to downtown Toronto?

    Take the UP Express to Union Station: 28 minutes, $12.35 one way for an adult, or $9.25 when you pay with PRESTO or a contactless card. Children aged 12 and under travel free.

    How do you get from Billy Bishop airport to downtown?

    A free shuttle bus runs every 15 minutes between Billy Bishop Toronto City Airport and Union Station. You can also take the ferry to the mainland, free for pedestrians, then the 509 Harbourfront streetcar from the Bathurst and Queens Quay West stop, which reaches Union Station in under 15 minutes.

    How much is the TTC in Toronto?

    An adult fare is $3.30 with PRESTO or a debit or credit card, and $3.35 in cash. Customers who pay cash get no two-hour transfer and no monthly fare capping.

    Is there a hotel at Toronto Pearson Airport?

    Yes: Alt Hotel Toronto Airport is linked to the terminals by the free 24/7 LINK train. It is in Mississauga, outside the City of Toronto, so compare the final total rather than assuming the City's 6% tax applies to its bill.
