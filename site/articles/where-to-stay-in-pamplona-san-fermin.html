San Fermín turns the Old Town into the loudest neighbourhood in Navarre, so your hotel choice is mostly a choice about sleep. The city says the festival takes place between 6 and 14 July, and the daily bull run starts at 8:00. Spain charges 10% VAT on hotel stays, and we found no tourist tax in force in Navarra or Pamplona, though a council committee voted in September 2026 to push for one.

This guide covers four areas, from the Casco Antiguo on the run route to the calmer districts to the north, 12 hotels, what the city has published about the run, and the bus link to San Sebastián. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in November 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

The dates, and what we do not know yet

The city’s own route page says the San Fermín festival takes place between the 6th and 14th July. Its 2027 working calendar lists 6, 7, 8, 9, 12, 13 and 14 July as the festival’s municipal holidays. The council has not yet published the 2027 programme or the 2027 bando, the annual rulebook for the fiesta, so where this guide describes rules and timings, it describes the 2026 bando and says so. Hotel Pompaelo’s own page puts the opening rocket, the Chupinazo, at noon on 6 July, and the 2026 bando shut every business facing Plaza Consistorial from 10:00 to 13:00 that day. The closing ceremony is the Pobre de mí on 14 July.

The run, as the city describes it

The 2026 bando says every bull run starts at 8:00, barring force majeure, and that the route goes through Santo Domingo, Plaza Consistorial, Mercaderes and Estafeta streets. Anyone under 18 may not take part. After 07:15 only people who are running, or authorised, may stay on the route, and runners may not enter after that time. Bars and shops with access to the route must be shut by 06:00, and doorways must be closed from 07:15. The bando also bans carrying objects that could cause trouble, listing rucksacks, bags, bottles, cameras and recording devices.

The independent fiesta guide sanfermin.com, which is run with the Navarra travel agency Destino Navarra and sells balcony bookings itself, says the course is 875 metres, that runs usually last two to three minutes, and that nobody runs the whole course. It also says spectators must stay behind the second line of fencing. It describes the streets as hosed down in the early hours to remove the litter left over from the previous night, so expect the Old Town to be busy well before the run.

On balconies, the city’s bando makes owners responsible for the safety of facades and balconies, and says they must not let more people stand on one than is sensible. Some hotels also offer balcony viewing for the Chupinazo; Hotel Pompaelo’s page says so but gives no price or terms. The city does not publish a rental price list, and neither do we.

The criticism

Animal-rights groups oppose the bull run and the bullfights held during the fiesta. Euronews reported that a protest in 2022 was organised by the Spanish group AnimaNaturalis and PETA, and said it was the 18th year in a row that demonstrations had taken place ahead of San Fermín. The city’s tourism page traces records of the festival back to the 12th century. We note the criticism because it shapes the mood around the city, and we take no side.

Casco Antiguo, on the route: the squares and Estafeta

This is the festival at full volume. Estafeta Street is on the run route, Plaza del Castillo is the social centre, and the town hall square hosts the Chupinazo. By the city’s own account some 12,500 people gather there on 6 July. If you want to step out of the door and into the crowd, this is the place. If you want to sleep before 4 a.m., it is not, and our advice is to ask for a soundproofed room at the back.

Casco Antiguo, away from the route: walls and side streets

The three hotels here stand on streets that the bando’s route list does not include, which gives you the old town on foot with one fewer layer of crowd. It is still the Old Town: music and bars run late, and the run’s morning closures still affect how you leave.

Where to stay in the Casco Antiguo, away from the route

  • Pamplona Catedral Hotel, comfortable. A four-star boutique hotel in a former 19th-century convent beside the Portal de Francia and the city walls, a few metres from the cathedral. Rooms are described as outward-facing, bright and soundproof, and it has a private car park with valet service, a seasonal outdoor pool and a gym. Choose it for a quieter corner of the old town. Watch out: its page does not state San Fermín rates or a minimum stay. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Maisonnave, comfortable. At Calle Nueva 20, a four-star hotel whose page says it lets you walk to the main sights, with a cafe-bar, parking booked through its site, and a 12% discount for booking direct. It says children under six stay free. Choose it for a central four-star on a side street. Watch out: the discount is a general web offer, not a San Fermín rate. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Alda Centro Pamplona, budget. On Plaza Virgen de la O, which its page puts five minutes from the Museo de Navarra and the town hall, with free Wi-Fi, a bicycle park and rooms for one to four people. Check-in is from 14:00 and check-out until 12:00. Choose it for a cheap bed near the walls. Watch out: it gives a 20-minute walk to the train station, and no festival terms. Ensanche: wider streets, still walkable

The Ensanche is the district beside the old town. Our reading of the map is that the streets are wider and the Casco Antiguo is a short walk away, and that neither hotel here faces a route street.

Where to stay in the Ensanche

  • Hotel Tres Reyes, splurge. An iconic five-star hotel in the city centre, with rooms that look onto the Taconera Gardens, an outdoor pool and a restaurant. Choose it for a pool and garden views in July. Watch out: its site gives no San Fermín rates or minimum stay. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Yoldi, good value. A three-star hotel on Avenida San Ignacio with 50 recently renovated rooms, free Wi-Fi, air conditioning, and a special rate in the public car park on Plaza del Castillo. It puts the main symbolic sights five minutes away on foot. Choose it for a plain, central three-star. Watch out: late check-out until 14:00 is subject to availability, and it states no San Fermín terms. Check rates on Booking.com →

Iturrama, San Juan and the station side: calmer, with a walk or a bus

North and west of the Ensanche are residential districts away from the route, and two of the four hotels here state parking. The price is distance: you will walk, take a villavesa (the city bus) or a taxi to the Old Town. Noticias de Navarra reported in 2026 that most villavesa lines ran 24 hours during the fiesta, with changes around the fireworks and concerts, so check the 2027 plan when it is published.

Where to stay in Iturrama, San Juan and the station side

  • Hotel Albret, good value. On Calle Ermitagaña 3, with 106 exterior rooms in six types, on an avenue that its page says connects directly with the Old Town, near the Clínica Universidad de Navarra. Its page offers up to 20% off for stays between 5 and 14 July under a San Fermín 2027 banner. Choose it for the only hotel here that states a San Fermín 2027 offer. Watch out: read the conditions of that offer before you rely on it. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Blanca de Navarra, comfortable. A hotel of more than 30 years’ standing with a bar-restaurant, flexible meeting rooms and a position its page puts 850 metres from the Parque de la Ciudadela. Choose it for a calm base with a park nearby. Watch out: the Old Town is further than the hotel’s own distance to the park suggests, and no festival terms are stated. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Sancho Ramírez, good value. At Sancho Ramírez 11, a family-run hotel its page puts five minutes from the historic centre, with a buffet breakfast, private parking for guests at €10 a night subject to availability, and free cancellation until 12:00 on the day of arrival. Choose it for easy parking close to town. Watch out: the late check-out and cancellation terms are the general ones, not festival rules. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Pamplona Plaza, good value. A three-star hotel on Avenida Marcelo Celayeta near the train station, with free underground parking, 24-hour reception, a buffet breakfast, and rooms or apartments. Its page gives a 20-minute walk to the Casco Antiguo and 2.8 km to Plaza del Castillo. Choose it if you are arriving by train or with a car. Watch out: those two distances do not agree, so plan on a bus or taxi after the fireworks. Check rates on Booking.com →

Getting in, and around

  • From the airport. Aena’s Pamplona page lists the car, taxi and shared taxi as ways to reach the airport, and no bus. It gives no fare, so ask for the taxi price before you get in.
  • By bus from San Sebastián. Alsa’s published Pamplona to San Sebastián timetable shows coaches such as 09:45 to 11:00, with first departures at 06:55. The page does not say whether extra services run during San Fermín, so check closer to the date.
  • From Logroño. We could not confirm Logroño to Pamplona bus or train times from an operator’s own timetable, so we give none here.
  • Inside the city. Villavesas and your own feet. The Old Town is compact and the 2026 bando regulated parking across a wide zone from 5 to 14 July, so leave the car at the hotel.

    The tax: 10% VAT and no tourist tax found

    Charge Rate in 2026-27
    VAT on hotel stays (IVA) 10%, the reduced rate for hospitality
    Spain’s general VAT rate, for comparison 21%
    Navarra or Pamplona tourist tax None found

    The Agencia Tributaria says the general rate is 21%, and that the 10% rate applies to hospitality services. For a room quoted at €200 including VAT, VAT is €18.18 and the room before VAT is €181.82 (our arithmetic from the 10% rate). We could not confirm from every hotel’s site whether its rates include VAT, so check the total at the payment step. We found no tourist tax in force in Navarra. On 29 September 2026 the Pamplona council’s Presidency Committee approved a declaration urging the Government of Navarra to let local councils charge a tourist tax, and saying the council will study a tourist-ecological tax for San Fermín. That is a political position, not a rule, and we found no law or decree creating a charge.

    How to book Pamplona for San Fermín

    • Book early. The 2027 programme is not out, but the dates follow the city’s usual 6 to 14 July.
    • Ask about minimum stays. None of the 12 hotels states a San Fermín minimum stay on its own site, so ask before you pay. Only Hotel Albret states a San Fermín 2027 offer.
    • Pick your noise level. Ask for a rear-facing, soundproofed room if you are on the route or around the squares.
    • Count the tax. Check whether the rate includes 10% VAT; we found no tourist tax to add.

    Frequently asked questions

    When is San Fermín in 2027?

    The city of Pamplona says the San Fermín festival takes place between 6 and 14 July, and its 2027 calendar lists 6, 7, 8, 9, 12, 13 and 14 July as San Fermín holidays. The council had not published the 2027 programme when we checked. The 2026 bando started every bull run at 8:00.

    Where is the best area to stay for San Fermín?

    It depends on how much noise you want. The Casco Antiguo puts you on the run route and the squares, with the most noise. The side streets of the old town are a few steps away, the Ensanche is beside it, and Iturrama, San Juan and the station side are calmer but a walk or a bus from the festival.

    Does Pamplona charge a tourist tax?

    We found no tourist tax in force in Navarra or Pamplona. On 29 September 2026 the Pamplona council's Presidency Committee approved a declaration urging the Government of Navarra to regulate a local tourist tax, and saying the council would study a tourist-ecological tax for San Fermín. That is a political position, not a rule, and we found no law or decree creating a charge.

    How much VAT is charged on a Pamplona hotel room?

    Spain applies 10% VAT to hospitality services, including hotels, and the general rate is 21%, according to the Agencia Tributaria. On a room quoted at 200 euros including VAT, the VAT is 18.18 euros, our arithmetic from the 10% rate. Check whether each hotel's rate includes VAT.

    What are the rules for the bull run?

    The 2026 bando, the council's rulebook for last year, said each run starts at 8:00 and goes through Santo Domingo, Plaza Consistorial, Mercaderes and Estafeta streets. It bars anyone under 18, limits the route to runners and authorised people from 07:15, and bans objects such as rucksacks, bags and bottles. The 2027 rules had not been published when we checked.

    Can I stay outside Pamplona and travel in?

    Yes. Alsa's published timetable for Pamplona to San Sebastián shows coaches such as 09:45 to 11:00, with first departures at 06:55, though the page does not say whether extra services run during San Fermín. We could not confirm Logroño times from an operator. Pamplona's villavesas, the city buses, ran 24 hours on most lines during the 2026 fiesta.
