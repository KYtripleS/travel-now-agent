Hotels around Temple Bar, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Temple Bar that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

Dublin is one of the few European capitals with no tourist tax. What it has instead is VAT: hotel rooms in Ireland carry 13.5%, the reduced rate, and since 1 July 2026 the breakfast in a room-and-breakfast rate is taxed at 9%. Both are normally inside the price you are quoted, so there is nothing to add at checkout. The real cost of a Dublin stay is the room rate, which climbs on big rugby and concert weekends, and the walk or bus ride from where you sleep to where you want to be.

This guide covers six areas, from Temple Bar to Ballsbridge, 12 hotels, the airport buses and the tax. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

Dublin’s hotel tax: VAT, and no tourist tax

Ireland has no tourist or city tax on hotel stays. Dublin’s councils have asked the Government for the power to charge one, but a levy would need new national legislation, and the tax changes in the Budget announced on 6 October 2026 did not include one. What you pay is VAT, set by Revenue, Ireland’s tax authority:

What you pay for VAT rate
A room in a hotel, guesthouse or B&B 13.5%, the reduced rate
Meals, including the breakfast in a room-and-breakfast rate, from 1 July 2026 9%, the second reduced rate
Alcohol, bottled water and soft drinks 23%, the standard rate
Tourist or city tax None

Rooms were taxed at 9% from 1 November 2020 to 31 August 2023, as a pandemic-era measure, and have been back at 13.5% since. When a hotel sells a room with breakfast for one price, it splits the bill: Revenue’s own example is a €150 night with breakfast at a B&B, which carries €15.46 of VAT on the room and €1.65 on the breakfast.

The VAT is already in the price. On a €200 room, €23.79 of it is VAT (our arithmetic: €200 × 13.5 ÷ 113.5). Over three nights that is €71.37 you have paid without seeing it added.

Getting in from the airport, and around

There is no train to Dublin Airport. MetroLink, a planned metro from Swords through the airport to the city centre and on to Charlemont, has been approved by the Government to proceed to detailed tender stage; it is not running and has no confirmed opening date. Until then, it is the bus.

  • Dublin Express coaches run between the airport and the city every 7½ minutes, in as little as 14 minutes on the fastest service, from €9 one way when you book online. Stops include Aston Quay (for O’Connell Bridge and Temple Bar), Wellington Quay (for Temple Bar and Dublin Castle), Trinity College, Heuston Station and North Wall Quay in the Docklands. Drivers take cards only, not cash, and the coaches do not take Leap cards.
  • Aircoach runs the 700 and 700X to O’Connell Street 24 hours a day, up to every 15 minutes; the 700 continues by Kildare Street and Leeson Street Lower, south of the river. The 702 runs by the 3Arena, Grand Canal Dock and Ballsbridge, hourly at peak times. The trip takes between 20 and 40 minutes, depending on traffic.
  • Dublin Bus 16 and 41 are ordinary city buses. The 41 runs around the clock to Lower Abbey Street, off O’Connell Street; the 16 runs through the city centre to Ballinteer, about every 10 to 15 minutes. The fare is €2 with a Leap card or €2.60 in coins; drivers take no notes, no bank cards and no phone payments.
  • In town, the TFI 90 Minute Fare is €2 for an adult on a Leap card and covers any combination of Dublin Bus, Go-Ahead Ireland’s city buses, the Luas and the DART within Zone 1 for 90 minutes from your first tap; spending is capped at €6 a day and €24 a week. Express and Nitelink buses cost €2.40.
  • For a short stay, the TFI Leap Visitor Card gives unlimited travel on Dublin’s city buses, the Luas, the DART and Commuter Rail in Zone 1: €8 for 24 hours, €18 for 72 hours, €24 for seven days. It is on sale in the arrivals halls, at the T1 Convenience Store and WHSmith in Terminal 1 and at SPAR in Terminal 2. It does not cover Dublin Express or Aircoach.

You cannot yet pay on Dublin’s buses by tapping a bank card or phone; the National Transport Authority is building an account-based ticketing system for bus, rail and Luas, starting in the Dublin area. For now, the Leap card is the way to pay.

Temple Bar: the first-trip base

Temple Bar is the grid of cobbled lanes on the south bank of the Liffey, between Dublin Castle and Trinity College, with the Ha’penny Bridge at its edge and Grafton Street a few minutes’ walk away. It is the most central place to sleep in Dublin, three Dublin Express stops are on its edges, and it is also where the city’s late-night pub crowd ends up. Both hotels below are on Fleet Street, at the eastern end, nearest Trinity College.

St Stephen’s Green and Georgian Dublin: the classic base

South of Trinity College the city changes: St Stephen’s Green, Grafton Street and, to the east, Merrion Square and the streets of brick Georgian terraces and painted front doors. The National Gallery and the National Museum are here, and Temple Bar is a short walk north. Evenings are quieter than in Temple Bar, and the Aircoach 700 stops on Kildare Street and Leeson Street Lower.

Where to stay around St Stephen’s Green

  • The Shelbourne, splurge. On St Stephen’s Green since 1824. In 1922 a committee chaired by Michael Collins drafted the first Irish Constitution in its Room 112, now called the Constitution Room. It has the Horseshoe Bar and the Saddle Room restaurant, and the Heritage Parkview rooms look over the Green. Choose it for the city’s history with a view of the park. Watch out: price, and the King Deluxe rooms are 25 m², so pick the category with care. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • The Merrion, splurge. Four restored Georgian townhouses on Upper Merrion Street, opposite Government Buildings: 142 rooms and suites, an 18-metre pool, a spa and a gym, and Restaurant Patrick Guilbaud, which holds two Michelin stars. Choose it for the five-star stay in Georgian Dublin. Watch out: price. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Number 31, comfortable. A townhouse of 22 individually designed rooms on Leeson Close, off Leeson Street, moments from St Stephen’s Green. The architect Sam Stephenson turned its old stable block into his home at the end of the 1950s; today it has a sunken lounge, an honesty bar and a garden patio. Choose it for character and a celebrated breakfast. Watch out: there is no lift, the rooms are over four floors, and the house does not take children. The advance-purchase rate is non-refundable; the best available rate can be cancelled free up to 48 hours before check-in. Check rates on Booking.com →

O’Connell Street and Connolly: the transport hubs

North of the river, O’Connell Street is the city’s main avenue, with the General Post Office, the Spire and, at its top, Parnell Square. East of it are Connolly station, for the DART along the bay, and Busáras, the central bus station. This is where the airport buses arrive: the Aircoach 700 and 700X stop on O’Connell Street, the 41 ends at Lower Abbey Street, and Dublin Express stops at Aston Quay, by O’Connell Bridge. Hotels are cheaper than south of the river, and Temple Bar is across O’Connell Bridge.

Where to stay near O’Connell Street

  • Riu Plaza The Gresham Dublin, comfortable. At 23 Upper O’Connell Street, with a buffet breakfast, a lounge bar, a rooftop bar and a 24-hour gym, and the Aircoach stop opposite. Choose it for a central hotel at the end of the airport coach route. Watch out: O’Connell Street is a main bus corridor, busy by day and at night. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Latroupe Jacobs Inn, budget. A hostel at 21–28 Talbot Place, Dublin 1, near Connolly station and Busáras, with private rooms with their own bathrooms, family rooms, and dorms of four to 12 beds, plus a large bar with daily events and live music, a terrace and a 24-hour reception. Choose it for the lowest price near the hubs. Watch out: the large dorms sleep 10 or 12, and the bar is part of the deal; light sleepers should book a private room. Check rates on Booking.com →

Smithfield and Stoneybatter: the neighbourhood base

West of the centre, on the north bank, Smithfield is a long cobbled square beside the old Jameson Distillery on Bow Street, with the Four Courts on the quays below. Stoneybatter, just north, is a neighbourhood of small terraced streets, cafés and pubs. The Luas Red line stops at Smithfield, and Heuston Station, a Dublin Express stop, leads to the Phoenix Park. Temple Bar is a walk along the quays rather than at the door, which is the trade-off for lower prices.

Where to stay in Smithfield

  • Maldron Hotel Smithfield, good value. A four-star hotel with 92 rooms, including triples, quads, and family and interconnecting rooms, a bar and restaurant, Grain & Grill, and the Luas moments away. Choose it for families and groups on a budget. Watch out: Grafton Street and St Stephen’s Green are across the river, a Luas ride or a long walk. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Generator Dublin, budget. A design-led hostel on Smithfield Square, next to the Jameson Distillery, with private rooms, shared dorms and female-only dorms, a café, a restaurant and a bar. Choose it for the lowest price in a lively square. Watch out: the hostel markets itself to stag and hen parties, so expect a busy bar at weekends. Check rates on Booking.com →

The Docklands: modern hotels and the 3Arena

East of the Custom House, the old docks on both banks of the Liffey are now offices, the Convention Centre and the 3Arena on the north side, and Grand Canal Dock with the Bord Gáis Energy Theatre on the south. The Aircoach 702 stops at the 3Arena and Grand Canal Dock, Dublin Express at North Wall Quay, and the DART at Pearse and Grand Canal Dock. The hotels are newer and larger than in the old centre, often with pools.

Where to stay in the Docklands

  • Anantara The Marker Dublin Hotel, splurge. On Grand Canal Square, with 187 rooms of 30 to 35 m² with floor-to-ceiling windows, a rooftop bar and terrace, and a spa with a 23-metre infinity pool. Choose it for space, a pool and the view. Watch out: price, and the old centre is a walk of some distance; the hotel puts College Green and Grafton Street within walking distance rather than at the door. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Clayton Hotel Cardiff Lane, good value. A four-star hotel on Cardiff Lane, near Sir John Rogerson’s Quay, with free access for guests to its pool, one of the largest in the city by the hotel’s account, plus a separate children’s pool, and the 3Arena, the Aviva Stadium and Pearse DART station within walking distance. Choose it for a pool at a mid-range price. Watch out: the Docklands empty out after office hours. Check rates on Booking.com →

Ballsbridge: quiet streets and the Aviva Stadium

Ballsbridge, in Dublin 4, is the embassy district south-east of the centre: wide roads, red-brick houses, the RDS showground and the Aviva Stadium, home of Irish rugby and football. The Aircoach 702 stops here, and Lansdowne Road DART station runs into town.

Where to stay in Ballsbridge

  • Herbert Park Hotel & Park Residence, comfortable. A four-star hotel looking over the 48-acre Herbert Park, with self-catering studio apartments in its Park Residence, underground parking and a gym, a 10-minute walk from Lansdowne Road DART station. Choose it for a calm base on a match weekend or a longer stay. Watch out: the city centre is a DART or bus ride away. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Dublin

  • Count the tax. It is already counted: Irish room rates normally include the 13.5% VAT. Check instead whether breakfast is included, and whether parking is extra if you drive.
  • Watch the calendar, not just the season. Rates rise for big events: Six Nations rugby at the Aviva Stadium in February and March, St Patrick’s Day on 17 March, and concerts at the 3Arena and Croke Park. Check the fixtures before you pick dates, and book early if your trip falls on one.
  • Check the room size. Some central rooms are small by design: the Cosy Double at Temple Bar Inn is 12 m², and The Morgan’s Solo Room, about 14 m², is for one. At the other end, rooms at The Marker start at 30 m².
  • Read the cancellation terms. Many hotels sell a cheaper non-refundable rate beside a flexible one; at Number 31, the best available rate can be cancelled free up to 48 hours before arrival.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Dublin for a first trip?

Temple Bar, on the south bank of the Liffey between Trinity College and Dublin Castle: it is the most central place to sleep, Grafton Street is a few minutes' walk away and three Dublin Express airport coach stops are on its edges. Choose St Stephen's Green for a quieter classic base, Smithfield for lower prices and the Docklands for modern hotels with pools.

Does Dublin have a tourist tax?

No. Ireland has no tourist or city tax on hotel stays. Dublin's councils have asked the Government for the power to charge one, but a levy would need new national legislation, and the tax changes in the Budget announced on 6 October 2026 did not include one.

How much VAT is charged on hotel rooms in Ireland?

13.5%, the reduced rate, which is normally included in the price. Rooms were taxed at 9% from 1 November 2020 to 31 August 2023. Since 1 July 2026, meals, including the breakfast in a room-and-breakfast rate, are taxed at 9%. On a €200 room, €23.79 of the price is VAT.

How do you get from Dublin Airport to the city centre?

By bus: there is no train. Dublin Express coaches run every 7½ minutes, from €9 one way online, to stops including Temple Bar, Trinity College and Heuston Station. Aircoach's 700 and 700X run to O'Connell Street 24 hours a day, and Dublin Bus routes 16 and 41 cost €2 with a Leap card or €2.60 in coins.

Is there a train or metro from Dublin Airport?

Not yet. MetroLink, a planned metro from Swords through Dublin Airport to the city centre and Charlemont, has been approved by the Government to proceed to detailed tender stage, but it is not running and has no confirmed opening date. Until then, the airport is served by coaches and city buses.

How much is public transport in Dublin?

With a Leap card, the TFI 90 Minute Fare is €2 for an adult and covers Dublin Bus, the Luas and the DART within Zone 1 for 90 minutes, capped at €6 a day and €24 a week. The TFI Leap Visitor Card costs €8 for 24 hours, €18 for 72 hours and €24 for seven days, and is on sale at the airport.
