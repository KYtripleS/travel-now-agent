Hotels around Downtown Vancouver, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Downtown Vancouver that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

In Vancouver the room rate is only the start of the bill. In the City of Vancouver a room carries 8% provincial sales tax, a 3% municipal and regional district tax and a 2.5% Major Events tax, and then 5% GST on the room and on those taxes together. On a $300 room that is $57.53 a night before you have left the lobby. Where you stay decides the rest: how far you walk to the seawall, and how often you pay the airport surcharge on the train.

This guide covers five areas, from Coal Harbour to Granville Island, plus a note on Richmond by the airport, 12 hotels, the train from YVR and the taxes, in Canadian dollars. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

Vancouver’s hotel taxes

British Columbia taxes a hotel room in layers, and the City of Vancouver adds one more:

Charge Rate Where
Provincial sales tax (PST) 8% of the room rate All of British Columbia
Municipal and regional district tax (MRDT) 3% of the room rate City of Vancouver; Richmond also charges 3%
Major Events MRDT 2.5% of the room rate City of Vancouver only, from 1 February 2023 to 31 January 2030
Goods and services tax (GST) 5% On the room and, in the City of Vancouver, on the three taxes above as well

Because the provincial taxes in the City of Vancouver add up to more than 12%, GST is charged on them too: about 19.2% in all. In the province’s own example, a $200 room becomes $238.35.

Our arithmetic for a longer stay: three nights at $300 is $900 of room. PST adds $72, the MRDT $27 and the Major Events MRDT $22.50, which makes $1,021.50; GST at 5% of that adds $51.08, for a total of $1,072.58. In Richmond, by the airport, there is no Major Events MRDT and GST falls only on the room, so the same $300 night costs $348 rather than $357.53.

The taxes also apply to any fees the hotel adds, such as a resort, cleaning or destination marketing fee. Compare other cities in hotel taxes around the world.

Getting in from the airport, and around

  • From YVR, the Canada Line runs from YVR–Airport station to Waterfront station downtown in about 25 minutes.
  • The airport surcharge. Trips that start at YVR–Airport, Sea Island Centre or Templeton stations add a $6.50 YVR AddFare. There is no AddFare on the way to the airport.
  • What you pay. The airport is in Richmond, fare zone 2, and downtown is in zone 1. On weekdays before 6:30pm, tapping a contactless card costs a two-zone fare of $5.10 plus the $6.50 AddFare, so $11.60. After 6:30pm on weekdays, at weekends and on public holidays every trip is one zone, $3.50, so $10.
  • How to pay. Tap a contactless Visa, Mastercard or American Express card, or a phone wallet, when you enter and leave a SkyTrain station. A Compass Card costs a $6 refundable deposit, and its stored value is cheaper: $2.85 for one zone, $4.20 for two.
  • Around town, bus trips are one zone at all times, and an adult DayPass for all zones is $12.55.

Before you fly: entry to Canada

  • Most visitors need an eTA. Visa-exempt foreign nationals need an electronic travel authorization to fly to or transit through a Canadian airport. It costs CAD $7 and most applications are approved within minutes.
  • Apply only on the Government of Canada site. The eTA is valid for up to five years, or until the passport expires if sooner.
  • Who does not need one. U.S. citizens are exempt, and visitors arriving by car, bus, train or boat do not need an eTA.

Downtown and Coal Harbour: the first-trip base

Downtown Vancouver sits on a peninsula, and Coal Harbour is its northern shore: the Vancouver Convention Centre, the floatplane docks and the seawall path running west towards Stanley Park. Waterfront station, the end of the Canada Line, is a few minutes’ walk away. Further south, Georgia, Robson and Burrard streets hold the stores, the Vancouver Art Gallery and the offices, and empty a little in the evenings.

The West End: Stanley Park and English Bay

The West End is the residential side of the peninsula: apartment towers and older houses on tree-lined streets between Robson Street’s shops and English Bay Beach, with Stanley Park at its western edge. Denman and Davie streets are lined with small restaurants, and Davie Village is the heart of the city’s LGBTQ+ community. It is the area for the seawall before breakfast and the sunset over the bay.

Where to stay in the West End

  • Sylvia Hotel, good value. At 1154 Gilford Street on English Bay, a designated heritage building from 1912 with 120 rooms and suites, steps from English Bay Beach and Stanley Park. Its lounge is, the hotel says, the first cocktail bar in Vancouver, and pets stay free. Choose it for the beach at the door. Watch out: room types run from a Petite Queen to suites with a kitchen, so check which one you are booking. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Blue Horizon Hotel, comfortable. At 1225 Robson Street, where every room is a corner room with a private balcony, with a small indoor lap pool. Choose it for light and a balcony on the shopping street. Watch out: the pool closes daily from 2pm to 3pm, and the pool and fitness area is not wheelchair accessible. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • HI Vancouver Downtown, budget. At 1114 Burnaby Street, just off the Davie strip and within walking distance of English Bay and Stanley Park, with private double rooms, four-bed shared rooms and a fully equipped kitchen. Choose it to keep money for the trip. Watch out: the four-bed rooms are shared; book the private double for a room of your own. Check rates on Booking.com →

Gastown and Canada Place: the old town and the cruise port

Gastown is where Vancouver began, a district of brick and stone buildings, cobbled Water Street and the steam clock, now full of restaurants, bars and shops. Canada Place, with its white sail-like roofs, is its waterfront neighbour: the cruise ship terminal and Waterfront station, where the Canada Line, the SeaBus and the Expo Line meet.

Be ready for one thing. East of Gastown, along East Hastings Street, is the Downtown Eastside, where the city’s housing and drug crises are most visible. Expect street homelessness a block or two from some of the hotels below, and keep to Water Street and the waterfront at night if that worries you.

Where to stay in Gastown and at Canada Place

  • Pan Pacific Vancouver, splurge. At 999 Canada Place, above the cruise terminal, with a direct elevator down to it, a heated rooftop saltwater pool open all year, and harbour and mountain views. Choose it before or after a cruise, or for the harbour with Gastown a walk away. Watch out: it shares the building with the cruise terminal, so expect crowds on ship days. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Victorian Hotel, good value. At 514 Homer Street, a boutique hotel in an 1898 building on the edge of Gastown, with exposed brick, original wood floors, an Italian restaurant and a new Sterling Wing of rooms. Choose it for heritage character a walk from Gastown, Yaletown and the stadiums. Watch out: rooms differ a lot, from a 247 sq ft Legacy King to the 450 sq ft Rockwell Suite, as the hotel lists them; read the room description. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Skwachàys Lodge, comfortable. At 31 West Pender Street, an Indigenous boutique hotel and social enterprise with 18 individually designed rooms, opened in 2012, and housing and studio space for 24 Indigenous artists in the same building. Choose it for a stay whose money goes to a purpose. Watch out: there is no parking on site, and the hotel is at the edge of the Downtown Eastside.

Yaletown and Granville Island: the seawall and the market

Yaletown grew up around the railway yards on False Creek, and its brick warehouses are now restaurants with terraces, with the seawall and the marinas below. Yaletown–Roundhouse station on the Canada Line runs straight to the airport. Across False Creek, Granville Island is a former industrial island under the Granville Street Bridge, known for its public market, studios and theatres, reached by bus or by the small False Creek ferries. Kitsilano, to the west, is residential, with beaches and few hotels.

Where to stay in Yaletown and on Granville Island

  • Opus Vancouver, comfortable. At 322 Davie Street in Yaletown, a 96-room boutique hotel with Capo, an Italian restaurant serving weekend brunch, The Spritz cocktail bar, digital check-in and dog-friendly stays. Choose it for Yaletown’s restaurants and a short walk to the Canada Line. Watch out: Yaletown’s terraces are busy and lively at weekends. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Granville Island Hotel, comfortable. At 1253 Johnston Street, the only hotel on Granville Island, on the False Creek waterfront with the seawall outside, views of the water and the downtown skyline, some rooms with balconies or terraces, and the Dockside Restaurant. Choose it for the market at breakfast and a quiet evening by the water. Watch out: there is no SkyTrain station on the island; downtown is a ferry or bus ride away. Check rates on Booking.com →

Richmond, at the airport: an honest note

Richmond is a separate city south of Vancouver, and YVR sits on Sea Island within it. Its hotels are useful for one night only: before an early flight or after a late arrival. Every Canada Line trip from YVR into Vancouver pays the $6.50 AddFare, which soon eats the $9.53 a night that the lower tax saves on a $300 room.

Where to stay at the airport

  • Fairmont Vancouver Airport, comfortable. Inside Vancouver International Airport, with soundproofed rooms, a pool and hot tub, a spa, the Globe@YVR restaurant and the Jetside Bar. Choose it for the walk from the check-in hall to bed. Watch out: it is a night by the runway, not a base for the city. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Vancouver

  • Count the tax. In the City of Vancouver add about 19.2% to every room rate: a $300 room costs $357.53. Any fee the hotel adds is taxed as well.
  • Watch the season. Summer, when the cruise ships are in, is when rooms are hardest to find. We did not compare rates by season for this guide; check your dates against a week either side.
  • Check the room. Several hotels here mix room types widely. The Sylvia Hotel’s range runs from a Petite Queen to suites with a kitchen, and the Victorian Hotel lists rooms from 247 sq ft for a Legacy King to 450 sq ft for the Rockwell Suite.
  • Staying a month? PST and MRDT stop once the same guest has stayed continuously for more than 26 days.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Vancouver for a first trip?

Downtown and Coal Harbour: the waterfront, the seawall towards Stanley Park and Waterfront station, the end of the Canada Line to the airport, are all a short walk away. Choose the West End for Stanley Park and English Bay, Gastown for the old town, and Yaletown or Granville Island for False Creek and the public market.

How much is the hotel tax in Vancouver?

In the City of Vancouver a room carries 8% provincial sales tax, a 3% municipal and regional district tax and a 2.5% Major Events tax, and then 5% GST on the room and on those taxes together: about 19.2% in all. On a $300 room that is $57.53 a night. The taxes also apply to fees the hotel adds.

How do you get from Vancouver Airport (YVR) to downtown?

Take the Canada Line from YVR–Airport station to Waterfront station, about 25 minutes. Trips from the airport add a $6.50 AddFare: tapping a contactless card costs $11.60 on weekdays before 6:30pm and $10 in the evenings, at weekends and on public holidays. There is no AddFare on the way to the airport.

Do I need an eTA to visit Canada?

If you are a visa-exempt foreign national flying to or through a Canadian airport, yes. It costs CAD $7 on the Government of Canada website, most applications are approved within minutes, and it is valid for up to five years or until the passport expires. U.S. citizens are exempt, and visitors arriving by car, bus, train or boat do not need one.

Is it worth staying in Richmond near Vancouver Airport?

Only for one night, before an early flight or after a late arrival. Richmond has no Major Events tax, but every Canada Line trip from YVR into Vancouver pays the $6.50 AddFare, which soon eats the $9.53 a night that the lower tax saves on a $300 room.

Which Vancouver hotels are close to Stanley Park?

In the West End, the Sylvia Hotel on English Bay is steps from the park, and HI Vancouver Downtown and the Blue Horizon Hotel on Robson Street are a walk away. On Coal Harbour, the Fairmont Pacific Rim has rooms that look over the harbour to Stanley Park, with the seawall at the door.
