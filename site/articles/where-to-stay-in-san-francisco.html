Hotels around Union Square, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Union Square that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

San Francisco’s hotel bill has three layers. The city’s hotel tax is 14%; most downtown hotels also sit in a district zone whose assessments, up to 3.5% of the room rate, may be passed on to guests; and many hotels add a daily fee of $35 or more. On a $300 room that is up to $52.50 in tax and assessments, by our arithmetic below, before the fee. Where you stay decides the rest: the city is compact and hilly, and the airport train runs to Powell Street and Montgomery Street.

This guide covers five areas, from Union Square to the Presidio, 12 hotels, the airport links and the tax. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

San Francisco’s hotel tax and district assessments

The Treasurer & Tax Collector’s page sets out three charges on a hotel room, one tax and two assessments:

Charge Zone 1 (on or east of Van Ness Avenue) Zone 2 (west of Van Ness, and south of 16th Street)
Transient occupancy (hotel) tax 14% of the room rate 14%
Tourism Improvement District assessment 2.25% 2.00%
Moscone Expansion District assessment 1.25% 0.3125%

The 14% tax applies to stays of less than 30 days. The two assessments are charged to the hotel, which may pass them on to guests as a separate item on the bill; the Treasurer’s 2013 notice on the Moscone assessment says neither may be included in the tax billed to the guest. The Tourism Improvement District’s plan draws the zones: Zone 1 covers addresses on or east of Van Ness Avenue, and Zone 2 those west of it. The plan’s list of assessed hotels puts nine of the 12 here, across Union Square, Nob Hill, the Wharf, North Beach and SoMa, in Zone 1. By the plan’s boundary text, the Presidio hotels and the Fort Mason hostel are west of Van Ness, so Zone 2 is the one to ask about; they are not in that list.

Our arithmetic for a $300 room, if the hotel passes everything on:

  • Zone 1 hotel: $42.00 tax, $6.75 and $3.75 in assessments, so $352.50 a night.
  • Zone 2 hotel: $42.00 tax, $6.00 and $0.94 in assessments, so $348.94 a night.

Daily fees. Many hotels add a daily fee on top, so check the hotel’s own site. The Clift lists a $39 destination fee, inclusive of tax; Hotel Zeppelin, Hotel Zetta and Hotel Zephyr each list a $35 amenity fee plus tax a room a night; Stanford Court lists $38 plus tax, and says the fee is itself subject to the city’s occupancy tax. The Mosser’s homepage says “No Amenity Fees”. At $35 plus 14% tax, the fee adds $39.90 a night, which would bring the Zone 1 example to $392.40.

Getting in from SFO, and around

  • By BART, the airport station is in the International Terminal, reached from the other terminals by the free AirTrain, and trains run to Powell Street and Montgomery Street. SF Travel, the city’s tourism board, lists the fare to downtown as $11.15 one way, $22.30 round trip, and BART’s airport page puts the ride at about 30 minutes; check the fare in BART’s own trip planner before you go, as BART adjusts fares from time to time. Hotel Zetta’s FAQ allows about 45 minutes by BART to its door and 20 to 30 minutes by car.
  • To pay, tap a Clipper card or a contactless bank card when you enter and when you leave the BART gates, says Clipper. The city’s visitor page says buses, subways, trains and ferries all take a credit or debit card, or a phone wallet.
  • By SamTrans bus, routes 292 and 397 and the late-night ECR OWL stop at the terminals. The adult fare is $2.05 with Clipper and $2.25 by cash or mobile ticket. SamTrans’s page does not list the downtown stops, so check the route before you rely on it.
  • By taxi or app, several hotels here say they run no airport shuttle, among them Stanford Court and Hotel Zeppelin, and point guests to BART or a ride app. Hotel Zetta puts SFO about 13.5 miles away. Fares depend on traffic and the hour, so look at the app’s quote before you decide.
  • On Muni, an adult ride is $2.85, good for 120 minutes. A cable car ride is $9. The 1-Day Visitor Passport costs $15 and covers Muni buses, Muni Metro, historic streetcars and cable cars, and the 3-Day Passport $35, so by our arithmetic two cable car rides already cost more than the one-day pass.

Union Square: the first-trip base

Union Square is the city’s department-store and boutique district, with Maiden Lane and the theatres on its streets and the Powell Street BART and Muni station close by. Hotel Zeppelin’s FAQ puts it steps from the Powell-Hyde cable car lines and an 8-minute walk from Chinatown. It is the simplest base for a first trip without a car.

A note on the streets. Downtown San Francisco changes character from block to block, and the streets beyond Union Square include the Tenderloin and the Mid-Market stretch of Market Street. The city’s visitor page gives the same advice for everywhere: keep belongings secure in crowded areas and on transit, plan routes in advance, save your hotel address and the nearest intersection in your phone, and call 311 for non-emergency help or ask a uniformed City employee or ambassador. Check the exact block on a map before you book. Hotel Zeppelin’s FAQ answers its own safety question by pointing to 24-hour security and property-wide alarm systems.

  • The Clift Royal Sonesta San Francisco, comfortable. At 495 Geary Street, one block from Union Square and the Theatre District by the hotel’s count, in a building over a century old, with the art deco-inspired Redwood Room bar. Nob Hill: the hilltop and the cable cars

Nob Hill is the crest above Union Square, home to grand old hotels and to the cable car lines that climb it. The trade-off is the slope: Stanford Court’s FAQ tells guests walking from the Powell Street and Montgomery Street BART stations that the 15 minutes are uphill.

Where to stay on Nob Hill

  • Fairmont San Francisco, splurge. At 950 Mason Street, a Beaux Arts landmark with 606 guestrooms and suites in the historic Main Building and the Tower, the Tonga Room & Hurricane Bar and Laurel Court, where afternoon tea has been served since 1907. Choose it for the grand-hotel address on the hill. Watch out: the Moderate rooms, in the Main Building with interior views and one Double bed, which the hotel calls best suited to single occupancy. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Stanford Court Hotel, comfortable. At 905 California Street, at the corner of Powell and California Streets atop Nob Hill, with a gym, a library and a digital art gallery. Choose it for the hilltop in a lower-key hotel. Watch out: Standard rooms are interior-facing and about 205 to 220 sq ft, and the Nob Hill Destination Fee is $38 plus tax a night. Check rates on Booking.com →

Fisherman’s Wharf and North Beach: waterfront and Beat streets

Fisherman’s Wharf is the tourist waterfront, with Pier 39 and the seafront restaurants; the hotels there suit families and short stays. North Beach, behind it on Columbus Avenue, is the old Italian and Beat Generation district. Neither is close to Union Square on foot, and the Powell Street BART station is a ride away, so plan on Muni and the cable cars.

Where to stay at the Wharf and in North Beach

  • Hotel Zephyr, comfortable. At 250 Beach Street, opened in 2015 with a shipyard theme built from recycled cargo containers, 361 guest rooms and an outdoor play area of about 8,000 sq ft called The Yard. Choose it for the waterfront with children. Watch out: the guest amenities fee is $35 plus tax a room night, and overnight self-parking $65 plus tax. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Bohème, comfortable. At 444 Columbus Avenue in North Beach, a boutique hotel of 15 rooms inspired by the Beat Generation, with private baths. Choose it for North Beach’s cafés and bars at the door. Watch out: with only 15 rooms it has little slack in busy weeks, so book early. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • HI San Francisco Fisherman’s Wharf Hostel, budget. In Building 240 at Fort Mason, a waterfront park, with private queen rooms and dorms of four to 20 beds, free parking and a movie room. Choose it for the lowest price on the water. Watch out: it is at the Marina end, 0.8 miles from Lombard Street and 3 miles from the Golden Gate Bridge by the hostel’s count, and buses outside the park connect to the rest of the city.

SoMa and Moscone: museums and the Palace

SoMa, South of Market, is where the Moscone Center, SFMOMA and Yerba Buena Gardens sit; by Hotel Zetta’s FAQ all three are within about 7 minutes’ walk of its door, and Union Square is 7 minutes too. The Palace Hotel stands on the district’s edge, at the Financial District line.

Where to stay in SoMa

  • Palace Hotel, splurge. At 2 New Montgomery Street, a hotel since 1875 that reopened in 1909 after the earthquake, with the Garden Court under its glass dome, the Pied Piper bar and a heated indoor pool, in rooms from 300 sq ft with 11-foot ceilings. Choose it for a landmark address by Market Street. Watch out: the hotel itself places it in the Financial District, near Moscone and Union Square, so it is on the edge of SoMa, not in its centre. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Zetta, comfortable. At 55 Fifth Street, a 5-minute walk from SFMOMA and Yerba Buena Gardens and 7 from the Moscone Center by the hotel’s FAQ, with the Powell Street BART and Muni station 2 minutes away. Choose it for museums and conventions on foot. Watch out: a mandatory Guest Amenities Fee of $35 plus tax a room night, and a refundable $100 hold a night at check-in. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • The Mosser Hotel, good value. At 54 Fourth Street, between Union Square and the Moscone Center, in a Victorian building with space-efficient rooms and, the hotel says, no amenity fees. Choose it for a central bed for less. Watch out: the room list includes a Shared Bathroom Queen, so check the bathroom type before you book. Check rates on Booking.com →

The Presidio: the bridge and the woods

The Presidio is the former Army post at the bridge’s southern end, now a park with trails and museums. Its two hotels are in historic military buildings and offer a very different San Francisco: quiet, with a breakfast included, and a ride from the cable cars and the shops. Their own FAQ points guests to the Presidio Transit Center, Muni bus lines and the Presidio GO Shuttle.

Where to stay in the Presidio

  • Inn at the Presidio, comfortable. A Georgian Revival building that once served as Army officers’ quarters, with 22 rooms, most of them suites with fireplaces, complimentary breakfast and an evening wine and cheese hour. Choose it for a quiet, residential stay at the edge of the forest. Watch out: it is a bus ride from downtown, and overnight parking is $13 a night.
  • Lodge at the Presidio, comfortable. In the Montgomery Street Barracks, built in 1895 to 1897, on the Presidio’s Main Post, with views of the bay, the Golden Gate Bridge or the parkland, complimentary breakfast, and fire pits. Choose it for a base on the park’s main square. Watch out: both hotels charge the first night if you cancel within 72 hours of arrival.

How to book San Francisco

  • Count the tax and the assessments. Add 14%, then the district assessments if the hotel passes them on: a $300 room costs between $348.94 and $352.50.
  • Add the daily fee and the parking. Five hotels here list a daily fee of $35 to $39, and valet parking is $65 plus tax at Hotel Zeppelin and Hotel Zetta and $75 plus tax at Stanford Court. The Presidio hotels charge $13 a night and the hostel parks free.
  • Check room size and bathrooms. Stanford Court’s Standard rooms are about 205 to 220 sq ft, the Fairmont’s Moderate rooms suit one guest, and The Mosser has shared-bathroom rooms. The Palace’s rooms start at 300 sq ft.
  • Bring layers. The city’s visitor page says weather varies by neighbourhood, with fog and wind near the ocean, and advises a warm jacket.
  • Compare totals. Hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book, and hotel taxes around the world puts the rates side by side. Our guides to Los Angeles, New York and Honolulu cover the other big US tax stories.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in San Francisco for a first trip?

Union Square. The Powell Street BART and Muni station is close, the cable car lines are steps from hotels such as Hotel Zeppelin, and the shops and theatres are on foot. Choose Nob Hill for a hilltop base, Fisherman's Wharf and North Beach for the waterfront, SoMa for museums and the Moscone Center, and the Presidio for a quiet stay by the Golden Gate Bridge.

How much is the hotel tax in San Francisco?

14% of the room rate for stays of less than 30 days. Hotels also pay district assessments that they may pass on to guests as a separate item: 2.25% (Tourism Improvement District) plus 1.25% (Moscone Expansion District) in Zone 1, east of Van Ness Avenue, or 2.00% plus 0.3125% in Zone 2, to the west. On a $300 room that is between $348.94 and $352.50 a night if everything is passed on, by our arithmetic.

How do you get from SFO to downtown San Francisco?

By BART from the airport station in the International Terminal to Powell Street or Montgomery Street. SF Travel lists the fare as $11.15 one way ($22.30 round trip) and BART's airport page puts the ride at 30 minutes. Tap a Clipper card or a contactless bank card when you enter and when you leave the gates.

Do San Francisco hotels charge daily fees?

Many do. The hotels' own sites list a $39 destination fee inclusive of tax at The Clift, a $35 amenity fee plus tax a night at Hotel Zeppelin, Hotel Zetta and Hotel Zephyr, and $38 plus tax at Stanford Court. The Mosser's homepage says it charges no amenity fees. Check the fee and any parking charge before you compare prices.

How much does Muni cost, and what does a cable car ride cost?

An adult Muni ride is $2.85 and good for 120 minutes. A cable car ride is $9. The 1-Day Visitor Passport costs $15 and covers Muni buses, Muni Metro, historic streetcars and cable cars; the 3-Day Passport is $35. Two cable car rides cost more than the one-day pass.

Is the Presidio a good place to stay in San Francisco?

It suits a quiet trip. The Inn at the Presidio (22 rooms, mostly suites with fireplaces) and the Lodge at the Presidio (in the Montgomery Street Barracks, built 1895 to 1897) are in historic Army buildings with breakfast included. The trade-off is distance: the hotels point guests to Muni buses and the Presidio GO Shuttle, so downtown is a ride away.
