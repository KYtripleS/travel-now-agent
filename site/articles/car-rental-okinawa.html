Renting a Car in Okinawa (2026-27): Do You Need One, What It Costs and Where to Book

Car hire in Okinawa for one date: DiscoverCars against Toyota Rent a Car, plus the licence and IDP rules, ETC tolls and when to skip the car in Naha.

Outside Naha, the main island of Okinawa is built for cars. The Yui Rail monorail covers the capital, but its line, which we read on its own site, runs only from Naha Airport to Tedako-Uranishi in the Naha and Urasoe area. The aquarium coast, the beaches in the north and most of the cape viewpoints are not on it. If your trip goes beyond Naha, a car is the simplest tool. If you are staying in Naha for a long weekend, it is probably an extra cost, and we say below when to skip it.

Insurance and the excess

Toyota Rent a Car says on its insurance page that “Insurance/Compensation is Included in the Rental Car’s Basic Rate”. The page lists bodily injury and property damage, with property damage carrying a 50,000 yen cost to the customer, and car damage up to the actual value, also with a 50,000 yen deductible. It adds a Non Operation Charge: 20,000 yen if the damaged car is driven back to the shop and 50,000 yen if it is not. We read these terms on 19 November 2026. They also list cases that are not covered, including an accident not reported to the police and driving by someone other than the named applicant, so read the full page.

DiscoverCars treats its optional Full Coverage as a separate product. Its terms say that Full Coverage “is not a financial or insurance product” and that it reduces the customer’s costs when the rental firm charges for damage or theft. They also say the customer is “in most cases” required to leave a security deposit, and that buying Full Coverage does not remove that deposit. Its list of covered costs includes the excess for accident damage and theft, towing, roadside assistance and key loss, up to the limits printed on the voucher. In practice, per the terms, you pay the desk first and claim from DiscoverCars afterwards, with documents. Cancellation under the terms is up to 48 hours before pick-up. The terms describe the money back as booking credit, so read clause 6 before you assume a refund. We read these terms on 19 November 2026.

Rules of the road that change the booking

Your licence decides whether you can drive at all. JAF, the Japan Automobile Federation, lists the ways to drive in Japan with a foreign licence. The one most visitors use is an International Driving Permit issued under the 1949 Geneva Convention. JAF states that permits issued under other treaties, such as the 1968 Vienna Convention, are not valid in Japan. It sets validity at a maximum of one year from the date you enter Japan.

Six places have a different route. Holders of licences issued in Switzerland, Germany, France, Belgium, Monaco or Taiwan can drive by carrying a Japanese translation of the licence together with the original. JAF issues those translations. Its page says the average time is two to three business days, but also says that applications are only accepted from within Japan and points customers outside Japan to a separate link. If your licence is from one of those places, work out how you will get the translation before you fly, not at the airport counter.

Traffic keeps left. JAF’s traffic-rules page says drivers and cyclists must keep to the left and that drivers must yield to pedestrians. Where no sign shows a limit, the default is 60 km/h on ordinary roads and 100 km/h on expressways. Drivers and all passengers must wear seat belts, you must not use a mobile phone while driving, and a child under six must be in a child seat. Ask the rental firm for the child seat when you book.

Tolls and ETC. NEXCO West Japan’s English route and toll search lists Okinawa among its prefectures, so you can check the toll for a given pair of interchanges before you set out. Toyota’s recommended packages include an ETC card, with the note that some shops cannot supply one and would refund it if so. Times says every car is fitted with an ETC device but the card is not included and must be added as an option. If you plan to use the expressway, ask for the card at booking. We did not read a toll fare for Okinawa from an official page, so we print none.

Naha traffic and parking. We found no official figure for congestion, so we do not give one. What we can say from the rental firms’ own pages: the Toyota Naha Airport Shop is about five minutes from the airport by free shuttle and is open 08:00 to 20:00, and Times says to return its car 1 hour 30 minutes before your flight and that its Naha Airport station closes at 18:00. Ask each firm about hotel parking before you book a hotel with a car in mind.

When not to rent

  • You are in Naha for three or four nights. Yui Rail connects the airport, the city centre and the Shuri side of town, and the stops include Asahibashi, Omoromachi and Shuri. For Heiwa-dori and the central streets you do not need the car, and you avoid the parking question. Our Okinawa first-timers guide and things to do in Okinawa show what sits on the monorail line.
  • You are unsure of your licence. Toyota’s insurance page lists driving without a licence as not covered, so settle the paperwork first.
  • You do not want to drive on the left or expect a late-night drive after a long flight. Take the monorail to the hotel and rent for the days you need it.
  • Your plan is a single fixed day in the north. A guided day trip may suit you better than eight days of rental. Check the tours on our things-to-do guide.

    Frequently asked questions

    Do I need a car in Okinawa?

    Outside Naha, usually yes. The Yui Rail monorail, on its own site, runs from Naha Airport to Tedako-Uranishi, so the northern coast and most viewpoints are not on it. If you stay only in Naha, the monorail may be enough and a car adds parking to think about.

    Is DiscoverCars cheaper than renting direct in Okinawa?

    On 19 November 2026, for 21 to 29 November from Naha Airport, DiscoverCars listed a small car from $26.12 a day (about $209 for eight days, our arithmetic) against $574.93 for Toyota's C1 class on its own site. For the standard row it was $64.04 a day for a small SUV against $785.66 for Toyota's C3 class. The two are not like-for-like products, so read the inclusions.

    Which driving permit does Japan accept?

    JAF says an International Driving Permit issued under the 1949 Geneva Convention is valid, and that permits issued under other treaties such as the 1968 Vienna Convention are not. It is valid for a maximum of one year from the date of entry into Japan.

    Can I drive with a Swiss, German, French, Belgian, Monaco or Taiwan licence?

    JAF says holders of licences from those places can drive by carrying a Japanese translation together with the original licence. JAF issues the translation in about 2 to 3 business days on average, but says applications are only accepted from within Japan, so plan how you will get it before you fly.

    Do I need an ETC card for the expressway?

    Not strictly to rent a car, but it is the way the rental firms handle expressway tolls. Toyota's recommended packages include an ETC card where the shop has one, and Times fits an ETC device to every car but says the card is an extra option. Ask for it when you book.

    What is the speed limit and which side do you drive on?

    You drive on the left. JAF says that where no sign shows a limit, the default is 60 km/h on ordinary roads and 100 km/h on expressways, and that all occupants must wear seat belts.
