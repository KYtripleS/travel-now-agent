    The 2026 festive season: dates and what is on

    Forever Edinburgh gives the 2026 dates and the layout. It says there are three main sites. The Traditional Christmas Market is in East Princes Street Gardens, free to enter, with around 70 stalls of gifts, clothing and food and drink, and it is where the LNER Big Wheel stands, 46m tall, along with the Starflyer, 80m tall. West Princes Street Gardens has the Festive Family Funfair, Santa’s Stories and a letter-writing station and post box, and George Street is the third site.

    Edinburgh’s hotel tax: the Visitor Levy and VAT

    Edinburgh is the first place in Scotland to charge a visitor levy. The City of Edinburgh Council says it is a 5% payment on the cost of the paid, overnight accommodation, charged before VAT, for stays from 24 July 2026 onwards, at the same rate every day of the year, and only on the first 5 nights of a stay. It is not charged on extras such as parking, meals, drinks or transport. VAT on hotel rooms is separate: the UK’s standard rate is 20%.

    Item 2026 position
    Edinburgh Visitor Levy 5% of the accommodation-only price, before VAT
    How many nights The first 5 nights of a stay; later nights carry no levy
    Starts Stays from 24 July 2026 onwards
    Not charged on Extras such as parking, meals, drinks or transport
    VAT 20% standard rate, set by the UK government

    Our arithmetic, for illustration only: at a VAT-registered hotel the levy plus its VAT comes to 5% of the VAT-inclusive room price, so a £180 room carries £9 a night, and a four-night stay from 23 to 27 December adds 4 × £9 = £36. A stay of seven nights pays the levy on five of them. Hotels show it differently: Hampton by Hilton Edinburgh West End says the levy is included in the total price it shows, while Hotel du Vin and Malmaison each carry a line on their pages saying a 5% levy applies from 24 July 2026. Check whether your total already includes it.

    Getting in from the airport, and around

    Edinburgh Airport is linked to the centre by tram and by bus.

    • The tram. Trams depart every 7 minutes between 6:30am and 7pm, and every 10 minutes early in the morning and later in the evening, and the airport says the journey to the city centre takes 30 minutes. The first tram leaves the airport at 06:30 and the last at 22:52. An adult airport single is £7.90 and an open return £9.50. The route passes Haymarket, West End, Princes Street and St Andrew Square on its way to The Shore, Port of Leith and Newhaven.
    • Airlink 100. Lothian’s 24/7 express between the airport and the city centre, arriving at Waverley Bridge: £6.00 single and £8.50 open return for an adult. From the airport it runs up to every 10 minutes from 04:12 to 01:00 and up to every 20 minutes from 01:15 to 04:00.
    • In town. A single on the city-zone tram is £2.40 and the adult day cap is £5.70, from Edinburgh Trams.
    • A recent change. Trams had a long disruption this year: Edinburgh Trams said in August that full services between the airport and Newhaven would resume on Saturday 29 August 2026, when Princes Street reopened. We read no later notice.
    • Christmas Eve to Boxing Day. Neither Edinburgh Trams nor Lothian Buses had posted a festive timetable on the pages we read. The tram timetable page says to check its social media or its app for service updates, so confirm the 24, 25 and 26 December times with the operators nearer the date.

    New Town and Princes Street: beside the market

    East Princes Street Gardens and George Street are both in this part of town, and the tram stops at Princes Street and St Andrew Square. It is the busiest area while the market is open, and the one where you walk to everything.

    Old Town: castle views and cobbles

    The Old Town is the Royal Mile, the Grassmarket and the streets between, so a base here puts the castle above you and the market a walk down the hill. The cost is that the streets are steep and cobbled, which matters with luggage.

    Where to stay in the Old Town

    • Virgin Hotels Edinburgh, comfortable. At 1 India Buildings on Victoria Street, which the hotel’s page describes as a luxury hotel in the Old Town, with a red front door and the castle a stroll away. Choose it for Victoria Street’s shops and a short walk to the castle. Watch out: the page we read gave no room count, room sizes or festive offers. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Hotel du Vin Edinburgh, comfortable. At 11 Bristo Place, with 47 rooms and suites, a bistro, wine cellars and a Whisky Snug, which the hotel puts a whisper from the Royal Mile. Choose it for a bistro dinner downstairs. Watch out: the hotel is cashless, so cards and digital payments only. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Apex Grassmarket Hotel, comfortable. At 31-35 Grassmarket, with four room types including Castle View Deluxe and Castle View Superior, a heated indoor pool, a sauna, a gym and the Metro Bar & Brasserie. Choose it for castle views and a pool. Watch out: the castle is a 5-minute uphill walk by the hotel’s own count, and parking is £20 a day, first come, first served. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Radisson Blu Hotel, Edinburgh City Centre, comfortable. At 80 High Street on the Royal Mile, with the Sleeping Beauty Spa, an indoor heated pool, a sauna, a steam room and a gym, and Itchycoo Bar & Kitchen; check-in is from 3pm.     West End and Haymarket: a cheaper bed on the tram line

    The West End and Haymarket sit west of Princes Street. Haymarket and West End are both tram stops, so the market is one tram ride from here, and the airport tram passes through.

    Where to stay in the West End

    • Hampton by Hilton Edinburgh West End, good value. At 166 Fountainbridge, with a free hot breakfast, a 10-minute walk from the Edinburgh International Conference Centre and, by its own page, one mile to Edinburgh Castle. Choose it for a lower price with breakfast included. Watch out: its page puts the castle a mile away, so plan on the tram or a longer walk. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Stockbridge: a village street

    Stockbridge is the area north of the New Town with its own high street, and it suits a quiet night after a day at the market. The cost is distance from the market sites, for which the hotel below gives no walking time.

    Where to stay in Stockbridge

    • The Raeburn, comfortable. At 112 Raeburn Place, built in 1832 as a Georgian family home, with ten bedrooms, a bar, dining, a library, private dining and outdoor terraces. Choose it for a small house in a village setting. Watch out: with ten bedrooms there are few rooms, so book early, and the page gives no walking time to the market. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Southside: a country-house Christmas

    The Southside lies south of the Old Town, towards Arthur’s Seat, and is calmer. Prestonfield House is the one to know here.

    Where to stay in the Southside

    • Prestonfield House, splurge. At Priestfield Road, which says it is just five minutes from Edinburgh’s Old Town, with 23 bedrooms and suites and 20 acres of gardens and parkland. Choose it for a house in parkland that still sits close to the centre. Watch out: the hotel does not say how you make the five minutes, by car or on foot, so check the route. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Leith: the waterfront and the end of the tram line

    Leith is the port area north-east of the centre, and the tram runs through it from Princes Street to Newhaven, with The Shore, Port of Leith and Ocean Terminal among the stops.

    Where to stay in Leith

    • Fingal, splurge. A former Northern Lighthouse Board ship at Alexandra Dock, now a hotel with 23 cabins, each named after a Stevenson lighthouse; the hotel says Port of Leith tram stop is only a 2-minute walk away. Choose it for a night on a ship with the tram at the door. Watch out: it is out from the centre, so allow for the tram ride in. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Malmaison Edinburgh, comfortable. At 1 Tower Place on the Leith waterfront, which says it is only 10 minutes by taxi from the city centre, with the Chez Mal Brasserie & Bar and a banner for its Christmas 2026 offers. Choose it for a brasserie downstairs and a waterfront walk. Watch out: the 10 minutes is by taxi, not on foot, and the page lists no specific Christmas offer. Check rates on Booking.com →

    How to book Edinburgh at Christmas

    • Book early and read the terms. We did not check live availability or prices, so we quote none; the tiers reflect category and facilities, not rates.
    • Count the levy. Make sure the total includes the 5% levy for the first five nights; at a VAT-registered hotel it is 5% of the VAT-inclusive room price.
    • Check the market date. The season starts on 14 November, so a visit before then misses it. If you stay on to 31 December, read the Hogmanay guide first.
    • Check the trams for 24 to 26 December. No festive timetable was posted when we looked, so confirm with the operators.
    • Check room size. Several city-centre rooms are small, so look at the room type if you have luggage.
    • Compare totals. Hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book, and direct booking versus online agencies covers when to go direct. Apex Grassmarket advertised 20% off a stay of two nights or more booked 28 days ahead, and The Scotsman offered a bottle of Prosecco for booking direct online; check the terms.

    Frequently asked questions

    When is Edinburgh's Christmas market in 2026?

    Forever Edinburgh gives 14 November 2026 to 3 January 2027 for Edinburgh's Christmas, with the free Traditional Christmas Market in East Princes Street Gardens, a funfair in West Princes Street Gardens and a third site on George Street. We could not open the organiser's own site, so we have no confirmed daily hours or hours for 24 to 26 December; check it before you go.

    What is the best area to stay for the Christmas market?

    The New Town around Princes Street puts you beside the East Princes Street Gardens market, with Gleneagles Townhouse, Motel One Edinburgh-Princes and The Scotsman Hotel. The Old Town is a walk down the hill, and Leith and the West End are a tram ride away.

    Is there a tourist tax in Edinburgh in 2026?

    Yes. The Edinburgh Visitor Levy is 5% of the cost of paid overnight accommodation, charged before VAT, on the first 5 nights of a stay, for stays from 24 July 2026 onwards. By our arithmetic a £180 VAT-inclusive room carries £9 a night, so four nights add £36.

    How do I get from Edinburgh Airport to the city centre?

    The tram takes about 30 minutes by the airport's own figure, with an adult single at £7.90 and an open return at £9.50. Lothian's Airlink 100 runs 24 hours to Waverley Bridge for £6.00 single or £8.50 return.

    Do the trams and buses run on Christmas Day 2026?

    We could not confirm it. Neither Edinburgh Trams nor Lothian Buses had posted a festive timetable on the pages we read, so check the operators' updates for 24, 25 and 26 December before you travel.

    Can I stay on for Hogmanay after the Christmas market?

    Yes, but book separately and check the terms: New Year's Eve closes Princes Street and carries its own hotel offers, which our Hogmanay guide covers. The 5% levy applies to the first five nights of a stay.
