Kyoto’s maples turn late, and lately later still. The Kyoto observatory’s sample maple counts as fully red on 5 December in a normal year, by the normals for 1991 to 2020; in 2025 it turned on 10 December, and in 2024 not until 20 December, the latest on record. The temples plan their autumn around the second half of November: for 2026, Eikan-do’s autumn opening runs from 11 November to 6 December, with its light-up from 20 November, and Tofuku-ji’s autumn hours run from 14 November to 6 December. When to go: the dates to aim for

By the Kyoto Local Meteorological Office’s count, for its sample trees:

Maple fully red Ginkgo fully yellow
Normal (1991–2020) 5 December 24 November
2025 10 December 25 November
Latest on record 20 December (2024) 7 December (1982)

The observatory’s date is the day its sample tree has turned almost entirely. Gardens with many trees, in sun and in shade, colour over several weeks, and the Japan Weather Association gives Arashiyama’s usual best as mid-November to early December. For 2026, its first forecast, on 1 October, expects Arashiyama at its best from about 13 November; Weathernews, on 3 September, put Arashiyama’s peak around 24 November. Both expect a warm autumn to hold the colour back: Weathernews says later than normal across Japan, the Japan Weather Association normal or later.

Aim for the last ten days of November, and stay four or five nights rather than two, so that an early or a late year still overlaps with your stay.

The 2026 openings, hours and fees

The temples publish their autumn arrangements on their own sites, and several have changed this year:

Temple, autumn 2026 Dates Hours Adult
Eikan-do, autumn treasures exhibition 11 November to 6 December Tickets 9am to 4pm ¥1,500
Eikan-do, light-up 20 November to 6 December Tickets 5.30pm to 8.30pm ¥1,000
Tofuku-ji, Tsuten-kyo bridge and Kaisan-do 14 November to 6 December 8.30am to 4pm ¥1,000
Kodai-ji, autumn night opening 23 October to 13 December 9am to 10pm, last entry 9.30pm ¥800
  • Eikan-do has changed its prices this year. The day and night openings are separate tickets, sold only on the day, and private cars and tour buses are barred from the grounds during the season.
  • Tofuku-ji charges ¥600 for the bridge at other times of year, and sells no combined ticket during its autumn period. Its Hojo garden goes up from ¥500 to ¥600 on 1 November 2026.
  • Kodai-ji is lit from 5pm, and one ticket covers the day and the evening, so you can arrive in daylight and stay; once you leave, you cannot go back in on the same ticket.

Okazaki and Nanzen-ji: Eikan-do on foot

Okazaki is the cultural quarter around Heian Shrine, at the foot of the Higashiyama hills, and it puts the eastern temples on foot. Eikan-do and Nanzen-ji are neighbours, and the Philosopher’s Path runs for about 2 kilometres beside a canal from Ginkaku-ji to Nyakuoji Shrine. Staying here lets you reach Eikan-do as it opens and walk back after the light-up. Keage and Higashiyama stations, on the Tozai subway line, take you downtown in a few minutes.

Gion and Higashiyama: the old city, by day and at night

Gion and Higashiyama are the Kyoto of the postcards, and in leaf season they are also the most crowded. Kodai-ji, off Nene-no-michi near the Ninenzaka slope, stays open until 10pm for its autumn night opening, and Kiyomizu-dera, at the top of Sannenzaka, looks out over a slope of maples from its wooden stage. Staying here lets you walk out at dawn, before the tour groups, and back to the lanes after dark. The Keihan line at Gion-Shijo and the Tozai line at Higashiyama are the nearest stations.

Kyoto Station: Tofuku-ji, and plenty of rooms

Kyoto Station has a large cluster of hotels around it, and in the leaf season that matters: when the old city is full, there are often still rooms here. It is also the closest base for Tofuku-ji, one stop south of Kyoto Station on the JR Nara line; the Keihan line stops there too. JR runs to Arashiyama and to Nara, and the Karasuma subway line runs north through downtown. The trade-off is that the eastern temples are a ride away.

Downtown: the river and the most choice

Downtown, between Karasuma and the Kamo River, has the widest choice of hotels and restaurants, with the Takase canal along Kiyamachi and the banks of the Kamo River for an evening walk. The Tozai line runs east from Sanjo Keihan to Higashiyama and Keage for the eastern temples, and the Keihan line runs south to Tofuku-ji. If the areas beside the famous temples are full, this is where to look first.

Arashiyama: the river and the hills

Arashiyama, on the city’s western edge, is where the river leaves the hills, and in autumn the slopes above Togetsukyo Bridge turn in patches of red and gold. Day-trippers fill it by late morning; staying the night gives you the riverbank early and late. JR’s Sagano line, the Hankyu line and the Randen tram all reach it.

Where to stay in Arashiyama

  • Suiran, a Luxury Collection Hotel, Kyoto, splurge. 39 rooms built around a Meiji-era summer residence on the river, 17 of them with their own hot-spring bath, a three-minute walk from Tenryu-ji and five from Togetsukyo Bridge. Choose it for the river at dusk once the crowds have gone. Watch out: price, and a 15-minute walk from Saga-Arashiyama station. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • HOSHINOYA Kyoto, splurge, ryokan. 25 rooms upriver, every one facing the water; you check in near Togetsukyo Bridge and arrive by the hotel’s own boat. Choose it for a ryokan night reached by river. Watch out: it is not on Booking.com, so book with the hotel.

How to book for a season that moves

  • Book a refundable rate now for the nights you want, then adjust when the forecasts firm up in late October and November. A booking you can cancel costs nothing if the leaves come early or late; check each rate’s cancellation deadline before you pay.
  • Stay longer, not later. Four or five nights across the last ten days of November give you better odds than two perfectly timed ones.
  • Mind the tax. Since 1 March 2026, Kyoto has charged an accommodation tax per person per night, set by the room rate, and peak-season rates can push a room into a higher band:
Room rate per person per night Tax per person per night
Under ¥6,000 ¥200
¥6,000 to under ¥20,000 ¥400
¥20,000 to under ¥50,000 ¥1,000
¥50,000 to under ¥100,000 ¥4,000
¥100,000 or more ¥10,000
  • If Kyoto is full, look at Kyoto Station and downtown first, then at Osaka, under half an hour away by JR Special Rapid; our guide to where to stay in Osaka covers it. For which booking site to use, see hotel booking sites compared, and for the taxes in full, Japan’s tourist taxes in 2026.

Getting around in leaf season

  • Take the train when you can. City buses to the eastern temples and Arashiyama fill up in late November; the Tozai subway line (Higashiyama, Keage), the Keihan line along the Kamo River and the JR Nara line to Tofuku-ji are steadier.
  • Go early. Tofuku-ji’s autumn hours start at 8.30am, half an hour earlier than usual, and the first hour is the calmest.

Frequently asked questions

When are the autumn leaves best in Kyoto in 2026?

Aim for the last ten days of November. The Japan Weather Association's first 2026 forecast expects Arashiyama at its best from about 13 November, and Weathernews put its peak around 24 November. The Kyoto observatory's sample maple turns fully red on 5 December in a normal year, and did so on 10 December in 2025.

Where should I stay in Kyoto for the autumn leaves?

In Okazaki, near Nanzen-ji, for Eikan-do, Nanzen-ji and the Philosopher's Path on foot. Choose Gion and Higashiyama for Kodai-ji and Kiyomizu-dera, Kyoto Station for Tofuku-ji, one stop away on the JR Nara line, downtown for the most choice, and Arashiyama for the river and the hills.

When is the Eikan-do light-up in 2026?

From 20 November to 6 December 2026, with tickets sold from 5.30pm to 8.30pm for ¥1,000. Eikan-do's daytime autumn opening runs from 11 November to 6 December, tickets 9am to 4pm, ¥1,500. Day and night are separate tickets, sold only on the day.

How much does Tofuku-ji cost in autumn 2026?

From 14 November to 6 December 2026, the Tsuten-kyo bridge and Kaisan-do cost ¥1,000 for an adult, against ¥600 at other times, and open at 8.30am. There is no combined ticket in that period, and the Hojo garden costs ¥600 from 1 November 2026.

Which Kyoto temples are open at night in autumn 2026?

Among those in this guide, Eikan-do holds its light-up from 20 November to 6 December, and Kodai-ji opens until 10pm, last entry 9.30pm, from 23 October to 13 December, lit from 5pm, with one ¥800 ticket for the day and the evening.

How much is Kyoto's hotel tax?

Since 1 March 2026 it is charged per person per night by room rate: ¥200 under ¥6,000, ¥400 from ¥6,000, ¥1,000 from ¥20,000, ¥4,000 from ¥50,000 and ¥10,000 from ¥100,000.
