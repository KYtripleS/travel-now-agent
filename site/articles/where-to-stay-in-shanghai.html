Hotels around People's Square, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around People's Square that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

Shanghai’s hotel bill has no tourist tax that we could find, but it has two lines worth checking before you book: 6% VAT, and at some hotels a service charge on top. Whether a rate already includes them differs from one hotel to the next, so the same ¥1,000 room can end up costing ¥1,000 or ¥1,166. Where you stay decides the rest: the city is big, and the metro is what joins its areas.

This guide covers six areas, from the Bund to Hongqiao, 12 hotels, the airport links and the bill. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

What a Shanghai hotel bill adds

Three things can change the total, and none of them is a per-night tourist tax:

Line What we found
VAT on the room 6%. China’s VAT Law, in force since 1 January 2026, taxes services at 6% unless they are listed at 13%, 9% or zero, and hotel rooms are not on those lists
Whether VAT is in the price The law treats VAT as separate from the sales amount, shown on its own on the invoice, but the implementing rules also allow a price that already includes it, so check each rate
Service charge Charged by some hotels, not set by the city. HUALUXE Shanghai Twelve at Hengshan, for one, shows its rate as excluding a 10% service charge
Tourist tax We found no separate per-night tax in the Shanghai government and tax authority pages we read

Hotel pages are not consistent about this. On the day we looked, the Holiday Inn Shanghai Nanjing Road marked its price as including taxes and fees, and the InterContinental Shanghai Pudong marked its price as excluding them. Read the line under the rate.

Here is our arithmetic for a room quoted at ¥1,000, assuming VAT applies to the room and any service charge together; a hotel may calculate it differently, so ask:

If the quoted rate… You pay
already includes VAT and any service charge ¥1,000
includes VAT but excludes a 10% service charge ¥1,100
excludes both a 10% service charge and 6% VAT ¥1,166

A 15% service charge on the same room would add ¥150 instead of ¥100. We did not see one at any hotel in this guide.

Getting in from the airports, and around

  • From Pudong (PVG), which the Shanghai government puts about 30 km from downtown, the maglev runs to Longyang Road station, where you change to metro line 2. The operator sells a single for ¥50, ¥40 with a same-day plane ticket or a public transport card, and a return for ¥80, valid for seven days; it describes the ride as eight minutes. Visa, Mastercard and American Express contactless cards are accepted at its gates at the ¥50 fare.
  • By metro from Pudong, line 2 is the cheapest way in: about ¥7 from Terminals 1 and 2 to People’s Square, and about an hour.
  • By taxi from Pudong, the government’s page gives around ¥180 and 50 minutes to People’s Square.
  • From Hongqiao (SHA), 13 km from the centre, metro line 10 serves both terminals and line 2 serves Terminal 2. A taxi to the centre takes around 30 minutes and costs ¥100 to ¥150.
  • Between the airports, the Airport Link Line takes about 40 minutes, with fares from ¥4 to ¥26 for the full journey.
  • On the metro, a ride is ¥3 for up to 6 km, plus ¥1 for every further 10 km, and a one-day pass is ¥18. At the gates you can tap Visa, Mastercard, American Express, JCB or UnionPay contactless cards, if the card has small-payment contactless switched on.

Checking in, and paying

Hotels register foreign guests: you show your passport at check-in and fill in a form, and the hotel reports the registration to the police, within 24 hours. In 2024 the authorities said hotels must not turn away foreign guests on the grounds that they lack the capacity to process the paperwork. Entry rules vary by passport, so check the National Immigration Administration before you travel.

People’s Square and Nanjing Road: the first-trip base

People’s Square is the middle of the map: lines 1, 2 and 8 meet at its station, People’s Park is beside it, and Nanjing Road, a major shopping street, runs east from here towards the Bund. If you want to reach most of the city without changing much, this is the base to choose.

The Bund: the riverfront and the old banks

The Bund is a 1.5 km waterfront and a protected historical district on the west bank of the Huangpu River. The Shanghai government describes 52 buildings, once the homes of old Shanghai’s financial institutions and trading companies, facing the river. It is busy from the morning exercisers to the evening lights, and it is the place to wake up to a view of Pudong.

Where to stay on the Bund

  • Fairmont Peace Hotel, splurge. A landmark on the Bund for more than a century, with Art Deco-inspired furnishings, a Fairmont Gold executive floor, and a complimentary 30-minute guided tour of its Peace Museum for guests. Choose it to sleep in the Bund’s best-known hotel. Watch out: the hotel describes its Fairmont Gold rooms as interior-facing; for the Pudong view, look at the Signature Riverview Suites. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Indigo Shanghai on the Bund, comfortable. At 585 Zhongshan East 2nd Road, on the South Bund, next to Shiliupu Wharf and Yu Garden, with a swimming pool and two restaurants. Choose it for the river and a pool without the splurge price. Watch out: the hotel puts the nearest metro stations it names, Yuyuan Garden and East Nanjing Road, at about a kilometre. Check rates on Booking.com →

The Former French Concession: historic streets

The Former French Concession is the Xuhui area around Hengshan Road and Huaihai Road, and metro line 1 runs through it. The two hotels below sit close to the line and to the shopping streets, and the older buildings that give the area its name are a walk away.

Where to stay in the Former French Concession

  • Okura Garden Hotel Shanghai, comfortable. A five-star hotel in a 1920s building that was the French Club, with a 30,000 sq m garden, on South Maoming Road in the Huaihai Road shopping district, 100 m from line 1’s South Shaanxi Road station, and with five restaurants and three bars. Choose it for a heritage hotel with a garden. Watch out: it is a large hotel in a busy shopping district, not a lane hotel, and the Superior Room page lists no room size. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • HUALUXE Shanghai Twelve at Hengshan, comfortable. At 12 Hengshan Road, with 169 rooms, a neo-Chinese design, 20 m from metro line 1, and the Former Residence of Soong Ching-ling, Wukang Road and Anfu Road nearby. Choose it for a boutique base on Hengshan Road. Watch out: on 20 October its page showed a rate from ¥1,169.45 and said it excludes a 10% service charge. Check rates on Booking.com →

Jing’an: a park, a temple and shopping

Jing’an is the district around Jing’an Park and Jing’an Temple, with West Nanjing Road’s flagship stores, and metro lines 2, 7 and 14 serve it. It is a base for shops, a park and a temple, with the Bund a metro ride away.

Where to stay in Jing’an

  • The PuLi Shanghai, splurge. Beside Jing’an Park, with a Michelin-starred French restaurant, Phénix, a 25 m indoor infinity pool with views of the park, and rooms from Deluxe to the Jing’an Suite. Choose it for calm in the middle of the city. Watch out: the page lists no rates, so check the total, including any service charge, before you book.
  • Holiday Inn Express Shanghai Jing’an Temple, good value. At 839 Middle Yan’an Road, next to the Shanghai Exhibition Center and Jing’an Temple, near metro lines 2, 7 and 14, with a fitness centre, Wi-Fi and a restaurant. Choose it for a modern hotel on the metro at a lower price. Watch out: standard rooms run from 20 to 37 sq m, so ask for the size of the one you book; there is no pool.

Lujiazui: the skyline

Lujiazui lies directly across the river from the Bund, and Pudong’s government calls it the core of Shanghai’s international financial centre. It holds the Oriental Pearl Tower, the Shanghai International Convention Center and the Shanghai World Financial Center. Hotels here are mostly high-rise, with views to match; the old streets are on the other bank.

Where to stay in Lujiazui

  • Regent Shanghai Pudong, splurge. At 210 Century Avenue, with floor-to-ceiling windows on the skyline, an infinity pool high above the city, and three restaurants: Camelia for Italian food, Mi Teppanyaki, and Shang-Xi for Cantonese dishes. Choose it for the skyline from your room. Watch out: the page lists no rates, so compare the total with any service charge added. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • InterContinental Shanghai Pudong, comfortable. At 777 Zhangyang Road, with a pool, a spa and three restaurants, and Lane 1192, a re-creation of old Shanghai in the Century Link mall, a five-minute walk away. Choose it for Pudong with a pool and a spa. Watch out: its booking page marks the price as excluding taxes and fees. Check rates on Booking.com →

Hongqiao: the airport and the fast trains

Hongqiao is in the west of the city, 13 km from the centre. It is the base for an early flight or a fast train, because the airport’s Terminal 2, the railway station and metro lines 2 and 10 are in one hub. It suits the night before a flight or a train more than a first stay.

Where to stay in Hongqiao

  • Traders Hongqiao Airport, Shanghai, good value. A Shangri-La group hotel connected to Terminal 2, with easy access to metro lines 2 and 10 and the railway station, 520 rooms, and an indoor heated pool. Choose it for an early flight or train. Watch out: the Premier Airport View rooms look out on landings and take-offs. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Shangri-La Hongqiao Airport, Shanghai, splurge. Next door and also connected to Terminal 2, with Horizon Club rooms and suites of 45 to 176 sq m on floors 9 to 12, with lounge access, and two restaurants it shares with Traders. Choose it for a larger room at the transport hub. Watch out: the hotel places itself in Hongqiao’s business district, well away from the Bund. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Shanghai

  • Read the line under the rate. Look for whether the price includes taxes and fees, and whether a service charge is added, then compare totals: a ¥1,000 rate can mean ¥1,000 or ¥1,166.
  • Choose the area for your first morning. If you will be on line 1, 2 or 8, People’s Square puts you on all three. If you have an early flight or train, Hongqiao saves a ride across the city.
  • Check room size. Several of these hotels do not print a size for their entry rooms, and Holiday Inn Express Jing’an Temple’s standard rooms start at 20 sq m.
  • Compare totals. Hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book, and hotel taxes around the world shows how other cities charge. For a nearby comparison, see where to stay in Hong Kong and where to stay in Taipei.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Shanghai for a first trip?

People's Square and Nanjing Road: metro lines 1, 2 and 8 meet at People's Square, and Nanjing Road, a major shopping street, runs east from there towards the Bund. Choose the Bund for the riverfront, the Former French Concession for historic streets on metro line 1, Jing'an for a park and a temple, Lujiazui for the skyline and Hongqiao for an early flight or train.

Is there a tourist tax on Shanghai hotels?

We found no separate per-night tourist tax in the Shanghai government and tax authority pages we read. The extras on a hotel bill are 6% VAT, which a hotel may already include in its price, and at some hotels a service charge: HUALUXE Shanghai Twelve at Hengshan, for one, shows its rate as excluding a 10% service charge.

What is the VAT rate on a hotel room in China?

6%. China's VAT Law, in force from 1 January 2026, taxes services at 6% unless they are listed at 13%, 9% or zero, and a hotel room is not among the services the law lists at 13% or 9%. The law also allows a price that already includes VAT, so check each rate. On a room quoted at ¥1,000 that excludes both a 10% service charge and 6% VAT, our arithmetic gives ¥1,166.

How do you get from Pudong Airport to central Shanghai?

By maglev, metro or taxi. The maglev runs to Longyang Road station, where you change to metro line 2: ¥50 for a single, ¥40 with a same-day plane ticket or a public transport card, and about eight minutes by the operator's description. Metro line 2 is about ¥7 from Terminals 1 and 2 to People's Square and takes about an hour. A taxi to People's Square is around ¥180 and 50 minutes.

Do foreign guests have to register with the police at a Shanghai hotel?

The hotel does it for you. Under Shanghai's official guidance, foreign guests show a valid passport or residence permit and fill in a temporary accommodation form at check-in, and the hotel reports the registration to the local police within 24 hours. In 2024 the authorities said hotels must not turn away foreign guests on the grounds that they lack the capacity to process the paperwork.

Which Shanghai area is best for an early flight or train?

Hongqiao. Airport Terminal 2, the railway station and metro lines 2 and 10 are in one hub, and Traders Hongqiao Airport and the Shangri-La Hongqiao Airport are both connected to Terminal 2. It is 13 km from the centre, so it suits the night before a departure more than a first stay.
