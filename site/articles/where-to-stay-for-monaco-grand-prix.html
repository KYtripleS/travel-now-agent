    The 2027 dates: what is published

    Both official sources have published the 2027 race. Formula 1’s calendar lists the Monaco Grand Prix as round 8, on 4 to 6 June. The Automobile Club de Monaco (ACM) gives 3 to 6 June for the 84th edition, so its weekend starts on the Thursday, with Formula 2, Formula 3 and the Porsche Mobil 1 Supercup also on track. The two describe the same event: Formula 1 lists only its own sessions, from Friday.

    The big change is the format. The ACM says Monaco will host a sprint weekend for the first time, with two qualifying sessions and two races. Formula 1 marks every start time as still to be confirmed.

    What When
    ACM event dates Thursday 3 to Sunday 6 June 2027
    Formula 1 sessions Friday 4 to Sunday 6 June, times to be confirmed
    Friday 4 June, per the ACM Free practice, then a first qualifying session that sets the sprint grid
    Saturday 5 June, per the ACM The sprint from midday, then qualifying in the afternoon
    Sunday 6 June The 84th race
    Tickets On sale since the ACM’s article of 1 October 2026

    Tickets. The ACM’s ticketing article names three official routes: the monaco-grandprix.com site, the official Monaco Grand Prix app, and the ACM ticket office at 44 rue Grimaldi, open Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 17:00. The ticket page lists a Weekend Pass for Saturday and Sunday, a Grand Prix Pass for Friday to Sunday, and hospitality offers. We did not read prices or availability, so check the page itself. We give no resale advice: buy from those channels, and fix your nights once you know which days you hold.

    Tourist tax and the hotel bill

    Monaco. Monaco’s contribution touristique is set per hotel, per person and per night, by ministerial order. The latest order we could verify is Arrêté Ministériel 2024-72 of 5 February 2024, published in the Journal de Monaco on 9 February 2024. We found no later order, so treat these as the last published amounts and ask the hotel to confirm. For the five Monaco hotels in this guide:

    France. The French towns around Monaco each set their own grid, and each includes the 34% regional surcharge for the Ligne Nouvelle Provence Côte d’Azur. Cap-d’Ail is one of the 51 communes of the Métropole Nice Côte d’Azur, so it follows Nice’s grid, which the Métropole’s portal says stays the same in 2027.     The tax is a small part of a race-weekend bill, so use it as a tie-breaker. Monaco’s government says its VAT is levied on the same basis and at the same rate as in France; we found no hotel-specific VAT line, so compare the final total at checkout.

    Getting in and around

    The train is the link that matters. Monaco’s tourist office says there are around sixty round trips a day between Nice and Monaco, with a journey time of 23 minutes, and that regional trains serving Monaco Monte-Carlo station do not require a reservation. The station is underground. The ACM’s page for the 2025 race said the station exits are close to the access points to the public grandstands.

    Link What the official source says
    Nice to Monaco by train Around sixty round trips a day, journey time 23 minutes, no reservation needed on regional trains
    Nice airport to the city The airport lists tram line 2 to the centre in under 30 minutes
    Nice airport to Monaco The airport lists a ZOU! intercity bus 80, Monaco to Menton, at the Terminal 2 coach station; the page gives no fare or time
    Nice airport by rail A direct link with Nice Saint-Augustin station, from where you continue to Monaco Monte-Carlo

    What ran in 2026. The 2027 race-weekend train plan had not been published by early November. For the 2026 race, Riviera Radio reported on 3 June that SNCF Voyageurs and Sud Azur said most regional trains serving Monaco would run in long formation, that train frequency would not change, and that the last trains from Monaco to Nice and Menton would leave between 1am and 2am on Friday and Saturday nights, with Sunday finishing slightly earlier. The same report gave a Grand Prix Pass at €10 a day between Nice and Monaco, and a wider Alpes-Maritimes pass at €20. These are 2026 figures from a news report, so treat any 2027 version as unannounced.

    Not found. We found no official 2027 road-closure plan, airport bus or helicopter schedule, or operator page for extra trains. Read the ACM’s practical information again nearer the date.

    Monte-Carlo: the Casino and the hairpin

    Monte-Carlo is the centre of the circuit and of the price list. The Hôtel de Paris describes itself as in the heart of Place du Casino, and the Fairmont’s page lists a Grand Prix Suite and talks of the iconic Fairmont Hairpin turn. The Novotel is the plainer option, a few minutes’ walk from the train station and Casino Square, according to its own site.

    La Condamine and Fontvieille: Monaco without the palace rates

    La Condamine is the low ground around Port Hercule, and Fontvieille is the newer district to the west. Both are inside Monaco, so the Monaco contribution applies. For the 2025 race the ACM’s page said the event shuttle stopped on Avenue Albert II in Fontvieille, with a further 15-minute walk to the circuit, so Fontvieille is at the edge of the action; that is a 2025 detail.

    Where to stay in La Condamine and Fontvieille

    • Hôtel de France, good value. A boutique-style hotel at 6 Rue de la Turbie in La Condamine, a short walk from Port Hercule and Place d’Armes, with reception open around the clock. Choose it for the lowest Monaco tax in this guide, and for a Monaco address that is not a palace. Watch out: its page gives no room count and no walk to the station, and the 2-star rating comes from the ministerial order, not from the hotel’s page. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Columbus Hotel Monte-Carlo, comfortable. Located in Fontvieille, with 181 rooms including 29 suites, entirely renovated in 2018. Choose it for a larger hotel with more rooms to book. Watch out: its menu carries a Your Grand Prix Experience link with no details, and Fontvieille is further from the circuit than Monte-Carlo. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Beausoleil: the French side, 300 m from the Casino

    Beausoleil sits on the hillside directly above Monte-Carlo, in France. Its tax grid is the town’s own and is lower than Monaco’s. Two hotels here publish a distance to the Casino, and both put it at 300 m.

    Where to stay in Beausoleil

    • Hotel Capitole, good value. A family-owned three-star at 19 Boulevard du Général Leclerc with 19 rooms, which it places 300 m from the Monte-Carlo Casino. Choose it for a small hotel on the French side with the lower three-star grid. Watch out: 19 rooms will go first on a sprint weekend, and its page does not mention parking or the race. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Hôtel Villa Boeri, good value. A hotel on the same boulevard with 30 air-conditioned, soundproofed rooms, a lift, public parking about 100 m away, 300 m on foot from Casino Square and seven minutes from the beach. Choose it for the distance to the Casino and a lift. Watch out: its page does not state a star rating, so the tax category is unclear, and the site looks dated, so confirm your booking directly. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Cap-d’Ail: a quiet stop next to Monaco

    Cap-d’Ail is the next commune west of Monaco and part of the Métropole Nice Côte d’Azur, so Nice’s tax grid applies. The hotel here describes itself as just a few minutes from the Principality.

    Where to stay in Cap-d’Ail

    • Miramar Hôtel, good value. A three-star at 126 Avenue du 3 Septembre with 24 rooms and a sea-view terrace, a fifteen-minute stroll from the Mala beach. Choose it for a small coastal hotel a few minutes from Monaco. Watch out: its page gives no walk to a station, so check how you will travel on race days, and do not confuse it with the Miramar in Monaco. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Menton: the border resort

    Menton is the last French town before the Italian border, which the Royal Westminster’s page describes as a few minutes away. We have no official Menton to Monaco journey time, so check a journey planner. Menton’s grid matches Nice’s at the three- and four-star levels.

    Where to stay in Menton

    • Hotel Napoléon, comfortable. A four-star at 29 Porte de France with a lounge bar and an outdoor pool. Choose it for a pool day before or after the race. Watch out: its page gives no distance to the station and no room count. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Royal Westminster, comfortable. A three-star Belle Epoque grand hotel on the Promenade du Soleil with 92 air-conditioned rooms, run by Vacances Bleues. Choose it for sea views and a lower three-star tax. Watch out: its page says it is open from 13 February to 30 November 2027, and it mentions a half-board formula, so check what your rate includes. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Nice: the train base

    Nice has the most hotels, and the cost is the commute: about 23 minutes each way by train, plus whatever the race-day crowds add. The two hotels below sit near Nice-Ville station. For every Nice area and the tax in detail, see where to stay in Nice.

    Where to stay in Nice

    • Splendid Hôtel & Spa, comfortable. A four-star at 50 Boulevard Victor Hugo with 130 rooms and suites. Choose it for a full-service hotel as a base for the train to Monaco. Watch out: its site gives no walk to Nice-Ville, so measure it yourself. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Maison Durante, budget. A late nineteenth-century townhouse at 16 Avenue Durante with only 23 rooms around a garden, renovated in 2022, which the hotel puts 300 metres from Nice-Ville station. Choose it for being close to the platform, with rooms from €88 a night on its own page. Watch out: the €88 is a starting price for an ordinary night, not a race-weekend rate, and the page does not state a star rating. Check rates on Booking.com →

    How to book Monaco for the Grand Prix

    • Fix your days first. Decide whether you hold the Weekend Pass (Saturday and Sunday), the Grand Prix Pass (Friday to Sunday) or a single seat, then count your nights.
    • Watch the last train. In 2026 the last trains left between 1am and 2am on Friday and Saturday and earlier on Sunday. If you sleep in Nice or Menton, check the 2027 plan once it is out.
    • Check the terms. Small hotels such as Hotel Capitole, with 19 rooms, sell out first, and event terms can appear at checkout.

    Frequently asked questions

    When is the Monaco Grand Prix in 2027?

    Formula 1's calendar lists the Monaco Grand Prix as round 8, on 4 to 6 June 2027. The Automobile Club de Monaco gives 3 to 6 June for the 84th edition, so its weekend starts on the Thursday. 2027 is Monaco's first sprint weekend, and Formula 1 lists every session time as still to be confirmed.

    Where is the best place to stay for the Monaco Grand Prix?

    Monte-Carlo puts you beside the Casino and the circuit at the highest prices. Beausoleil, on the French side, has hotels that say they are 300 m from the Casino. Menton, Cap-d'Ail and Nice are cheaper bases that depend on the train, which takes about 23 minutes between Nice and Monaco.

    How do you buy Monaco Grand Prix tickets?

    The Automobile Club de Monaco names three official routes: the monaco-grandprix.com site, the official Monaco Grand Prix app, and its ticket office at 44 rue Grimaldi, open Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 17:00. The ticket page lists a Weekend Pass, a Grand Prix Pass and hospitality offers. We did not read prices or availability.

    Is there a hotel tax in Monaco?

    Yes. Monaco's contribution touristique is set hotel by hotel, per person and per night. The latest order we could verify, of 5 February 2024, sets 7 euros at the Hôtel de Paris, 5 euros at the Fairmont, 3 euros at the Novotel and Columbus and 2 euros at the Hôtel de France. We found no later order.

    Is the tourist tax lower in Beausoleil, Menton or Nice?

    Per person per night, a four-star pays 3.48 euros in Nice and Menton and 3.08 euros in Beausoleil, against 5 euros at the Fairmont in Monaco. A three-star pays 2.28 euros in Nice and Menton and 2.01 euros in Beausoleil. Those French rates include a 34% regional surcharge and are for 2026.

    How do you get from Nice to Monaco for the Grand Prix?

    Monaco's tourist office says there are around sixty round trips a day between Nice and Monaco, taking 23 minutes, with no reservation needed on regional trains. The 2027 race-weekend train plan had not been published; in 2026, trains ran longer and later, with the last trains leaving Monaco between 1am and 2am on Friday and Saturday nights.
