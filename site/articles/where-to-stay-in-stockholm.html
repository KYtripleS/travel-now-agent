Hotels around Gamla Stan, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Gamla Stan that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

Unlike Amsterdam, Berlin or Paris, Stockholm adds no tourist tax to the hotel bill. Sweden has none, national or local, so the only tax on your room is Sweden’s 12% VAT, and it is already inside the price you are quoted. What changes the cost of a Stockholm stay is the island you sleep on and how you get in from Arlanda, where the fastest train and the cheapest bus are more than 200 kronor apart for one adult.

This guide covers five areas, from Gamla Stan to Djurgården, 12 hotels, the airport links and what the bill really includes. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

Stockholm’s hotel tax: none, and 12% VAT already in the price

Sweden has no tourist tax, national or local. The idea keeps returning to the Riksdag only as a proposal: a 2025/26 motion still asks the government to look into letting municipalities charge a local bed fee. Until something changes, nothing is added per person or per night.

Charge Rate in Stockholm, 2026
Tourist tax, per person and per night None
VAT (moms) on hotel rooms 12%
VAT on breakfast, parking and other services closely tied to the room 12%
Sweden’s standard VAT rate, for comparison 25%

Skatteverket, the Swedish Tax Agency, sets the 12% for room rental in hotels, guesthouses and hostels. The cut in VAT on food to 6% from 1 April 2026 does not touch hotel rooms or restaurant meals, which stay at 12%. And because Swedish consumer rules expect prices to private customers to include VAT, the rate you see is the rate you pay; the Konsumentverket, Sweden’s consumer agency, says that as a private person you should be able to count on prices being quoted with VAT included.

Our arithmetic: a room at 2,000 SEK a night costs 8,000 SEK for four nights, and that total already holds 857.14 SEK of VAT (2,000 × 12 ÷ 112 = 214.29 SEK a night). Nothing is added at checkout. In Amsterdam, by contrast, a 12.5% tourist tax comes on top of 21% VAT. For other cities, see hotel taxes around the world.

Getting in from the airport, and around

Stockholm Arlanda is north of the city. Three public links run into the centre, and which is cheapest depends on how many of you there are.

  • Arlanda Express. The non-stop train to Stockholm Central Station takes 18 minutes and leaves every 12 to 15 minutes. One adult pays 340 SEK one way or 640 SEK return, but groups pay much less each: 460 SEK for two, 580 SEK for three and 700 SEK for four, and up to seven more passengers on the same train cost 120 SEK each. Travellers aged 18 to 25 pay 160 SEK with ID, pensioners over 65 pay 210 SEK, and under-18s ride free with an adult or pensioner ticket holder. It stops at Arlanda South for Terminals 2 and 4 and at Arlanda North for Terminal 5. SL tickets are not valid on it.
  • SL commuter train. The regular commuter trains between Uppsala and Stockholm stop at Arlanda Central Station, in SkyCity between Terminals 4 and 5, and take 38 minutes to Stockholm Central Station. The catch is the station fee: an SL single ticket including the passage fee costs 200 SEK for an adult and 183 SEK at the discounted fare. If you already hold an SL travelcard, you pay only the 157 SEK passage fee, which everyone over 18 pays. Buy tickets in the SL app or at Arlanda Central Station.
  • Flygbussarna. The airport coach from Terminal 5 and Terminal 2 to Stockholm C (Cityterminalen) is advertised at about 40 minutes, with departures every 20 minutes on the route timetable. Tickets online cost 129 SEK for adults and 99 SEK for travellers aged 8 to 25, and up to four children under 8 travel free with each adult.

Our arithmetic for one way into town: two adults pay 460 SEK on the Arlanda Express, 400 SEK on the commuter train and 258 SEK on the coach; four adults pay 700 SEK on the Arlanda Express against 800 SEK on the commuter train. By taxi, Swedavia, which runs the airport, advises you to ask whether a fixed price applies and to check the yellow comparison-price label on the window.

In town, tap a contactless Visa, Mastercard or American Express card, or a phone, on the reader: a single journey is 43 SEK and is valid for 75 minutes of transfers, and there is no touch-out. Each card can pay only for its owner. Travelcards cost 180 SEK for 24 hours, 360 SEK for 72 hours and 470 SEK for 7 days, plus 50 SEK for the green SL card unless you use the SL app, and they cover the ferry between Slussen and Djurgården.

Gamla Stan: the old town base

Gamla Stan is the island where Stockholm began: the Royal Palace, the cathedral, Stortorget with the Nobel Prize Museum, and lanes such as Västerlånggatan that have kept their medieval plan. Riddarholmen, next door, holds the church where Swedish monarchs are buried. Central Station is across a bridge, and Gamla stan metro station is on the island. The lanes are cobbled and busy with visitors by day.

Where to stay in Gamla Stan

  • Lord Nelson Hotel, budget. At Västerlånggatan 22, in a 17th-century house less than six metres wide that the hotel calls Sweden’s narrowest hotel, taking guests since 1974. The rooms carry ship models and a maritime theme, and the owners’ collection of marine antiques fills the house. Choose it for the old town at the lowest price on this list. Watch out: two room types, the Captain’s Family Room and one Small Double, have no window; check the room name before you book. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Victory Hotel, comfortable. The sister hotel, close by in Gamla Stan, where every room is named after a Swedish sea captain, with photos, memorabilia and a model of his ship, from single rooms up to an 18th-century suite. The Leijontornet restaurant and bar are in the building. Choose it for character and the maritime collection. Watch out: the décor is antiques throughout; if you want clean Scandinavian design, look at Hobo or NOFO instead. Check rates on Booking.com →

Norrmalm and the City: trains, shops and the waterfront

Norrmalm is the modern centre: Central Station and T-Centralen, the shopping street Drottninggatan, Kungsträdgården park and the waterfront of Blasieholmen facing the palace. It is the practical base for a short stay, since the Arlanda Express and the airport coach both end here. It is busier and less pretty than the islands.

Where to stay in Norrmalm

  • Grand Hôtel Stockholm, splurge. On the Blasieholmen waterfront, overlooking the Royal Palace and Gamla Stan, receiving guests since 1874, with five restaurants including Mathias Dahlgren and The Veranda, the Cadier Bar and a spa with saunas and pools. Choose it for the classic Stockholm address. Watch out: price. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hobo, good value. A 201-room hotel on Brunkebergstorg, with breakfast on the 13th floor, the TAK rooftop restaurant and a bar with comedy, quiz nights and concerts. Standard and Superior rooms measure 18 to 22 m², and breakfast is almost always included when you book on the hotel’s own website. Choose it for the centre at a fair price. Watch out: the Sleeper rooms are windowless and 12 m². Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Generator Stockholm, budget. A hostel on Torsgatan, about ten minutes from Central Station by the hostel’s count, with dorms of four and six beds, including a women-only dorm, private rooms up to a family room for four, a 24-hour reception, bike hire and laundry. Choose it to keep money for the city. Watch out: a dorm bed means sharing; book a private room if you need quiet. Check rates on Booking.com →

Östermalm: grand waterfronts and food halls

Östermalm is Stockholm’s formal side: the grand façades of Strandvägen along the water, the market hall Östermalms Saluhall, the boutiques of Biblioteksgatan and the bars and clubs around Stureplan. It is a short walk to Djurgården across the bridge, and Östermalmstorg and Karlaplan stations are on the red line.

Where to stay in Östermalm

  • Hotel Diplomat, splurge. An Art Nouveau building at Strandvägen 7C, a hotel since 1911 and the first of the family-owned Diplomat Collection, with views of the waterfront, a restaurant and a wellness area. Choose it for Strandvägen at the door, between Djurgården and Norrmalmstorg. Watch out: price. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Ett Hem, splurge. A private townhouse built in 1910 in Lärkstaden, a quiet residential corner of the district, turned into a small hotel with interiors by the designer Ilse Crawford; the hotel’s own line is that staying there “is like being in a home”. Guests book a table in the kitchen by email. Choose it for something more personal than a grand hotel. Watch out: price, and the quiet is the point: it is a residential street, not a waterfront. Check rates on Booking.com →

Södermalm and SoFo: bars, vintage shops and views

Södermalm, the big island south of Gamla Stan, is where Stockholm goes out. SoFo, the blocks south of Folkungagatan around Nytorget, is full of independent shops, vintage stores and cafés; Mariatorget is a calmer square to the west; Götgatan climbs through the middle to Medborgarplatsen. The northern cliffs, along Monteliusvägen, look back over the old town. The metro’s green and red lines cross the island.

Where to stay in Södermalm

  • The Rival, comfortable. A privately owned hotel on Mariatorget with 99 rooms, a theatre seating 735, The Tavern and Watson’s Bar. The breakfast buffet is always included in the room rate, and some rooms have furnished balconies. Choose it for a quieter square with shows downstairs. Watch out: the room price is set by size and location, so check what the category includes before you book. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • NOFO Hotel, good value. A family-owned hotel of 109 rooms in a heritage-listed building on Tjärhovsgatan in SoFo, with a wine bar and a green courtyard, part of WorldHotels Crafted. Breakfast is always included when you book directly. Choose it for SoFo at the door. Watch out: the Tiny Double is up to 14 m² with a 140 cm bed; the Standard Double, up to 22 m², is the safer choice for two. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Scandic Malmen, good value. A newly renovated hotel at Götgatan 49–51, on the edge of SoFo, with a 1950s look, a restaurant, two bars, an outdoor terrace and bikes to borrow. Standard rooms measure 15 to 17 m². Choose it for a lively base by the metro. Watch out: the hotel sells itself as “the place to start your night”, and it is cash-free. Check rates on Booking.com →

Djurgården and Skeppsholmen: museums and water

Djurgården is the green island east of the centre, home to the Vasa Museum, Skansen, the Nordic Museum, ABBA The Museum and the Gröna Lund amusement park. Skeppsholmen, a smaller island between it and the old town, holds Moderna Museet and the sailing ship af Chapman. There are few hotels and no metro; the ferry from Slussen to Djurgården is covered by SL travelcards. Stay here for mornings in the museums and evenings by the water, not for nightlife.

Where to stay on Djurgården and Skeppsholmen

  • Hotel Skeppsholmen, comfortable. Two buildings from 1699 on Skeppsholmen, converted with the architects Claesson Koivisto Rune, with the Långa Raden restaurant and a tennis court the hotel says King Oscar II built in 1882 as Sweden’s first outdoor court. Choose it for a small island in the middle of the city. Watch out: only the room types named “sea & garden view” promise the view; the plain Standard does not. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Villa Källhagen, comfortable. A hotel and restaurant on the Djurgårdsbrunn canal, surrounded by trees and water, with a reception open around the clock and a restaurant serving breakfast, lunch and dinner. Choose it for green space and quiet. Watch out: evenings are quiet by design: dinner ends at 21:00 from Monday to Thursday and the bar closes at 23:00. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Stockholm

  • Count the tax. There is no tourist tax to add, and the 12% VAT is already in the quoted price: a 2,000 SEK room is 2,000 SEK.
  • Read what breakfast costs. Three hotels here include it on their own terms: The Rival always, NOFO when you book directly, and Hobo almost always on its own website. A booking-site rate without breakfast can end up dearer.
  • Check the room size and the window. Small rooms are common: Hobo’s Sleeper rooms are 12 m² with no window, NOFO’s Tiny Double is up to 14 m², Scandic Malmen’s Standard is 15 to 17 m², and two room types at the Lord Nelson have no window. If you need space or light, book the category above the cheapest.
  • Price the airport run by group size. For two or more adults, the Arlanda Express group fare closes much of the gap with the commuter train; for one person, the coach is cheapest.
  • Compare totals. Hotel booking sites compared and booking direct or through a booking site go through where to book.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the best area to stay in Stockholm for a first trip?

    Gamla Stan, the old town: the Royal Palace, Stortorget and the old lanes are on foot, and Gamla stan metro station is on the green and red lines. Choose Norrmalm for Central Station and the shops, Östermalm for Strandvägen and the food halls, Södermalm and SoFo for bars and vintage shops, and Djurgården or Skeppsholmen for the museums.

    Does Stockholm have a tourist tax?

    No. Sweden has no tourist tax, national or local, so nothing is added per person or per night. The only tax on a hotel room is Sweden's 12% VAT, which is already included in the quoted price.

    What is the VAT on hotel rooms in Sweden?

    12%. Skatteverket sets the 12% rate for room rental in hotels, guesthouses and hostels, and for breakfast, parking and other services closely tied to the room. The cut in VAT on food to 6% from 1 April 2026 does not apply to hotel rooms or restaurant meals.

    How much is the Arlanda Express?

    340 SEK one way or 640 SEK return for one adult in 2026, with group fares of 460 SEK for two, 580 SEK for three and 700 SEK for four. Travellers aged 18 to 25 pay 160 SEK, pensioners over 65 pay 210 SEK, and under-18s ride free with an adult or pensioner. The non-stop trip to Stockholm Central Station takes 18 minutes.

    What is the cheapest way from Arlanda to Stockholm?

    For one adult, the Flygbussarna airport coach: 129 SEK online, about 40 minutes to Stockholm C. The SL commuter train costs 200 SEK including the Arlanda station passage fee and takes 38 minutes. For two or more adults, compare the Arlanda Express group fare: 460 SEK for two.

    Can you pay for Stockholm public transport with a contactless card?

    Yes. Tap a contactless Visa, Mastercard or American Express card, or a phone, on SL's readers: a single journey is 43 SEK and is valid for 75 minutes. Each card can pay only for its owner. Travelcards cost 180 SEK for 24 hours, 360 SEK for 72 hours and 470 SEK for 7 days.
