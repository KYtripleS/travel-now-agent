Hotels around South Beach, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around South Beach that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

Miami hotels charge two things on top of the room rate: tax of 13% on the mainland and 14% on Miami Beach, and, at eight of the 12 hotels here, a daily resort or destination fee that the hotels themselves state at $23 to $65 a night. On a $300 room with a $60 fee, that is about $110 a night before you have left the lobby. Where you sleep decides the rest: Miami Beach is on a barrier island across the bay from downtown, and the airport trains, the free trolleys and the bus all serve different parts of it.

This guide covers five areas, from South Beach to Coral Gables, 12 hotels, the airport links and the tax. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

Miami’s hotel tax

Several charges apply to a hotel room, and which ones depends on whether you sleep on Miami Beach or the mainland:

Charge Miami Beach Mainland Miami, Brickell to Coral Gables
Florida sales tax on transient rentals 6% 6%
Miami-Dade discretionary sales surtax 1% 1%
Convention Development Tax 3% 3%
Tourist Development Tax none 2%
Professional Sports Franchise Facilities Tax none 1%
City of Miami Beach resort tax 4% none
Combined, our arithmetic 14% 13%

The state sets the 6% in Florida law, and the Department of Revenue says the county’s surtax also applies to hotel rooms, with no $5,000 cap. Miami-Dade County says the 3% convention tax applies across the county apart from Surfside and Bal Harbour, and that the 2% tourist and 1% sports-facilities taxes do not apply in Miami Beach, which charges its own 4% resort tax instead. Hotels here match the totals on their own sites: Faena and Freehand both say 14%, and EAST Miami quotes 13% tax on its extra-bed fee.

Resort fees are separate, and each hotel sets its own. Four of the 12 state no amount on the pages we read, the National, the Four Seasons, the Langford and Colonnade, and the guide says so below. Worked example, our arithmetic: a $300 room with a $60 daily fee, taxed on both as Faena and Freehand say they do, costs $410.40 a night on Miami Beach and $406.80 on the mainland. That is $110.40 and $106.80 above the room rate.

Getting in from the airports, and around

  • From Miami International (MIA) by Metrorail, the Orange Line costs $2.25 on Miami-Dade Transit’s fare page and runs from the airport station to Brickell, Government Center and Historic Overtown/Lyric Theatre, with Coconut Grove on the same line. Metrorail runs from 5 a.m. to midnight, and you can tap a contactless card at the gate. On weekends the county says Orange Line trains run only as an airport shuttle between MIA and Earlington Heights, where you change to the Green Line.
  • To Miami Beach by bus, the airport’s page lists the Miami Beach Airport Express, Route 150, from the MIA Metrorail station to Miami Beach, from 41st Street to South Pointe Drive. It runs every 30 minutes from 6 a.m. to 11:40 p.m. at $2.25 each way.
  • By taxi, the airport lists metered fares, a $2.00 airport fee and a $15 minimum from MIA, and says to look for taxis on the arrivals level.
  • By car, three hotels give their own distances from MIA: the Biltmore says 3.7 miles and about 18 minutes, Arlo Wynwood 7.2 miles and 15 to 20 minutes, and the Four Seasons 8 miles and 20 minutes.
  • From Fort Lauderdale (FLL), in Broward County, Tri-Rail runs free shuttle buses from the terminals to its station at Dania Beach, and Broward County Transit’s Route 1 stops at the Rental Car Center. Brightline’s airport shuttle, $12 a guest plus a $3 fuel surcharge with a same-day train ticket, runs twice an hour from 8 a.m. to 7 p.m. between Brightline’s Fort Lauderdale station and FLL, and a matching shuttle serves MIA from MiamiCentral. Arlo Wynwood quotes 30 to 45 minutes by car from FLL, and the Four Seasons 45.
  • Brightline runs between Miami and Orlando, with stops in Aventura, Fort Lauderdale, Boca Raton and West Palm Beach. Its Miami station, MiamiCentral, is at 600 NW 1st Avenue, and Metrorail’s Historic Overtown/Lyric Theatre station is the transfer point.
  • Free transport: Metromover is free, seven days a week, across downtown, Omni and Brickell. The county says the Financial District station is closing for five weeks from Saturday 24 October, with the Brickell Loop still running from the Brickell station. The Miami Beach South Beach trolley and Middle Beach loop run from 8 a.m. to 11 p.m. every 20 minutes or so, and the City of Miami trolley stops on NW 2nd Avenue in Wynwood, Monday to Saturday.
  • Paid transport: Metrobus and Metrorail are $2.25 a ride, a one-day pass is $5.65, and transfers between bus and rail are free.

South Beach: the first-trip base

South Beach is the southern end of Miami Beach, and the part that suits a first trip because you can do most of it on foot: Ocean Drive’s hotels look across Lummus Park to the sand, and the free South Beach trolley reaches Lincoln Road Mall, the Bass Museum and the New World Symphony. The Miami Design Preservation League runs a daily two-hour Art Deco walking tour from the Art Deco Welcome Center at 1001 Ocean Drive, about 1.5 miles on foot, at $50 for adults. Ocean Drive has bars and crowds until late, so ask about a quieter room.

Mid-Beach: big rooms and a quieter beach

North of South Beach, Collins Avenue runs past larger hotels, and the city’s Middle Beach trolley loop, which runs from 8 a.m. to 11 p.m., links them to the rest of the island. You trade South Beach’s streets, which you ride to instead of walking, for room size: Faena’s rooms are roughly twice the size of the Betsy’s.

Where to stay in Mid-Beach

  • Faena Hotel Miami Beach, splurge. At 3201 Collins Avenue, with a pool and beach lounges, a cabaret theatre, a wet spa and big rooms: the Bay View Room is 489 to 615 sq ft, and a Premier Oceanfront Room 727 sq ft. Choose it for space and an ocean view in a design hotel. Watch out: the resort fee is $65 plus 14% tax, and overnight valet is $68++ a vehicle. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Freehand Miami, budget. At 2727 Indian Creek Drive, a five-minute walk from the beach and less than a mile from South Beach, with a pool terrace, two bars and rooms designed by Roman and Williams. It no longer takes bookings by the bed, and a Standard Family Room is 240 sq ft. Choose it for a lower price a short walk from the beach. Watch out: the fee is $35 plus 14% tax, there is no car park of its own, and a $100 deposit applies. Check rates on Booking.com →

Brickell and Downtown: trains, towers and the Metromover

Brickell is the base for towers and trains: Brickell City Centre for shops and restaurants, Metrorail to the airport, and the free Metromover, which runs from Brickell through downtown to the Omni area, past the Kaseya Center. It is a city base, not a beach one, and the sand is a bus or taxi ride away.

Where to stay in Brickell and Downtown

  • Four Seasons Hotel Miami, splurge. At 1435 Brickell Avenue, with what the hotel calls the city’s largest rooftop deck, two acres with a pool, cabanas and hammocks, and rooms of 500 sq ft. Choose it for space and a pool in the middle of Brickell. Watch out: valet parking is $65 a day and self-parking $60. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • EAST Miami, comfortable. Next to Brickell City Centre, with four outdoor pools, a gym and an Urban King room of 300 sq ft with a private balcony. Choose it for Brickell’s shops at the door. Watch out: the destination fee is $54 plus tax a night, you must be 21 to check in, and a $250 a night security deposit is held on your card. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Eurostars Langford, good value. At 121 SE 1st Street in a Beaux Arts building from 1925, once the Miami National Bank, with 126 rooms; the Deluxe room is 26 m². Choose it for a historic building downtown at a lower price. Watch out: the hotel says its rooftop is temporarily closed, and parking is external at $64.20 a night with taxes. Check rates on Booking.com →

Wynwood: murals and bars

Wynwood is the arts district north of downtown. Wynwood Walls, at 2516 NW 2nd Avenue, charges $12 for adults and $5 for 13 to 17 year olds, and is open from 10:30 a.m. to 6:30 p.m. It has no Metrorail station: the nearest are Historic Overtown/Lyric Theatre and Culmer, about 1.5 miles south, with Metrobus Route 2 running up NW 2nd Avenue and the free City of Miami trolley stopping on it.

Where to stay in Wynwood

  • Arlo Wynwood, good value. At 2217 NW Miami Court, with a rooftop pool and bar, a 24-hour gym and bikes for guests, and MIA 7.2 miles away. Choose it for the murals at the door at a fair price. Watch out: the urban fee is $35 plus tax a room a night, parking is valet only from $45 a night, and the hotel says it does not accept tap-to-pay wallets. Check rates on Booking.com →

Coconut Grove and Coral Gables: gardens and a historic pool

Coconut Grove sits on the bay south of Brickell, with Vizcaya Museum and Gardens nearby; Vizcaya says restoration work is under way on site and has cut adult admission from $32 to $20 for now. Coral Gables, further inland, is the city of Miracle Mile and the Venetian Pool, which the city says is fed by spring water from an underground aquifer, opens Tuesday to Sunday and is closed on Mondays. Neither is on the beach.

Where to stay in Coconut Grove and Coral Gables

  • Mr. C Miami Coconut Grove, comfortable. At 2988 McFarlane Road, a boutique hotel with a rooftop pool and Bay views, the Italian restaurant Bellini, and Superior King rooms of 304 sq ft. Choose it for the Grove at the door. Watch out: it has no shuttle or house car, parking is valet only at $55 overnight, and the nightly amenity fee is $28 with tax included. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Biltmore Hotel, splurge. At 1200 Anastasia Avenue, a Coral Gables landmark with a pool of about 23,000 square feet that it calls one of the largest hotel pools in the continental United States, a 10,000 sq ft gym and Miracle Mile about 1.5 miles away. Choose it for a historic resort and its grounds. Watch out: the resort fee is $23 a day, and the hotel is in Coral Gables, not on the sand. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Colonnade, comfortable. At 180 Aragon Avenue, on Miracle Mile, with an outdoor pool and Colonnade Rooms of 309 sq ft. Choose it for walking the Gables’ restaurants. Watch out: a daily destination fee applies, which the hotel’s page does not price, and overnight valet is $46 plus tax. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Miami

  • Count the tax and the fee. Add 14% on Miami Beach and 13% on the mainland, then the hotel’s daily fee: a $300 room with a $60 fee costs $410.40 on the Beach.
  • Pick the side of the bay. The mainland is a point cheaper in tax and closer to the trains; the Beach is closer to the sand.
  • Check the room size. The Betsy’s Classic King is 250 to 275 sq ft, while Faena’s Bay View Room is 489 to 615 sq ft. If you need space, book the category above the cheapest.
  • Check the age rule. The Betsy, Catalina, EAST and Arlo Wynwood ask guests to be 21 at check-in, and the National is adults-only.
  • Compare totals. Hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book, and direct booking against OTAs covers whether to go direct.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the best area to stay in Miami for a first trip?

    South Beach: Ocean Drive's hotels, Lummus Park and the free South Beach trolley are on foot. Choose Brickell and Downtown for Metrorail and the free Metromover, Wynwood for murals, and Coconut Grove and Coral Gables for gardens and quieter streets.

    How much is the hotel tax in Miami?

    14% on Miami Beach and 13% on the mainland, by our arithmetic from the official rates: 6% state sales tax, 1% county surtax and 3% Convention Development Tax everywhere, plus the city's 4% resort tax on Miami Beach, or the 2% Tourist Development Tax and 1% sports-facilities tax on the mainland. Hotel fees are extra.

    How do you get from Miami International Airport to your hotel?

    By Metrorail's Orange Line for $2.25 to Brickell, Government Center or Historic Overtown/Lyric Theatre, or by the Route 150 Miami Beach Airport Express at $2.25 each way. On weekends the Orange Line runs only between MIA and Earlington Heights. A taxi is metered, with a $2.00 airport fee and a $15 minimum.

    Do Miami hotels charge resort fees?

    Most here do. Eight of the 12 state an amount: from $23 a day at the Biltmore to $65 at Faena, plus tax at most. The National and Colonnade say a fee applies without pricing it, and the Four Seasons and Langford pages state none.

    Is there free public transport in Miami?

    Yes. Metromover is free, seven days a week, across downtown, Omni and Brickell, though its Financial District station closes for five weeks from 24 October. The Miami Beach South Beach and Middle Beach trolleys run from 8 a.m. to 11 p.m., and the City of Miami trolley stops in Wynwood Monday to Saturday.

    Can you fly into Fort Lauderdale and stay in Miami?

    Yes. Tri-Rail runs free shuttle buses from the FLL terminals to its Dania Beach station, and Brightline's airport shuttle costs $12 a guest plus a $3 fuel surcharge with a same-day train ticket, running twice an hour from 8 a.m. to 7 p.m. to its Fort Lauderdale station.
