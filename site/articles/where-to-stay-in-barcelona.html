Hotels around Eixample, with prices

The map shows the hotels and apartments around Eixample that booking sites such as Booking.com and Expedia list, with nightly prices; our own picks follow further down. It comes from our partner Stay22, which pays us a commission if you book through it.

Barcelona charges its tourist tax twice over: Catalonia sets a rate per person and per night, and the city adds a surcharge on top. Since 1 April 2026 the two come to €8.40 a person a night at a four-star hotel and €12 at a five-star, and the city’s part rises by €1 every April until 2029. Where you stay decides the rest: how much of the old town you see on foot, and how you get in from the airport.

This guide covers six areas, from the Gothic Quarter to Poble-sec, 12 hotels, the airport links and the tax. The hotels are researched picks, chosen for location, what each property publishes and consistent guest feedback, and each was checked as operating in October 2026. No hotel pays to appear here.

Barcelona’s tourist tax in 2026

Every hotel, hostel and tourist flat in Catalonia collects the tax per person and per night and shows it on the bill. In Barcelona the city’s surcharge is added. From 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027:

Type of accommodation Catalan rate City surcharge Total per person, per night
Five-star and grand luxe hotels €7.00 €5.00 €12.00
Four-star and four-star superior hotels €3.40 €5.00 €8.40
Tourist flats (habitatges d’ús turístic) €4.50 €5.00 €9.50
Youth hostels €1.00 €5.00 €6.00
Other hotels, guesthouses and campsites, including three-star €2.00 €5.00 €7.00

A few rules change the total:

  • Under-17s pay nothing.
  • The tax stops after seven nights per person in the same place over a continuous stay.
  • The surcharge rises every April. The city’s ordinance, approved on 30 January 2026, sets it at €6 from 1 April 2027, €7 from 1 April 2028 and €8 from 1 April 2029.
  • Booking early can lock the rate, if you pay the booking and the tax when you book.
  • Next door it is lower. From 1 October 2026 El Prat de Llobregat, where the airport is, and L’Hospitalet de Llobregat add surcharges of their own: a four-star there costs €3.60 a person a night in all.

Our arithmetic: a couple spending four nights in a four-star hotel in Barcelona pays €67.20 on top of the room (2 people × 4 nights × €8.40). The same four nights in a five-star cost €96. The tax also counts towards the base on which VAT is charged.

Getting in from the airport, and around

  • The Aerobús is the official airport shuttle bus: line A1 from T1 and line A2 from T2, 24 hours a day, every day of the year. Into town it stops at Plaça d’Espanya, Gran Via–Borrell and Plaça Universitat and ends at Plaça de Catalunya. A single costs €7.75 and a return €13.30; under-fours ride free. City travel cards are not valid on it.
  • The R2 Nord train leaves from T2 only (a free shuttle bus links T1), roughly every 30 minutes from 6am to midnight, and stops at Barcelona Sants, Passeig de Gràcia and El Clot-Aragó. Every integrated travel card, the T-casual included, is valid on it.
  • Metro line L9 Sud stops at both terminals, every 7 minutes. Change to L1 at Torrassa, L5 at Collblanc or L3 at Zona Universitària; TMB, the operator, puts the trip to the centre at 32 minutes. The airport stations need the Airport ticket, €5.90, or a pass that covers them: the single ticket and the T-casual are not valid there.
  • In town, a single metro or bus ticket is €2.90. The T-casual, €13 for one zone, gives 10 journeys with changes allowed within 75 minutes. The Hola Barcelona Travel Card gives unlimited travel for 1 to 5 days, from €12.50 for 24 hours (10% less online), and includes one metro journey from the airport and one back.
  • The metro runs from 5am to midnight Monday to Thursday, until 2am on Fridays, and all night on Saturdays.

Eixample: the first-trip base

The Eixample is the 19th-century grid built beyond the old walls: wide streets, chamfered corners and the modernista buildings of Passeig de Gràcia, among them Gaudí’s Casa Batlló and La Pedrera. It suits a first trip: it is central and flat, the Aerobús ends at Plaça de Catalunya on its southern edge, and the R2 Nord stops at Passeig de Gràcia. The old town is a short walk downhill.

The Gothic Quarter: medieval streets

The Barri Gòtic is the oldest part of the city, between La Rambla and Via Laietana: the cathedral, Plaça Sant Jaume with the city hall and the Catalan government face to face, and the lanes of the old Jewish quarter, the Call. The lanes are narrow and busy from morning into the night.

Where to stay in the Gothic Quarter

  • Hotel Neri, splurge. Twenty-two rooms and suites in two historic buildings on Plaça Sant Felip Neri, one of them a 12th-century palace whose courtyard and sgraffito wall decoration were restored. It has a restaurant on the square and a rooftop terrace, Roba Estesa. Choose it for a quiet square in the old town. Watch out: only one room, the Deluxe Balcony, has a balcony over the square, and the hotel’s own package for one night in a Deluxe room with breakfast costs €530. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Gótico, comfortable. A four-star on Carrer de Jaume I, steps from the cathedral and Plaça Sant Jaume, with a 24-hour reception and triple rooms of 24 m² for three. Booking on the hotel’s own website brings early check-in from 10am and late check-out until 4pm. Choose it for the cathedral on foot at four-star tax. Watch out: the double and twin rooms are 18 m², and the singles 13 m². Check rates on Booking.com →

El Born: Santa Maria del Mar and the park

East of Via Laietana, El Born and neighbouring Sant Pere hold Santa Maria del Mar, the 14th-century church of the medieval merchants, the Picasso Museum, the Palau de la Música Catalana and the Parc de la Ciutadella. The streets are as old as the Gothic Quarter’s, with more small bars and shops.

Where to stay in El Born

  • Grand Hotel Central, splurge. A five-star on Via Laietana, between the Gothic Quarter and El Born, in a 1920s building by the Noucentista architect Adolf Florensa that was the home of the politician Francesc Cambó, whose library it keeps. It has a rooftop infinity pool and bar, La Terraza del Central, and a spa. Choose it for a rooftop pool over the old town. Watch out: the rooftop holds regular DJ sessions, and at a five-star the tax is €12 a person a night. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel REC Barcelona, good value. An adults-only hotel of 99 rooms on Carrer del Rec Comtal, about 300 metres from Arc de Triomf station, on metro line L1 and the local trains, with a rooftop pool and a buffet breakfast. Its Quiet REC rooms, of about 15 m², have a private balcony over the inner courtyard. Choose it for El Born and the park at a lower price. Watch out: it takes adults only, so it does not suit families. Check rates on Booking.com →

Gràcia: village squares

Gràcia was a separate town until the end of the 19th century, and it still feels like one: small squares with café terraces, low buildings, and the Festa Major street festival every August. It starts where Passeig de Gràcia crosses the Diagonal, with Park Güell on the hill above. The old town is a metro ride away.

Where to stay in Gràcia

  • Hotel Casa Fuster, splurge. A five-star grand luxe hotel in a building by Lluís Domènech i Montaner, finished in 1908, at the top of Passeig de Gràcia where Gràcia begins: 105 rooms and suites, a spa, a rooftop terrace, an outdoor pool and a jazz club on Thursdays from 9pm to 11pm. Choose it for a modernista landmark you can sleep in. Watch out: the old town is a metro ride away, and the tax is €12 a person a night. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Generator Barcelona, budget. On Carrer de Còrsega, at the edge of Gràcia, with dorms of four, six and eight beds with privacy boards and their own bathrooms, female-only dorms, private rooms, some with terraces, and a bar, Lights of Gràcia, hung with 300 lanterns. Choose it for the lowest price here. Watch out: the larger dorms sleep eight. Check rates on Booking.com →

Barceloneta and Port Vell: the beach and the harbour

Port Vell, the old harbour, starts at the foot of La Rambla. Barceloneta, the 18th-century fishermen’s quarter beyond it, has the city beaches, Barceloneta and Sant Sebastià, and restaurants for paella and fish. The Gothic Quarter and El Born are a walk away.

Where to stay in Barceloneta and Port Vell

  • Hotel Duquesa de Cardona, comfortable. A four-star superior of 64 rooms on Passeig de Colom, facing Port Vell, in a building that belonged to the Dukes of Cardona in the 16th century and was restored in 1850; it has been a hotel since 2003. The rooftop terrace looks over the sea and has a pool and a programme of live music. Choose it for the harbour with the Gothic Quarter behind you. Watch out: many rooms look over Port Vell, not all; book a room sold with the view. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel 54 Barceloneta, good value. A three-star of 28 rooms in the old fishermen’s guild building on Passeig de Joan de Borbó, facing Port Vell and a few steps from the beach, with a roof terrace, Sky H54, over the marina. Barceloneta metro station is 10 minutes’ walk. Choose it for the beach at three-star tax. Watch out: a double or two singles is subject to availability on arrival.

Poble-sec: a neighbourhood by Montjuïc

Poble-sec climbs the slope of Montjuïc between the Avinguda del Paral·lel and the hill, with the gardens, the Olympic grounds and the museums above it. It is residential and known for its bars and restaurants, and the Aerobús stops at Plaça d’Espanya, close by.

Where to stay in Poble-sec

  • Hotel Brummell, comfortable. A hotel of 20 rooms on Carrer Nou de la Rambla behind an 1870 façade, with a pool, a sauna, free yoga classes and a breakfast café, Café Schallert. By the hotel’s count the centre is a 15-minute walk. Choose it for a small hotel in a residential neighbourhood. Watch out: the rooms are small, from 12 m² for the penthouse with its outdoor bathtub and 13 m² for the Petite to 20 m² for the largest. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Barcelona

  • Count the tax. It is per person and follows the category: €8.40 a night at a four-star, €12 at a five-star, €7 at a three-star, €6 in a youth hostel and €9.50 in a tourist flat. It stops after seven nights, and under-17s pay nothing.
  • Book and pay before April 2027 to keep the €5 surcharge; it becomes €6 on 1 April.
  • Check the room size. The smallest rooms here run from 12 to 13 m² at the Hotel Brummell to 15 m² at the Hotel REC and 16 m² for the Passage Mini at Casa Bonay. If you need space, book the category above the cheapest.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Barcelona for a first trip?

The Eixample, around Passeig de Gràcia: it is central and flat, Casa Batlló and La Pedrera are on the avenue, the Aerobús from the airport ends at Plaça de Catalunya and the R2 Nord train stops at Passeig de Gràcia. Choose the Gothic Quarter or El Born for the medieval streets, Gràcia for village squares, and Barceloneta for the beach.

How much is the tourist tax in Barcelona in 2026?

From 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027 it is €12 a person a night at a five-star hotel, €8.40 at a four-star, €7 at a three-star and most other hotels, €9.50 in a tourist flat and €6 in a youth hostel. Each total is Catalonia's rate plus the city's €5 surcharge. Under-17s pay nothing, and the tax stops after seven nights.

Is Barcelona's tourist tax going up?

Yes. The city's surcharge, €5 a person a night until 31 March 2027, becomes €6 from 1 April 2027, €7 from 1 April 2028 and €8 from 1 April 2029. The rate in force when you book applies if you pay the booking and the tax at that time.

How do you get from Barcelona airport to the city centre?

The Aerobús runs 24 hours from both terminals to Plaça de Catalunya, €7.75 one way. The R2 Nord train leaves Terminal 2 roughly every 30 minutes for Sants and Passeig de Gràcia, and the T-casual is valid on it. Metro line L9 Sud serves both terminals but needs the €5.90 Airport ticket or a pass that covers it.

Is the T-casual valid to Barcelona airport?

On the R2 Nord train, yes: every integrated travel card is valid to and from the airport on the train. On the metro, no: the T-casual and the single ticket are not valid at the Aeroport T1 and T2 stations on L9 Sud, where you need the €5.90 Airport ticket or a pass such as the Hola Barcelona Travel Card.

Which travel card is best for a short stay in Barcelona?

For a few rides a day, the T-casual: 10 journeys in one zone for €13. For unlimited travel, the Hola Barcelona Travel Card, for 1 to 5 days, from €12.50 for 24 hours and 10% less online; it also covers one metro journey from the airport and one back.
