Budapest’s two best-known Christmas markets open on the same day this year, 13 November 2026, according to their organisers’ own pages: the Christmas Fair on Vörösmarty Square, and the Advent Basilica in front of St Stephen’s Basilica, which runs to 1 January 2027. The hotel bill has one line that is easy to miss: Budapest has no single city tax, because each district sets its own tourist tax, and in the central districts it is 4% of the room price per guest night, added by the hotel.

Budapest’s tourist tax in 2026

The tourist tax, the idegenforgalmi adó, is set by each district. The decrees and district pages we read for Districts I, V, VII and VIII all give the rate as 4% of the tax base. District VI’s notice for hotel operators, which our general Budapest guide cites, gave the same rate; we did not re-open it for this guide. Under-18s are exempt under the national local-taxes act, which also lists other exempt groups such as hospital inpatients and some students.

Item 2026 position
Rate 4% per guest night in Districts I, V, VII and VIII, and District VI per our earlier guide
District IX We could not open the district’s own page, so we state no rate; ask the hostel
Who collects it The hotel, usually on top of the room price
Exempt Under-18s, and other groups the act lists

The base is where the districts differ in wording. District V’s decree says the room price less VAT, without compulsory breakfast and other services, while District I’s page says the gross room price. The difference is the VAT, so ask the hotel which it uses.

Our arithmetic, for illustration only: two adults sharing a room at 60,000 forints a night before VAT, with no breakfast, for four nights have a base of 240,000 forints, and the tax at 4% is 9,600 forints, or 2,400 forints a night. Hotel taxes around the world compares this with other cities.

Getting in from the airport, and around

The airport’s own pages say the 100E Airport Express runs between Budapest Airport and Deák Ferenc tér, with drop-offs at Kálvin tér, Astoria and Deák Ferenc tér. It runs around the clock: every 5 to 10 minutes by day, every 10 to 15 minutes in the morning and evening, and every 20 to 40 minutes on average at night. It needs its own ticket, the airport shuttle bus single ticket, which BKK, the city’s transport authority, prices at 2,500 forints. Pass holders can buy a 1,000-forint add-on instead, and you can tap a bank card on the bus. BKK’s site was blocked to our automated checks, so we read that price from an archived copy of its ticket page dated April 2026 and found no later report of a change; confirm it on bkk.hu.

The other route is bus 200E, which runs day and night from Terminal 2 to Kőbánya-Kispest, where you change to metro line M3. The airport shuttle ticket is not accepted on it; you need a regular BKK ticket or pass.

    District V: Vörösmarty Square and the Basilica

    District V is the heart of Pest, and it holds both big markets. Vörösmarty Square and Szent István tér are in the same district, so a hotel here puts both markets within a walk, and the 100E ends nearby at Deák Ferenc tér. The cost is that it is the busiest and, in this season, the most expensive part of town to book.

    • Aria Hotel Budapest, splurge. A five-star boutique hotel at Hercegprímás utca 5, which says it is beside St Stephen’s Basilica, with a music-themed design, complimentary breakfast each morning, a year-round roof garden and the High Note SkyBar. The hotel’s site also cites a Condé Nast Traveler Reader’s Choice ranking for 2026. Choose it for a base beside the Basilica, where the Advent market is held. Choose it for the festive offers and the spa. Watch out: the offers are packages with their own terms, and the page lists no room sizes. Check rates on Booking.com →

    District VII: the Jewish Quarter, for lively evenings

    The Jewish Quarter is the late-night side of town, and it lies next to District V, so the two markets are a short way off; we did not time the walk. The noise at night is the trade-off.

    Where to stay in the Jewish Quarter and its edge

    • Corinthia Budapest, splurge. A five-star hotel at Erzsébet körút 43-49 that says it has served the city since 1896, with a six-storey sunlit atrium and the Royal Spa, which it calls the crowning glory. Choose it for a grand hotel with a spa. Watch out: it stands on the ring boulevard rather than in a market square, and the page we read gives no room sizes. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Danubius Hotel Astoria City Center, comfortable. A four-star hotel in District V at Kossuth Lajos utca 19-21, on the edge of the Jewish Quarter, which opened in 1914, with 138 rooms and a metro station and tram line a few steps away. The hotel says the Hungarian National Museum, the Great Synagogue and Váci Street are a 10-minute walk away. Choose it for a historic building and a metro link. Watch out: the hotel says it is only partially accessible, and the garage 300 metres away is HUF 13,000 a day. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Maverick Budapest Soho, budget. A boutique hostel at Kazinczy utca 24-26 that says it is in the heart of the party district, with dorm beds, a female-only dorm floor and private ensuite doubles on the second and third floors. Choose it for the lowest bed price in this area. Watch out: the hostel’s own tagline is the party district, so expect nightlife noise outside. Check rates on Booking.com →

    District VI: Andrássy Avenue and the Opera side

    District VI, Terézváros, is the grand-avenue side of town. It suits a visit that is about more than the markets, and a hotel here means a metro or tram ride, or a good walk, to both squares.

    Where to stay in District VI

    • Mamaison Hotel Andrássy Budapest, comfortable. A hotel at Munkácsy Mihály utca 5-7, which Booking.com lists as Andrássy út 111, whose pages put Heroes’ Square five minutes’ walk away, with a Classic Room of 18 m². Choose it for Andrássy Avenue and Heroes’ Square. Watch out: that is the far, park end of Andrássy Avenue, so the Basilica and Vörösmarty Square are a ride away, not a stroll.     • Radisson Blu Béke Hotel, comfortable. The former Hotel Britannia, a hotel of 174 rooms and 6 suites near Nyugati railway station and a few steps from Andrássy Avenue, close to the theatres and concert halls. Choose it for a large hotel with a restaurant and railway access. Watch out: its parking is EUR 15 to 25 a day, and it is not in a market square. Check rates on Booking.com →

    Buda Castle District: a calmer night across the river

    The Buda side has the views and the quiet, and it is on the other bank from both markets. You cross the Chain Bridge or take a tram or taxi to reach them, which also means a quieter walk home.

    Where to stay in the Buda Castle District

    • Hilton Budapest, comfortable. A hotel at Hess András tér 1-3 that says it is next to Fisherman’s Bastion and Matthias Church, which says it overlooks the Danube and the Hungarian Parliament, in the Buda Castle District, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Choose it for the Castle District setting. Watch out: the hotel itself says the city centre is 15 minutes away. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Hotel Clark Budapest, splurge. A hotel at Clark Ádám tér 1, at the Chain Bridge, with a Castle view room category, a Leo Rooftop bar and a One Michelin Key, which it says it has kept for a second year. Choose it for the Chain Bridge end of the river, a walk from Pest. Watch out: Booking.com lists it as adults only, which the hotel’s pages we read do not state, so check if you travel with children.     Districts VIII and IX: the lowest bill

    South and east of the Grand Boulevard the hotels are older and the prices are lower. You pay for it in walking time or a tram ride to the markets.

    Where to stay in Districts VIII and IX

    • Eurostars Palazzo Zichy, good value. A four-star hotel at Lőrinc pap tér 2 in the Palace District, in a 19th-century neo-baroque palace built by Count Nándor Zichy for his family, with a spectacular dome and 80 rooms. Choose it for a palace setting in the Palace District. Watch out: the hotel’s page gives no walking time to the markets, so check the map. Check rates on Booking.com →
    • Maverick Central Market, budget. A social hostel at Lónyay utca 31 that says it is right next to the iconic market hall, with a bar, games, regular social events and dorm beds. Choose it for the cheapest bed and a food-hall neighbour. Watch out: it is in District IX, whose tourist-tax rate we did not confirm, and it is a ride from the two markets.     How to book Budapest at Christmas

    • Book early and read the terms. We did not check live availability or prices, so we quote none; the tiers reflect category and facilities, not rates. Festive packages such as Kempinski’s carry their own conditions.
    • Count the tax. The 4% is usually added at the hotel, not in the online price, and the base can be net or gross of VAT. Ask which.
    • Choose by how you will move. In District V you walk to both markets; in District VII or VI you add a short tram or metro ride; in Buda you cross the river.
    • Plan for the 28 December and 1 January edges. The Vörösmarty fair’s craft stalls end on 28 December, so a late-December trip is a Basilica trip.
    • Check room size. Mamaison’s Classic Room is 18 m², and several hotels here publish no room sizes, so look at the room type if you have luggage.
    • Compare totals. Hotel booking sites compared goes through where to book, and direct booking versus online agencies covers when to go direct.

    Frequently asked questions

    When are the Budapest Christmas markets in 2026?

    The organisers' pages give 13 November 2026 for both big markets. The Vörösmarty Square fair runs until the dawn of the New Year, usually 11am to 9pm Monday to Thursday and Sunday and 11am to 10pm on Friday and Saturday. The Advent Basilica on Szent István tér runs to 1 January 2027, usually 11am to 10pm Monday to Thursday and Sunday and 11am to 11pm on Friday and Saturday.

    What is the best area to stay for the Budapest Christmas markets?

    District V holds both big markets, on Vörösmarty Square and Szent István tér. Aria Hotel says it is beside St Stephen's Basilica, and Kempinski's festive offer says it is a stone's throw from both markets. The Jewish Quarter is livelier at night, and the Buda Castle District is calmer but across the river.

    Is there a tourist tax in Budapest in 2026?

    Yes. Each district sets its own, and the decrees and district pages we read for Districts I, V, VII and VIII give 4% of the tax base per guest night, collected by the hotel. Under-18s are exempt. By our arithmetic, two adults paying 60,000 forints a night before VAT for four nights pay 9,600 forints. We could not confirm District IX's rate.

    How do I get from Budapest airport to the Christmas markets?

    The 100E Airport Express runs around the clock between the airport and Deák Ferenc tér, with drop-offs at Kálvin tér and Astoria on the way, every 5 to 10 minutes by day. It needs the airport shuttle bus single ticket, which BKK prices at 2,500 forints; the cheaper bus 200E to metro M3 does not accept that ticket.

    What are the Budapest Christmas market hours on New Year's Eve?

    Both organisers list 31 December 2026 as 11am to 3am. The Advent Basilica is open again on 1 January 2027 from 11am to 6pm. The Vörösmarty organiser says its craft exhibitors and small railway are there for the last time on 28 December, before preparations for a New Year's Eve party.

    Which hotel is closest to the Budapest Christmas markets?

    Two hotels say so themselves: Aria Hotel Budapest says it is beside St Stephen's Basilica, where the Advent market is held, and Kempinski Hotel Corvinus Budapest has a festive offer a stone's throw from both markets. Most other hotels here give no walking time to a market, so check a map.
