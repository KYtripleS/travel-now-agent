A Montreal hotel bill has three taxes on it, not one. Quebec’s tax on lodging adds 3.5% of the price of the night, and then the 5% federal GST and the 9.975% Quebec sales tax, the QST, are charged on the room and the lodging tax together. On a $250 room that is $297.50 a night, about 19% more than the rate you were quoted. Prices in this guide are in Canadian dollars.

Quebec’s tax on lodging, and the sales taxes on top

Every hotel in Montreal adds three charges to the room:

Charge Rate Charged on
Quebec tax on lodging 3.5% The price of the night only
GST (federal) 5% The room, the lodging tax and anything sold with the room
QST (Quebec) 9.975% The room, the lodging tax and anything sold with the room

The lodging tax is worked out on the price of the overnight stay alone. Breakfast, parking and anything else sold with the room are left out of it, though they still carry GST and QST. It is charged per room, not per person. When a room is rented to an intermediary such as a tour operator, the lodging tax is a flat $3.50 a night instead.

Revenu Québec lists what is exempt: campsites, rooms in youth tourist accommodation establishments, stays of six hours or less and stays of more than 31 consecutive days.

Our arithmetic, for a $250 room: the lodging tax is $8.75, which makes $258.75; the GST on that is $12.94 and the QST $25.81, for a total of $297.50 a night. Two nights cost $595 rather than $500.

Getting in from the airport, and around

  • From the airport, the STM’s 747 bus leaves from door 28 at Montréal-Trudeau (YUL). Its route 1 serves Lionel-Groulx métro station, then runs along René-Lévesque Boulevard, past many downtown hotels, to its terminus at René-Lévesque and Berri. Route 2 runs only to Lionel-Groulx. Departures are generally every 10 to 20 minutes in the day, and buses keep running through the night at longer intervals.
  • The 747 fare is $11.25, and it is a 24-hour pass for the bus, métro, exo trains and the REM in Zone A. On the bus you must pay in coins with exact change; drivers take no paper bills. You can also buy it from the machines at door 28, which take cash, debit and Visa or Mastercard, or in the Chrono app; activate the app fare before you board and show it to the driver.
  • The REM does not reach the airport yet. The REM’s own update of August 2026 says its YUL-Aéroport-Montréal-Trudeau station is still being built and that the airport branch is planned to open by the end of 2027. Until then, the 747 is the public transport link.
  • In town, a single ride is $3.75, a 24-hour pass $11.25, a 3-day pass $21.75 and a Monday-to-Sunday weekly pass $33.25. The single ticket is not valid on the 747; the 24-hour, 3-day and weekly passes are.

Old Montreal (Vieux-Montréal): cobbles and the Old Port

Old Montreal is the city’s oldest quarter, between the downtown towers and the St. Lawrence River. Notre-Dame Basilica faces Place d’Armes, rue Saint-Paul runs past shops and restaurants, and Place Jacques-Cartier slopes down to the Old Port and its waterfront. The orange line’s Place-d’Armes and Champ-de-Mars stations sit on its edge. It is the most walkable base for a first trip, and the busiest with visitors.

Downtown and the Golden Square Mile: shops, museums and the mountain

Downtown is the commercial centre: rue Sainte-Catherine for shopping, McGill University, and the Montreal Museum of Fine Arts on rue Sherbrooke in the Golden Square Mile, the district of the old merchant mansions. Mount Royal rises behind it, with paths to the lookouts over the city. The green line runs beneath, and the 747 airport bus stops along René-Lévesque Boulevard, which makes this the easiest area to reach from YUL.

Where to stay downtown

  • Four Seasons Hotel Montreal, splurge. At 1440 rue de la Montagne in the Golden Square Mile, with Marcus restaurant and bar, a Guerlain spa on its wellness floor, and the Holt Renfrew Ogilvy department store in the same building. Choose it for luxury beside the city’s high-end shopping. Watch out: price; this is the top of the market. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Le Germain Hotel Montreal, comfortable. A downtown boutique hotel with sixties-inspired interiors by the designer Zébulon Perron, Le Boulevardier restaurant and the Flâneur bar, close to the métro, the REM and the Quartier des Spectacles. Choose it for a design hotel at the centre of things. Watch out: the free cancellation and flexible check-out the hotel advertises apply to bookings on its own website, with conditions.   • Saintlo Montréal, budget. A hostel at 1030 Mackay Street, near Concordia University and the Museum of Fine Arts, with free breakfast in a café that turns into a bar at night, dorms of four, six or ten beds with co-ed or women-only rooms, and private rooms with their own bathroom. Choose it for the lowest price downtown. Watch out: bring a padlock; locks for the lockers are rented. Check rates on Booking.com →

Quartier des Spectacles and the Latin Quarter: festivals and theatres

The Quartier des Spectacles is the city’s entertainment district, around Place des Arts, where the big summer festivals fill the streets and squares. To its east, the Latin Quarter runs along rue Saint-Denis, with the Université du Québec à Montréal, bars and theatres. Berri-UQAM is the hub where the green, orange and yellow lines meet, and the 747’s downtown terminus is a few steps away. Old Montreal is a walk south.

Where to stay in the Quartier des Spectacles

  • Hotel Zero1, good value. At 1 René-Lévesque Boulevard East, with 165 rooms and suites that all have a kitchenette, breakfast on site, a fitness centre and a seasonal outdoor terrace; two métro lines are a block away. Choose it for a kitchenette in the festival district at a moderate price. Watch out: the outdoor terrace is seasonal. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • M Montreal, budget. A hostel at 1245 rue Saint-André, between the Latin Quarter and the Village, with private rooms and dorms, a bar and a café, 24-hour access, RFID keys and lockers. Choose it for a sociable base near Berri-UQAM. Watch out: breakfast and parking cost extra. Check rates on Booking.com →

The Plateau and Mile End: outdoor staircases and bagels

The Plateau-Mont-Royal is the Montreal of the postcards: rows of brick houses with iron outdoor staircases, rue Saint-Denis and boulevard Saint-Laurent lined with cafés and shops, and Parc La Fontaine on its eastern side. Mile End, to the north, is quieter, known for its bagel bakeries on rue Saint-Viateur and rue Fairmount, its cafés and its artists’ studios. The orange line’s Sherbrooke and Mont-Royal stations serve the Plateau. There are few hotels here; most visitors stay on the Plateau’s southern edge.

Where to stay on the Plateau

  • Hôtel de l’ITHQ, good value. At 3535 rue Saint-Denis, the teaching hotel of Quebec’s institute of tourism and hotel management, the ITHQ, where students supervised by professionals look after guests, breakfast is included and a métro station is on site. Choose it for breakfast included and the métro at the door. Watch out: service comes partly from students in training; that is the point of the place.
  • Auberge de La Fontaine, good value. At 1301 rue Rachel Est, a 21-room inn overlooking Parc La Fontaine, with suites that have a park view, some with a balcony. Choose it for a small hotel on the park. Watch out: there is no lift, and breakfast costs extra. Check rates on Booking.com →

Griffintown: the Lachine Canal and new towers

Griffintown was an industrial and working-class Irish district by the Lachine Canal, and much of it is now new apartment towers, with restaurants along rue Notre-Dame Ouest. The canal’s towpath is a walking and cycling route, the Bell Centre and downtown are a short walk north, and Old Montreal is a walk east. It suits a return visit, or anyone who wants to cycle the canal.

Where to stay in Griffintown

  • Alt Hotel Montreal, good value. At the corner of Peel and Wellington, a few minutes’ walk from downtown and the Lachine Canal, with free fast Wi-Fi, a 24-hour gym, and specialty coffee and croissants in the lobby in the morning. Choose it for a simple, modern room between downtown and the canal. Watch out: there is no restaurant, and the 7th-floor terrace is only for private group bookings.
  • Maison Grinder, comfortable. At 1730 rue Notre-Dame Ouest, a 15-room boutique hotel from the Grinder restaurant group; its Chambre Salon rooms are 200 to 265 sq ft, with a king bed, walnut floors and heated stone bathrooms. Choose it for a small hotel run by restaurateurs, among Griffintown’s restaurants. Watch out: the Chambre Salon rooms are on the lobby floor and face rue Notre-Dame; ask for a room higher up if you sleep lightly. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Montreal

  • Count the tax. Add about 19% to every room rate: 3.5% lodging tax, then 5% GST and 9.975% QST on the room plus that tax. A $250 room costs $297.50.
  • Watch the season. Summer is festival season in the Quartier des Spectacles, and the Formula 1 race weekend is big enough that the Four Seasons, for one, sells a race-weekend package. Check the city’s event calendar before you fix your dates, and book early for those weeks.
  • Check the room size. Rooms vary more than the star ratings suggest: the Chambre Salon rooms at Maison Grinder are 200 to 265 sq ft, while Hotel Saint-Sulpice’s lofts start at 500 sq ft. If you need space, read the room description before you book.

Before you go

  • Check whether you need an eTA. Visa-exempt visitors flying to Canada, or transiting through a Canadian airport, need an electronic travel authorization. It costs CAN$7 on the Government of Canada’s official site, most applications are approved within minutes, and it is valid for up to five years or until the passport expires, whichever comes first. US nationals and US permanent residents do not need one. An eTA does not guarantee entry.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Montreal for a first trip?

Old Montreal: Notre-Dame Basilica, Place Jacques-Cartier and the Old Port are on foot, and the orange line's Place-d'Armes and Champ-de-Mars stations sit on its edge. Choose downtown for shops, museums and the easiest airport bus, the Quartier des Spectacles for festivals, the Plateau and Mile End for local streets, and Griffintown for the Lachine Canal.

How much is the hotel tax in Montreal?

Quebec's tax on lodging is 3.5% of the price of the night. The 5% GST and the 9.975% QST are then charged on the room and the lodging tax together. On a $250 room that comes to $297.50 a night, about 19% more than the quoted rate.

How do you get from Montreal airport to downtown?

Take the STM's 747 bus from door 28 at Montréal-Trudeau. Its route 1 runs via Lionel-Groulx métro station along René-Lévesque Boulevard to René-Lévesque and Berri, and buses keep running through the night. The fare is $11.25 and works as a 24-hour pass for the bus, métro, exo trains and the REM in Zone A.

Can you pay for the 747 airport bus by card?

Not on board: on the bus you must pay the $11.25 fare in coins with exact change, and drivers take no paper bills. The machines at door 28 take cash, debit and Visa or Mastercard, and the fare is also sold in the Chrono app.

Does the REM train go to Montreal airport?

Not yet. The REM says its YUL-Aéroport-Montréal-Trudeau station is still being built and that the airport branch is planned to open by the end of 2027. Until then, the 747 bus is the public transport link.

Do I need an eTA to visit Montreal?

If you are a visa-exempt visitor flying to Canada, yes. It costs CAN$7 on the Government of Canada's official site, most applications are approved within minutes, and it is valid for up to five years or until the passport expires. US nationals and US permanent residents do not need one.
