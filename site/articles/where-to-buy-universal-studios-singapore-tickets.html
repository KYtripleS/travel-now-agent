Two of the sellers we could read, KKday and GetYourGuide, showed exactly the same figures. So price is not the main difference this time. The terms are: one of those two listings could not be cancelled, the other could be cancelled free until 15 December, and a third seller, Tiqets, showed only an open-dated ticket for that day.

    How to read it:

    • KKday and GetYourGuide matched the park on the adult and child rows, S$89 and S$69. Neither shows a saving, so choose between them on terms, not price.
    • 16 December is Super Peak. The park’s own booking page labelled it “Adult - Super Peak” at S$89, and KKday’s listing says Super-Peak covers the full months of July, August and December. A visit in a non-peak month would show lower figures.
    • Tiqets showed only open-dated tickets for that day. Its page listed an adult “Flexible Date Admission Ticket” at S$84.99 and a child one at S$78, both described as open-dated, with a visit date to be pre-booked after purchase. We did not see a dated 16 December ticket on Tiqets, and we did not test the pre-booking step.
    • The Express row is not like for like. The park’s S$120 is the price on its own booking page for that date. KKday’s “from S$133.27” is a calendar “from” figure for its separate Express add-on and may depend on the option chosen. GetYourGuide’s Express listing showed only a yuan price to our browser, so we left it out.
    • Viator is “not priced”. Its Universal Studios Singapore page lists many products. The ones we opened were packages, for example a 9 to 10 hour listing with a guide, showing “from $99.50”. We found no plain one-day ticket there that we could price for the date.

    What each seller’s listing says

    The park, rwsentosa.com

    The booking page offers a dated one-day ticket with adult (ages 13 and above) and child (ages 4 to 12) fares, and sells Universal Express only after a ticket is in your cart. Its terms say tickets are “Non-transferable, non-refundable, and not for sale or exchange”, including for events outside its control such as weather. Its terms also say “Universal Studios Singapore admission is not valid for re-entry” and that the park uses facial recognition for access management. The page names other products as well: a Season Pass, an evening ticket, a 3-park pass, and hotel stays that include two one-day tickets. Read 19 November 2026.

    Rules that change the buying decision

    • A date matters. Dated tickets are tied to the day. Tiqets’ open-dated tickets need a date registered after purchase, and the park’s Express passes are “dated and can only be used on the selected date”.
    • Child ages. The park lists child as 4 to 12 and adult as 13 and over; KKday’s page says children under 4 enter free.
    • No re-entry. The park’s terms say admission is not valid for re-entry, so plan meals and bags before you go in.
    • Express is an add-on. The park’s page says Universal Express “is available for purchase only after a ticket has been added to your cart”, and describes Express as skipping the regular queue once at participating rides and shows. It also gives priority entry for the first two hours the park is open. KKday describes the same one-use-per-ride rule and notes that Express “does not cover every attraction or guarantee no waiting”.
    • BTS Fan Village, 16 to 22 December. The park says that on those dates standard adult admission includes complimentary entry to BTS THE CITY ARIRANG Fan Village, collected by showing a valid adult one-day ticket at the Fan Village counter. KKday’s listing describes the same entry for visits on those dates. Whether a particular reseller voucher is accepted at that counter is not stated on the pages we read, so check your listing.
    • Resold tickets. The resort’s general terms say any resale of tickets “is strictly prohibited unless authorized in writing by RWS” and that it may invalidate tickets from unauthorised resale. Buy from a booking platform’s own listing, never a second-hand ticket passed on by another visitor.
    • Special events. Standard admission is for regular operating hours and, per the park’s terms, “not valid during Special Events”.
    • Hours can change. The GetYourGuide listing gave 10:00 to 19:00 for 16 December, and its notes list shorter days for private events on 12 and 14 December. Check the park’s calendar for your date.

    Which seller for which trip

    • You have fixed dates and want to keep the option to cancel. GetYourGuide’s listing said you could cancel free until 15 December for a 16 December visit.
    • You want to decide the day later. Tiqets’ open-dated ticket is the one in our checks built for that, with the visit date registered after purchase.
    • You want Express. KKday lists it as its own product. Buy admission for the same date first, as its page recommends.
    • You are bringing children. The child fare was S$69 on KKday and GetYourGuide for that date. On Tiqets we saw S$78 for an open-dated child ticket.

    Five checks before you pay

    1. Same date, same ticket type. An open-dated ticket and a fixed-date ticket are different products.
    2. The cancellation line. KKday: no cancellation. Tiqets: nonrefundable. GetYourGuide: free until 15 December for our date.
    3. Child age. Ages 4 to 12 are a child ticket; ages 13 and over are adult.
    4. Express needs admission. Buy the ticket for the same day first.
    5. The total in your own currency, after any fee shown at checkout; Tiqets charges in USD.

    Frequently asked questions

    How much is a Universal Studios Singapore ticket on 16 December 2026?

    On 19 November 2026 the park's booking page showed the date as Super Peak: S$89 for an adult (13 and over) and S$69 for a child (4 to 12). KKday and GetYourGuide showed the same figures. Prices change daily and by season, so check your own date.

    Can I cancel a Universal Studios Singapore ticket?

    The park's terms say tickets are non-refundable. KKday's listing says the order cannot be cancelled, modified or refunded, and Tiqets says the ticket is nonrefundable. GetYourGuide's listing for 16 December said "Cancel for free until Dec 15".

    Is a ticket from KKday, GetYourGuide or Tiqets as good as the park's own?

    Each listing describes admission to the park on its date, with a voucher or ticket. The terms differ, mainly on cancellation and date rules, so read the line for your date. We did not read the Klook, Trip.com or Viator terms for a plain one-day ticket.

    Is Universal Express included in the ticket?

    No. The park sells Universal Express only after a ticket is in your cart, and its S$120 price for 16 December was on its booking page. KKday lists Express separately, from S$133.27 for that date, and GetYourGuide's listing says Express is not included.

    How old must a child be to need a ticket?

    The park lists child tickets for ages 4 to 12 and adult tickets for ages 13 and over. KKday's page says children under 4 enter free, and Tiqets says children aged 0 to 3 are free with no ticket needed.

    Can I leave the park and come back?

    The park's terms say admission is not valid for re-entry, and the Tiqets and KKday listings say the same for standard tickets.
