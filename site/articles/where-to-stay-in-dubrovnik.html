Dubrovnik’s tourist tax and VAT

The tourist tax (turistička pristojba) is set each year by the county assembly of Dubrovnik-Neretva, for the City of Dubrovnik among others. For 2026 it is, per person and per night:

Period Hotels and other catering accommodation Campsites
1 April to 30 September €2.65 €1.95
The rest of the year €1.85 €1.30

Under the national Tourist Tax Act, children under 12 do not pay, and people aged 12 to 18 pay half. The tax is charged per person, not per room, so families and groups pay more.

Our arithmetic, not an official example: two adults for five nights in July pay 2 × 5 × €2.65 = €26.50 in tourist tax. The same five nights in February cost 2 × 5 × €1.85 = €18.50. Croatian VAT on hotel accommodation is 13%, against 25% for most goods and services, according to the Tax Administration. When you compare offers, check whether the tourist tax is in the total or payable at the hotel.

Getting in from the airport, and around

  • By the Platanus shuttle, the licensed airport bus, a one-way ticket costs €10 and a return €15, valid for 15 days. The bus leaves from outside the Arrivals hall about 30 minutes after each flight lands. Children under 6 travel free.
  • The stops are the Old Town at the Ploče Gate, about 30 minutes from the airport, then the main bus station at Gruž, about 35 minutes. Platanus lists those two stops, not Pile. The bus station is 500 m from the ferry port for the islands.
  • Back to the airport, the bus leaves the Gruž bus station, then stops at Grawe and at the cable car near the Old Town. From the cable car stop the airport is about 25 minutes away.
  • By Libertas bus, the airport lists city-operator lines 11, 27 and 38 as running daily to and from it. In town, Libertas’s published price list gives a single ticket bought on the bus as €2.50, a 24-hour ticket €5.31 and a three-day ticket €11.95; check the current fare at the stop. Nine of the 14 lines on its city timetable, such as lines 4 and 6, have Pile in their name.
  • By taxi, the airport says the drive takes 30 to 35 minutes on average and up to an hour in summer. It publishes no fare online: a price list is at the taxi information window in the arrivals building.
  • By car, the city’s special traffic regime zone around the Old Town applies from 1 March to 30 November, by the city’s published decision, and vehicle access is by permit. A notice dated 30 July 2026 from Adriatic Luxury Hotels, which runs three hotels here, says the same. Ask your hotel where to arrive.

The Old Town: the first-trip base

The walled city is the reason to come: the Stradun, the walls and the lanes off it, all on foot, with the Pile Gate to the west and the Ploče Gate to the east. The hotels here are in old houses and palaces, so there are few of them. Cars need a permit, so plan the last stretch on foot with your luggage.

Where to stay in the Old Town

Ploče: the sea and the walls

Ploče is the district east of the walls, past the Ploče Gate: the airport shuttle’s Old Town stop, the lower cable car station and the shore facing Lokrum. The hotels below stand on the shore and the cliff above it, and both look back at the Old Town.

  • Hotel Excelsior Dubrovnik, splurge. A five-star at Frana Supila 12 with 158 rooms and suites, split between the original 1913 Villa Odak and a newer building, The Tower, and by the hotel’s count 0.6 km from the Old Town. It has an indoor pool, a spa, a beach and three restaurants. Choose it for a beach hotel beside the city. Watch out: only the Classic rooms in The Tower are listed with a city view instead of sea views. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Villa Dubrovnik, splurge. At Vlaha Bukovca 6, designed in 1961 and a small hotel since 1970, set on a cliff with views of Lokrum and the Old Town, with rooms by Studio Arthur Casas. The hotel says the Sveti Jakov Beach, Orsula Park and the city walls are a short walk away. Choose it for a quiet cliff-top base in Ploče. Watch out: price, and the cliff setting: ask about the walking route when you book. Check rates on Booking.com →

Pile and Miramare: the city buses

Pile is the west gate and the hub of the Libertas city buses. Miramare Bay lies west of the walls, and both hotels below are within a walk of it: by their own count, 10 to 15 minutes from the Old Town.

  • Hotel Bellevue Dubrovnik, splurge. A five-star boutique hotel at Pera Čingrije 7, carved into a cliff over Miramare Bay, with a pebble beach at the cliff’s base; beach services are available for all hotel guests from the summer of 2026. The hotel gives 1.4 km and a 10-minute walk down to the Old Town. Choose it for sea views from the cliff. Watch out: it sits on a 30 m cliff, so the walk back from the Old Town climbs. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Lero, good value. Open since 1972, at Iva Vojnovića 14, 15 minutes’ walk from the walls and 5 from Miramare Beach, with a garage and Classic rooms of 20 m². The newer Superior wing, from 2015, has rooms of 23 to 30 m². Choose it for a walkable base for less. Watch out: the pool is open from 1 May to 1 October, so it is closed until May. Check rates on Booking.com →

Lapad: the beach peninsula

Lapad is the peninsula north-west of the Old Town, with Lapad Bay, a tree-lined promenade and hotels at several prices. The hotels below put themselves 2 to 4 km from the Old Town, which suits a longer stay more than a short sightseeing trip.

  • Hotel Lapad, good value. A family-owned hotel at Lapadska obala 37, begun in 1912 and opened in March 1914, with a Victorian façade, a historic Superior wing and a Classic wing from 1987, 3 km from the Old Town by the hotel’s count. Choose it for a century-old hotel for less. Watch out: the Superior rooms in the historic wing have no balcony. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Kompas Dubrovnik, comfortable. A four-plus-star hotel at kardinala Stepinca 21, facing Lapad beach, with a spa, a bar, two restaurants and rooms with balconies. Choose it for the bay and a spa. Watch out: the hotel puts it 4.1 km from the Old Town. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Hotel Ivka, budget. A small family-owned hotel at Ulica od Sv. Mihajla 21, which describes itself as offering comfort at a reasonable price, with a closed garage, free Wi-Fi and a bus station nearby; it gives the Old Town as 2 km away and the beach as 10 minutes’ walk. Choose it for a small hotel with parking. Watch out: the rooms are compact: 17 m² for a single and 20 m² for a double or twin. Check rates on Booking.com →

Babin Kuk: pools and spas

Babin Kuk is a peninsula where three Valamar hotels stand along Iva Dulčića. The hotels list the town centre as 6,000 m away and a bus terminal 3,000 m.

  • Valamar Lacroma Dubrovnik Hotel, comfortable. A four-star at Iva Dulčića 34, with the 940 m² Balance Mediterranean Spa and Maro World, a 1,800 m² play and entertainment centre. Choose it for a family stay with a spa. Watch out: the resort is built around families: Maro World is billed as the Adriatic’s largest family entertainment centre. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Valamar Tirena Hotel, good value. A four-star at Iva Dulčića 36, 200 m from the sea, with an outdoor pool with water slides and a swim-up bar. Choose it for a pool holiday with children. Watch out: its autumn offer runs only to 22 October 2026, against 29 December at the Lacroma, so check that it is open for winter dates. Check rates on Booking.com →
  • Valamar Argosy Hotel, comfortable. A hotel designed for adults at Iva Dulčića 140, on the Babin Kuk peninsula, with outdoor and indoor pools, including an infinity pool, and a wellness centre. Choose it for a quiet couple’s stay. Watch out: it is for adults, and its autumn offer ends on 24 October 2026. Check rates on Booking.com →

How to book Dubrovnik

  • Count the tax. It is per person, so a family or group pays most: €2.65 a night each from April to September and €1.85 for the rest of the year; children under 12 pay nothing and 12 to 18 pay half.
  • Check the distance. By their own count the hotels here are 0.6 km (Excelsior), 1.4 km (Bellevue), 2 km (Ivka), 3 km (Lapad), 4.1 km (Kompas) and 6 km (the Valamar hotels) from the Old Town. A cheaper room farther out costs a bus ride each way.
  • Check the dates. Hotel Lero’s pool is closed from 1 October, and Valamar’s autumn offers differ by hotel. Confirm the hotel is open for your nights.
  • Check the room size. Some rooms here are small: 12 m² at the Prijeko Palace’s Trnski room, 17 m² at the Ivka’s single. If you need space, book the category above the cheapest.
  • Read the tiers as our judgement. They reflect star rating, setting and size, not a live price list; always compare totals.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the best area to stay in Dubrovnik for a first trip?

    The Old Town, inside the walls, if you want to do the Stradun and the walls on foot, with the Pile and Ploče gates at either end. Choose Ploče, east of the walls, for the sea and the lower cable car station, Pile and Miramare for the city buses, Lapad for beaches and lower prices, and Babin Kuk for pools and spas.

    How much is the tourist tax in Dubrovnik?

    For 2026 it is €2.65 per person a night from 1 April to 30 September and €1.85 the rest of the year at hotels and other catering accommodation, set by the Dubrovnik-Neretva county assembly. Children under 12 pay nothing and people aged 12 to 18 pay half. Two adults for five nights in July pay 2 × 5 × €2.65 = €26.50.

    How do you get from Dubrovnik airport to the Old Town?

    The licensed Platanus shuttle leaves outside the Arrivals hall about 30 minutes after each flight lands. A one-way ticket is €10 and a return €15, valid for 15 days. It takes about 30 minutes to the Old Town stop at the Ploče Gate and about 35 minutes to the Gruž bus station.

    Does the airport shuttle stop at Pile Gate?

    Platanus lists two stops from the airport, the Ploče Gate on the Old Town's eastern side and the main bus station at Gruž, and does not list Pile. For Pile, Libertas runs city buses: nine of its 14 city lines have Pile in their name.

    Can you drive to a hotel in Dubrovnik's Old Town?

    Only with a permit. The city's special traffic regime zone around the Old Town applies from 1 March to 30 November by the city's published decision, and vehicle access is by permit. Ask your hotel where to arrive, and plan the last stretch on foot.

    How much is a bus ticket in Dubrovnik?

    On Libertas's price list a single ticket bought on the bus is €2.50, a 24-hour ticket €5.31 and a three-day ticket €11.95. The airport says Libertas lines 11, 27 and 38 run daily to and from it.
